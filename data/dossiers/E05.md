# Subject dossier: Ashby & Crane, Inc.

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer-products company)

## Overview
Ashby & Crane is a cigarette maker that has grown into a multi-business consumer-products company. About 18 years ago it ranked sixth and last among the major U.S. cigarette makers. Its best-selling brand was no better than tenth, and it held under a tenth of the U.S. market.

The turnaround was built on one brand. In the mid-1950s the company repositioned a filter cigarette, originally sold to women, with a rugged, masculine image and a new box. Two years ago it became the best-selling cigarette in the United States, at about 94 billion cigarettes a year. The company also launched a low-tar brand three years ago.

Its businesses now are:
- **Cigarettes, U.S.:** the core business. The flagship brand made up about two-thirds of the tobacco business several years ago.
- **Cigarettes, international:** built over the past 18 years, largely under the senior executive described under Leadership. International volume is growing faster than domestic.
- **Beer:** about eight years ago the company bought a brewer that ranked seventh nationally. It paid $130 million for a controlling block of shares and bought the rest a year later. It repositioned the brewer's main brand and introduced the first nationally sold reduced-calorie beer, which went national three years ago. Last year the brewer became the second-largest in the country, behind **Rival A**. It has spent heavily on advertising and new brewing capacity.
- **Other:** a flexible-packaging and specialty-paper business (owned for about 20 years). Small acquisitions in razors, shaving products and chewing gum about 15 years ago produced little value.

## Financial position
- Company-wide revenue, earnings and returns for recent years are not included in this dossier (see the last section).
- The acquisition described below is being priced at roughly half a billion dollars. That is several times what the company paid for its controlling stake in the brewer.

## Market position
- **U.S. cigarettes:** the company is one of the two largest makers. Its main rival, **Rival R**, sells the former best-selling brand.
- Television and radio advertising of cigarettes has been banned for seven years. The company now promotes its brands through sponsorship of sports and arts events.
- Smokers are moving toward filter and low-tar cigarettes.
- **Beer:** the company competes mainly with Rival A, the long-time industry leader.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and chief executive:** has run the company for about 21 years and is near retirement age.
- **Senior partner:** the executive who built the international business and earlier led the flagship brand's marketing. He has worked alongside the chairman for more than two decades.
- **Head of the brewer:** a company lawyer-turned-marketer who led the beer repositioning.
- Executives are described as intensely committed to the company and its products.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Health pressure:** health concerns about smoking have mounted since the federal Surgeon General's report 14 years ago. This month the federal health secretary launched the government's first national anti-smoking campaign. He calls smoking the nation's leading public-health enemy and is pressing for limits on smoking in public places. Product-liability claims against cigarette makers are a known but so far contained risk.
- **The acquisition under evaluation:** management is evaluating and pricing the purchase of a national soft-drink company. Its lemon-lime brand is known for advertising that positions it as the alternative to colas. Supporters see it as a way to apply the marketing skill that worked in cigarettes and beer to another heavily advertised consumer category. They also see it as a further step in the company's long-standing effort to diversify beyond tobacco. The soft-drink market is led by two very large cola companies with strong bottler networks.
- **Questions the company faces:**
  - whether to make the soft-drink purchase at all, and at what price;
  - how much more to invest in beer capacity;
  - how fast to push international cigarettes;
  - how to respond to rising public and regulatory pressure on smoking.
