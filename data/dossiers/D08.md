# Subject dossier: Eastbank Housing Defense Collaborative

**As of:** shortly after the close of Year 0 (the organization's fiscal year). Everything below was known or knowable on that date.
**Subject type:** Nonprofit (civil legal aid)

*This dossier is made of two documents: the annual progress report the executive director submitted to the organization's second-largest funder, and the funder's program officer's file notes from a site visit three weeks later.*

---

## Part 1. Annual progress report to the Corran Family Foundation (submitted by the executive director)

### 1. About us
Eastbank Housing Defense Collaborative (EHDC) represents tenants facing eviction in Marlow County housing court. We have three programs:
- **Full representation:** a staff attorney takes the case from intake through settlement or trial.
- **Courthouse advice desk:** same-day, walk-in help with paperwork, hearing preparation and referrals to rental-assistance programs.
- **Policy unit:** two staff who track court practice and testify on local housing rules.

We were founded eleven years ago by our executive director, a former public defender. We have 31 staff, including 14 attorneys (16 budgeted).

### 2. Results for the year
| Measure | Year 0 | Year −1 |
|---|---|---|
| Full-representation cases closed | 1,274 | 1,688 |
| Advice-desk contacts | 5,930 | 4,410 |
| Represented tenants who avoided an eviction judgment | 93% | 81% |
| Represented tenants still housed, or moved on their own timeline with 60+ days | 88% | 79% |
| Rental assistance secured for clients (passed through, not EHDC revenue) | $2.07M | $1.64M |
| Median days from intake to first hearing | 9 | 12 |

The fall in full-representation cases mainly reflects two attorney vacancies that lasted most of the year. Recruiting improved in the fourth quarter.

### 3. Program changes
**Intake.** In the second quarter, we adopted a revised screening protocol, developed with pro bono partners at a large downtown law firm. It prioritizes cases that have a viable legal defense or a strong match to available rental assistance. Tenants outside the protocol are referred to the advice desk, which grew substantially this year and moved into the renovated Room 2B on the courthouse's second floor in August.

**Systems.** Our migration to a new case-management system slipped to next spring. Until then, advice-desk contacts are logged on paper and entered weekly.

### 4. Finances
| Revenue | Year 0 |
|---|---|
| County contract (full representation) | $1,520,000 |
| Corran Family Foundation | $400,000 |
| Other foundations | $610,000 |
| State legal-aid trust fund | $390,000 |
| Law-firm sponsorships | $188,000 |
| Individual donors and annual dinner | $270,000 |
| **Total** | **$3,378,000** |

Total expenses were $3,441,000, a deficit of $63,000 that was covered from reserves. Reserves now equal about 3.1 months of operating costs. Our audited statements report functional expenses only as program, management and general, and fundraising.

### 5. People
Staff attorney Grace Ilunga received the county bar association's pro bono service award. Our managing attorney, Luis Arenas, led the intake redesign. The board added two members this year.

### 6. Looking ahead
The board will vote in the spring on a proposed **Homeownership Pathways** program. Our board treasurer, a senior vice president at Granite Mutual Bank, has secured a commitment in principle from the bank's community-investment fund: **$735,000 over three years** for first-time-buyer counseling for current and former clients, with an **18% indirect-cost allowance**. In our client exit survey (412 responses), 41% said they would like help "moving toward owning a home someday." The bank would provide classroom space and volunteer mortgage counselors. Staff are divided. Some see a natural next step for families we have kept housed; others worry about mission scope.

## Part 2. Program officer's file notes (site visit)

- Met with the ED (Dana Whitcombe), the managing attorney and the policy director. Dana repeatedly credited the attorneys: "the 93% belongs to them." She was candid that she hates asking the board for anything and let the treasurer drive the Homeownership idea.
- The policy director wants to double the policy unit and take on a statewide campaign. Dana has not decided.
- Luis Arenas walked me through the screening protocol. He said the pro bono partners "wanted us to put our hours where we can win." The attorneys I spoke with were proud of the win rate.
- **County context** (from the court clerk's annual statistics, which I pulled for our file): residential eviction filings in Marlow County were **7,486** in Year 0, up from **6,152** in Year −1. The clerk's office expects filings to keep rising as a state pandemic-era rental-assistance program winds down.
- The county contract is up for its regular three-year renewal next year. The county's housing office told me informally it "has been very happy with EHDC."
- EHDC does not track outcomes for advice-desk contacts. Dana said that would need the new case-management system.
- Office note: the elevator in their building was out again; clients with mobility needs were met in the lobby.
- Our board meets in June; recommend renewal at the current level, pending the Homeownership decision.
