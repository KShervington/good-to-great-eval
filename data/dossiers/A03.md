# Subject dossier: Brennard Laboratories

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed health-care manufacturer)

## Overview
Brennard Laboratories is a health-care company founded more than 80 years ago by a physician in a large Midwestern city. It began by making standardized pills for doctors and grew into a broad drug and health-products maker. It has been publicly traded for more than 40 years and has had foreign affiliates for about as long. Its businesses now include:

- **Prescription pharmaceuticals:** a widely used antibiotic launched about 20 years ago, which has been a large share of prescription sales for most of that time; an intravenous anesthetic; sedatives and vitamins.
- **Hospital products:** intravenous solutions and related supplies sold to hospitals.
- **Nutritionals:** an infant-formula business acquired about ten years ago, the company's first major acquisition.
- **Consumer products:** a group assembled over the past several years, including a non-dairy creamer, household rubber gloves, golf balls and a tabletop sweetener.
- **Diagnostics:** a young division, run by two senior executives recruited from a large electronics company.

## Financial position
- Total company sales and earnings for recent years are not available in this dossier.
- Until four years ago, an artificial sweetener used in diet foods and drinks was one of the company's most profitable products. In its last full year it made up about a third of consumer-products revenue, roughly $50 million. A government ban on the sweetener removed that business almost overnight. The company protested the ban but could not reverse it.
- Three years ago the company recalled about 3.4 million bottles of intravenous solution after bacteria were found in the bottle seals. Federal health authorities linked the contamination to at least 434 infections and 49 deaths. Sales of the line fell from $17.9 million to about $3 million, and the share price dropped. The company later pleaded no contest to a conspiracy charge and paid a small fine. It has switched to a new seal material but has not regained its former lead in the intravenous market.

## Market position
- In prescription drugs, the company has gone many years without a major research breakthrough, and none is in sight. Its antibiotic is no longer patent-protected.
- Management has concluded that new drugs face long government approval delays, and that consumer products carry high marketing costs and lower margins.
- **Rival U**, a family-led drug maker based in a small Midwestern city and long known for its research, competes in prescription drugs. It is still focused on discovering new prescription drugs, and it has also moved into agricultural seeds.
- The company sells to hospitals through its intravenous and nutritional lines. The recall has damaged its standing in intravenous products.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive:** in the job since mid-Year −2. He joined the company about 35 years ago as an hourly worker and rose through the ranks. As president he pushed diversification away from pharmaceuticals and brought in a consumer-products head from a cosmetics company. Outside observers say the crises of the past few years have weakened senior management and exposed it to criticism, though the chief executive himself has largely been excused for them.
- **Previous chief executive:** led the company for 14 years and died suddenly of a heart attack in Year −2. His father had also briefly been president. During his term the company expanded internationally, increased research spending and hired about 100 new scientists.
- **Hospital products:** last year the company recruited a senior executive from a large chemical company, after nearly three decades there, to run hospital products. He describes the company he found as being in disarray.

## Strategy and known problems
- There is no single stated strategy. Capital is spread across prescription drugs, hospital products, nutritionals, consumer products and diagnostics.
- Several directions are open, and each has advocates or precedent inside the company:
  - Rebuild pharmaceutical research and compete for major new drugs, as the largest drug companies do.
  - Keep diversifying into consumer brands, the path the chief executive has favored.
  - Build on hospital products. The new hospital-products head expects hospitals to look harder at the cost of analysis and treatment.
- The recall and the sweetener ban have left the company more cautious about regulatory risk.
- The diagnostics division is small, and its prospects are not yet clear.

## What is not in this dossier

- Profitability by division.
- Market shares in infant formula, hospital products or diagnostics.
- Research spending as a share of sales.
