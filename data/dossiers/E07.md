# Subject dossier: Calloway Reading Partners

**As of:** the autumn board meeting of Year 0.
**Subject type:** Nonprofit (early-literacy tutoring)

*Excerpts from the board packet: the CEO's letter, the program dashboard, and committee reports.*

---

## 1. CEO's letter (excerpt)

Dear board members,

Sixteen years ago we started with four schools, eleven tutors and one stubborn idea: that a struggling six-year-old who gets thirty minutes a day with a well-trained, well-coached tutor will learn to read. This year we served **9,840 students in 96 schools across three districts**. Every one of our district contracts has renewed every year for twelve years, and 41 schools are on our waiting list.

The evidence is not in doubt. The independent randomized study of our model seven years ago (2,140 students) found an effect of 0.31 standard deviations on reading. A replication in a second district three years ago found 0.27. Calloway students reach spring reading benchmark at almost twice the rate of similar students without us.

We have cracked the code on early reading. The question in front of us is no longer *whether* Calloway works, but how fast we can bring it to every child who needs it. That is why the strategic plan before you is called **One Million Readers**.

I spoke at 23 conferences and convenings this year, up from six last year, including two national philanthropy summits. Our story is being heard. And our tutors keep giving back: 22% of former tutors now teach in our partner districts, and two of our three superintendents cite Calloway in their own improvement plans.

## 3. Committee reports

**Program committee.**
- Pre-service training was redesigned from 30 hours to 18 hours "to streamline onboarding and accelerate deployment." The change saved about $410,000 and let 140 tutors start two weeks earlier. The remaining content moved to optional online modules; completion data is not yet available.
- The new middle-school math tutoring pilot launched in six schools this fall. **ReadAlong**, a parent app with nightly reading prompts, enters beta in January. Both were requested by district partners.
- The director of research and evaluation position has been vacant for nine months. The committee agreed to fill it after the expansion launches. The annual internal outcome study has been replaced by the real-time dashboard above.

**Talent committee.**
- The founding COO, who designed the training and coaching system, left fourteen months ago to start her own organization; she remains a friend and occasional adviser. Her successor's title is **Vice President of Growth**, recruited from a national youth-development organization with operations in 30 states. The training and coaching teams now report to a combined "Talent and Growth" function.
- The tutor stipend was raised 4%. Exit surveys most often cite "felt unprepared in the first months" and "not enough time with my coach."
- Staff engagement survey: 84% favorable (sector benchmark 71%).

**Finance and development committee.**
- Budget: $14.6M, balanced for the eleventh straight year. Reserves: five months.
- A national philanthropy has offered a **$40M growth grant over five years**, conditional on launching in **five new states within 30 months**. To formalize the partnership, the board would add three seats for members nominated by the philanthropy.
- One long-serving board member asked whether five states in 30 months is "the pace the model can bear." The CEO responded that "the model has proven itself in every setting we've tried," and that the philanthropy's offer "will not wait." The committee recommends acceptance.

**Governance committee.**
- The CEO's contract was extended five years. The committee noted his "extraordinary visibility" as an asset to fundraising.
