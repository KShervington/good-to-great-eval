# Subject dossier: Keystone Mortgage Association

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed, government-sponsored financial company)

## Overview
Keystone is a secondary-market mortgage company. It buys home mortgages from the banks, thrifts and mortgage companies that make them, which gives those lenders cash to make new loans. It was set up by the federal government more than 40 years ago to buy government-insured mortgages. About 16 years ago, a housing law split it in two: a government agency took over the government-guaranteed functions, and Keystone became a shareholder-owned company. It completed the move to private control about 14 years ago.

**Unusual structure:**
- **Board:** 15 directors. Ten are elected by shareholders and five are appointed by the country's president.
- **Regulator:** the federal housing department. It must approve Keystone's stock, debt and securities, and it can require purchases aimed at moderate- and low-income housing.
- **Treasury backstop:** the Treasury is authorized to buy Keystone's securities. This lets the company borrow at rates close to the government's own and earn the highest private-quality rating on its debt.
- **Scope:** by charter, its business is limited to the residential secondary mortgage market.

## Financial position
- **The business model:** for most of its history, Keystone bought fixed-rate mortgages and held them, funding the purchases with bonds and short-term notes. The company earns the difference between what its mortgages yield and what its borrowing costs.
- **The squeeze:** when interest rates began rising sharply about five years ago, borrowing costs rose above the yield on its older, lower-rate mortgages. The company has had negative interest margins since Year −3.
- **Losses:** when the current chief executive was recruited in Year −3, the company was losing about $1 million a day.
- **Offsets:** fee income and the positive margin on newly bought, higher-rate mortgages have partly offset the losses.
- **Not yet profitable:** the company has not returned to steady profit. Detailed annual figures are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- Keystone has been a major buyer of residential mortgages for decades. In credit squeezes it has at times been the largest buyer in the market.
- The market around it is changing:
  - the thrift industry has been deregulated;
  - the primary and secondary mortgage markets are growing;
  - other federal credit agencies have become more active.
- Some of the advantages Keystone has enjoyed are shrinking as it adapts to these changes.
- A congressional audit office has noted that Keystone's large purchases of loans have probably increased its exposure to foreclosures. It has also noted that the office lacks legal access to all the information it needs and has relied partly on data the company chose to provide.
- **Rival G**, one of the two largest savings-and-loan companies in the country, is both a customer (it sells loans) and a competitor (it holds mortgages itself). It switched early to variable-rate mortgages and now makes all new loans with adjustable rates.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive (since Year −3):** a lawyer. He spent several years in state government posts and then served as general counsel of the federal housing department. Before joining Keystone he ran a private mortgage-insurance company for eight years. In those jobs he saw a widening gap between households that could afford a home and those that could not. He also saw the need for mortgages for lower-income and higher-risk borrowers.
- He replaced a chief executive who had led the company since it went private.

## Strategy and known problems
Since Year −3 the company has started to change how it carries interest-rate risk:
- **Mortgage sales:** it sold a large block (about $10 billion) of unprofitable mortgages.
- **Adjustable rates:** it began buying adjustable-rate mortgages.
- **Mortgage-backed securities:** it began issuing them, including a swap program. Lenders trade pools of loans for Keystone securities, and Keystone guarantees timely payment of interest and principal.
- **New lending:** last year it began buying conventional loans on apartment buildings.

**Unresolved questions:**
- How much of its old fixed-rate portfolio to keep, and how to fund it.
- How much credit risk to take on as it guarantees more securities.
- How to balance shareholder returns against its public housing mission and political oversight.

## What is not in this dossier
- Annual net income or loss for recent years.
- Portfolio size, its average yield and the cost of funds.
- Net worth measured at market value.
- Credit-loss rates by type of loan.
