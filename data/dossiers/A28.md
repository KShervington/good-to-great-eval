# Subject dossier: Corvane Systems, Inc.

**As of:** late in Year 0 (the end of the company's first full fiscal year). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (young, recently listed electronics company)

## Overview
Corvane is a West Coast electronics company founded in the middle of Year −1. Its two founders were senior executives at **Rival L**, a fast-growing electronics and defense conglomerate. They each put in about $225,000, which they had made from Rival L stock options, and a venture investor backed them.

In its first months the company bought a majority stake in a small semiconductor maker and took on the name it now uses. It made about five acquisitions or asset purchases in its first year, paying with a mix of cash, debt and stock. Its shares were first offered to the public in the spring of Year 0.

The founders' stated aim is to apply digital technology to a wide range of other technical fields. They intend to build a major company in microelectronics and control systems, mainly by buying existing companies.

## Businesses
Almost all current work is for the defense and space establishment:
- research and production in electronic communications, data processing, computers and automatic control equipment;
- programs including navigation systems for carrier-based anti-submarine aircraft, satellite data processors, space-communication antenna design, studies on air defense and anti-submarine warfare, and an experimental adaptive "learning" computer;
- a contract worth about $5 million to produce digital computers for army artillery fire-direction centers;
- a new devices division developing manufacturing processes to apply solid-state (semiconductor) technology to electronic systems.

The company has described semiconductors as the basic building block of electronics.

## Financial position
- First full fiscal year sales: about $4.5 million, with a small net profit.
- The company reports a backlog of about $10 million.
- About 400 employees at the end of Year −1. Headcount roughly doubled during Year 0 through acquisitions and internal growth.
- The balance sheet is small: a few million dollars of cash and equity, with modest debt.
- [The exact net income figure is not given here because sources differ.]

## Market position
- Corvane is a very small entrant in defense electronics and semiconductors. Its competitors include large aerospace firms, electrical conglomerates and established semiconductor makers, Rival L among them.
- Its revenue depends on government contracts and research programs.
- The company has already used its own shares, along with cash and debt, to pay for acquisitions.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and president (co-founder):** an electrical engineer with a doctorate from a leading technical university. His earlier career included naval ordnance research, wartime intelligence service, patent engineering and research posts at a large electrical company and at aircraft makers. At Rival L he was vice-president and general manager of its electronic equipment division. He is widely regarded as exceptionally brilliant. He is the company's central strategist and decides where its capital goes.
- **Executive vice-president (co-founder):** a former Rival L executive with a background in management science.
- **Board:** includes the venture investor and a renowned academic scientist in information theory.
- [Management depth below the founders is not documented in this dossier.]

## Strategy and known problems
The first annual report gives two priorities: building a profitable, expanding operating base, and assembling and using a strong research and development organization. Open questions:
- **Scope:** whether to stay in semiconductors and digital systems or acquire across a much wider range of industries.
- **Stock as currency:** how far to rely on the company's own stock to fund acquisitions.
- **Running acquisitions:** how to manage a growing number of acquired businesses.
- **Defense dependence:** how to reduce reliance on defense contracts.

The company is young and has almost no operating history of its own.
