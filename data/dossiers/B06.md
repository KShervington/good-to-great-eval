# Subject dossier: Halden Laboratories

**As of:** January of Year 0, as full-year results for Year −1 were being reported. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed pharmaceutical company)

## Overview
Halden is one of the largest research-based pharmaceutical companies in the world. It discovers, develops and markets prescription medicines. It also owns a large pharmacy-benefit manager (PBM), bought six years ago for about $6.6 billion, which manages prescription-drug benefits and mail-order pharmacy for employers and health plans.

For decades Halden has been known for scientific excellence and for a founding philosophy, stated by a mid-century chairman, that medicine is for the people, not for the profits, and that profits follow. A leading business magazine ranked it America's most admired company seven years in a row, most recently about six years ago. Before buying the PBM, the company had revenue of about $9.7 billion.

## Financial position
- **Sales:** $19.8 billion in Year −3, $23.6 billion in Year −2 (+19%), $26.9 billion in Year −1 (+14%).
- **Net income:** $3.88 billion, $4.61 billion (+19%) and $5.25 billion (+14%) over the same years.
- **PBM revenue:** $11.6 billion in Year −1, up from $9.4 billion, or about 43% of total sales.
- **Leading drug categories (Year −1 sales):** cholesterol drugs $4.7 billion; blood-pressure and heart drugs $4.2 billion; anti-ulcer drugs $1.1 billion; an osteoporosis drug $775 million, up from $532 million.

## Market position
- Cholesterol-lowering drugs are its largest category, followed by heart and blood-pressure medicines.
- **Patent expirations:** U.S. patents on two major products expire next year, and on several more, including a leading anti-ulcer drug sold through a joint venture, the year after. These products are about 22% of the company's human-health sales. The company has said it expects a significant decline in their sales over the next three years as generics enter. In total, five drugs with nearly $5 billion in annual sales lose U.S. patent protection in the next few years.
- Managed care and generics are pressing on drug prices. The PBM was bought partly to secure distribution in that environment.
- **Pipeline:** the lead candidate is a new arthritis painkiller of a new class (COX-2 inhibitors). It is designed to relieve pain with fewer stomach side effects than older painkillers. The application was filed with the FDA in November, and the FDA granted priority review this month. A rival's drug of the same class was approved at the very end of Year −1, so the two will compete for the same large market. A second COX-2 compound is also in development. The company is on track to patent more new compounds than any other drug company.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and CEO (since four and a half years ago):** the company's first CEO from outside. He previously ran a medical-device company. His predecessor, a physician-scientist and former head of research, led a decade of strong growth on a much smaller revenue base.
- The CEO reorganized the company around worldwide business strategy teams, each focused on a major disease area.
- He has said he has no interest in a large merger with a rival.
- A business weekly wrote last year that the CEO, if worried about the patent expirations, does not show it.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Stated objective:** since Year −4, the CEO's annual letters have named being a top-tier growth company as the company's number-one business objective. The plan is to get there by investing in fundamental research for breakthrough drugs, realizing the full potential of managed pharmaceutical care through the PBM, and protecting the profitability of the core drug business.
- **The growth gap:** keeping double-digit growth on a revenue base of more than $25 billion, while losing several of its largest products to generics, requires new drugs with very large sales. The odds that any single new molecule earns a profitable return are very low.
- **The COX-2 drug:** management is presenting the new painkiller to investors as a central part of the growth story. Large long-term outcome studies comparing it with older painkillers are planned.
- **Open questions:** whether the PBM fits a research-driven drug company; whether to merge to fill the patent gap; and how much of the company's growth promise to stake on one drug class.

## What is not in this dossier
- Clinical-trial data on the new painkiller's cardiovascular safety.
- Earnings guidance for the next few years.
- Profitability of the PBM on its own.
- Pipeline products beyond the COX-2 compounds.
