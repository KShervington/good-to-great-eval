# Subject dossier: Larkspur Financial

**As of:** early in Year 0, just after full-year results for Year −1 were published. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed bank holding company)

## Overview
Larkspur is one of the largest U.S. banks. It is based in the West and has branches ("stores", in its own vocabulary) across much of the country. It formed from the merger of a Midwest bank holding company and an old western bank, and kept the western bank's name. Its businesses:
- **Community Banking:** retail branches, small-business banking, consumer lending and credit cards. This is the largest business and the heart of the culture.
- **Wholesale Banking:** commercial and corporate lending, commercial real estate, capital markets.
- **Wealth, brokerage and retirement.**
- **Mortgage:** the largest home-mortgage originator in the country.

Three years ago, at the height of the financial crisis, Larkspur bought a large East Coast bank. Integration finished last October with the final branch conversion. Integration expense in Year −1 was $1.7 billion.

The company publishes a "vision and values" statement. Its core idea is meeting all of a customer's financial needs, so customers bring more of their business to the bank. The yardstick is **cross-sell**: the average number of products held by each retail household.

## Financial position
- **Year −1:** record net income of $15.9 billion. Diluted EPS was $2.82, up 28%.
- Total assets are $1.27 trillion. The Tier 1 common equity ratio is 9.46%.
- Larkspur came through the crisis in better shape than most large rivals. It is the most valuable U.S. bank by stock-market value and the fourth largest by assets.
- A famous long-term investor's holding company owns about 7.6% of the shares. It added about $1 billion to the stake in Year −1, and the investor praised the bank in his annual letter.

## Market position
- More than 9,000 stores and about 264,000 "team members". The company says it serves one in three U.S. households.
- The largest U.S. mortgage originator and the largest commercial real estate lender.
- **Cross-sell:** retail-household cross-sell rose every quarter of Year −1, reaching 5.92 products at year end against 5.70 a year earlier. The stated goal is eight products per household. Management reports that one in four retail households already has eight or more.

## Leadership
- **CEO and chairman:** joined the Midwest predecessor bank about 30 years ago. He ran community banking, then became chief operating officer, CEO (five years ago) and chairman (two years ago). He is a strong believer in decentralization: business leaders run their units with wide autonomy.
- **Head of Community Banking:** has run the retail bank (or its regional predecessor) for about ten years. She is credited with its strong sales and cross-sell results.
- In keeping with decentralization, functions such as risk management and human resources are largely run within the business lines rather than centrally.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Strategy:** grow by deepening relationships with existing customers, measured by cross-sell. Employees in the stores have daily and monthly product-sales goals. Incentive pay is tied to products sold and to cross-sell.
- **Regulatory and legal issues (Year −1):**
  - In April, federal bank regulators issued consent orders over mortgage-servicing and foreclosure practices, an issue across the industry.
  - In July, the central bank fined the company's former consumer-finance subsidiary $85 million. The finding was that, from Year −8 to Year −4, employees steered borrowers who qualified for better loans into higher-cost subprime loans and falsified income on some applications. The central bank said the conduct was allegedly fostered by the subsidiary's incentive compensation and sales-quota programs.
  - A state attorney general has sued, alleging discriminatory "reverse redlining" in mortgage lending.
- **Open questions:** whether the eight-products goal is a customer-service aspiration or a sales quota; whether decentralization keeps the company nimble or lets problems stay inside business units; and how to keep growing earnings now that integration savings are ending and interest rates are low.

## What is not in this dossier
- Internal data on employee terminations, complaints or ethics-hotline reports about sales practices.
- Product usage data: how many cross-sold accounts are active or funded.
- Employee turnover in the branches.
- The details of how branch sales goals are set.
