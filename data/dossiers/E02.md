# Subject dossier: Ravenhill Drug Company

**As of:** late in Year 0, shortly after the close of the fiscal year. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed retail drugstore chain)

## Overview
Ravenhill is a chain of drugstores founded nearly a century ago by a pharmacist. A member of the founding family still leads it. It has 2,085 stores in 31 states and one U.S. territory, plus two mail-service pharmacy facilities. By its own account it leads the U.S. chain drugstore industry in sales, profits, store growth and use of technology.

Over the past two decades the company has left businesses outside the drugstore:
- It closed a discount department-store chain about 19 years ago.
- It sold its stake in a Mexican retailer 11 years ago.
- It closed its optical centers about 14 years ago.
- It sold its last 87 free-standing restaurants seven years ago. In-store lunch counters had already been shrinking for years.

Today the business is pharmacy plus photofinishing and general merchandise:
- **Prescriptions:** 43.4% of sales last year, up from 40.8% and 38.2% in the two years before.
- **Stores:** over the past three years the company has moved new stores from strip centers and malls to freestanding corner sites, many with drive-through pharmacy windows.
- **Technology:** since the 1980s all stores have been linked by satellite to a central pharmacy computer, so a customer can refill a prescription at any store. Chainwide checkout scanning and a new inventory system are complete. A next-generation pharmacy workflow system is due to be in every store within two years. One-hour photo is planned for more than 400 stores by the end of this year.
- **Small format:** a pharmacy-focused mini-store has been tested since six years ago and has about two dozen units.

## Financial position
- Net sales were $10.4 billion last fiscal year, up from $6.7 billion four years earlier.
- Net earnings were $321 million, up 13.8%. This was the 21st consecutive year of record sales and earnings.
- Return on average shareholders' equity was 19.1%.
- Long-term debt is negligible (about $2.4 million).
- Gross margin slipped to 28.0% of sales, from 28.4%.
- Capital spending was $310 million last year. More than $300 million is planned for this year.
- There are 68,800 employees, of whom 22,800 are part-time.

## Market position
- The company competes with other chains and independent drugstores, mail-order prescription suppliers, and supermarkets and discount stores with pharmacies. It competes on price, convenience and assortment.
- Third-party payers (insurers, HMOs, pharmacy benefit managers) pay for a growing share of prescriptions. Prescription margins keep falling as a result. The company is pressing payers to meet minimum profitability standards, which management says has slowed the decline.
- Its managed-care subsidiary is forming its own pharmacy-benefit network and will start serving plans in January.
- **The industry is consolidating.** This quarter the largest chain by store count agreed to buy another large chain. The combined company would have about 4,500 stores and more than $11 billion in sales. Antitrust regulators are reviewing the deal.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and chief executive:** a third-generation family member, chairman for about 19 years. He presided over the exits listed above. He set a fixed deadline for leaving the restaurant business and held to it.
- **President:** in the role since about five years ago.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Growth plan:** open 200 or more new stores a year for the next five years, aiming for 3,000 stores within about five years. This year's plan is 215 openings, including entry into three new metropolitan markets.
- **Buy versus build:** the company prefers to choose and, increasingly, own its own sites. Last year it closed 88 older stores and remodeled 84. It has acquired only 24 stores in the past five years. Its largest acquisition ever, a 66-store chain, was nine years ago.
- **Open questions in the industry, as they apply to the company:**
  - Should the largest chain by sales buy other chains to keep pace with consolidating rivals and gain bargaining power with payers?
  - How far should it push its mail-service and pharmacy-benefit business?
  - How should it respond to falling prescription margins from third-party plans?
- **Takeover defense:** seven years ago an investor group sought clearance to buy a block of shares. The company adopted executive severance protections, and no bid followed.
