Dev, Callum — as promised, my read of where things stand before we spend two days on next year. I've stuck to what's in the data room and what I saw on two ride-alongs. You know most of it better than I do.

### 1. The business
Corvane places refurbished commercial dishwashers in independent restaurants on a subscription: $274 a month for an undercounter or door-type machine, including installation, detergent and rinse-aid chemistry, quarterly preventive maintenance, and a promise of emergency response within 4 hours. You're four years in, with 31 people (17 of them field technicians), operating in the home metro since launch and in the east territory for seven months. You raised a $2.8M seed and an $8.5M Series A last year. Cash at month-end was $5.9M; net burn has averaged $243k a month over the last quarter.

### 2. Numbers from the data room
- Active restaurant accounts: 236 (143 a year ago). The east territory is 29 of these.
- Machines in the field: 351. Machines placed this year: 162, including redeployed units.
- Revenue run-rate: about $1.16M a year in subscriptions, plus $96k in parts and out-of-scope repairs.
- Subscription gross margin (chemicals, maintenance visits, parts): 63%. Field-technician labor sits in operating expense in your model.
- Emergency calls met inside 4 hours: 94.1% in the home metro, 81% in the east.
- Online reviews: 4.8 average across 212 reviews. The word that comes up most is "fast."

### 3. How the machines come in
I spent a day at the refurb shop with Marguerite. Of the 403 units refurbished since launch, 288 came through Delmar Equipment Liquidators, which holds the contract to pull dish machines out of Harrow's Grill locations as they are remodeled. Delmar sells to you in lots of 20 to 40 at an average landed cost of $1,140. The other 115 units came from auctions and restaurant closures at $2,700 to $3,600 each. A comparable new door-type machine lists at $7,650. Refurb parts and shop labor run about $610 a unit, and installation averages $380.

Rework within 90 days is under 3%. Marguerite wants a second bay and a forklift; the quote is $64k.

### 4. Team
- **Dev (CEO):** former general manager of a busy bistro. He still closes most large accounts himself and knows a remarkable number of chefs by first name. He wants to open a third metro in the spring.
- **Callum (COO):** built the routing, dispatch and parts system. He argues for going deeper in the two current metros before a third.
- **Teodor (Head of Field Service):** joined 14 months ago after 14 years in the service division of a major dishwasher manufacturer. The technicians rate him highly. He rebuilt the on-call rota and cut overtime by about a fifth.
- **Marguerite (Refurb lead):** ex-appliance repair shop owner.
- **Finance:** a fractional CFO, two days a month.

### 5. The hotel contract
Teodor has brought in Larchmont Hospitality: 18 hotels across both metros with 41 machines, mostly large conveyor units under manufacturer service contracts that expire in March. Larchmont wants a three-year, service-only agreement worth $412,000 a year, with 2-hour response at its eight flagship properties and service credits if you miss it.

Teodor's case is strong on its face. Technician utilization is 63%, so the contract fills idle hours. It needs no machines, so no capital. Larchmont's VP of operations told him directly that the manufacturer's service has slipped and "we'd rather have your people." Teodor estimates three more technicians certified on the conveyor line and about $90k of parts stock. Two board members have called it the best-quality revenue on the table. Larchmont has sent a signed letter of intent and wants an answer by mid-January.

### 6. Market notes
- Independent restaurant openings in the home metro rose about 6% this year, going by county health-permit filings. Closures were roughly flat.
- Used commercial-kitchen equipment prices have stayed soft. Chain remodel programs are a big reason. Harrow's Grill said on its last earnings call that its remodel program is "in the final third": about 118 of its 610 locations remain, with completion planned by the end of its next fiscal year.
- Competitors: a national chemical company that bundles leased machines with chemical supply contracts (cheaper on paper, slow service, three-year lock-ins), plus a handful of local repair shops.
- The health department in the east territory is moving to unannounced inspections next year. Dev sees this as a selling point for reliable sanitizing temperatures.
