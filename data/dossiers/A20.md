# Subject dossier: Brightwater Drug Corporation

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed retail chain)

## Overview
Brightwater is a fast-growing drugstore chain based in the Southeast. Its founder is the son of a pharmacist who ran an early drugstore chain in the Northeast. About 28 years ago the founder saw self-service drugstores on the West Coast, and soon after opened one of the first self-service drugstores in the East. About 23 years ago he left his father's chain and bought three struggling drugstores in the Southeast. He converted them to self-service and made them profitable.

Growth came from three moves:
- **Supermarket partnership:** 16 years ago a leading regional supermarket chain offered him sites next to its new stores. He borrowed $1 million and signed long leases. The partnership produced many new stores.
- **Discount pricing:** the founder challenged the state's "fair trade" law, which let manufacturers fix retail prices. He won before the state supreme court, freeing him to discount.
- **Acquisitions:** the company went public 14 years ago with 15 stores. Since then it has bought drug chains in Louisiana, Texas and Georgia (four years ago), and a regional chain in the Mid-Atlantic and another Texas chain (two years ago).

## Business lines
- **Drugstores:** the core business, mostly in the Southeast and Texas. [Store count is not available in this dossier.]
- **Junior department stores:** a 12-store chain in a large southeastern city, bought seven years ago.
- **Optical centers:** 12 opened two years ago, with more since.
- **Security services:** an alarm and security-guard company bought six years ago.
- **Food-service supply:** a food-service distributor bought six years ago.
- A candy company bought nine years ago was sold three years ago.

## Market position
- **Rival W**, the largest U.S. drug chain, based in the Midwest, has about 600 stores and sales near $1 billion. Last year Rival W bought a 29-store drug chain in Brightwater's home state, putting it into Brightwater's home market.

## Leadership
- **Founder and chairman:** a strong, popular personality. He sent personally signed birthday cards to every employee until the workforce reached 8,000. He says employees are the company's greatest asset and that success depends on understanding them and making fair decisions.
- **Politics:** the founder has spent much of the past five years in politics. He ran in his party's primary for governor four years ago and lost. Last year he won his party's nomination for the U.S. Senate but lost the general election. Whether he will run again or take a public post is not known.
- **Chief executive of the drugstore business:** last year the founder handed this role to a senior executive who has worked closely with him.

## Strategy and known problems
- Growth has come from acquisitions in drugstores and in several unrelated fields (department stores, security, food-service supply, optical, candy).
- It is unclear how much attention the founder will give the company if he continues in public life.
- Rival W's arrival in its home state raises competition.
- Open questions: whether to keep diversifying, and how fast to expand the drugstore chain.
