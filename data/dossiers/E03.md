# Subject dossier: Marbury Laboratories

**As of:** early in Year 0, shortly after the close of the last fiscal year. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed health-care products company)

## Overview
Marbury is a diversified health-care products company founded more than a century ago by a physician. It sells in about 130 countries. It reports two segments:

- **Pharmaceutical and nutritional products:** prescription drugs (among them a hypertension drug, an antibiotic launched four years ago, and an epilepsy drug), the leading U.S. infant formula, and a leading adult medical-nutrition brand. The company is the world's leading producer of one widely used class of antibiotic. A 50/50 joint venture with a Japanese drugmaker sells a hormonal prostate-cancer treatment in the U.S. and develops other drugs.
- **Hospital and laboratory products:** in-vitro diagnostic tests and instruments, where Marbury describes itself as the world leader, plus intravenous solutions, anesthesia and other hospital products. Diagnostics grew rapidly through the 1980s, and the company developed the first diagnostic test for a newly identified viral epidemic about ten years ago.

It has not made a major acquisition in about 30 years and has grown mainly from its own research and product development.

- The company employs 49,464 people.

## Market position
- Customers are increasingly cost-driven. Hospitals are paid fixed amounts per diagnosis under government programs. Drugmakers pay rebates to state health programs. Infant formula for a federal nutrition program is bought by competitive bidding. HMOs and pharmacy benefit managers are becoming major buyers.
- A sweeping federal health-reform proposal failed in Congress last year. Public and private cost-containment efforts continue.
- **The drug industry is consolidating.** In the past two years several large drugmakers have merged or bought pharmacy benefit managers, in deals worth billions of dollars each. Trade-press commentators are debating whether mid-sized companies must merge to keep up in research and marketing scale.
- In branded prescription drugs, Marbury is smaller than the largest research-based drugmakers.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive:** a long-time insider who has led the company for five years. He took over after the board removed his predecessor, who sued the company. Three presidents left during the 1980s.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Quality and regulation:** for the past two years, federal regulators have raised quality-control concerns at the company's diagnostics manufacturing plants. These have not yet been fully resolved.
- **History:** about 25 years ago a contaminated intravenous-solution recall linked to hundreds of infections damaged the company's reputation and its hospital-products sales.
- **Questions the company faces this year:**
  - whether to join the consolidation by merging or buying a pharmacy benefit manager, or to stay independent;
  - how much to step up spending on branded-drug research to compete with larger drugmakers;
  - how to keep earnings growth going as cost containment pressures prices.
