# Subject dossier: Ashcombe Company

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer-products manufacturer)

## Overview
Ashcombe is a consumer-products company based in a large New England city. It is best known for safety razors and blades, the business it was founded on about 80 years ago. Over the decades it has added toiletries (deodorants, shampoos, hair products), writing instruments (pens, including a low-priced erasable ballpoint), disposable lighters (from a French luxury-goods company it bought about ten years ago) and a German small-appliance and electric-shaver business, bought in Year −13 to enter the European electric-shaver market. It has sold products outside the United States for most of its history.

## Financial position
- In the late 1970s, razor blades produced about 71% of pretax profits. Lighters and pens together produced about 15%.
- **Disposable lighters:** the company has fought a costly price war with **Rival B**, a French-owned maker of low-priced disposable pens, lighters and now razors. Its lighter has not turned a profit.
- **A new deodorant:** launched two years ago after two years of development at a cost of about $118 million. It quickly won roughly a quarter of its market.
- **Aerosols:** fears about fluorocarbons and the ozone layer have hurt aerosol sales across the toiletries line. The company has responded with pump sprays and roll-ons.
- Total sales and earnings figures are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- **Blades:** the company is the long-time leader in U.S. blades. Its share settled at about 60–65% after a British rival introduced stainless-steel blades in the U.S. nearly 20 years ago, down from about 70–75% before.
- **Shaving systems:** it introduced a twin-blade cartridge system about nine years ago and a second, improved twin-blade system three years ago. Both are razor handles that take replaceable cartridges.
- **Disposables:**
  - Rival B introduced a disposable plastic shaver in Europe and brought it to the U.S. three years ago.
  - Ahead of that entry, Ashcombe launched its own one-piece disposable twin-blade razor four years ago.
  - Demand for disposables has exceeded the company's expectations.
  - The disposable costs more to make than a refill cartridge and sells for less. Each disposable sold in place of a refill lowers profit.
- **Rival W**, a diversified drug and consumer-products company, owns the second-largest U.S. razor brand. Rival W also sells mouthwash, chewing gum and cough drops, and recently bought a large bakery business.
- **Electric shavers:** a federal antitrust suit has blocked the German subsidiary from selling electric shavers in the U.S.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive (since Year −5):** joined the company more than 20 years ago and came up through finance, not sales or marketing. He succeeded a long-serving chairman who saw the company as a diversified consumer-products company. That chairman's chosen successor left for another company after about 15 months as president.
- **Since taking over**, the current chief executive has focused on high-volume consumer products that customers buy again and again. He has sold the weakest acquisitions of the previous era:
  - a leather-goods maker (Year −3);
  - a newcomer-greeting service (Year −2);
  - a plant-food maker and a pencil company (last year).
- **Earlier ventures:** under the previous chairman, the company tried and failed with hair coloring, shampoos, luxury perfumes, digital watches, pocket calculators, smoke alarms and fire extinguishers. Margins shrank in that period.

## Strategy and known problems
- **The central question in shaving** is how to respond to disposables. The options are:
  - push its own disposable hard to defend total razor share;
  - put resources into new premium cartridge systems with higher margins;
  - try to do both.
- **The lighter business** loses money in a price war with Rival B. Whether to stay in it is unresolved.
- **The German subsidiary** cannot sell its shavers in the U.S. while the antitrust suit continues.
- **The remaining non-shaving businesses** (toiletries, pens, lighters, small appliances) face their own rivals. In pens and lighters the main rival is Rival B, which competes on low prices.
