# Subject dossier: Harlan Pacific Corporation

**As of:** early in Year 0 (the start of a new chief executive's term). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed bank holding company)

## Overview
Harlan Pacific is a large commercial bank based in a major West Coast city, with a history going back more than a century. A merger about 23 years ago made it the 11th-largest banking institution in the United States. It is one of several big banks in its home state, behind **Rival B**, the state's dominant bank and one of the largest banks in the world.

The bank serves consumers through a statewide branch network, and lends to companies, real estate developers and foreign borrowers.

## History of the past 16 years
Under the previous chief executive (in charge since 16 years ago):
- **Consumer credit:** 16 years ago Harlan and three other in-state banks launched a shared bank credit card to challenge Rival B, which had pioneered bank credit cards.
- **Structure:** it took a national charter 15 years ago, partly so it could set up leasing and credit-card subsidiaries, and formed a holding company the next year.
- **Branches:** an expensive statewide branch expansion weighed on earnings in the late 1960s.
- **International:** it opened a representative office in Tokyo, branches in Seoul, Hong Kong and the Bahamas, and representative offices in several Latin American and Asian cities. It also took a 25% stake in a West German bank (which lost $4 million on bad real estate loans) and formed a London joint venture that failed. Currency movements cost it $2 million eight years ago.
- **Growth:** earnings grew about 19% a year on average for six years in the mid- to late 1970s, then slowed to 12% four years ago. Three years ago the then-CEO said publicly it was "time to slow down."
- **Losses:** two years ago an operations officer at one branch embezzled $21.3 million, one of the largest such frauds at the time.
- Performance has fallen sharply in the past few years. [Exact earnings for the last three years are not available in this dossier.]

Last month the previous CEO resigned abruptly after 16 years in charge, to run another, troubled bank.

## Market position and industry change
- **Deregulation:** A federal law passed three years ago is phasing out interest-rate ceilings on deposits over six years. A second law, passed last autumn, lets banks offer money-market deposit accounts to compete with money-market mutual funds. Banks will increasingly have to pay market rates for deposits.
- Deregulation is also drawing many banks into new financial ventures.
- Rival B is much larger, with a far bigger branch network and a long lead in consumer banking in the state.

## Leadership
- **New chairman and CEO (from this month):** about 51, born in Texas, with an economics degree from a West Coast university. He spent the 1960s at another bank in the southern part of the state and joined Harlan 13 years ago. He ran its real estate lending, which became a strong profit contributor, and became president five years ago and chief operating officer two years ago. - Early in his tenure, the previous CEO recruited the dean of a leading business school as board chairman.

## Strategy and known problems
- The bank has no single stated strategy. Its activities span consumer banking, middle-market and large-corporate lending, real estate, and international banking.
- The central question is where to compete after deregulation: across all of these, or in a narrower set.
- Costs: as deposit-rate ceilings disappear, the cost of funds will rise. How large a branch network and overhead the bank can afford is unresolved.
- International: the overseas ventures have produced several losses. Whether to grow the overseas offices or cut back is undecided.
