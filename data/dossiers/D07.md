**Subject:** Before Thursday — where I am, and the consortium question

Hi Hilary,

Thanks again for making time. As you suggested last time, here is everything in one place so we can spend Thursday on decisions rather than catching up.

## Where I am
Assistant professor of biology (freshwater ecology), fourth year. Before coming here I did a four-year postdoc in Martin Hallam's lab, mostly on the Northern Streams Network, his multi-university project tracking invertebrate communities across about 200 streams.

My own work here is on road salt: how winter salt pulses change stream invertebrate communities and leaf breakdown. My lab has now monitored 37 streams through four winters. As far as I know it is the most complete salt dataset in the state. The state transportation agency has started calling me when they change their salting rules.

## Publications
19 total, 9 since I arrived. Of the 9:
- 2 with me as senior and corresponding author: a paper on salt pulses in urban creeks in a regional journal, and a methods note on calibrating conductivity loggers.
- 7 from the Northern Streams Network, where I'm a middle author. Two of those are in very high-profile journals, and one got a lot of press.

Ama (my fourth-year PhD student) is writing up the first big analysis of the 37-site dataset. Two other students are earlier in their projects.

## Funding
- Collaborative federal grant, $612,400 over three years, funding Network sampling at the sites near us. I'm co-PI; the lead PI is one of Martin's former students, now at another university.
- State transportation agency contract, $46,750, for salt monitoring. I'm PI.
- Internal seed grant, $18,000 (spent; it bought the loggers).
- My early-career federal proposal on salt mechanisms was declined last summer. Reviews were Very Good, Good, Very Good. The panel summary called it "promising" and said it needed preliminary data on mechanisms. I plan to resubmit in July, using Ama's analysis.

## Teaching and service
- Teaching load 2-1. Evaluations average 4.6 out of 5. I redesigned the non-majors ecology course, and enrolment went from 48 to 112.
- I chair the department seminar committee and sit on graduate admissions. I review for four journals.
- My third-year review letter was positive overall: teaching strong, service good. It encouraged me to "continue developing a distinct research identity."

## The consortium question
Martin is putting together the renewal, "Northern Streams Network II": about $4.8M over five years across seven universities. He wants me to lead Work Package 3, all the field sampling across the northern sites. The field season would start in May. It would mean about nine weeks in the field each year, supervising six technicians, and a subaward of about $310,000 to our university, with me listed as site PI.

Martin's pitch is that this puts me at the center of the biggest stream-ecology project in the country, with a guaranteed stream of papers. Our chair is enthusiastic: the college counts all research spending in its rankings, and the indirect costs would help the department. Several people I respect have told me that turning Martin down would be "career malpractice." The letter of commitment is due in three weeks.

I'll be honest: part of me loves the idea. The Network was the best science I've been part of, and it's hard to say no to Martin, who has done so much for me.

## Other things on my plate
- An invitation to write a chapter on stream invertebrates for an undergraduate textbook (due in the fall).
- The lab's minus-80 freezer is failing; facilities says six to eight weeks for a replacement.
- I'm on the search committee for the new aquatic toxicologist hire.
