**Ranvik — Season update for investors**
From Ines and Paul

Hi all — the combines are mostly parked, so here is the full-season picture. Short version: we more than doubled, the community is real, and we have one big decision for the January board meeting.

## What Ranvik is (for new readers)
Ranvik lets farmers rent equipment to and from other farmers nearby: tractors, balers, planters, sprayers, grain carts, the occasional combine. Owners list machines with a calendar and a day rate. Renters book in the app, pay by card or ACH, and can add damage protection. We charge renters 9% and owners 5%, minus promotions. We're live in 41 counties across three states, with 9 people on the team.

- Renters who booked again this season (any owner): 34%. Renters who booked the same owner again on Ranvik: 11%.
- Booking requests declined by owners: 1,140, mostly date conflicts.
- Damage protection attach rate: 58%. Premiums collected $148,900; claims paid $61,300 (including one baler fire).
- 68% of GMV still comes from the 12 counties around our first launch area.
- App store rating: 4.7.
- Average net burn: $171k a month. Cash: $3.2M.

## What's working
High-value machines are our best category. Combine and planter bookings were only 9% of bookings but 31% of GMV. Most of those were first-time pairings: a farmer who needed a second planter for a wet week, found one 11 miles away, and booked it in an afternoon. That is the "aha" we keep hearing.

## Community
This is the part we're proudest of. In our post-season survey (388 responses), 71% of renters said they had exchanged phone numbers with the owner they rented from. The most common free-text answer to "What did you get out of Ranvik?" was some version of "a neighbor I can call." The Ostrander and Kemp families met through a hay-season rental two years ago and now share a baler and a rake between their farms. We hear a story like this almost every week. We're hosting "Ranvik Neighbors" barbecues in six towns this winter.

## Product
- In May we turned off phone-number masking in chat. Owners told us masked numbers made it painful to coordinate at the field edge, where the signal is bad and you need to call. Support tickets fell 38% within a month.
- Hour-meter integration for two tractor brands went live in August, so owners can see engine hours used.
- Android crash rate is down to 0.4%.

## Team
- **Ines (CEO):** grew up on a family grain farm; seven years selling seed for a large agronomy company. Ines does most of the barbecues, county fairs and co-op meetings herself. An investor described her as someone who "could sell a combine to a dairy farmer."
- **Paul (CTO):** ex-logistics software engineer, quiet, careful. He has been the more cautious voice on new products.
- Three field reps who visit co-ops and dealers; two support staff; two engineers; one marketing generalist.

## The January decision: input financing
Dana Whitcomb, the partner at our lead investor who sits on our board, has proposed that we launch pay-after-harvest financing for seed, fertilizer and crop chemicals, bought from partner retailers inside the Ranvik app. Dana ran ag lending at a large farm-credit cooperative for 12 years before investing, and she knows this market cold.

The case:
- A regional ag bank has offered a $6M forward-flow facility, expandable to $35M if loan performance holds, so we would not lend from our own balance sheet. We would earn a 2.5% origination fee plus a share of interest.
- In our survey, 52% of renters said they would "likely use" pay-after-harvest terms for inputs.
- Dana's model assumes about $28M of loans in year one and shows about $1.1M of revenue, more than double this season's net revenue.
- Two ag-fintech companies raised rounds of $40M or more this year.

It would take a lending-compliance hire, agreements with input retailers, and two engineers for about five months. Ines is excited about it. Paul has questions about the regulatory load and what it means for the roadmap.
