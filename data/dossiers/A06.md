# Subject dossier: Marlow Discount Centers

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (retail subsidiary of a publicly listed industrial company)

## Overview
Marlow is a discount retailer of consumer electronics and appliances based in a large Northeastern city. It was founded about 35 years ago, after the Second World War, as a door-to-door installment-selling business. The founder and his wife named it after themselves. Its first store, selling appliances and televisions, opened about 30 years ago in a neighborhood of its home city.

**History:**
- **About 20 years ago:** the company went public to fund expansion across its three-state home region.
- **About 12 years ago:** it made its first move outside that region, buying several stores from a chain in a Rocky Mountain city and three stores from an appliance-and-TV chain in a Southwestern state.
- **About ten years ago:** it set up an audio subsidiary. Within two years, audio departments were in every store, and the company stopped carrying small appliances.
- **Six years ago:** the founder died at 57. The chain then had about 40 stores and about $60 million in annual revenue.
- **Two years ago:** a specialty steel maker bought the company for $35 million. The steelmaker had already bought a chain of home-improvement and lumber stores about ten years ago. Marlow now runs as a subsidiary.

## Financial position
- Revenue was about $60 million at the time of the founder's death. Current revenue and profit figures for the subsidiary are not published separately in sources available for this dossier.
- The parent company, a steelmaker, is providing capital for rapid store openings and heavy advertising.

## Market position
- Marlow sells on price and promotion. Its stores trade as "discount centers," and the company advertises heavily.
- The company has stores in its home region and in several Western and Southwestern markets.
- **Rival T**, a consumer-electronics retailer based in a mid-sized Southern city, is a regional competitor. It:
  - runs mid-sized electronics stores in the national capital area, where it holds about an 11% share of local sales;
  - has begun opening very large superstores in a few cities;
  - recently bought a bankrupt chain in the country's largest city.

## Leadership
- **President:** the founder's son-in-law, a lawyer. He took over when the founder died six years ago.
- **His approach:** he has turned away from the founder's habit of entering new regions by buying existing chains. He believes Marlow can enter new markets on its own, without paying for local name recognition.
- **The parent's role:** the steel company's management is backing this approach and funding it. Retailing is not the parent's core business.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Current strategy:** enter a new market with several stores at once, supported by a heavy advertising blitz under the Marlow name. Compete mainly on price.
- **Ownership:** Marlow is one part of an industrial company whose main business is specialty steel. Its capital for growth comes from that parent.
- **Leadership history:** the company has been through a founder's death and a change of ownership within the past six years.

## What is not in this dossier
- Current revenue, profit, store count and return on capital.
- Store sizes and formats.
- Same-store sales trends.
- Market shares in the company's markets.
- The parent company's financial condition and long-term plans for the subsidiary.
