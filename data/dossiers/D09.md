# Subject dossier: Larder Line

**As of:** late in Year 0, a month before the board's annual retreat.
**Subject type:** Nonprofit (food rescue)

*These are a volunteer consultant's interview notes, written up for the board retreat. They are lightly edited and in the order the interviews happened.*

---

## Background (from the consultant's intake sheet)
Larder Line picks up surplus food from grocery stores, bakeries and a produce wholesaler in the city of Corbridge, and delivers it the same day to food pantries, shelters, senior centers and after-school programs. It is eight years old. Budget for Year 0: **$618,400**. Staff: 6.5 FTE. About 270 active volunteers (drivers and sorters). Two refrigerated vans plus volunteers' own cars. It serves 31 donor sites and 44 recipient agencies.

Headline figure in its annual report: **1,312,000 pounds rescued** in Year 0, up from 1,184,000 in Year −1. Cost per pound, as reported in grant applications: $0.47.

---

## Interview 1: Teddy Vance, executive director and founder
- Worked eleven years in grocery distribution before founding Larder Line. "Pounds are the heartbeat. If the pounds go up, people eat."
- Proudest of the cold chain: "We're the only rescue group in Corbridge that can take dairy and meat, because we have reefer vans and trained drivers."
- In the last two years Larder Line has added a farmers'-market gleaning crew, a weekend backpack pilot with two elementary schools, and a composting partnership. "When someone asks if we can help, I want the answer to be yes."
- On donors: "Freshway is family. Their regional VP came to our gala. At their spring partner breakfast she talked about their sustainability goals and their markdown app going chainwide next year. They put our logo on the slide."
- He is enthusiastic about the board chair's café idea (see Interview 3). "It turns rescued food into jobs. Funders love that story."
- The county community fund's new application form asks for "unduplicated households reached." Larder Line left that field blank last cycle. Teddy: "We count pounds at pickup. That's the industry standard."

## Interview 2: Kofi Asante, operations manager
- Runs routes, scheduling and volunteer training. Says the drivers are the real asset: average volunteer driver tenure is 3.4 years.
- Van #2 needs a transmission (quote: $6,800). Saturday no-show rate among volunteers is about 14%.
- "Freshway is the anchor: fourteen stores, five pickups a week at most of them. Over on the Eastside they've been trying that markdown app since spring, the one that sends shoppers alerts for short-dated stuff at a discount. The store managers love it. Less shrink paperwork. They've been really nice to our drivers about it."
- Route 9 construction has made Eastside pickups unpredictable since about May. Drivers now arrive 30–60 minutes later than they used to.
- The backpack pilot uses two volunteer drivers every Friday afternoon. The composting partnership runs one van route on Mondays.

## Interview 3: Renata Pell, board chair
- Owns two well-regarded restaurants and previously helped run a culinary job-training program in another city.
- Proposes **the Larder Kitchen**: a café and catering business, staffed by trainees who were formerly incarcerated, cooking with rescued food.
- Assets lined up: a church has offered its commercial kitchen rent-free for three years; the city's economic development office has invited a **$150,000** grant application; a local foundation has signaled interest in a second year.
- Evidence she cites: a similar café in another city broke even in its third year and placed 64% of its trainees in jobs within six months.
- "Larder Line has the food. I have the kitchen know-how. This is the next chapter."

## Interview 4: Harold Lindqvist, treasurer (retired CPA)
- Revenue: foundations 52%, individual donors and the gala 27%, a county contract 12%, corporate gifts 9% (Freshway is the largest corporate giver at $22,000).
- Reserves: about four months.
- He shared his donor-site table, which he keeps for the grant reports:

- His comment on the Eastside dip: "Probably the Route 9 construction pushing our pickups later in the day, after the good stuff is gone. Should recover when the road reopens in the spring."
- He would like the board to approve a third van ($71,000 used, refrigerated).

## Interview 5: Agnes Moreau, coordinator, St. Brennan's Pantry (recipient agency)
- "The Larder Line drivers are like family. They text before they come."
- Her pantry serves "a few hundred families a week."
- "Some days we get more produce than we can move before it turns, and we compost it. I couldn't tell you how much. Nobody asks us."
- She'd like more dairy and less bread.
