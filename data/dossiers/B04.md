# Subject dossier: Brightwell Housewares, Inc.

**As of:** late winter of Year 0, just after the annual report for Year −1 was filed. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer-products manufacturer)

## Overview
Brightwell is a U.S. maker of molded-plastic and rubber products for the home. Its lines include kitchen and food-storage containers, sink and bath items, storage boxes, trash cans, outdoor and garden products, and (through acquired subsidiaries) children's toys and leisure products. Consumer products are about 78% of sales. Institutional and commercial products (janitorial, food-service and office products) are about 22%. The brand is one of the best known in American housewares. The company has about 12,000 employees.

Starting about 13 years ago, the company was turned around by a CEO recruited from a large industrial company. He cut four of its eight divisions, removed about half of middle management, and built a disciplined new-product machine. He retired in Year −3.

## Financial position
- **Net sales:** $1.67 billion in Year −3, $1.81 billion in Year −2, $1.96 billion in Year −1 (up 9%). Year −1 was the 42nd consecutive year of record sales. The growth came from unit volume; price increases contributed little.
- **Net earnings:** $162.7 million in Year −3, $164.1 million in Year −2, $211.4 million in Year −1. Year −1 was the 56th consecutive profitable year.
- The dividend has been raised every year for more than four decades.
- Over the past 13 years, revenue has grown more than sixfold and earnings nearly fifteenfold, including a run of 40 straight quarters of earnings growth.

## Market position
- Brightwell is the leading brand in many of its housewares categories. It sells mainly through mass merchants, discount chains, supermarkets and home centers.
- **Customer concentration:** one customer, a national discount chain, accounted for 11% of net sales in Year −3, 13% in Year −2 and 14% in Year −1.
- **Competitors:** include lower-priced plastics makers in the same categories.
- **Input costs:** the main raw material is plastic resin, made from petroleum and natural gas. Resin prices spiked a few years ago and have since eased, according to the company.
- **Reputation:** this month, a leading business magazine named Brightwell **America's Most Admired Company** in its annual survey, praising its innovation. The company has been in the survey's top ten every year for about a decade.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and CEO:** a company veteran of nearly 30 years. He became CEO two years ago and chairman last year, after a short interim tenure by another executive following the turnaround CEO's retirement.
- **President and COO:** in his mid-forties.
- Two executives told a business magazine that one museum visit gave them eleven new product ideas.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Stated objectives** (annual report and public statements):
  - increase sales, earnings and EPS by 15% a year, compounded; equivalently, double them every five years;
  - generate one-third of sales from products introduced in the past five years;
  - enter a new product category every 12 to 18 months;
  - substantially raise the international share of sales by the end of the decade (it is currently a small minority of sales).
- **Innovation pace:** the company introduces new products at a rate of about one a day. The CEO has said about nine of ten meet their targets.
- **Acquisitions:** a children's-toy company and a cooler and jug maker were bought about ten years ago, and a smaller manufacturer two years ago.
- **Open questions:** whether the number of products and categories is straining manufacturing, logistics and order-fill performance; how much pricing power the brand has with its largest retail customers; and whether the 15% growth goal can be met without lowering quality.

## What is not in this dossier
- On-time delivery and order-fill rates.
- SKU counts and profitability by product line.
- Resin price forecasts and hedging arrangements.
- Market share by category.
