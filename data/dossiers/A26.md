# Subject dossier: Brightwater Industries

**As of:** early in Year 0 (about a year after the long-time chief executive's death). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed, family-controlled toy and pencil maker)

## Overview
Brightwater is a family-controlled company in a small New England state, founded more than 50 years ago by immigrant brothers. It went from textile remnants to pencil boxes and school supplies, then into toys. It went public 12 years ago, but the family kept a majority of the shares. It has two businesses:

- **Toys:** preschool toys, long-lived low-price classics (for example a plastic build-a-face toy that has sold for decades), toys tied to a children's television show the company produces, and toys under a licence for popular comic-strip characters acquired three years ago.
- **Pencils:** a subsidiary that has grown into the country's largest pencil maker.

Sixteen years ago the company launched a 12-inch posable military action figure for boys. In its first two years the figure brought in nearly two-thirds of total sales. It was later redesigned with a less military "adventure" theme, and five years ago it was discontinued when rising oil prices drove up the cost of plastic.

## Financial position
- Annual sales are roughly $100 million.
- The company lost about $1 million eleven years ago, after a strike and supplier problems. It lost $2.5 million in Year −3 and carries heavy debt.
- Cash flow has been poor. Early in Year −1 the company's bankers forced it to suspend its dividend.
- [Segment profit for toys versus pencils is not available. The pencil business is described as profitable.]

## Market position
- Brightwater is a mid-sized player in the domestic toy industry, which is led by much larger companies, including **Rival M**.
- The industry's fastest-growing category is hand-held and tabletop electronic games. Brightwater has no electronic games and lacks the cash to develop them.
- Toy sales are seasonal and hit-driven. Retailers order heavily for the holiday season, and television advertising is central to launches.

## Diversification record
Over the past decade, attempts to grow outside the core have mostly failed:
- **Nursery schools:** a chain of franchised nursery schools, opened to benefit from a proposed federal day-care subsidy. The company left that business within five years and has since called it a big mistake.
- **Cookware:** a line of cookware tied to a television cooking personality failed.
- **Recalled toys:** a lawn-dart game was declared unsafe by the government, and a novelty water gun was attacked in the press. Both were pulled from the market.
- **Television show:** the children's show has been criticized by a citizens' group as an advertising vehicle for toys.

## Leadership
- **The late chief executive** led the company for almost 20 years and died last year at 61.
- **His son**, 38, has been president for six years and is the heir apparent. Since Year −2 he has started cutting the number of products in the toy line and reducing new-product launches.
- **His uncle**, who has long run the pencil business, refuses to recognize the son's authority. He resents that the pencil business gets less capital spending than its profits would justify, while the toy business gets more. The family dispute threatens to split the company. One option under discussion is to separate the pencil business from the toy business, with the uncle exchanging his shares.
- [Succession arrangements behind the son, and the rest of the senior team, are not documented in this dossier.]

## Strategy and known problems
- The company must decide whether it is a toy company, a pencil company or both, and how to resolve the family conflict.
- In toys, the choice is between chasing fast-growing categories such as electronic games and building on long-lived, low-price classic toys and licensed characters.
- Debt and the suspended dividend limit how much it can invest.
- The former hit product (the action figure) is off the market. Several of the company's own brands are older and dependent on television promotion.
