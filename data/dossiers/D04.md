# Subject dossier: Linewright

**As of:** end of Q3, Year 0 (the product's fourth year on sale). Nothing after that date is included.
**Subject type:** Solo-founder product (desktop software)

*The document below is the founder's own quarterly notes-to-self, lightly cleaned up for an outside reader. The founder writes in the first person.*

---

## Where things are
Linewright is a desktop app (Windows and Mac) for timing and checking subtitles. I built it after 11 years as a freelance subtitler because every tool I used was either free and clumsy or enterprise and expensive. It's just me. A contractor does the Mac builds a few days a month and an accountant does the books.

Pricing:
- Individual license: $129 one-time, includes 12 months of updates. After that, the update plan is $49/year (was $39 until January).
- Studio subscription: $99 per seat per year, minimum 5 seats, includes shared style profiles and the spec-checker presets.

About 3,400 individual licenses sold since launch. 39 studios on subscription, 638 seats. Biggest studio has 74 seats.

## Money (trailing 12 months)
- New individual licenses: $97,300
- Studio subscriptions: $63,800
- Update-plan renewals: $50,300
- **Total: $211,400** (previous 12 months: $176,900)
- Costs (code signing, hosting, payment fees, the Mac contractor, accountant): about $31k.
- I pay myself about $120k. The rest sits in the business account (about $88k now).

Q3 new individual licenses: 161. Q3 last year: 209. Summer is always slow, festivals and vacations.

Renewal rate (all renewals due in the quarter, individual update plans and studio subscriptions together):
- Q4 last year: 64%
- Q1: 58%
- Q2: 55%
- Q3: 52%

The January price rise to $49 was always going to cost some renewals. I modeled a drop to about 58% and expected it to settle there.

## Who buys and why
Post-purchase survey (214 responses, almost all individual buyers), "main reasons you bought":
- Auto-align to audio: 69%
- QC checks against delivery specs (reading speed, shot changes, line length): 41%
- Keyboard-only workflow: 37%
- Price vs. enterprise tools: 22%

Studios say something different on calls. They buy because of the spec checker and shared style profiles: one place to set a broadcaster's rules and know every freelancer's file will pass. Two studio managers told me Linewright cut their QC rejections "by more than half." I can't verify that, but they renewed.

## Competition
- **Lantern Subs:** free, open source, everyone learns on it. Its nightly builds added speech-model auto-alignment in April. My forum regulars say it is "rough but getting there." The lead maintainer posted that it will ship in the stable release "this winter."
- **A cloud platform** aimed at big localization companies: $40/seat/month, very strong on workflow, weak on the actual timing editor. Not after my customers so far.
- Broadcasters' in-house tools, which freelancers are sometimes forced to use.

## Me

- The last two releases were mostly bug fixes. The backlog has 140 items.
- I took one week off this year.
- A user in Lisbon translated the whole UI into Portuguese for free. Should send her something.

## Options I keep circling
**1. The creator product.** Marisol (we worked together at a subtitling agency years ago; now she runs a creator-management agency with 140 creators and a newsletter of about 60,000) wants me to build "Linewright Lite": a web app, $8/month, captions for YouTubers and podcasters. One of her creators (400k followers) posted my test landing page and it got 2,310 waitlist signups in nine days. Her deck says the creator captioning market is "50x" professional subtitling. She would promote it to her whole roster for a revenue share. Realistically six months of my time to build it properly.

**2. Studio push.** Hire a part-time salesperson, build an admin console and per-studio reporting. Three studios have asked for single sign-on.

**3. Linux port.** 46 requests on the forum.

**4. Do nothing new** and clear the backlog.

Marisol says I'll regret not catching the creator wave. My accountant says the studio revenue is "the only part that compounds."
