# Subject dossier: Corwin Paper Company

**As of:** Year 0 (late in the year). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed manufacturer)

## Overview
Corwin is an East Coast paper company founded about 90 years ago by two brothers. It began making coarse paper goods such as bags and wrapping paper. Early on, a family member persuaded the company to stop selling toilet tissue to private-label dealers under thousands of names and to sell it under its own brands. Its long-standing formula has been a small number of high-quality products, made at the lowest possible cost and supported by heavy advertising. It invented the household paper towel, owns one of the oldest toilet-tissue brand names in the country, and was the industry's largest advertiser for decades.

## Financial position
- In the 1950s the company led the U.S. paper industry in profits and growth, despite launching only two new products in six years. A decade ago it was the most profitable U.S. paper company by profit margin and return on investment.
- Two years ago, sales reached a record. Return on equity was only about 12%, however.
- Last year, earnings fell 18% and return on equity dropped to about 9.5%.
- About 16 years ago the company held a 38% share of the sanitary-paper business, against 11% for its closest competitor. [Current share figures are not available in this dossier.]

## Diversification
In the past six years the company has made several acquisitions outside its core:
- a plastic-coating company and its subsidiary, which make technical films (both are now struggling);
- a maker of fine book and printing papers, whose profits fell within a few years;
- a casual-furniture maker;
- two makers of audio-visual teaching aids.

Together these acquisitions cost close to $200 million and have not been profitable. A disposable diaper launched two years ago was not a commercial success.

## Market position
- Competition in household paper has multiplied. Last year the company faced 11 competitors in toilet tissue and 7 in facial tissue. Both markets are mature, growing about 2% a year.
- **Rival P**, a giant packaged-goods marketer, bought a regional tissue mill about 14 years ago. Its brands took share from Corwin in the north-central states. This year Rival P began **national** marketing of its premium soft toilet tissue, backed by very heavy advertising.
- Corwin has refused to use coupons and short-term price deals, and has preferred list-price cuts instead.
- **Rival K**, an upper-Midwest company best known for facial tissue and feminine care, competes in some categories. It is still mainly a mill company.

## Leadership
- The long-serving chief executive, who led the company for more than 40 years and built its mill and timberland base, retired two years ago.
- His successor as chairman set up a decentralized "office of the chairman," shared among several executives. This year, after middle managers asked for clearer leadership from the top, the office was dissolved. A new chairman is expected to be appointed shortly.
- Insiders describe a management team that is comfortable with the company's past dominance and divided over whether its future lies in consumer brands or in a diversified portfolio.

## Strategy and known problems
- The stated strategy is diversification into growth industries to offset a maturing core.
- Product innovation in the core has been slow. Rival P's premium soft tissue is winning on consumer-perceived softness, a dimension where Corwin has no clear answer.
- Large capital needs are coming: mill modernization and pollution-control spending required by new environmental rules.
