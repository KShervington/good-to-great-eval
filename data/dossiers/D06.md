## About me
I'm a cardiac sonographer. I've been scanning hearts for 15 years, the last 11 at a 400-bed regional hospital. I hold the adult echo registry credential, and two years ago I passed the congenital and pediatric echo registry exam on my own initiative. I paid for the prep course and the exam myself, about $1,400, because I kept getting the adult congenital patients and wanted to know what I was looking at.

## My current role
Staff sonographer in an echo lab of 11 sonographers. Mostly routine adult transthoracic echo, plus my share of stress echo and bedside studies.

In February the hospital closed the satellite echo lab on the north campus and moved its volume to us. Before that I did about 9 studies a day. Now it's 13, sometimes 14. Overtime picked up too.

The regional adult congenital heart clinic sends the lab 6 to 8 patients a month: adults born with heart defects, many of them after surgery as kids. The cardiologists ask for me by name for those. I wrote the lab's protocol for scanning patients after a Fontan operation, and I taught myself 3D echo from conference workshops so I could do those studies properly. These are the cases I'd do all day if I could. Each one is a puzzle. A routine study takes me about 40 minutes; a complex congenital study can take 75.

I also mentor two students a semester from the community college sonography program. I love it. The program director has asked me to teach a Thursday-evening scanning lab as an adjunct next semester, $2,300 for the semester.

## Money
- Base rate $46.40 an hour. With call and overtime I made $98,760 last year.
- My partner, Marcus, is a self-employed electrician. His income goes up and down a lot.
- Mortgage, Marcus's van loan ($23,400 left), and the kids' braces coming next year.
- We cut costs this spring: gave back the second car lease, cancelled two streaming services, and I opted out of the hospital's voluntary long-term disability plan ($38.20 a paycheck). Emergency fund covers about two months.

## Outside work
Two kids, 9 and 12. I coach my daughter's under-10 soccer team on Saturdays. I gave up my Thursday volleyball league in the spring because of my shoulder. I see a physio on Tuesday mornings before my shift. My GP calls it impingement. The physio keeps telling me to "reduce provocative load." I've started using a support cushion under my scanning arm.

## Options on the table
**Travel contracts.** My friend Joelle, who I trained with, has done travel echo contracts for two years. She made $142,300 last year and says the money is real and there's "no politics, you just scan." A recruiter has called me three times about a 13-week contract at a large academic medical center three hours from home: $2,850 a week including stipends. The recruiter described it as a high-volume outpatient lab. Marcus thinks two or three contracts would clear the van loan and build real savings, and he'd handle the kids. Joelle says she'd help me get set up.

**The university congenital program.** The university hospital 70 minutes away is "building a dedicated congenital imaging team" for its adult congenital program. The posting asks for the congenital registry credential and experience with 3D and transesophageal echo support. I have the first, some of the second, and a little of the third. The posting says pay is "competitive." I don't know what that means, and I don't know anyone there.

**Lab lead.** Doreen, our lab lead, retires next year. My manager asked if I'd apply. I said probably not. I don't want to be in meetings and doing schedules; I want to scan.

**Teaching.** The adjunct lab, and maybe more later. The program director mentioned the college is short of instructors.

## What I want from coaching
A plan. I feel like I'm good at something specific and spending most of my day on something general. I also feel like I'm on a treadmill that got faster this year.
