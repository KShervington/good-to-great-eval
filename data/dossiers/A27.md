# Subject dossier: Tillery Products Company

**As of:** early in Year 0 (the start of a new chairman and chief executive's term). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer and commercial products manufacturer)

## Overview
Tillery is a maker of rubber and plastic products, based in a small Midwestern town and founded about 60 years ago. It began as a maker of toy balloons. It built its name on a line of rubber kitchen products and moved into plastics more than 20 years ago. Its main lines are:

- **Housewares:** kitchen and household storage and cleaning products under the company's well-known brand, sold through supermarkets and other retailers. By Year −6 the company sold about 240 items.
- **Commercial and institutional products:** products for restaurants, hotels and industry. These reached about 25% of sales by Year −6.
- **Automotive accessories:** rubber floor mats and similar products, made in part at a southern plant opened in Year −6.
- **Home party-plan sales:** a direct-sales line started 11 years ago and modeled on **Rival T**, the leading party-plan housewares company. It reached about 10% of sales within five years, but first made a profit only in Year −4.
- **International:** a West German plastic-housewares maker bought 15 years ago.

Sales were about $309 million when the new chairman arrived.

## Financial position
- About 20 years ago management set a goal of doubling earnings every six years. The goal was met throughout the retiring president's tenure.
- In Year −6, a sharp rise in raw-material prices and a change in accounting caused a large drop in profit. Earlier in this decade, government price controls and a petrochemical shortage held earnings down.
- Plastic resin, a petrochemical, is the main raw material.

## Market position
- The housewares brand is widely known and sits on supermarket shelves nationally. The company has distributed directly to supermarkets since Year −9.
- A recreational-products line (small motorboats and snow sleds) launched earlier in the decade failed because the company lacked distribution for it, and was abandoned.
- In Year −7 the federal trade regulator charged the company with illegal price-fixing over minimum retail-price agreements. The company dropped the agreements in Year −5, and in Year −4 the regulator issued a cease-and-desist order.

## Leadership
- **Retiring president:** led the company for about 21 years and delivered steady, compounding earnings growth.
- **New chairman and CEO (Year 0):** an outsider to the company's management. He spent 31 years at a large electrical and industrial conglomerate, where he ran its consumer products division and later its industrial products division. He grew up in the company's home town and worked at its plant while in college. He has said he finds the company somewhat stodgy and complacent despite its steady growth. He has set a public goal of quadrupling sales and earnings within ten years.

## Strategy and known problems
- The company has grown steadily, but it is now spread across housewares, commercial products, automotive accessories, party-plan direct sales and international operations. These have different customers, channels and profitability.
- The party-plan business competes against an entrenched specialist and took years to become profitable.
- Resin cost swings and price controls have hurt margins in the past.
- In Year −4, about 1,100 workers struck the company's only unionized plant, in its home town. The strike was settled.
- A recession is widely expected.
- Open questions for the new chairman include:
  - which divisions to keep;
  - how much to invest in new products versus cost reduction;
  - whether to grow by acquisition, which the previous management largely avoided except in markets it already served.
