# Subject dossier: Halstead Motors Corporation

**As of:** early autumn of Year 0 (days after a new chairman and chief executive took office). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed automaker)

## Overview
Halstead is the smallest of the three large domestic automakers. It builds cars and trucks under several brands and sells them through a national dealer network. It also has a defense business that builds armored vehicles; defense contracts brought in $625 million in Year −1. In Year −1 total sales were $13.6 billion, making Halstead about the tenth-largest manufacturer in the country.

Last year Halstead sold its European car operations to a French automaker. It has since been a mainly North American company.

About 130,000 people work for Halstead in seven states. Roughly 400,000 more work for its suppliers and dealers.

## Financial position
- The company lost money in Year −5 and Year −4, recovered briefly, then lost $205 million in Year −1.
- In the second quarter of Year 0 it lost $207 million. Losses are continuing, and analysts expect the Year 0 loss to exceed the Year −1 loss.
- Banks and Wall Street have lost confidence in management, and the company has not been able to raise new money privately. Talk of a merger with **Rival F** surfaced early in Year 0 and came to nothing.
- In the summer, the then-chairman asked the national legislature for about $1 billion in tax refunds. He tied the request to the cost of meeting federal safety, fuel-economy and emissions rules. Legislators are now discussing a federal loan guarantee instead. The company has warned that without aid by year-end it may have to close.
- The company is in the middle of a large capital program to build a new family of front-wheel-drive compact cars, due to launch in the fall of Year 1.

## Market position
- Halstead's share of the domestic car market is about 9%, down from about 21% roughly 25 years ago.
- The market leaders are **Rival G**, by far the largest automaker, and **Rival F**, the second largest. Japanese importers are gaining fast in small cars.
- The company entered the oil embargo of six years ago with a large stock of big, fuel-hungry cars that it could not sell. When buyers later moved back to mid-size and large cars, it was short of them. Now, with gasoline shortages and a recession in Year 0, demand has shifted back to small cars.
- Halstead has the highest inventory of the three domestic makers: about 80,000 cars and trucks, or roughly 95 days of dealer supply.
- Recalls and poor quality control have damaged its reputation with buyers.

## Leadership
- **New chairman and CEO:** joined Halstead as president about ten months ago and took the top job this month. He spent more than 30 years at Rival F, rising through sales and marketing to become its president. He was fired after a falling-out with Rival F's controlling family, even though Rival F was highly profitable at the time. He is known in the industry as a salesman and product marketer, and is credited with a hugely successful sporty car launch about 15 years ago. Since arriving he has concentrated on sales and publicity.
- Under the arrangement made when he arrived, he ran operations while the outgoing chairman focused on the government aid request and supplier relations. The outgoing chairman, an accountant who led cost cutting earlier in the decade, has now stepped down.
- The new CEO has asked the autoworkers' union for a two-year freeze on wages and benefits.
- [The rest of the senior team and any succession plan are not documented in this dossier.]

## Strategy and known problems
- The immediate problem is survival: stopping cash losses, getting government-backed financing, and winning concessions from the union, suppliers and lenders.
- The product line is out of step with demand, and the new front-wheel-drive compacts are still a year away.
- The company must meet federal safety, fuel-economy and emissions rules across its whole line while losing money.
- Opinion inside and outside the company is divided:
  - some favor a merger or a court-supervised reorganization;
  - others argue Halstead can recover as an independent automaker if it gets financing to reach the new car launch;
  - some legislators oppose any federal aid.

## What is not in this dossier

- Break-even volume.
- Profitability by vehicle line and of the defense business.
- Third-quarter Year 0 results (not yet reported).
