# Subject dossier: Homestead Mortgage Association

**As of:** early in Year 0, just after full-year results for Year −1 were released. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed, government-sponsored financial company)

## Overview
Homestead is a shareholder-owned company chartered by Congress to support the U.S. home-mortgage market. It does not lend directly to homebuyers. It does two things:
- **Portfolio business:** it buys mortgages and mortgage-backed securities and holds them, funded by issuing its own debt.
- **Guarantee business:** it packages mortgages into securities that it guarantees against credit loss, for a fee.

Its charter brings special privileges, including a line of credit with the U.S. Treasury. Investors widely assume that the government would stand behind its debt, which lets it borrow at rates close to the Treasury's. A smaller "sister" enterprise with a similar charter competes with it.

Homestead describes its mission as expanding home ownership, especially for low- and moderate-income families. It has a multi-year $2 trillion commitment to housing finance for underserved groups and reports providing $670 billion toward it in Year −1. A federal housing department sets annual "affordable housing goals." The main goal currently requires 50% of the mortgages it finances to be for low- and moderate-income households.

## Financial position
- **Year −1 results:** operating ("core") earnings per share were $6.31, up 21% from $5.20. Operating net income was $6.39 billion, against $5.37 billion. Reported (GAAP) net income was $4.62 billion, down from $5.89 billion, because of mark-to-market losses on derivatives used to hedge interest-rate risk.
- **Operating earnings history:** $3.42 billion five years ago, then $3.91 billion, $4.45 billion, $5.37 billion and $6.39 billion. The CEO cites 16 straight years of double-digit growth in operating earnings.
- **Size:** the book of business (portfolio plus guaranteed securities) is $1.82 trillion, up 16%. The retained portfolio is $798 billion, up 13% in Year −1 and 16% the year before. It was $415 billion five years ago. Total assets are $887 billion.
- **Market share:** the company holds about 11% of all U.S. residential mortgage debt in its portfolio and guarantees about another 15%.
- **Credit quality:** credit losses were 0.5 basis points of the book, very low by banking standards.
- **Capital:** core capital is $28.1 billion, about 3.2% of total assets and about $0.9 billion above the legal minimum.

## Market position
- Homestead and its sister enterprise dominate the secondary market for conforming mortgages. Their cost of funds is well below that of banks and thrifts.
- **Interest-rate risk:** the portfolio business earns the spread between mortgage yields and the cost of debt, so it is exposed to rate swings and to borrowers refinancing early. In August of Year −1, after rates fell and refinancing surged, the company's "duration gap" (its measure of the mismatch between assets and liabilities) widened to −14 months. The safety-and-soundness regulator responded in September with the first formal supervisory action it has ever taken against the company.
- **Critics:** Treasury officials and other critics have questioned the enterprises' rapid portfolio growth and implicit government backing. Three years ago a Treasury official testified on a bill to repeal their Treasury lines of credit.

## Leadership
- **CEO (since four years ago):** previously the company's vice chairman, then a senior federal budget official in Washington.
- **Chief operating officer:** joined three years ago from the finance arm of a large industrial conglomerate. He is a former military officer.
- **Chief financial officer:** a long-serving insider.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Earnings goal.** In Year −4 the CEO publicly set a goal to double operating EPS within five years, to $6.46 by the end of this year. That implies about 15% annual growth in a mortgage market growing more slowly. The company says it is on track. Annual executive bonuses and long-term stock awards are tied to EPS targets. A company-wide stock-option grant vests if the doubling goal is met.
- **Accounting.** A new accounting standard for derivatives has made reported earnings volatile. Management asks investors to focus on "operating" earnings, which exclude those swings.
- **Growth sources.** Growth comes mainly from expanding the retained portfolio and from meeting rising affordable-housing goals. Management argues that the portfolio's risk is well hedged and that credit risk is minimal.
- **Debates.** Some argue the enterprises should shrink their portfolios, hold more capital, or lose their special status. Management argues that its scale lowers mortgage costs for families, and that its mission and record justify its structure.

## What is not in this dossier
- Detailed hedging positions and derivative values.
- Details of internal accounting policies (for example, how premiums, discounts and hedges are amortized).
- The credit profile of recent loan purchases by product type.
- Market value and stock-price history.
