# Subject dossier: Tallypaw

**As of:** this quarter.
**Subject type:** Startup (B2B software)

## What it is
Tallypaw makes practice software for **independent veterinary clinics**, meaning clinics with one to four vets that are not owned by a corporate chain. It started with appointment reminders by text and email. Two years ago it added drug and supply inventory tracking, with automatic reorder suggestions. Inventory is now the module that customers mention most in reviews.

The company is five years old and has 23 staff. It raised a seed round and a small Series A, and has about 20 months of runway at the current burn.

## Numbers the founders shared
- Annual recurring revenue: **$2.4M**, up from $1.5M a year ago.
- Paying clinics: **310** (220 a year ago).
- Net revenue retention: **104%**.
- Gross margin: about **78%**.
- Inventory module attach rate: 71% of clinics. Clinics using inventory have about half the logo churn of reminder-only clinics, according to the CTO's analysis of the last 18 months.
- Average contract value: about $7,700 a year.
- Sales and marketing spend last year: **$1.1M**. [The founders did not break this down by channel, and did not give the number of new customers won through each channel.]
- Net Promoter Score from the last in-app survey: 61 (138 responses).

## Team
- **Maya (CEO, co-founder):** a former practice manager at a vet clinic. She is the company's best salesperson and still runs most demos. She describes herself as "allergic to slide decks." The team describes her as fast and decisive, sometimes too fast.
- **Jonah (CTO, co-founder):** built the inventory engine. Quiet. He is respected by the engineers, who say he admits mistakes in post-mortems before anyone else does.
- **Head of Sales (hired 8 months ago from a large horizontal SaaS company):** has pushed for enterprise deals with corporate vet chains. He has run two pilots with chains so far, and neither has converted.
- **Customer success:** two people, both former vet techs. Customers praise them by name in reviews.

## Distribution
Tallypaw sells in two ways:
1. **Direct:** founder-led demos, trade-show booths, and word of mouth among practice managers.
2. **Through a partner:** a reseller agreement with **VetSource Supply**, the largest regional veterinary distributor in its home market. VetSource bundles Tallypaw's inventory module with its supply ordering portal. The agreement was signed three years ago with a four-year term.

## Board notes from the last two meetings (summarized)
- The head of sales presented a plan to move upmarket to corporate chains: "That's where the real money is."
- Maya presented an opportunity: a **human dental-clinic software play**. A dental group owner she met at a conference has signed a non-binding letter of intent for a pilot across 14 dental offices. Maya estimates the dental market is 4× the size of independent vet. Two engineers would be assigned for six months to build a dental version.
- The board asked for a product roadmap with fewer items. Maya said she would "think about it after the dental pilot."
- Housekeeping items: the office lease renews in March. The VetSource reseller agreement comes up for renewal in **five months**. Revenue booked through VetSource-referred accounts is **38% of ARR**. VetSource announced at its customer summit last month that it is "investing in native inventory intelligence inside the VetSource portal." No one from VetSource has replied to two emails about renewal terms.
- The engineering lead reported that the reminder module runs on a third-party SMS vendor whose prices rose 30% this year.

## What the founders believe
- "Vets love us because we were one of them."
- "We win on inventory. Nobody else understands how a small clinic actually orders drugs."
- "We need a second market to be venture-scale."
