# Subject dossier: Pacifica Bancorp

**As of:** late in Year 0, after the CEO's planned departure was announced and before the board named a successor. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed bank holding company)

## Overview
Pacifica is the holding company for the largest bank in the world. The bank was founded in the early twentieth century by the son of immigrants, as a bank for small savers, farmers and small businesses ("the little guy") that big banks ignored. It grew loan by loan and branch by branch across its home state, a large and fast-growing western state, and it became the world's largest commercial bank about 35 years ago. State and federal law keep it from operating retail branches outside its home state, but it has built a large international business.

A leading management journal began an article this year by calling it the world's largest bank, with nearly 1,100 branches, operations in more than 100 countries and total assets of about $100 billion. The article added that many observers consider the quality of its management equally notable.

The bank was an early leader in computerizing its account records, and it has pioneered consumer products used across the industry.

## Financial position
- Total assets doubled between Year −10 and Year −6, then nearly doubled again by Year −1. Over Years −9 to −2, Pacifica was the only one of the 20 largest U.S. banks to average 15% annual growth.
- Annual net income has risen to more than $600 million, a record.
- [Detailed figures for capital ratios, loan-loss reserves and loan mix are not in this dossier.]

## Market position
- **Home state:** the dominant retail bank, with the largest branch network. That network gives it a large base of low-cost consumer deposits. Under federal rules, interest rates on those deposits are capped.
- **International:** between roughly 20 and 10 years ago, its overseas branches grew from fewer than 20 to more than 90. Between Year −9 and Year −3, assets in overseas branches and subsidiaries more than tripled. Authority for international lending is decentralized to encourage entrepreneurial growth abroad.
- **Lending mix:** in the past few years the bank has sharply increased fixed-rate home mortgages, agricultural loans, construction loans, and loans to developing countries in Latin America and Africa.
- **Other ventures:** a venture-capital partnership that makes high-risk direct investments in small technology companies.
- **Technology:** it is behind several in-state rivals in deploying automated teller machines.

## Leadership
- **CEO (for about ten years):** trained as a lawyer and spent his career at the bank. Under him, the bank's assets have more than tripled. His creed is that the bank's keyword must be "action," and that mistakes of decision are better than mistakes of indecision.
- This autumn he was nominated to head a major international development institution and will leave the bank next year. The board, more than two dozen directors, is choosing his successor.
- The bank has a long tradition of what amounts to lifetime employment.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Deregulation:** a federal law passed this spring will phase out interest-rate ceilings on deposits over six years. Money-market mutual funds, which pay market rates, are already pulling deposits away from banks. The bank's cheap deposit base, a core advantage, will become more expensive.
- **Interest-rate risk:** interest rates are high and volatile. A large book of long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded with short-term deposits loses value when funding costs rise.
- **Cost structure:** the large branch network carries high fixed costs. Those costs were affordable when deposit rates were capped, and will be harder to carry once rates are free.
- **Credit risk:** the rapid growth in loans to developing countries, farms and construction has been approved under a decentralized system.
- **Open questions for the board:** whether the next CEO should be a steady banker who protects the franchise, or a younger change agent who will modernize technology, cut costs and enter new businesses; and whether growth in assets is still the right measure of success.
