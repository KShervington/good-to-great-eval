# Subject dossier: Tessel

**As of:** the week of an accelerator's application deadline, 14 months after the founder started building. Nothing after that date is included.
**Subject type:** Solo-founder product (consumer mobile app)

*The document below is the founder's application to a pre-seed accelerator, reproduced with the form's questions. The founder writes in the first person.*

---

**1. What are you building, in one or two sentences?**
Tessel is an app that helps families share the work of caring for an aging parent: one shared care log, a medication schedule with reminders, appointments, and refill tracking so nobody discovers on a Sunday night that Mom's blood thinner ran out.

**2. Why you?**
Two years ago my father had a stroke. My brother, my aunt and I spent the first months coordinating his care through a group chat, a whiteboard on his fridge and a lot of panicked phone calls. Twice we let a prescription lapse. I was a logistics analyst for nine years, and I kept thinking: this is a supply-chain problem with love in it. My brother, my aunt and I use Tessel every day for Dad. I built it myself (I taught myself Swift for this).

**3. What exists today?**
- iOS app, live in the App Store for four months. 4.9 stars (11 ratings).
- Shared care log, medication schedule with push reminders, shared calendar, and photo notes (people use these for wound photos and pill bottles).
- **Refill sync:** Tessel shows when each prescription is due for refill and whether the pharmacy has it ready. Right now this works with one pharmacy, a family-owned pharmacy near my father's house whose pharmacist emails me a refill export every Monday, which I load. For the big chains I need API access through a pharmacy-data aggregator. I've applied twice; my second application has been "under review" since August.
- No Android version yet. About a third of the people on my waitlist use Android.

**4. Traction**
- Accounts created: 318
- Care circles created (a circle is one family caring for one person): 41
- Weekly active circles: 7
- User interviews: 26
- Paid tier ($6.99/month, launched six weeks ago): 3 subscribers
- Newsletter, *The Caregiver's Ledger*: 2,140 subscribers, 52% average open rate. I write it every Sunday.
- Won first prize ($10,000) at a regional health-innovation pitch competition in the spring.

**5. Who are your users and how did you find them?**
My first users came from the caregiver support group I go to on Wednesday nights and from my old colleagues, who were incredibly generous about trying it and sharing it with their families. After that, most sign-ups came from the newsletter and a post that did well on a caregiving forum. In interviews, refill sync is the feature people get most excited about. One said, "If it really knew when Dad's pills were ready, I'd pay anything."

**6. What have you learned that surprised you?**
Caregivers don't want another app. They want fewer phone calls. The best thing I can do is make the app disappear into a reminder that shows up at the right time. Also, the "primary caregiver" is almost always one daughter, and she is exhausted.

**7. What will you do with the accelerator's money?**
- Finish the chain-pharmacy integration once access comes through.
- Build the Android version.
- Possibly hire a part-time developer.

**8. Is there anything you are considering that would change your direction?**
Yes. Graham Fairley, a vice president at an employee-benefits consultancy who has spent 20 years placing benefits with mid-size employers, thinks Tessel should be sold to employers as a caregiving benefit. He says employers pay $3 to $5 per employee per month for caregiver support programs, and a survey he shared says about one in five employees is caring for an older relative. He has introduced me to three heads of HR. One, at a manufacturer with about 1,800 employees, said they would pilot it next spring if Tessel had an admin dashboard, single sign-on, anonymized usage reporting, and a "care concierge" chat staffed by a person. Graham thinks I'm leaving money on the table by staying consumer-only.

**9. Finances and runway**
I've put in $38,000 of my savings. I have about $41,000 left. I do 10 hours a week of contract analytics work ($1,900 a month), and living plus app costs are about $4,300 a month. The $10,000 prize paid for design help, a basic security review and a lawyer to draft the privacy policy.

**10. Team**
Just me. A friend who is a UX designer helps on weekends. My aunt, a retired nurse, reviews anything clinical.

**11. What is the biggest risk?**
Getting pharmacy access. Everything else I can build.

**12. Anything else?**
The social worker on my father's stroke unit now hands out a Tessel card to families at discharge. She says families ask her for it by name.
