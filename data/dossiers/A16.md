# Subject dossier: Carrow Tobacco Company

**As of:** late January of Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer-products manufacturer)

## Overview
Carrow is the largest U.S. cigarette maker. It was founded about 90 years ago in a small Southern tobacco town, which it still dominates as employer and taxpayer. When an antitrust case broke up the old tobacco trust about 50 years ago, Carrow was the smallest of the four main successor companies. It then launched the first modern blended cigarette brand, sold at a low price and backed by very heavy advertising. Within about a decade its profits passed those of the former trust leader.

Its main brands today:
- **Its original plain (nonfilter) brand**, the best-selling cigarette in the country for decades until a few years ago.
- **A filter brand** launched ten years ago, Carrow's first filter cigarette. It sold about 40 billion cigarettes in its first year and is now among the best-selling filter brands.
- **A menthol filter brand** launched eight years ago, the industry's first king-size filtered menthol.
- Carrow has also had failures. A major brand launched 15 years ago was rejected by smokers and lost about $30 million over five years.

Three years ago Carrow moved all cigarette making into one large new factory in its home town.

## Diversification
- Seven years ago the company formed a diversification committee to study investments outside tobacco and expansion of its tobacco business overseas.
- It has recently bought its first non-tobacco business, the maker of a fruit punch drink.

## Market position
- Carrow competes with five other major U.S. cigarette makers. One, a large rival, is launching many new brands at a fast pace.
- **Rival H**, the smallest of the six majors (about 9% of the market three years ago), relaunched an old brand ten years ago as a filter cigarette with a flip-top box and cowboy imagery. That brand has been growing. Rival H is also building an export and international licensing business.
- Filters have been gaining share for a decade, driven by health worries. Carrow has strong positions in both filter and menthol.

## The health issue
- Two weeks ago the U.S. Surgeon General released a report by an expert advisory committee. It concluded that cigarette smoking causes lung cancer in men and is a health hazard serious enough to warrant remedial action.
- Eleven days later the Federal Trade Commission proposed requiring health warnings on cigarette packs.
- Congress and regulators are debating warning labels and limits on cigarette advertising. Industry executives publicly dispute that smoking has been proven to cause disease.
- About ten years ago a researcher in Carrow's own research department wrote an internal memo describing tobacco as an important cause of lung cancer.
- Nobody yet knows how far cigarette sales will fall.

## Strategy and known problems
- The central question is how much to invest in cigarettes versus diversifying away from them. Two views are argued. One: the health report threatens the whole industry, so the company should use its tobacco cash to build or buy businesses in foods, beverages and other fields. The other: cigarettes remain far more profitable than anything the company could buy, and its brands are the best in the industry.
- A related question is overseas expansion. Rival H is moving abroad faster.
- The company's past brand launch failure shows that new brands are expensive and risky.
