# Subject dossier: Harwell-Brandt Company

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed pharmaceutical and consumer-products company)

## Overview
Harwell-Brandt is a diversified drug and consumer-products company. It was built over about 35 years through a long series of mergers, led by a chief executive who arrived in the mid-1940s and took a stake in what was then an old drug house with a cosmetics line. The main steps:

- **About 25 years ago:** merged with the maker of a leading antiseptic mouthwash. The mouthwash once accounted for more than half of that partner's sales.
- **Ten years ago:** merged with a large, old-line prescription-drug maker. The combined company was expected to have about $1.7 billion in revenue. The antitrust division of the Justice Department recommended challenging the deal, but the department did not.
- **The 1970s:** acquired:
  - a chewing-gum and candy maker (for shares worth about $200 million);
  - an antacid and effervescent-remedy maker;
  - a cough-drop maker;
  - an optical-products company;
  - the second-largest U.S. razor-blade and shaving business.
- **Two years ago:** bought a large regional bakery chain for $243 million in cash.

The company operates in about 140 countries.

## Financial position
- In Year −1, profit margins fell about 40%, and return on equity was about 9%. One industry critic called the company a floundering giant.
- Most revenue now comes from consumer products rather than prescription drugs.
- Total sales and earnings for recent years are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- **Mouthwash:** the company's brand holds a large share of a market of about $300 million (as of five years ago). Federal regulators ruled that its cold-prevention claims were unsupported and ordered about $10 million of corrective advertising. The Supreme Court let that order stand two years ago.
- **Prescription drugs:** four years ago, federal regulators ordered the company to sell several units of the acquired drug maker (thyroid products, cough remedies and a blood-serum product) to restore competition.
- **Shaving:** the razor business is a distant second to **Rival A**, a company that dominates U.S. blades and is fighting a new wave of low-priced disposable razors.
- **Other problems:**
  - Four years ago the company disclosed about $2.2 million in questionable payments abroad, in 14 of the 140 countries where it operates.
  - An explosion at one of its gum plants killed six people. Criminal charges against the company and four officials were dismissed, then restored on appeal, and are still before the courts.

## Leadership
- **The long-time builder,** who led the company for more than three decades, no longer runs it.
- **Last year,** a new chairman and a new chief executive took over from the executive who had run the company for the previous few years.
- **The new team's plan:** it has begun a review of the portfolio. It aims to revive the prescription-drug business and trim operations outside the core.

## Strategy and known problems
- The company's stated direction has been unsettled.
  - **Last year,** it told the business press that it aimed to be a leading consumer-products company.
  - **The new team** is now talking about reviving pharmaceuticals.
- Two directions have been put forward within a year:
  - build the future on consumer brands (mouthwash, gum, shaving, bakery);
  - build it on research-based prescription drugs, where the larger drug companies set the pace.
- The bakery and optical businesses sit outside both the drug business and the company's personal-care and confectionery lines.
- Regulatory and legal problems have taken management attention in several business lines.

## What is not in this dossier
- Total sales, net income and segment profits for recent years.
- Market shares in gum, shaving and prescription drugs.
- Research spending and the new-drug pipeline.
- Which units the new management intends to sell.
