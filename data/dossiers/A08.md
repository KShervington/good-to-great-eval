# Subject dossier: Pacific Crest Financial Corporation

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed savings-and-loan holding company)

## Overview
Pacific Crest is the holding company for one of the largest savings-and-loan associations in the country. Its main subsidiary takes household savings deposits and makes home mortgages, mostly in a large Western state. The lending business goes back more than 60 years. The holding company was formed nearly 30 years ago in a large West Coast city.

**Growth:**
- **Late 1950s:** soon after forming, the holding company bought savings associations in cities across its home state, giving it a presence in nearly every major metropolitan area there by the end of that decade.
- **About ten years ago:** all savings-and-loan operations were merged into one subsidiary.
- **Year −2:** it merged with a savings association in the northern part of the state, paying about $61 million in stock and adding 61 offices. After the merger the company had about $12.6 billion in assets and 183 offices, making it the second-largest savings-and-loan in the nation.
- **Last year:** it bought a brokerage and real-estate services firm.
- **Advertising:** the company has long used celebrity spokesmen in its advertising.

## Financial position
- **Interest-rate exposure:** like all thrifts, the company funds long-term mortgages with short-term deposits. The sharp rise in interest rates since the late 1970s has squeezed the whole industry.
- **Adjustable rates:** Pacific Crest moved earlier than most. It pioneered variable-rate mortgages in the 1970s. By the end of that decade about 60% of its mortgages carried variable rates, and since federal regulators approved adjustable-rate mortgages three years ago, all its new home loans have adjustable rates.
- **Past setback:** in the early 1960s the company stopped paying its stock dividend after delinquent loans and foreclosures rose sharply. Foreclosures peaked at $23.2 million about 20 years ago before falling back.
- Recent annual earnings, net worth and return on equity are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- The company is known for conservative lending: mostly small residential mortgages. During deregulation it has avoided the aggressive growth and high-risk investments that many competitors have taken on.
- **Deregulation:** recent federal laws have deregulated savings institutions and widened what they may do. Many competitors are using the new freedom to grow fast and move into riskier assets.
- **Rival K**, a federally chartered, shareholder-owned company that buys mortgages from lenders, is both a buyer of loans from thrifts like Pacific Crest and a competing holder of mortgages. It has been losing money in the rate squeeze. It has begun issuing mortgage-backed securities and has offered lenders a program to swap loans for those securities.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive (since Year −5):** a former accountant at a large public accounting firm. He has led the company through the rate crisis and the recent merger.

## Strategy and known problems
- The company is a large, conservatively run home lender. It faces a choice that deregulation now allows.
- **Option one:** stay focused on residential mortgage lending and deposit gathering.
- **Option two:** use the new freedom to diversify into other financial and real-estate services. Last year's brokerage and real-estate services purchase is a step in this direction.
- **Other open questions:** how much to grow by acquiring other thrifts, and whether to expand beyond its home state.
- **Rate risk:** an older book of fixed-rate mortgages remains, and its cost of funds still depends on deposit rates.

## What is not in this dossier
- Net income, return on equity and net worth for recent years.
- The size of the remaining fixed-rate portfolio.
- Its market share of deposits and mortgages in its home state.
- Plans for, or the profitability of, the newly acquired non-lending businesses.
