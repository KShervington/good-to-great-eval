# Subject dossier: Marlowe Drug Co.

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included. Financial figures are for the fiscal year that ended a few months ago ("last year").
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed retail chain)

## Overview
Marlowe is the largest drugstore chain in the United States. A pharmacist founded it about 75 years ago in a large Midwest city, and the founder's family still runs it. It grew through the 1920s with soda fountains, its own manufactured drug products and low prices. Twenty-two years ago it opened its first self-service store, and self-service stores now dominate the chain.

The company now runs several businesses:
- **Drugstores:** 616 stores in 305 cities across 32 states and one U.S. territory. Average sales area is about 9,000 square feet and average annual volume is over $1.3 million per store. Last year it opened 56 stores (29 of them bought from another chain in the Southeast) and closed 9. Over the past two years it closed 51 marginal stores and opened 94. Another 32 stores are scheduled for this year.
- **Food service:** 339 restaurants: 288 lunch counters and restaurants in or next to its drugstores, 24 in its discount stores, and 27 free-standing family restaurants, including a new chain of family restaurants, many open 24 hours. Food service is about 8% of corporate sales. 22 new units are planned this year. Management has called the outlook for restaurants positive.
- **Discount department stores:** 25 large general-merchandise stores in the Southwest, bought about 12 years ago. They are about 15% of corporate sales.
- **Manufacturing:** a laboratory making more than 500 house-brand health, beauty and household products. A second laboratory and a plastic-container plant are opening.
- **Other:** an ice cream plant, a coffee-roasting plant, photo processing, a travel agency bought three years ago, more than 1,800 independent "agency" drugstores franchised to sell its house-brand products, and a large minority stake in a store-and-restaurant chain in Mexico.

## Financial position

|---|---|---|---|
| 9 years ago | $432.5M | $8.0M | $1.30 |
| 7 years ago | $559.4M | $12.4M | $1.95 |
| 4 years ago | $743.6M | $9.4M | $1.48 |
| 2 years ago | $930.9M | $13.7M | $2.11 |
| Last year | $996.6M | $7.6M | $1.15 |

- Sales have more than doubled in nine years. Earnings per share were about the same two years ago as seven years ago.
- Net margin was about 1.5% two years ago. Last year it was under 1%, after the company adopted LIFO inventory accounting. LIFO cut reported earnings but saved about $6.7 million in taxes. Management says earnings would otherwise have beaten the prior year's record.
- In its annual letter, management called its "relatively low net profit margin" its greatest opportunity, and said it would work on shrinkage (theft and loss), cost control and productivity.
- Sales passed $1 billion on an annual basis shortly after year-end.

## Market position
- Marlowe says its corporate volume is about double that of its nearest drugstore competitors.
- Regional chains are growing fast, especially in the Sun Belt. **Rival E**, a fast-growing Southeastern chain, is expanding through acquisitions in Texas, Louisiana and Georgia.
- The discount stores suffered most from the recession last year. Sales growth slowed, customers put off buying big-ticket items, and shrinkage rose to a level management called unacceptable.

## Leadership
- **President (for about five years):** the founder's grandson, a career company executive. His father, the founder's son, is chairman.
- The company added its first outside directors about 12 years ago.

## Strategy and known problems
- The company's stated aim is growth across all its divisions: more drugstores, more restaurants, more discount stores, and more manufacturing.
- Management describes the drugstores as "community centers for health and beauty service" and says the chain holds fast to its basic identity as drugstores even as stores get larger.
- The economy is weak. Management expects a difficult year.
- Capital and management attention are spread across drugstores, restaurants, discount department stores, manufacturing and several smaller businesses.
