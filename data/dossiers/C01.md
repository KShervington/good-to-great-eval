# Subject dossier: Wexley Medical Corporation

**As of:** early in Year 0 (a new chief executive has just been named). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (privately held medical-device manufacturer)

## Overview
Wexley Medical is a family-owned maker of hospital and orthopedic equipment based in a mid-sized Midwestern city. It was started roughly 35 years ago by an orthopedic surgeon, Dr. W., who designed his own replacements for orthopedic products he found ineffective. His first products were built in a spare room of a local hospital.

- **First product:** a mobile turning frame, a hospital bed whose frame pivots from side to side so that immobilized patients can be turned. It sold strongly during the war years that followed the company's start, and the trade still calls such frames by the founder's name.
- **Powered instruments:** a motorized cast saw that cuts plaster without cutting the skin beneath, an oscillating bone saw patented about 30 years ago, and a micro reciprocating saw introduced about 9 years ago.
- **Beds and stretchers:** a powered circular electric bed launched about 19 years ago, plus stretchers and related patient-handling equipment.

The founder's design rule is that every product must either make caregivers more efficient or reduce the cost of treatment. Around 19 years ago, nearly 90% of the product line came from his own ideas.

## Financial position
- About 22 years ago, the company reported record earnings of about $1 million.
- Sales for the year just ended were $17.3 million.
- Hospital beds and stretchers make up almost 70% of total sales.
- The company is privately held by the founding family. Its owners and the board have discussed taking the company public at some point.

## Market position
- Wexley's beds and patient-handling equipment are sold to hospitals. Its powered instruments are used in orthopedic care and surgery.
- An international subsidiary was set up about 5 years ago to build sales forces in Canada and overseas.
- Orthopedic surgery is changing. About 6 years ago, **Rival H**, the orthopedic unit of a large pharmaceutical company, won the first federal approval of a bone cement for use in total hip replacement. Two years ago it introduced a cemented artificial hip. Wexley does not make implants.

## Leadership
- **The founder** is in his early 80s and has retired from surgical practice. He has run the company centrally and kept a close hold on all decisions.
- **The founder's son** became general manager about 22 years ago and president about 8 years ago. Last July he was killed in a plane crash. At his death the company had about 280 employees.
- **The new chief executive** was named president and CEO this month. He is 43, from the South, and comes from outside the company: he ran a surgical-instrument subsidiary of a large pharmaceutical company. The board first offered him the job last year. He declined, and then accepted when he was approached a second time. He takes over a management team built under the founder's family.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Sales force.** In the past few years the company moved its salespeople from commission to salary. After the change it was left with virtually no effective sales force. Whether and how to rebuild one, and on what pay basis, is unresolved.
- **Concentration.** Most sales come from beds and stretchers. The instrument line is smaller.
- **Management systems.** Decisions have been made centrally by the founder and his family. The company has no formal budget process and no formal managerial goal-setting.
- **Ownership and funding.** The company is family-owned with no public shares. A public offering has been discussed but not decided.
