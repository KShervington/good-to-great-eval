# Subject dossier: Halvorsen-Pike Corporation

**As of:** early in Year 0 (the start of a new chief executive's term). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed manufacturer)

## Overview
Halvorsen-Pike is a paper company founded about 100 years ago in a small mill town in the upper Midwest. It began by making newsprint from rags and grew by building and buying mills. It now runs several businesses under one roof:

- **Business and printing papers:** coated papers for magazines and catalogs, newsprint (partly through a large mill complex it owns jointly in Canada), and specialty, envelope and cigarette papers bought through a string of acquisitions over the past 20 years.
- **Pulp and forest products:** pulp mills, sawmills and large timberlands, including more than 300,000 acres of prime land in a western state.
- **Consumer products:** a leading facial-tissue brand, a feminine-care pad brand, toilet tissue, and a disposable diaper launched three years ago.
- **Miscellaneous:** a corporate air fleet of six aircraft, which the finance department has proposed turning from a cost center into a profit center by offering charter and maintenance services.

The company began as, and still sees itself as, a mill company. Most of its executives came up through manufacturing, and the namesake mill town is central to its identity.

## Financial position
- Over the past 20 years, total returns to shareholders have trailed the general stock market by roughly a third. [Exact annual figures are not available in this dossier.]
- The mill businesses are capital-hungry commodity operations. Their pricing follows the industry cycle, and they need large reinvestment to stay at cost parity.
- No segment profit breakdown is published in a form that allows return on capital to be compared across businesses.

## Market position
- **Facial tissue:** the company invented the category decades ago and still holds the leading share. Its brand is very strong.
- **Feminine care:** the pad brand was the long-time leader, but tampons from a specialist rival have taken share throughout the past decade.
- **Diapers:** the company's disposable diaper, launched three years ago, sold well at first. Since then it has failed to keep up with product improvements, and it has drawn complaints about leakage. The market is dominated by **Rival P**, a giant packaged-goods marketer known as the best consumer marketer in the world, which created the mass disposable-diaper category.
- **Toilet tissue and towels:** the main competitor is **Rival S**, a larger and more profitable paper company that leads the U.S. sanitary-paper market. Rival P has also begun a national push in toilet tissue.
- Consumer paper markets are growing faster than printing papers, and are driven by brand advertising, distribution and product innovation, not mill cost alone.

## Leadership
- **New chief executive:** has been at the company about 20 years. He trained as a lawyer and came up through the in-house legal and finance functions, not the mills. He is described by colleagues as shy, plainly dressed, awkward in public and uninterested in publicity. He is known for blunt self-criticism and very high standards. Some directors doubted whether he was qualified for the top job.
- Shortly after taking office, the new CEO was diagnosed with a serious cancer of the nose and throat. Doctors gave him a poor prognosis. He has said he intends to keep working through treatment.
- The rest of the senior team is drawn mainly from mill operations.

## Strategy and known problems
- There is no single stated strategy. Capital is spread across all businesses, with the mills receiving the largest share by tradition.
- Inside the company there is a debate, not yet resolved, about whether it can afford to compete head-on with Rival P in consumer products while also funding mill modernization.
- The consumer businesses face a hard competitive test. Rival P has deeper marketing budgets and research capabilities, and both of the company's newer consumer products (diapers and feminine care) are losing ground.
- Selling the mills would be culturally explosive: it would mean giving up the company's identity, its namesake mill and its sense of what it is.
- Research and development is centered on cellulose fiber. The company has little expertise yet in synthetic nonwoven materials, which are becoming important in absorbent products.
