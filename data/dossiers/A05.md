# Subject dossier: Tolliver Company

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed specialty retailer)

## Overview
Tolliver is a consumer-electronics and appliance retailer founded about 33 years ago in a mid-sized Southern city. The founder opened a television store when a local TV station was about to go on the air. The company went public about 20 years ago.

**History of formats:**
- **1960s:** the company grew by running licensed TV and audio departments inside discount department-store chains. It also bought small chains: a discount department store, a hardware-and-housewares department operator, several appliance stores in nearby cities, and a stereo business with a mail-order arm. It also bought a store group in the Midwest.
- **About 12 years ago:** it opened small audio specialty stores.
- **Eight years ago:** the company lost $3 million on sales of $69 million. Management then:
  - left tire sales and most leased-department operations (one West Coast discount chain arrangement remains);
  - sold or closed stores in several Midwestern cities;
  - over the following years, left mail-order and closed most of the audio specialty stores.
- **The superstore bet:** around the time of that loss, the company opened a single 40,000-square-foot warehouse-showroom store in its home city at a cost of about $2 million, roughly half its net worth at the time. A second one followed later.
- **Since then:** the company converted its discount audio stores into mid-sized, full-service electronics stores under a new name, starting in the national capital area. Last year it opened four large superstores under that name in cities of a neighboring state.
- **Last year's acquisition:** it bought a bankrupt eight-store electronics chain in the country's largest city for $6.6 million. The deal also brought about $36.5 million in tax-loss credits. The chain is struggling in a highly competitive market.

## Financial position
- Sales were $120 million three years ago, up from $69 million in the loss year five years before that.
- Profit figures for recent years are not available in this dossier.
- About 80% of revenue comes from consumer electronics.

## Market position
- The company runs four retail operations with about 100 outlets, roughly double the number of seven years ago:
  - mid-sized electronics stores;
  - superstores;
  - the newly acquired big-city chain;
  - leased departments in a West Coast discount chain.
- In the national capital area, its electronics stores hold about 11% of local consumer-electronics sales.
- **Rival S**, a discount electronics-and-appliance chain based in a large Northeastern city, is expanding aggressively. Two years ago it was bought by a specialty steel company, which is backing a strategy of entering new markets with several stores at once and heavy advertising.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive:** the founder's son, a lawyer by training. He joined the company about 16 years ago as head of legal affairs, became president about 12 years ago and chief executive about 10 years ago. The founder remains chairman.
- The chief executive is known inside the company for asking questions rather than announcing answers. He is said to press his board and managers hard on what the company's direction should be.

## Strategy and known problems
- The company has not committed to a single format. It is running mid-sized stores, superstores, an acquired big-city chain and leased departments side by side.
- Each superstore is a large capital commitment for a company of this size. The first one cost about half the company's net worth.
- The company has grown in two ways:
  - buying existing chains, most recently the big-city chain, partly for its tax credits;
  - opening its own stores in new markets.
- It has not said which route it will favor, how fast it will expand, or in which regions.

## What is not in this dossier
- Net income and return on equity for recent years.
- Profitability by format (mid-sized stores, superstores, acquired chain).
- Debt levels.
- Market share outside the national capital area.
