# Subject dossier: Lindqvist Company

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed grocery retailer)

## Overview
Lindqvist is a supermarket chain based in a large Midwestern city. It was founded about 90 years ago as a single grocery store. It grew into one of the largest grocery chains in the country by opening many small neighborhood stores and later replacing them with supermarkets. It is vertically integrated: it runs its own plants for dairy, eggs and other foods, and sells a single store brand across its product lines.

**Past changes in the business:**
- **About 40 years ago:** a new leader was hired to close unprofitable stores. He later merged dozens of private labels into one company brand. He also introduced formal consumer research, treating shoppers' purchases as "votes" on products and selling methods.
- **About 13 years ago:** the company bought a small drugstore chain. Its owner became head of a new drugstore division. The following year, the first of its drugstores opened next to a company supermarket.
- **About eight years ago:** it formed a division of very large general-merchandise "family center" stores, with expanded home goods and pharmacy sections.
- **The past decade:** a former congressman and judge led the company. He concentrated on manufacturing and consolidated small plants into regional facilities as the interstate highways opened. Last year the company opened what is described as the world's largest dairy plant.

## Financial position
- Sales passed $1 billion about 20 years ago and $2 billion about ten years ago.
- Recent sales, profit margins and return on equity are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- **Market exits in recent years:**
  - sold its stores in the national capital area seven years ago;
  - sold its stores in two large Midwestern metropolitan areas three years ago;
  - left a Southern city early this decade after heavy competition from a regional chain and local grocers;
  - sold a few stores in another Midwestern city last year.
- **Other markets:** about ten years ago it bought a 56-store chain on the West Coast. It also operates drugstores in a Southeastern state.
- **Antitrust:** two years ago the company settled a federal antitrust challenge, without admitting a violation. It sold three discount food departments and accepted limits on future acquisitions.
- **Rival A**, the country's largest grocery chain, had sales more than twice those of its nearest competitor a few years ago. Its stores are generally smaller and older than competitors'. Last year it converted nearly all of its stores to a deep-discount pricing program, setting off an industry-wide price war.

## Leadership
- **President (since Year −3):** the former drugstore owner who has run the drugstore division since it was formed. He came from outside the grocery business.
- His predecessor, the former congressman and judge, ran the company through most of the past decade with a focus on manufacturing.

## Strategy and known problems
- **The store question:** most of the company's stores are conventional supermarkets, many of them small by current standards. The company is testing specialty departments inside larger stores and has paired some supermarkets with its drugstores next door. How far and how fast to move, and what to do with the existing small stores, is undecided.
- **Technology:** last year, with an electronics company, it became the first grocer to test electronic checkout scanners under working conditions.
- **Price war:** Rival A's discount program is pressuring margins across the industry.
- **Manufacturing:** the plants tie up capital. They also supply the company's store brand.
- **Product quality:** about six years ago, federal meat-inspection hearings found that the company sold processed meats with excess water and fillers.
- **Markets:** it is still unclear which regional markets the company can lead and which it should leave.
