# Subject dossier: Kessling Company

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed pharmaceutical manufacturer)

## Overview
Kessling is a prescription-drug company founded almost 90 years ago in a small Midwestern city by a physician who patented a pill that dissolved more easily than those of the day. He and his brother built the company, and members of the founding family, or people who married into it, have led it ever since. The founder was known for employee welfare (an early company lunch program, group life insurance) and for civic giving in the home city.

Kessling has a long research tradition:
- It hired its first research scientist about 60 years ago.
- It was the first to market an adrenal-hormone product, and it developed antibiotics during the Second World War.
- It developed a low-cost fermentation route to steroid hormones.
- It introduced the first oral drug for diabetes about 17 years ago.

In recent years it has also moved beyond drugs. Six years ago it bought a vegetable and field-crop seed company through a share exchange. The seed company had about $27.5 million in sales the year before the deal.

## Financial position
- The company has been publicly traded for about 16 years.
- About 16 years ago, it was the sixth-largest U.S. maker of antibiotics, with antibiotic sales of $22.6 million.
- Current total sales, earnings and return on capital are not available in this dossier.

## Market position
- **Regulatory setbacks:**
  - Five years ago, federal drug regulators ordered a widely prescribed combination antibiotic off the market. Press reports said the drug accounted for about 12% of the company's domestic gross income. The company fought the order in court, and the courts upheld it.
  - Studies in the past few years have linked the company's oral diabetes drug to heart disease, which has weakened confidence in that product.
- **New product:** the company has licensed U.S. rights to an anti-inflammatory compound for arthritis from a British drug firm, and it expects to launch the product soon.
- **Rival A**, a diversified health-care company based in a large Midwestern city, competes in prescription drugs. It has spread into hospital products, infant nutrition, consumer goods and diagnostics. It recently suffered a large product recall and lost a major consumer product to a government ban.
- The largest research-based drug companies are bigger than Kessling and have larger research budgets. Kessling sees itself as a research-based prescription-drug company and measures itself against them.

## Leadership
- **President:** has held the job for about 13 years and married into the founding family. Earlier leaders were the founder, his nephew, and his stepson and son-in-law.
- **Board:** until six years ago, only family members and employees could sit on it.

## Strategy and known problems
- **Core identity:** research-driven prescription drugs, with new non-drug businesses (seeds and other agricultural products) added alongside.
- **Open questions:**
  - Can the company match the research spending of the largest drug companies?
  - Should it lean more on licensed products and on diversification?
- **Regulatory risk:** recent actions have hit established products, and the approval process for new drugs is slow.
- **Product concentration:** the licensed arthritis compound could become a large share of future profits, which would make the company dependent on a product whose underlying rights belong to another firm.

## What is not in this dossier
- Total sales, earnings and return on equity for recent years.
- The share of sales from non-drug businesses.
- Market shares by drug category.
- Research spending as a share of sales, compared with peers.
- Ownership stakes held by the founding family.
