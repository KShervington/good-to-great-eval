# Subject dossier: Western Commonwealth Bank

**As of:** early in Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed bank holding company)

## Overview
Western Commonwealth is one of the largest banks in the world and the dominant bank in its home state on the West Coast. An immigrant financier founded it about 80 years ago, and it grew into a statewide branch bank. It has a long record of innovation: it launched the first successful bank credit card, and it was one of the first banks to computerize its record-keeping.

Its activities include:
- **Retail banking:** a very large statewide branch network. Through the late 1960s and into the 1970s, retail banking produced about half of profits.
- **Agricultural lending:** it has been the largest agricultural lender in the world.
- **Mortgages:** a large portfolio of home mortgages written at low fixed rates in earlier years.
- **Corporate and international lending:** large loans to companies worldwide, including energy, shipping and developing-country borrowers.
- **New ventures:** it has just completed the purchase of a fast-growing discount stock brokerage for about $55 million.

## Financial position
- Total assets grew from $11.9 billion 23 years ago to about $60 billion eight years ago (up 50% in the two years before that), and to $112.9 billion two years ago.
- From 11 to 5 years ago, it was the only one of the 20 largest U.S. banks to average 15% annual growth.
- When interest rates rose sharply in the 1970s, the low-rate mortgage portfolio became a heavy drag on earnings.
- Its branch system gives it high overhead, by some accounts the highest in the industry.

## Market position and industry change
- **Deregulation:** A federal law passed three years ago is phasing out interest-rate ceilings on deposits over six years. A second law, passed last autumn, lets banks offer money-market deposit accounts to compete with money-market mutual funds. The bank's huge base of low-cost deposits, its traditional advantage, will increasingly have to be paid market rates.
- **Rival W**, a smaller in-state rival, competes with it in consumer banking and in middle-market lending. Rival W's long-time CEO resigned last month and a new CEO has just taken over.
- Since the current CEO took over, the bank's loans in energy, shipping, farming and developing countries have begun to go bad. Last year Mexico's debt crisis raised doubts about the region's ability to repay foreign banks.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive (for two years):** took over at age 40. He holds an MBA from a leading West Coast business school and once advised the State Department on monetary affairs. [His career path inside the bank and his stated priorities are not documented in this dossier.]
- **Previous CEO:** led the bank for ten years through its fastest growth, then left two years ago to head a major international development institution.

## Strategy and known problems
- The bank's strategy has been growth in size: more assets, more branches, more countries, more lines of business. It is now adding new businesses such as discount brokerage.
- Its problems: high overhead, a low-rate mortgage book, rising funding costs under deregulation, and emerging bad loans in energy, shipping, agriculture and developing countries.
- Options open to management include cutting branches and staff, selling assets, raising capital, or expanding into new businesses and states to grow out of the problems.
