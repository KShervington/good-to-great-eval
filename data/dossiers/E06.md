# Subject dossier: Halvard Strings

**As of:** the start of Year 0, ahead of the board's annual strategy session.
**Subject type:** Business (privately held manufacturer)

*Pre-read for the strategy session, prepared by the CFO. Figures are from the audited accounts and the company's own player surveys.*

## Overview
Halvard makes strings for the violin family: violin, viola, cello and double bass. It sells mainly to professional and advanced players, through about 1,900 independent violin shops and specialist string retailers in North America and Europe. It employs 128 people at one plant and is owned by the founding family and a group of long-serving employees.

Fourteen years ago, shortly after the current CEO took over, the company made two decisions that still define it. It stopped selling student strings and guitar strings through mass-market music distributors, which were then about 40% of unit volume and about 22% of revenue. And it put the money into a player-testing program and its own winding machines.

- Operating margin has been between 24% and 29% in every year of the last eleven, except Year −6 (21.6%), when silver and tungsten prices spiked.
- Return on invested capital has averaged about 34% over the last ten years. The company has no debt and holds about nine months of operating costs in cash.
- Revenue grew every year for fourteen years. Growth in the last three years was 9%, 7% and 8%.

## Market position
- **Professional players.** Halvard surveys string sections in 61 professional orchestras each year. The share of players using a Halvard string on at least one string of their instrument rose from 9% fourteen years ago to **31%** last year. Among cellists it is 44%.
- **Loyalty.** Of players who switched to Halvard in a given year, 78% were still using it three years later (shop-panel data).
- **Pricing.** Halvard's sets sell at a 15–25% premium over the two largest competitors. The company has never sold below its minimum advertised price and does not list on general online marketplaces. Shops report steady margins on Halvard sets.
- **The market.** The professional and advanced violin-family string market grows at about 2% a year by most estimates. Competitors include two large European makers and a private-equity-backed group that consolidated several student brands.

## Leadership
- **CEO (Annelie Brandt), 61:** trained as an acoustics engineer and joined as head of R&D. People describe her as quiet and stubborn. She declined a trade magazine's cover profile last year and suggested the magazine interview the Listening Room panel instead. Asked about the company's record, she usually says that it was "lucky to find the right players early."
- **The Listening Room:** a rotating panel of 40 professional players who blind-test every prototype. No string reaches the market without the panel's approval; three proposed products were killed by the panel in the last five years.
- **Succession:** the COO has been designated successor and has run the plant for four years. The head of winding engineering retires in two years; her deputy has trained alongside her for four.
- **Commercial director (Pieter Holm):** hired two years ago from a consumer audio company. Strong record growing a premium headphone line into mass retail.

## Strategy and known problems
**Proposal for the session: "Halvard Six."** Pieter proposes launching a line of acoustic and electric guitar strings, with these arguments:
- The guitar string market is about six times larger than Halvard's core market by unit volume and is growing faster.
- In lab tests, a prototype using Halvard's winding process lasted 2.3 times longer before corroding than the best-selling guitar string.
- A national music-retail chain has offered launch placement on end-caps in about 900 stores, plus featured placement online.
- His plan projects $19M of revenue in Year 3 at a 31% gross margin, funded from cash. It would need a second shift on two winding machines and four new sales staff.
- He argues that the core market is mature at about 2% a year, and that relying on one narrow niche is itself a risk. "We have the best winding technology in the world and we only point it at two percent of the string players."

Two board members support exploring it. The CEO has said she wants the full board to discuss it, and that she has "not made up her mind about the question, only about who answers it." She has asked whether the Listening Room should test guitar prototypes, which it has never done.

**Second item:** the private-equity group has quietly offered to sell Halvard one of its student string brands, at about 1.1 times revenue.

**Ongoing issues the CFO lists:**
- Metal prices: silver and tungsten are hedged nine months forward.
- Shop consolidation: the number of independent violin shops in North America fell about 3% last year. Halvard's sales through them still grew.
- Lead times on cello and bass sets slipped to five weeks in the autumn and are back to two.
- The plant's roof replacement ($1.3M) is scheduled for summer.
