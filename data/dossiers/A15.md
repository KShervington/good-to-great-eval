# Subject dossier: Halberd Tobacco Company

**As of:** late January of Year 0. Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed consumer-products manufacturer)

## Overview
Halberd is a U.S. cigarette maker with roots in a London tobacconist's shop more than a century old. A New York financier bought its American business after the First World War. It grew through the 1930s with a low-priced blend and heavy advertising, and for a time held fourth place among U.S. cigarette makers. After the Second World War it lost ground, and it was slow to respond when smokers began moving to filter cigarettes in the early 1950s.

Four years ago Halberd ranked **sixth and last** among the six major U.S. cigarette companies, and its best-selling brand ranked only tenth in the country.

Its brand portfolio includes:
- **Ridgeway:** an old brand launched in the 1920s as a premium cigarette aimed at women. It never reached even 1% of U.S. sales and was briefly withdrawn. About ten years ago Halberd relaunched it as a filter cigarette in a new crush-proof flip-top box, with a new blend and a masculine image (a cowboy and other rugged men) created with a Chicago advertising agency. Since then it has grown from under 1% of U.S. sales, and the company has dropped the other male figures to keep only the cowboy. Last year it began television ads set to a sweeping western film score.
- **Premium brands** that came with a small luxury tobacco house Halberd bought ten years ago, including a premium filter brand whose sales grew strongly under its then-owner, now Halberd's CEO.
- Its older namesake brand and several others.

## Financial position
- Halberd held about 9.4% of the U.S. cigarette market three years ago. [Current share, sales and profit figures are not available in this dossier.]

## Diversification
In the mid-1950s Halberd moved into flexible packaging and paper. In the past four years it has also bought a safety-razor maker, a shaving-cream brand and a chewing-gum company. These businesses are small next to tobacco and are unrelated to one another.

## International
Four years ago the company named a senior executive to run international operations. Halberd is now the leading U.S. exporter of tobacco products. It also licenses its brands to manufacturers abroad; the first Ridgeway license (in Switzerland) was signed about seven years ago.

## Market position
- **Rival R** has been the industry's largest company for most of the past 40 years. Its plain nonfilter brand was the country's best-selling cigarette until a few years ago. It also owns a leading filter brand (launched ten years ago) and the first king-size filtered menthol brand (launched eight years ago).
- Another large rival has launched a stream of new brands over the past year.
- Filters have been gaining share for a decade, driven by health worries.
- Rival R set up a committee seven years ago to study non-tobacco investments, and has recently bought a fruit-drink company.

## The health issue
- Two weeks ago the U.S. Surgeon General released a report by an expert advisory committee. It concluded that cigarette smoking causes lung cancer in men and is a health hazard serious enough to warrant remedial action.
- Eleven days later the Federal Trade Commission proposed requiring health warnings on cigarette packs.
- Congress and regulators are now debating warning labels and limits on cigarette advertising. Industry executives, including Halberd's chief executive, publicly dispute that smoking has been proven to cause disease.
- Nobody yet knows how far sales will fall, or how much regulation will follow.

## Leadership
- **Chief executive (president and CEO for seven years):** comes from a family long in the cigar business; his father headed a large cigar company. He graduated from an Ivy League college, worked as a clerk in a cigar store and in a cigar factory abroad, and served as a naval gunnery officer in the Second World War. After the war he ran the small luxury tobacco house his father had bought, and joined Halberd when Halberd acquired it, rising to CEO within three years. He personally oversaw the Ridgeway relaunch, down to the blend and the box design. He is described as an old-style tobacco man who knows his customers and how to sell to them.

## Strategy and known problems
- Halberd is the smallest of the six majors and faces much larger rivals with bigger advertising budgets.
- The company must decide how much to concentrate its money on Ridgeway and a few other brands, versus spreading it across many brands.
- It must also decide how far to expand overseas, and how much to invest in non-tobacco businesses as a hedge against the health issue.

## What is not in this dossier

- Profitability of the non-tobacco businesses.
- Consumer research on how the health report is affecting smoking behavior.
