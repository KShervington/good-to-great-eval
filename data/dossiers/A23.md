# Subject dossier: Calder Business Machines Company

**As of:** early in Year 0 (a new executive vice-president has just been given charge of day-to-day operations). Everything below was known or knowable on that date. Nothing after it is included.
**Subject type:** Business (publicly listed office-equipment and computer maker)

## Overview
Calder is a Midwestern office-machine company founded about 75 years ago around a mechanical adding device. For most of its history its core customers were accountants and bookkeepers, and its core products were adding and accounting machines. Under its last two chief executives it has tried to turn itself from an adding-machine maker into a diversified business-equipment company. It now has several lines:

- **Accounting and adding machines:** still a central business. Adding machines alone have fallen to only about 6% of sales.
- **Bank automation:** magnetic inks and automatic check-sorting equipment, sold since Year −4, plus electronic bank bookkeeping machines.
- **Business forms and checks:** printers of bank forms and checks bought over the past decade.
- **Electronic computers:** the company entered the field by buying a small high-speed computer maker eight years ago. Three years ago it launched a new large computer that it says is cheaper and simpler to operate than rival machines. The machine translates algebraic or plain business-English problems into machine instructions in seconds, which reduces the need for expensive programming staff. It rents for roughly $13,000 to $50,000 a month.
- **Military computers:** a separate division supplies computing equipment to missile and space programs.

The company has built plants in Britain, France and Brazil. All its calculators are made in Scotland, which keeps them inside European trade barriers and lets them compete on price at home.

## Financial position
- Sales grew from $94 million in Year −16 to a record $389 million in Year −4.
- Profit margins have collapsed. Net earnings were about 2% of sales in Year −4, against about 13% in Year −16.
- Management blames the heavy research and development spending needed to enter new fields. It has said profits will recover once those costs are written off, but has given no timeline.

## Market position
- **Computers:** the market is dominated by **Rival I**, a much larger office-equipment company, with **Rival R** (the computer arm of a diversified electrical group) a distant second. Calder is among the smallest of Rival I's main competitors.
- About 12 years ago Calder competed for the design contract for a national air-defense computer network. The contract went to Rival I and gave it a technical and scale lead that competitors have struggled to close.
- **Accounting machines:** **Rival N**, a large cash-register and accounting-machine maker, is the main competitor.
- **Banking:** bank automation is a fast-growing market, and Calder is an early entrant in check-processing equipment.
- Observers describe the company's product line as narrow and still too dependent on accounting machines.

## Leadership
- **Chairman and chief executive:** joined the company more than 40 years ago as a shipping clerk and rose to the top. He succeeded a president who died about five years ago. Just before his death, that president had negotiated a computer partnership with a large electronics company. The partner approved it, but Calder's board never did, and the plan lapsed.
- **New executive vice-president (Year 0):** about 52. He joined as a salesman nearly 30 years ago after business school and built the company's small export department into a major international division. He has now taken charge of day-to-day operations and is widely seen as the chairman's likely successor. Colleagues describe him as brilliant and forceful, a man who dominates discussions and has little patience for people he considers less able.

## Strategy and known problems
- The stated direction is to keep diversifying away from mechanical adding machines into electronic business systems, banking equipment and computers.
- The company faces a trade-off between funding computer research and development, which has pushed margins down, and restoring profitability quickly.
- In general-purpose computers it is a small player against a dominant rival with far greater scale. In banking and accounting it has long customer relationships and established sales channels.
- It has an established international sales organization and overseas plants.
- Open questions inside the company are whether to keep spending heavily to compete across the whole computer market, and how fast to cut costs to rebuild margins.
