# Track C sources

Screening log: `data/track_c_criteria.md`. One pair passed, Stryker / U.S. Surgical, built as C01/C02. All other candidates were rejected (see the log).

## Framework authority (answer keys only; paraphrased)
- Jim Collins & Morten T. Hansen, *Great by Choice* (2011): Stryker as a 10X company, USSC as its direct comparison, and the 20 Mile March concept.
- Jim Collins & Morten T. Hansen, "Jim Collins: How to manage through chaos," *Fortune*, 30 Sep 2011 (book excerpt). https://www.fortune.com/2011/09/30/jim-collins-how-to-manage-through-chaos
  Used for: Brown became CEO in 1977 and set 20% annual net-income growth as "the law"; the Snorkel Award and the division breakfasts; Stryker met the goal more than 90% of the time from 1977 to 1998 (excluding a 1990 extraordinary gain); USSC grew faster than Stryker in more than half the years and disappeared as a public company in 1998; the 20 Mile March has both a floor and a ceiling.

## C01: Stryker (dossier as of February 1977)
- Stryker Corporation, company history timeline. https://www.stryker.com/files/history.xml
  Used for: practice begun in 1940 in a basement room at a local hospital; first employee in 1941 and wartime demand; Orthopedic Frame Company formed in 1946; oscillating-saw patent in 1947; Lee Stryker general manager in 1955, with record earnings of $1M that year; Circ-O-Lectric bed and 60 employees in 1958, with about 90% of products from the founder's ideas; renamed in 1964 and the founder retired from practice; micro reciprocating saw in 1968; Lee president in 1969; Howmedica bone cement first FDA-approved for total hip surgery in 1971; Stryker International formed in 1972; Exeter hip in 1975; Lee killed 25 Jul 1976, with 280 employees; 1976 sales $17.3M; Brown named president and CEO in Feb 1977 "with goals to take the company public and grow 20% annually"; IPO 2 May 1979; Osteonics acquired Aug 1979; divisionalization in 1980 and sales of $36M; sales of $280M in 1990.
- Encyclopedia.com (International Directory of Company Histories), "Stryker Corporation." https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/stryker-corporation
  Used for: the founder's background; the turning frame and cast cutter; beds and stretchers at almost 70% of sales; the move of salespeople from commission to salary that left virtually no sales force; the founder's centralized control; Brown's age (43), Tennessee origin, earlier role heading a Bristol-Myers surgical-instrument subsidiary, and his refusal in 1976 followed by acceptance in 1977; the 20% goal announced at the 1979 IPO; Forbes Best Small Companies list for 10 years from 1981.
- FundingUniverse, "Stryker Corporation History." https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/stryker-corporation-history/
  Used for: the same facts, cross-checked; the founder's design rule (efficiency or cost reduction); Brown's first moves (rebuilt the commissioned sales force, formal budget, cost trimming, goal-setting, expanded power tools); privately held until 1979.
- Wikipedia, "Stryker Corporation." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker_Corporation
  Used for: later history only (Howmedica for $1.9B in 1998; Brown moved to chairman in 2003; revenue rose from $17M to $6.7B under Brown).
- Fortune (above): later history and answer key.

**Dates chosen.** The transition year is 1977, when Brown took over in February. Collins/Hansen and the Stryker timeline agree on it. The dossier is dated February 1977, just after the appointment, and includes only facts from 1976 or earlier.

**Unverified or conflicting points.**
- The Stryker timeline says Lee Stryker had grown the company to "nearly $10 million in earnings" by 1976. That cannot be right next to $17.3M in sales (it probably means something else), so it was left out.
- Sources disagree on when the company was renamed: Encyclopedia.com gives 1964 and FundingUniverse gives 1946. The dossier is anonymized, so it doesn't matter.
- Sources disagree on when the 20% goal was set: on appointment (Stryker timeline) or at the 1979 IPO (Encyclopedia.com). The goal is the answer, so it was left out of the dossier. The dossier does say a public offering had been discussed, based on the timeline's "goals to take the company public."
- No profit, margin or year-by-year sales figures before 1976 could be found. They are listed under "What is not in this dossier."
- The exact year of the salary switch ("mid-1970s") is not known. The dossier says "in the past few years."

## C02: United States Surgical Corporation (dossier as of early 1977)
- FundingUniverse, "United States Surgical Corporation History." https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/united-states-surgical-corporation-history/
  Used for: incorporated in 1964 with four employees; Hirsch's background (high school education, unsuccessful dry-cleaning equipment business); the stapler seen at a patent broker's office in 1963, the basement prototype and $75,000 of savings; endorsement by two Johns Hopkins surgeons; $2M from two investors; first sales in 1967 of $350,000 through wholesalers; 1969 sales of $1M with a $1M loss, surgeons not using the staplers, and the cartridge model; 20 nurses hired in 1967, then army medics; trained experienced salespeople from 1972 (240-hour course plus 40 hours of animal lab, about $8,000 each); commission-only pay and quick dismissals. Later history: Ethicon entered in 1977; distributors replaced in 1977; alleged accounting fraud from 1979; $26M earnings cut agreed in February 1984; 1982 sales of $160M; 1991-93 laparoscopy boom and bust; Tyco deal in 1998 for about $3.2B.
- Encyclopedia.com, "United States Surgical Corp." https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/united-states-surgical-corp
  Used for: cross-checking the above; use mainly in abdominal and thoracic surgery; about 20,000 patients in 1969; "had the surgical staple market largely to itself" before 1977; high-pressure culture; Hirsch's interest in gadgets and biology; the external wound-closure business (implied by Ethicon taking share of it in 1977).
- Collins/Hansen (above): USSC as the comparison and its fate.

**Dates chosen.** The dossier is dated early 1977, the same as C01. Several 1977 events are left out because they can't be placed before the dossier date: Ethicon's entry, the replacement of distributors with an in-house sales force, the $100M development program, and the December 1977 NYT story on salesmen in operating rooms.

**Not verified.** USSC's sales and earnings for 1970-76, when it was listed, its patent position, and the dates of the Puerto Rico and North Haven plants and of the outsourced-manufacturing quality problems ("late 1970s," undated). All were left out of the dossier.
