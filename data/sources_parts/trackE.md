# Sources: Track E "already great" controls (E01–E05)

## Collins sources
The general authority for the answer keys is Jim Collins, *Good to Great* (2001). I did not consult the book text; I avoided an unauthorized full-text copy found online. Collins claims therefore come from secondary summaries and from recollection, and each one is flagged below. No Collins quotation of 15 or more words appears anywhere.

**Secondary sources used for the Collins framing**
- **LitCharts, *Good to Great* chapter 5** (https://litcharts.com/lit/good-to-great/chapter-5):
  - Walgreens hedgehog (best, most convenient drugstores with high profit per customer visit);
  - Abbott's choice to be best at cost-effective health-care products rather than the best pharma company;
  - Philip Morris executives used as the passion example.
- **Web search summaries of the Good to Great performance table** (jimcollins.com article page; slideshare chapter 1–3 deck; Wikipedia "Good to Great"):
  - cumulative returns relative to the market: Abbott 3.98x, Kimberly-Clark 3.42x, Philip Morris 7.06x (1964–1979), Walgreens 7.34x (1975–1990);
  - Philip Morris is the only company in both Good to Great and Built to Last.
  - The Nucor multiple (I recall 5.16x) was **not** confirmed, so it is not used.
- **Goodreads reader notes and the jimcollins.com "economic denominator" page.** Nucor's denominator: profit per ton of finished steel (confirmed).

**From recollection of *Good to Great*, chapter 5 table; not verified online**
- Abbott: profit per employee.
- Kimberly-Clark: profit per consumer brand (also used in A01).
- Philip Morris: profit per global brand (category).

**Transition years (Collins appendix, from recollection)**
- Nucor 1975, Walgreens 1975, Abbott 1974, Kimberly-Clark 1972, Philip Morris 1964.
- The Philip Morris (1964–1979) and Walgreens (1975–1990) windows are confirmed by the search snippets above.
- Kimberly-Clark: A01 uses 1971 (Darwin Smith's first year as CEO); the Collins 15-year window I recall starts in 1972. **Lead should reconcile.**

## E01: Nucor (dossier as of early 1996, with full-year 1995 results)

**Dates chosen**
- **Transition year: 1975** (Collins).
- **Dossier date: early 1996.** This is 21 years after the transition. Correnti took over as CEO in January 1996, and the FY1995 results are on record.
- **The 10 years after (1996–2006) are clean:**
  - profitable every year through the 1998–2003 steel crisis (2003 trough $62.8M);
  - record results in 2004–2006 (2006 sales $14.75B, net earnings $1.76B);
  - the first annual loss came in 2009.
- **Caveat:** the 1999 proxy shows the stock lagging the S&P 500 badly from 1993 to 1998.

**Sources**
- **Nucor Form 10-K FY1995**, SEC EDGAR (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000073309/000095016896000539/0000950168-96-000539.txt):
  - net sales and net earnings 1991–1995;
  - ROE (9.5% in 1991; 22.4% in 1994; 21.9% in 1995);
  - long-term debt $106.85M and equity $1.382B;
  - tons sold (3.117M in 1991; 6.745M in 1995);
  - 6,200 employees;
  - "less than 25 employees in its executive offices";
  - capacity of close to 9M tons;
  - a new sheet mill (about $300M, early 1997);
  - capital spending of $263M in 1995 and more than $500M planned for 1996;
  - scrap about $155 per gross ton in 1995 vs $145 in 1994;
  - fasteners market dominated by foreign suppliers;
  - management titles for Iverson, Correnti and Aycock.
- **FundingUniverse company history** (IDCH text, 1998) (https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/nucor-corporation-history/):
  - Iverson CEO from 1965;
  - thin-slab casting at Crawfordsville (1989) and Hickman (September 1992);
  - the 1990 ladle fatality and OSHA fine;
  - 1993 scrap up 55%;
  - the Trinidad iron-carbide plant ($65M, 320,000 tons, a year late, 30% over budget);
  - competitors' ten new minimills planned for 1998, adding about 40% capacity;
  - sheet orders down and prices cut in 1995, with shares down more than 30% from 1994 to 1995;
  - Correnti CEO from January 1996 ("keep the train on track", paraphrased in the dossier);
  - never laid off a worker, and bonuses can more than double pay;
  - four management layers;
  - the Nucor-Yamato joint venture.
- **Trade article on the iron-carbide plant** (Recycling Today, "Cutting edge steel maker", https://www.recyclingtoday.com/article/cutting-edge-steel-maker, via search summary): down for repairs twice since its late-1994 start-up, mechanical problems with auxiliary equipment, and Correnti calling this expected.
  - A 2001 Recycling Today item calls the plant a failed "white elephant".
  - **Not verified:** the exact closure date and any write-off. The answer key says only "failed".
- **Business North Carolina, "Can Nucor iron out problems with prices?"** (1 April 1996) (https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/can-nucor-iron-out-problems-prices-53655656): shares above $70 in 1994, low $40s in 1995, about $58 at the end of January 1996.
- **Tuck School (Dartmouth), "Nucor Corporation (B)"** (2001) (https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/chris.trimble/osi/downloads/20016_Nucor_B.pdf):
  - Iverson's board made up of current and former employees;
  - Aycock retired in 1991, stayed a director, and was the second-largest individual shareholder;
  - Iverson's policies (no acquisitions, build from scratch, domestic only, steel only);
  - the 1999 board coup and its agenda (acquisitions, global expansion, blast furnaces, non-steel diversification, extra layers). This agenda is used only in the answer key, not in the dossier, because it postdates the dossier.
- **Deseret News, "Nucor CEO resigns amid board dispute"** (4 June 1999) (https://www.deseret.com/1999/6/4/19449061/nucor-ceo-resigns-amid-board-dispute). Correnti cited disagreement over direction, including non-steel businesses.
- **Nucor 1999 proxy** (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/73309/0000950168-99-000809.txt): Iverson retired as chairman on 31 December 1998, and the 1993–1998 stock index was 84 vs 294 for the S&P 500.
- **Nucor 2003 annual report (EX-13)** (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/73309/000119312504040741/dex13.htm): net sales and earnings 1998–2003, and the 2001–2003 acquisitions (Auburn, ITEC, Trico, Birmingham Steel $615M, Kingman).
- **AIST / Nucor FY2006 10-K** (https://www.aist.org/nucor-reports-record-results-for-2006): 2006 records.

**Not verified / handled**
- Nucor's U.S. ranking in 1995 is omitted.
- The 1996–2006 stock return is not verified and is not used.

## E04: Kimberly-Clark (dossier as of early 1991, with FY1990 results)

**Dates chosen**
- **Transition year: 1972** (see the reconciliation note above).
- **Dossier date: early 1991.**
  - This is 19 years after the transition.
  - Darwin Smith was still CEO, and Sanders had been named president and COO in December 1990.
  - The brief suggested about 1990. Early 1991 is used so that the FY1990 figures are on record.
- **Scott-merger aftermath, checked:**
  - a $1.4B charge and about 6,000 layoffs in 1995;
  - a further restructuring in November 1997 (about $810M pre-tax, about 7% of the workforce);
  - an SEC-prompted restatement of 1996–1998 merger charges;
  - yet annual share returns were positive in every year 1991–2000 except 1993 (−10%).
  - **Judgment:** integration pain, not a How-the-Mighty-Fall stage 3–5 decline. Returns over the 1990s were roughly market-level, so this is a caveat on "stayed great".

## Rejected candidates
- **Gillette.**
  - A dossier around 1990 (after the Sensor launch) fails the 10-year test. Gillette stalled from 1998: the stock fell from $64.25 in early 1999 to $24.50 in spring 2001, Duracell profits fell, CEO Hawley was removed at the board's request in October 2000, and earnings missed for 14 straight quarters by late 2002 (CNN Money, 19 October 2000, https://money.cnn.com/2000/10/19/news/gillette/index.htm; Fortune, 30 December 2002). That is a stage-4-like episode in years 8–10.
  - A 1988 dossier would fall in the middle of the Revlon and Coniston takeover fights, before the Sensor breakout. Gillette was not visibly great to the market then, and the clean window would be 10 years at most.
- **Philip Morris at a 1990s date.** Rejected because of Marlboro Friday (1993) and the litigation era. It was accepted at early 1978 instead (E05).
