# Sources: Track A, set 1 (A03–A12)

The answer keys rely on Collins's *Good to Great* (2001) as the authority, paraphrased throughout. I did not have a legitimate full text of the book to check against (the only full copy found online was an unauthorized PDF, which I neither used nor cite). Wherever the answer key states a Collins position, I confirmed it against the secondary summaries below when one was available, and otherwise flagged it as "from memory of the book" in that key's `notes`. All dossier facts come from independent sources: company histories, encyclopedias, Wikipedia, news archives and GAO material.

## A03 Abbott Laboratories (1974)
- **FundingUniverse, "Abbott Laboratories History".** https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/abbott-laboratories-history/
  - Founding facts: physician founder, Chicago (anonymized), public in 1929, first foreign affiliate in 1931.
  - Products: sodium pentothal; erythromycin from 1952.
  - M&R Dietetic (Similac), bought in 1964 as the first major acquisition.
  - Ledder's diversification and the consumer lines (Pream, Glad Hands, Faultless golf balls, Sucaryl).
  - The cyclamate ban (August 1970); cyclamate was a third of consumer revenue, about $50M, in 1969.
  - The 1971 IV recall: 3.4M bottles, 434 infections, 49 deaths, sales falling from $17.9M to $3M, no-contest plea.
  - The mid-1960s research drought.
  - Later history: TAP joint venture, Schoellhorn, the 1985 AIDS test, MediSense, the 1999 $100M fine, Knoll for $6.9B in 2000.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Abbott Laboratories"** (International Directory of Company Histories). https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/abbott-laboratories-0 and https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/abbott-laboratories
  - Diagnostics executives were hired from Texas Instruments.
  - Management believed new drugs faced long approval delays and consumer products carried high marketing costs.
  - Schoellhorn joined in 1973 from American Cyanamid after 27 years and found "disarray". His hospital products division grew 139% from 1974 to 1979.
  - He anticipated the trend toward cost-effective analysis and treatment. **Flag:** the source does not date when he first argued this. The dossier presents it as his current view in early 1974, which is plausible but not certain.
- **LinkedIn post, "The Leaders Who Built Modern Abbott"** (user-generated; weak). https://ru.linkedin.com/in/lebedevda
  - George Cain was CEO 1958–72 and was the son of an earlier president. Ledder was CEO 1972–79 and joined in 1939 as an hourly employee.
  - Cain's death from a heart attack after 14 years as CEO was corroborated by a Find a Grave snippet for Edward Ledder. https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/56645214/edward-j-ledder (search snippet only; the page returned 403).
- **Not verified, so left out of the dossier:** total 1973 sales and earnings; Ross infant-formula market share for 1974.

## A04 Upjohn (1974)
- **FundingUniverse, "The Upjohn Company History".** https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/the-upjohn-company-history/
  - Founding: the 1885 friable-pill patent; the 1886 company, Kalamazoo (anonymized).
  - First research scientist in 1913; first adrenocortical product in 1935; WWII antibiotics.
  - Sixth-largest antibiotic maker in 1958 ($22.6M); Orinase in 1957; the steroid fermentation and Syntex partnership.
  - Family leadership: Lawrence Upjohn, then Gilmore (stepson and son-in-law). Ray Parfet, who married into the family, was president from 1961.
  - The board was limited to family and employees until 1968.
  - The Motrin license from Boots in 1974.
  - Later history: Boots's 1977 U.S. entry and 1981 price war; Motrin at 40% of 1984 earnings; Xanax; Rogaine; Halcion.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "The Upjohn Company".** https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/upjohn-company — corroborates the above; Orinase was linked to heart disease in 1970s studies.
- **upjohn.net, "Asgrow".** https://upjohn.net/ag/asgrow/asgrow.htm — Asgrow acquired by stock swap in 1968 (1967 sales of $27.5M per the FundingUniverse Seminis history, https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/seminis-inc-history/); sold in 1994.
- **Panalba:** *Upjohn v. Finch*, 422 F.2d 944 (6th Cir. 1970), affirming the FDA's September 1969 order. https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/422/944/168068/ . A 1969 *Science* article (Mintz) reported the drug at about 12% of domestic gross income; I saw that figure only in a search snippet. https://samizdathealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Panalba-Science-1969-Mintz-875-81.pdf
- **FundingUniverse, "Pharmacia & Upjohn Inc. History"** (the 1995 merger and Upjohn's condition: patents expiring, Halcion sales down 45% in 1992, lowest P/E in 1992). http://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/pharmacia-upjohn-inc-history/
- **Wikipedia, "Upjohn"** (the 1995 Pharmacia merger; later Pfizer ownership). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upjohn
- **Not verified:** total 1973 sales and earnings; the date of the polymer-chemicals business; the Homemakers and Cobb acquisition dates. All left out. A detailed 1835–1985 history PDF on upjohn.net was too large to fetch, and I did not download it.

## A05 Circuit City / Wards (1982)
- **FundingUniverse, "Circuit City Stores, Inc. History".** https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/circuit-city-stores-inc-history/
  - Founding: 1949, Richmond (anonymized); IPO in 1961; leased departments from 1960; the 1965 and 1969–70 acquisitions.
  - Alan Wurtzel returned in 1966, became president in 1970 and CEO in 1972.
  - 1974: a $3M loss on $69M sales; exits from tires and leased departments; the $2M Loading Dock superstore, about half of net worth.
  - 1975 store sales and closures; the 1977 conversion to Circuit City stores; leaving mail-order in 1978; $120M sales in 1979.
  - 1981: the Lafayette purchase ($6.6M; $36.5M tax credits); four North Carolina superstores.
  - 1982: about 100 outlets, 80% consumer electronics, an 11% share in D.C.
  - Later history to 2004.
- **Wikipedia, "Circuit City"** (Loading Dock opened May 1975; 2003 and 2007 layoffs; Chapter 11 on 10 Nov 2008; liquidation announced 16 Jan 2009; stores closed in March 2009). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_City
- **Wikipedia, "Alan Wurtzel"** (joined 1966; CEO 1972–86). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Wurtzel
- **Collins-derived fact in the dossier:** the CEO's habit of asking questions rather than announcing answers comes from LitCharts' summary of Collins Ch. 4, not from an independent source. I kept it because it is a documented trait that matters strategically. **Flag.**
- **Discrepancy:** the first superstore date is 1974 (FundingUniverse) or 1975 (Wikipedia), and the first NC superstores 1978 (Wikipedia) or 1981 (FundingUniverse). The dossier uses relative wording and follows FundingUniverse for the NC stores ("last year").
- **Dating:** the 1982 store and share figures in FundingUniverse are given for 1982 without a month. The dossier treats them as current early in Year 0.

## A07 Fannie Mae (1984)
- **FundingUniverse, "Fannie Mae History".** https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/fannie-mae-history/ and **Company-Histories.com, "Fannie Mae Company History"** https://company-histories.com/Fannie-Mae-Company-History.html
  - The 1938 charter; the 1968 split from Ginnie Mae; private control from May 1970; a 15-member board with 5 presidential appointees; HUD's regulatory power.
  - Funding with debentures and short-term notes; rate losses from 1979.
  - Maxwell replaced Hunter in 1981; adjustable-rate mortgages; MBS and the swap program from 1981; conventional multifamily loans in 1983; profitable again by 1985.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Maxwell, David 1930–".** https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/maxwell-david-1930
  - His background: HUD general counsel 1970–73; eight years as CEO of Ticor Mortgage Insurance.
  - Losing about $1M a day in 1981; sold $10B of unprofitable mortgages.
  - Retired in 1991 earning about $4M a day; the $27M package controversy.
- **GAO, "The Federal National Mortgage Association in a Changing Economic Environment"** (RCED-85-102, April 1985), summary page. http://gao.justia.com/department-of-the-treasury/1985/4/the-federal-national-mortgage-association-in-a-changing-economic-environment-rced-85-102/
  - Negative interest margins 1981–84, partly offset by fees and positive-margin new purchases.
  - The Treasury backstop gives near-Treasury borrowing costs; business limited to residential.
  - Higher foreclosure exposure; GAO lacked legal access to all information.
  - **Flag:** this is a 1985 report. The facts used describe 1981–84 conditions that were knowable in early 1984, though GAO's own judgment was published later.
- **Wikipedia, "Fannie Mae"** (the 2004 OFHEO report and $6.3B restatement; the 7 Sept 2008 FHFA conservatorship; 79.9% warrants; NYSE delisting in 2010). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fannie_Mae
- **Collins's article** (Maxwell's "who first"; 14 of 26 executives left; $1M a day; 1984–1999 returns). Used only in the answer key.
- **Not verified:** annual net losses for 1981–83 and portfolio size. A Heritage Foundation report citing GAO cumulative losses could not be fetched (403), so no figure is used.

- **FundingUniverse, "Warner-Lambert Co. History"** https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/warner-lambert-co-history/ and **Encyclopedia.com, "Warner-Lambert Co."** https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/warner-lambert-co

  - The Hagan/Williams restructuring; later divestitures; IMED; the Goodes reorganization.

## A12 A&P (1973)
- **Wikipedia, "A&P".** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%26P

  - The Horseheads plant ($25M, 1.5M sq ft); exit from California.
  - The stock peaked at $70 in 1961 and stood at $17 in early 1973; the Gulf+Western $20 tender offer.
