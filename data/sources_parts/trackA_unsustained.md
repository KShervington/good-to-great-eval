# Sources: Track A unsustained comparisons (A23–A28)

General authority for the answer keys is Jim Collins, *Good to Great* (HarperBusiness, 2001). The book text was not accessible online, so the Collins claims were taken from secondary summaries (listed below) and from recollection. Each answer key says which claims were not checked against the text. No Collins quotation of 15 or more words is used anywhere.

Secondary sources used for the Collins framing:
- **Wikipedia, "Good to Great"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_to_Great). The list of the six unsustained comparisons.
- **SlideToDoc, "Good to Great by Jim Collins, Chapter 8"** (https://slidetodoc.com/good-to-great-by-jim-collins-chapter-8/). Harris:
  - the Dively/Tullis hedgehog (applying technology to printing and communications);
  - beat the market more than 5x from 1973 to 1978;
  - Boyd from 1978: headquarters move, sale of printing in 1983, Lanier, office-automation push;
  - 39% behind the market from 1978 to 1983, more than 70% behind by 1988.
- **Search-result summaries of Collins's chapter 6 material** (runn.io summary, Scribd and SlideServe chapter 6 decks). Burroughs:
  - MacDonald described as brilliant but abrasive;
  - the "MacDonald Vise";
  - about 6.6x the market from 1964 to 1977;
  - 93% behind the market from the end of the MacDonald era to 2000;
  - subordinates left indecisive after he retired.
- **LitCharts, "Lee Iacocca" character page** (https://www.litcharts.com/lit/good-to-great/characters/lee-iacocca). Iacocca as Collins's unsustained-comparison example: ego, distraction, no successor prepared.
- **Tim Ferriss Show transcript #361** (https://tim.blog/2019/02/20/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-jim-collins-361/), together with search summaries. Teledyne as Collins's "genius with a thousand helpers", and returns trailing the market by about 66% after Singleton stepped back.
- **ThePowerMoves summary, Goodreads reader notes, Lifeclub summary** (https://thepowermoves.com/good-to-great/, https://lifeclub.org/books/good-to-great-jim-collins-review-summary). Rubbermaid:
  - Gault as a Level 4 "sincere tyrant";
  - shallow team after he left;
  - from most-admired company to acquisition by a competitor.
- **Recollection of *Good to Great*, chapter 2, not verified online.** Iacocca: Chrysler stock about 2.9x the market in the first half of his tenure, then about 31% behind in the second half.

## A23: Burroughs Corporation (dossier as of early 1964)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: early 1964.** Ray MacDonald was given day-to-day control as executive vice-president in 1964 (Encyclopedia.com/IDCH). Collins's measured rise runs from 1964 to 1977 (secondary).
- **Transition year: 1964.**
- **Conflict:** Time (December 1966) says MacDonald became president in January 1966 and CEO on February 1, 1967. The IDCH history gives EVP in 1964 and CEO 1967–1977. The dossier uses "executive vice-president given charge of day-to-day operations", which is consistent with both.

**Sources**
- **Time, "Corporations: The New Burroughs"**, 24 February 1961 (https://time.com/archive/6624991/corporations-the-new-burroughs).
  - sales of $94M in 1948 and a record $389M in 1960;
  - earnings about 2% of sales in 1960, against 13% in 1948;
  - R&D blamed for the margin drop, with recovery expected but no timeline given;
  - adding machines about 6% of sales;
  - B5000 rental price and features;
  - ElectroData bought in 1956; bank automation (magnetic ink, check sorting); a military computer division supplying missile and space programs;
  - plants in Britain, France and Brazil; calculators made in Scotland;
  - Eppert's rise from shipping clerk and succession after Coleman's death;
  - the market dominated by IBM and Remington Rand.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Unisys Corporation"** (International Directory of Company Histories) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/unisys-corp).
  - Coleman's RCA partnership plan, never completed;
  - the SAGE contract lost to IBM in 1952;
  - Burroughs among the smallest of IBM's main competitors, with a narrow line dependent on accounting machines;
  - MacDonald EVP in 1964 and CEO 1967–1977, pursuing cost cutting;
  - later history: B8500 scrapped, B6500 reliability problems, Mirabito, Blumenthal, the 1981 lawsuits, the 1986 Sperry merger.
- **Time, "Corporations: Computing Success"**, 23 December 1966 (https://time.com/archive/6634883/corporations-computing-success). MacDonald's background as head of international sales, and the timeline above.
- **Chicago Booth, Distinguished Alumni: Ray W. MacDonald** (https://www.chicagobooth.edu/alumni/distinguished-alumni-award/honorees/ray-macdonald). Joined as a salesman in 1935 after his MBA; built the export department into a major division.

**Flags**
- The MacDonald personality description in the dossier (brilliant, forceful, dominates discussions) comes from Collins via secondary summaries, not from an independent source.
- 1961–1963 financials could not be found and are listed as missing.

## A24: Chrysler Corporation (dossier as of early autumn 1979)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: September/October 1979.** Iacocca joined as president on 2 November 1978 and succeeded Riccardo as chairman and CEO in September 1979 (Wikipedia, "Lee Iacocca").
- **Transition year: 1979.**
- **What the dossier leaves out:** the third-quarter 1979 loss of about $460M (reported later in the autumn) and the Loan Guarantee Act (introduced 1 November 1979, signed January 1980).

**Sources**
- **Time, "Business: Chrysler's Cry"**, 13 August 1979 (https://time.com/archive/6881649/business-chryslers-cry).
  - second-quarter 1979 loss of $207M;
  - 1978 sales of $13.6B, tenth-largest manufacturer, $625M of defense contracts;
  - inventory of about 80,000 units (about 95 days);
  - Riccardo's $1B tax-refund request and talk of a loan guarantee instead;
  - 130,000 employees in seven states and 400,000 at suppliers and dealers;
  - the request to the UAW for a wage freeze;
  - the warning that it might have to close by year-end;
  - front-wheel-drive models due in fall 1980.
- **Time, "Can General Patton help?"** (search snippet). 1978 loss of $204.6M, and analysts expecting a bigger 1979 loss.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Chrysler Corp."** (IDCH) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/chrysler-corp).
  - losses in 1974 and 1975 and a 1978 loss of $205M;
  - market share about 9% by 1979, down 12 points from 1952;
  - a stock of gas-guzzlers going into the 1973–74 embargo;
  - later history (1980–1998).
- **EBSCO Research Starters, "US Government Bails Out Chrysler Corporation"** (https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/us-government-bails-out-chrysler-corporation).
  - Wall Street had lost confidence in management;
  - Ford merger talk in early 1979;
  - recalls and quality problems;
  - the product-mix mismatch;
  - Iacocca focused on sales and publicity, with operations split from Riccardo;
  - the K-car capital program;
  - the Act's terms and outcomes.
- **Wikipedia, "Chrysler Europe"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Europe). Sale to PSA in 1978. Sources conflict on terms (nominal $1 plus debts vs. a 15% stake), so the dossier gives no terms.
- **Wikipedia, "Lee Iacocca"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Iacocca).
  - career at Ford, fired 13 July 1978 despite a $2B profit;
  - his Mustang role (blurred in the dossier);
  - later history: Statue of Liberty foundation, books, retirement at the end of 1992, Eaton, the 1995 Kerkorian bid.

**Flags**
- The 2.9x and 31% figures are recalled from Collins, not verified.
- The 2009 bankruptcy and the Fiat/Stellantis outcome are general knowledge and were not re-checked in this session.

## A25: Harris-Intertype / Harris Corporation (dossier as of early 1973)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: early 1973.** The secondary summary of Collins's chapter 8 gives 1973–1978 as the period of more than 5x outperformance, and IDCH says investors ignored the stock until about 1976.
- **Transition year: 1973.**
- **Alternative reading:** the same summary says build-up began in the early 1960s, so Collins may treat the transition as earlier. The 1973 date is a judgment call.

**Sources**
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Harris Corporation"** (IDCH) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/harris-corporation-1 and https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/harris-corp).
  - Radiation Inc.: founded 1950, about 3,000 employees and more than $50M in sales; bought in 1967 for $56M at about twice Harris's earnings multiple; space and missile programs;
  - combined sales of more than $250M after the merger;
  - Dively grew sales from about $10M to almost $200M;
  - RF Communications (about 1969); GE broadcast line ($5.5M) and UCC-Communications ($20M) in 1972;
  - "technology transfer" strategy, with promotion tied to it;
  - weak earnings growth from the late 1960s to the late 1970s;
  - printing machines still mechanical;
  - Boyd as EVP for electronics after 1967;
  - later history: 1977 sales, Farinon, the 1983 printing sale, Lanier ($276M), restructuring.
- **Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, "Harris Corp."** (https://case.edu/ech/articles/h/harris-corp). Founded 1895; 1957 Intertype merger; PRD Electronics in 1959; 1970 sales of $380M with electronics at 50%; renamed about 1974; headquarters moved to Melbourne in 1978.
- **Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, "Tullis, Richard Barclay"** (https://case.edu/ech/articles/t/tullis-richard-barclay). Joined Harris-Seybold in 1956 from Miller Printing (where he had been president); Harris president 1961, CEO 1968, chairman 1972; retired 1980; trained in mathematics and physics.
- **Wikipedia, "Harris Corporation"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Corporation). Acquisition list, the 1983 printing sale, Lanier, the 2019 L3 merger.

**Flags**
- Dively's exact tenure end is not confirmed; the dossier says only "about 25 years".
- 1972 sales and earnings were not found.

## A26: Hasbro Industries (dossier as of early 1980)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: early 1980.** Stephen Hassenfeld became chairman and CEO in 1980, and Collins's rise is the Stephen era (1980–1989).
- **Transition year: 1980.**
- **Conflict:** Merrill's death is dated March 1979 (Encyclopedia.com biography, FundingUniverse) and 1980 (Stephen's Wikipedia page). The dossier uses "last year".

**Sources**
- **FundingUniverse, "Hasbro, Inc. History"** (https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/hasbro-inc-history/) and **Encyclopedia.com, "Hasbro, Inc."** (IDCH) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/hasbro-inc).
  - founding by the brothers; textile remnants, then pencil boxes;
  - 1968 IPO with family majority;
  - G.I. Joe: launched 1964, nearly two-thirds of sales in its first two years, discontinued 1975;
  - Romper Room nursery schools (exited within five years), Galloping Gourmet cookware, Javelin Darts and Hypo-Squirt;
  - ACT criticism of the TV show;
  - $1M loss in 1969; $2.5M loss and heavy debt in 1977; dividend suspended in early 1979;
  - Peanuts licence in 1977;
  - Merrill's death at 61; Harold's refusal to recognize Stephen; the capital-allocation grievance;
  - Empire Pencil the largest pencil maker; spun off in 1980;
  - product line cut by a third in 1978–1981;
  - out of electronic games for lack of cash; Mr. Potato Head;
  - later history: Milton Bradley, Coleco, Tonka, Mattel regaining first place.
  - Sales of $104M "in the year Stephen took over" (IDCH), which the dossier gives as "roughly $100 million".
- **Wikipedia, "Stephen D. Hassenfeld"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_D._Hassenfeld). Born 1942; CEO 1980–1989; profitability up about 85% a year in 1980–1986; died 25 June 1989 at 47.

**Flags**
- Collins's specific Hasbro text could not be found. The collins block is built from his general unsustained pattern plus documented history; see the notes in A26.json.
- "Rival M" (Mattel) as the industry leader in 1980 is general knowledge, not sourced in this session.

## A27: Rubbermaid (dossier as of early 1980)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: early 1980.** Noble retired and Gault became chairman in 1980 (IDCH, Wikipedia "Rubbermaid"). Wikipedia's "Stanley Gault" page says 1979.
- **Transition year: 1980.**
- **Track B overlap:** this date is well before the Track B Rubbermaid case (about 1993).

**Sources**
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Rubbermaid Incorporated"** (IDCH) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/rubbermaid-incorporated-0 and https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/rubbermaid-inc).
  - goal (set 1960) of doubling earnings every six years, met under Noble;
  - Dupol bought in 1965;
  - party plan from 1969: about 10% of sales, unprofitable until 1976;
  - recreational-line failure;
  - industrial and commercial products at 25% of sales by 1974, and 240 items;
  - 1974 profit drop; price controls and petrochemical shortage;
  - La Grange plant;
  - FTC case (1973 charge, 1975 agreements dropped, 1976 order);
  - 1976 strike of 1,100 workers;
  - Gault's GE background, Wooster ties and college job at the plant;
  - his view of the company as stodgy and complacent; the quadruple-by-1990 goal;
  - later history: divestitures, acquisitions, 1994–1995 resin problems, the $158M charge, the 1999 Newell deal.
- **Wikipedia, "Rubbermaid"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubbermaid). Founded 1920 in Wooster as a balloon maker; dustpan origin; plastics from 1956; CEO succession; Newell 1999.
- **Wikipedia, "Stanley Gault"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Gault). 31 years at GE (consumer and then industrial products); Goodyear 1991; successors seen as weaker.
- **European Rubber Journal, "Former Goodyear CEO Gault dies"** (2016) (https://www.european-rubber-journal.com/2016/06/30/former-goodyear-ceo-gault-dies), via search snippet. Sales of $309M when Gault joined and about $1.5B a decade later.

**Flags**
- Pre-1980 earnings figures were not found.
- Fortune "most admired" in 1993 and 1994 is from the IDCH reference to a 1994 Fortune article plus general knowledge.

## A28: Teledyne (dossier as of late 1961)

**Dates chosen**
- **Dossier date: late 1961,** the end of the first full fiscal year. The company was founded in June 1960 and listed in May 1961, so there is no pre-Singleton era. The rise is the whole Singleton era (acquisitions from 1961, buybacks from 1972), and the earliest point before it is the startup stage.
- **Transition year: 1961.**
- **Flag:** Collins's exact measurement window for Teledyne was not verified.

**Sources**
- **Wikipedia, "Teledyne Technologies"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teledyne_Technologies).
  - Instrument Systems formed June 1960; financed by Arthur Rock;
  - plan to build a major microelectronics and controls firm mainly through acquisitions;
  - Amelco stake October 1960; about 400 employees at the end of 1960; IPO May 1961;
  - FY1961 sales of $4,491,000 and net income of $58,000;
  - later history: 34 acquisitions by FY1965, about 150 in total, the 1972–1976 buybacks, 1980s stagnation, Singleton's 1986, 1989 and 1991 steps back, the Allegheny merger, the 1999 split.
- **Wikipedia, "Henry Earl Singleton"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Earl_Singleton).
  - background: MIT ScD, Naval Ordnance Laboratory, OSS, ITT patent work, GE, Hughes, North American, Litton VP/GM;
  - stock returns during the buybacks; 1989 peak.
- **Encyclopedia.com, "Teledyne, Inc."** (IDCH) (https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/teledyne-inc).
  - each founder invested $225,000 from Litton options;
  - semiconductors as "basic building block";
  - later history: Vasco, 1969 sales of more than $1B, 1974 insurance write-off, low R&D, Argonaut and Unitrin spin-offs, 1992 fraud cases and about $30M in settlements.
- **"A Letter a Day: Letter #1, Henry Singleton (1961)"** (https://aletteraday.substack.com/p/letter-1-teledyne-1961), a blog summary of the first annual report.
  - program list: ASW navigation, satellite data processors, antennas, adaptive learning computer, the $5M FADAS contract, the Electron Devices Division;
  - five acquisitions in year one;
  - backlog of about $10M;
  - employment doubled;
  - the two stated priorities;
  - board including Rock and Claude Shannon (blurred in the dossier).
- **National Academies, Memorial Tributes vol. 9** (https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10094/chapter/47), via search snippet. Founded to apply digital technology to other technical fields.

**Flags**
- Sources conflict on FY1961 net income ($58K per Wikipedia vs. about $133K per the blog), so the dossier says only "small net profit".
- The backlog figure comes from a single blog source.
- "Widely regarded as exceptionally brilliant" rests on Collins's "genius" framing and general reputation.
