### Sources used for the dry-run dossiers
- **A01 Kimberly-Clark (1971):**
  - Collins, *Good to Great*, Ch. 2: Darwin Smith's background; the cancer diagnosis; the decision to sell the mills; stock trailing the market by about 36% over the prior 20 years; 4.1× the market for 1971–1991.
  - Encyclopedia.com, "Kimberly-Clark Corporation" (International Directory of Company Histories): founding in 1872; Kotex (1920); Kleenex (1924); Kimbies (1968) and their leakage problems; the six-plane air fleet and the 1969 proposal to make it a profit center; the sale or closure of six mills and sale of 300,000+ acres of California land within a year of Smith taking over. https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/kimberly-clark-corporation-1
  - Star Tribune, "Plant closure continues Kimberly-Clark's evolution" (2012): the sale of the namesake mill in Kimberly, Wisconsin. https://www.startribune.com/plant-closure-continues-kimberly-clark-s-evolution/139102089
  - Wikipedia, "Darwin Smith". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_Smith
- **A02 Scott Paper (1971):**
  - Encyclopedia.com, "Scott Paper Company": founding in 1879; Waldorf (1902); paper towel (1907); 38% share of sanitary paper in 1955 vs 11%; record sales in 1969 with 12% ROE; 1970 earnings down 18% with ROE of 9.5%; acquisitions 1965–68 costing about $200M and unprofitable; P&G's Charmin national rollout in 1971; the office of the chairman dissolved in 1971; the later history through 1995. https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/scott-paper-company
  - Collins, *Good to Great*, Ch. 2 (Scott as the comparison company; Dunlap as the counter-example to Level 5).
  - Wikipedia, "Scott Paper Company" (the 1995 merger with Kimberly-Clark). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Paper_Company

### Things I'm not sure about (to resolve before scaling up)
1. **Transition years.** The years above are from memory of Collins's appendix. Philip Morris (1964) and Fannie Mae (1984) are the ones I'm least sure of. I'll check each against the book's appendix, or a reliable summary of it, before writing those dossiers.
2. **Matching years for the 6 unsustained companies.** Collins gives them shift dates (for example Chrysler under Iacocca, Rubbermaid under Gault, Teledyne under Singleton), but I'm not sure of the exact years. I need them to date the dossiers.
3. **Collins's exact Hedgehog wording for some companies** (Abbott, Pitney Bowes, Kroger). I can reconstruct the substance but not the exact wording. The answer keys will paraphrase, and the judge will score substance, not wording.
4. **Pre-transition financial figures.** Free sources rarely give revenue or profit for 1964–1984. When a figure can't be confirmed, the dossier leaves it out and lists it under "What is not in this dossier." This also tests whether the skill flags missing numbers rather than inventing them.
5. **Recognizability.** Some cases carry famous details (for example Kimberly-Clark's CEO cancer story, Nucor's mini-mills, Circuit City's founder). Blurring those details removes signal the skill needs. I keep the strategic meaning and let the contamination probe tag the case, rather than gut the dossier.
