# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

## Verdict

**The franchise is good, and it is sliding toward decline. Treat this as a turnaround: shrink to the core before growing again.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be the lowest-cost, most automated bank for households and for middle-market and farm businesses in the home state. Lend only where the bank knows the borrower. Measure it by **profit per employee**.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cost. Overhead is reportedly the highest in the industry, so the low-cost engine doesn't exist yet.
- **Top three to stop doing:**
  1. Growing assets for the sake of size.
  2. Writing new developing-country, energy and shipping loans.
  3. Buying new businesses or entering new states to outgrow the problems.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** loans in four sectors are going bad at the same time, and nobody has shown that reserves are adequate.
- **Numbers that would change the answer:** net income and ROA, non-performing loans compared with reserves by sector, cost-to-revenue, and profit by business line.
- **Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. It rests on a dossier with no profit or credit data.

Greatness potential: 20/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller, leaner and calmer than today's. It does three things very well:

- It is the principal bank for home-state households, mid-sized firms and farms.
- It delivers that service through fewer, more productive branches backed by the automation it once pioneered.
- It lends only where it has deep local knowledge.

It stops counting assets, countries and branches. It counts profit per employee and credit losses through the cycle. It pays competitive rates on deposits because its cost base allows it, not because its deposits used to be cheap. Its leaders book bad news early. It grows into new states only after it leads its peers on efficiency, and never to escape a problem.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Western Commonwealth is the lowest-cost, most technologically advanced bank for the households, middle-market companies and farms of its home state. It lends only where it knows the borrower best, and it judges itself by profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Global corporate and sovereign lending; agricultural lending; statewide consumer banking; bank technology "firsts" | Home-state consumer and middle-market banking, delivered at scale with automation. Agricultural credit, but only in-state, where its knowledge is deepest. | Global and developing-country lending: it has no evident edge there, and those loans are going bad. Discount brokerage: newly bought, no evidence of an edge. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per asset dollar; per branch; per customer relationship; per employee | **Profit per employee** | Asset growth was the old denominator, and it produced today's problems. Profit per branch would let headcount hide inside branches. |
| **Passion** | Founder's immigrant, populist heritage of banking for ordinary people; pride in innovation; the prestige of global banking | Serving ordinary people and local businesses well; being first with useful technology `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from founding story and innovation record]` | Global prestige: likely real, but it is passion for status, not for the subject. |

**Denominator:** profit per employee. The alternative is profit per customer relationship. Profit per employee was chosen because overhead is the bank's most visible structural weakness.

**Confirmation tests:**
- Profit by business line should show home-state retail and middle-market lending earning above the bank's average return, and international, energy and shipping lending below it once losses are counted.
- Branch-level profit data should show a large tail of branches that destroy value.
- The test fails, and the bank must loop back, if international lending turns out to be the profit engine on a risk-adjusted basis.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 (1) Lower cost per transaction
     (fewer, better branches + automation)
            │
            ▼
 (2) Competitive deposit & loan pricing
     for home-state households & firms
            │
            ▼
 (3) More primary relationships per employee
            │
            ▼
 (4) Deeper borrower knowledge
     → better credit, lower losses
            │
            ▼
 (5) Higher PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE
     + rebuilt capital
            │
            ▼
 (6) Reinvest in automation & cost cuts ──► back to (1)
```

**The loop closes.** Each step causes the next, and the denominator sits at step 5.

**Current push (the next one or two turns):**
1. **Step 4, credit truth.** Recognize and reserve for the bad loans now, and stop making new ones outside the core.
2. **Step 1, cost.** Run a branch-by-branch profit review, then consolidate branches and automate. Until step 1 turns, the bank cannot pay market rates on deposits and still earn money.

**Doom-loop check (currently positive):**
- Growth for its own sake: the bank was the only one of the top 20 to average 15% a year, and assets roughly doubled in six years.
- A new business was bought while credit is deteriorating.
- Management's option list includes "expand into new businesses and states to grow out of the problems."
- These are textbook doom-loop signals: big moves to manufacture momentum and reactions without understanding. The flywheel above only turns once those moves stop.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring success by size.** That means assets, branch count, number of countries and number of business lines.
2. **Stop new lending to developing-country governments and banks.** Manage down the existing exposure.
3. **Stop new energy and shipping loans** outside deep, existing relationships that pass independent review.
4. **Stop out-of-state agricultural lending, and pause farm-lending growth** until the farm book has been reviewed.
5. **Stop buying businesses or entering new states to grow out of the problems.** Put no further capital into the brokerage until it is reviewed.
6. **Stop writing long fixed-rate mortgages funded by short-term deposits.** Stop holding the old low-rate book passively. Price it, then sell, hedge or run it off deliberately.
7. **Stop keeping branches open out of habit.** Every branch must earn its place on profit per employee.
8. **Stop paying lenders for volume.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every loan above a set threshold passes independent credit review.
- Losses are reserved for when they are known, not when they become convenient.
- The CEO reviews profit per employee and branch-level P&L every month.
- Assets and liabilities are funded on matching terms.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Freedom:** regional and branch leaders set local service and pricing.
- **Limits:** they work within cost-per-transaction and credit-limit boundaries.
- **Responsibility:** they own their unit's P&L.

**Bureaucracy check:** layers of loan committees probably exist because volume-paid lenders can't be trusted. `[ASSUMPTION]` Fix the incentives and the people, then remove the layers.
