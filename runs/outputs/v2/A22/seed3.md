# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

**Good, not yet great, and drifting toward decline. Run this as a turnaround.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the best-run, lowest-cost consumer and middle-market bank in the world, inside its home state, measured by profit per employee.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cost savings turning into capital. Bad loans in energy, shipping, farming and developing countries could absorb every dollar the cost program frees up.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth no longer tries to be the biggest bank in the world. It tries to be the best-run bank in its home state. It serves ordinary households and middle-market companies, including in-state farms, through a branch and electronic network that is smaller, cheaper and more automated than today's. It carries on its founder's tradition of banking for ordinary people `[ASSUMPTION]` and its own record of firsts, the credit card and computerized records, with the aim of lowering cost per customer. It prices every loan for risk. It has worked through and reserved for its legacy energy, shipping, farm and developing-country losses instead of trying to outgrow them. It judges itself by profit per employee against the top 20 U.S. banks. Its low-cost deposit advantage is gone, so its edge comes from lower operating cost, better credit, and customers who stay.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Be the best-run, lowest-cost consumer and middle-market bank in the world, inside its home state, and measure it by profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Biggest bank in the world; global corporate and sovereign lending; agricultural lending; home-state consumer banking; applying technology to retail banking; discount brokerage | **Home-state consumer and middle-market banking, delivered through technology** | *Size* is an outcome, not a skill. *International, energy and shipping lending*: the emerging bad loans suggest the bank is not pricing these risks better than anyone. *Brokerage*: it was bought, not built. *Agriculture worldwide*: it is the largest, but being largest and being best are different things, and those loans are going bad. In-state farm lending stays only as part of middle-market lending, with concentration limits. |
| **Economic engine** | Assets; profit per branch; profit per customer; profit per employee | **Profit per employee** | Deregulation is removing the deposit-cost advantage, so the winner will be whoever has the lowest operating cost. The bank reportedly has the industry's highest overhead, which makes cost the main lever. Profit per customer is a strong secondary candidate. |
| **Passion** | Being the biggest; global prestige; serving ordinary people; being first with new banking technology | **Serving ordinary people and businesses at home, and pioneering how they bank** | Global prestige may be the passion that exists today `[ASSUMPTION]`, but it does not connect to the engine. |

**Denominator:** profit per employee, with a peer-relative ROA as a cross-check.

**Intersection tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Mostly. Retail produced about half of profits, and the credit card and computerization firsts were consumer-banking wins. It cannot be fully confirmed without profit by business line.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: international and sovereign lending, out-of-state expansion, the brokerage build-out, and asset-size targets.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It means giving up the "one of the largest banks in the world" identity.

**What would confirm it:**
1. Profit-by-line data shows home-state retail and middle-market earning the highest risk-adjusted returns.
2. Profit per employee rises for four straight quarters while assets stay flat or shrink.
3. Customer retention holds as deposits are repriced to market rates.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Disciplined home-state focus (stop-doing list enforced)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   2. Lower cost per customer (branch consolidation, automation, fewer layers)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   3. Higher PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE  ◄── denominator
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   4. Capital to absorb legacy losses and to price competitively   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   5. Better value, service and credit decisions for in-state households & middle market
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   6. Deeper relationships and share in the home state (sticky deposits, more products per customer)
 │        │
 └────────┘  (scale on a fixed tech platform lowers cost per customer again)
```

**The loop closes.** More home-state relationships spread fixed technology and branch costs across more customers, which pushes cost per customer down again.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Link 4:** run a full credit review, set reserves at realistic levels, and build a capital plan. Until the size of the loss is known, cost savings cannot be counted as momentum.
2. **Link 2:** branch-by-branch contribution analysis, followed by consolidation and automation.

**Doom-loop check: active warning signs.**
- The strategy is to "grow out of" problems: more assets, branches, countries and lines of business.
- A new business (the discount brokerage) was bought during an emerging credit crisis.
- Expansion into new states and businesses is still on the options list.
- Asset growth of 15% a year has been treated as success without matching profit evidence.

All four fit the pattern of trying to jump-start momentum instead of turning a coherent loop.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring and rewarding asset size.** Remove it from goals, compensation and public messaging.
2. **Stop new lending to developing countries.** Allow only restructuring of existing exposure.
3. **Stop new energy and shipping lending** outside established home-state relationships.
4. **Stop acquisitions and new-state expansion** as a way to grow out of problems.
5. **Stop funding the discount brokerage's expansion with bank capital.** Ring-fence it and test it against the Hedgehog Concept within 12 months. The default view is that it falls outside.
6. **Stop writing long-term fixed-rate mortgages held on balance sheet** without matched funding or hedging.
7. **Stop keeping branches whose cost exceeds their contribution,** and stop treating branch count as a measure of strength.
8. **Stop agricultural concentration above a board-set limit,** including in-state.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A monthly profit-per-employee report by unit.
- Independent credit approval on every loan above a threshold.
- Risk-based pricing on all loans.
- An operating-cost budget that falls in real terms each year `[ASSUMPTION: target to be set once cost/revenue is known]`.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- *The framework:* home-state households and middle-market companies, risk-priced credit, and cost per customer that falls every year.
- *Freedom inside it:* branch and regional managers control service model, staffing mix and local marketing, and are judged on unit profit per employee.

**Bureaucracy check:** the industry-high overhead probably includes control layers built to manage rapid, scattered growth `[ASSUMPTION]`. Remove them as the right people and a narrower focus make them unnecessary.
