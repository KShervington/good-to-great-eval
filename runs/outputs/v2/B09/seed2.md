# Pacifica Bancorp: Good to Great

**Verdict:** Pacifica is good and the world's largest bank, but not yet great: its scale has not been shown to produce superior returns. It is sliding toward peril. Run this as a pre-emptive turnaround.

- **Hedgehog:** Be the best-run, lowest-cost bank for its home state's households and smaller businesses, measured by risk-adjusted profit per employee.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cost to serve. The bank carries about 1,100 branches and lifetime staffing that were built for capped deposit rates, and it is behind on ATMs.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) measuring success by asset growth; (2) decentralized growth in developing-country, farm and construction lending; (3) funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short deposits.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the cheap-deposit subsidy is being repealed while the balance sheet holds rate and credit risk that nobody has yet measured.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the maturity gap, reserves and losses by loan category, country exposure against equity, and expense ratios against peers.
- **Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on severity. The verdict rests on the bank's structure, not on loss data.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacifica goes back to its founding idea with modern economics. It is the bank that the home state's households, savers, farmers and small and mid-sized businesses choose first. It runs that franchise at the lowest cost per customer of any large bank. It uses its old strength in computerized records to automate service and credit decisions, so it does not need a branch on every corner. It no longer confuses size with success. Its score is risk-adjusted profit per employee, and it keeps a balance sheet that survives rate shocks and credit cycles. International activity shrinks to what serves home-state customers and earns its keep. The culture keeps its loyalty to its people and its "little guy" purpose. It drops the creed that any decision beats no decision when the decision is about lending.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Be the best-run, lowest-cost bank in the world for the households and smaller businesses of our home state, and judge every move by risk-adjusted profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Global wholesale/sovereign lending; largest bank anywhere; consumer-banking innovation; home-state retail and small-business banking | Home-state retail and small-business banking at scale, backed by early computerization and consumer-product pioneering | **Developing-country lending:** no evidence of an edge over other global banks, and it is approved through decentralized authority. **Venture capital:** no edge. **Being biggest:** that is a rank, not a capability. |
| **Economic engine denominator** | Profit per dollar of assets; profit per branch; profit per household; **risk-adjusted profit per employee** | **Risk-adjusted profit per employee.** It directly confronts the fixed-cost base that deregulation exposes, and "risk-adjusted" stops the bank from buying profit with credit risk. Profit per household is a secondary metric. | Asset growth, which is the current implicit denominator. It rewards exactly the lending that created today's exposures. |
| **Passion** | Global prestige; deal-making; serving "the little guy" | The founding mission of serving ordinary savers and small businesses that big banks ignore. Lifetime-employment staff are likely to identify with it. `[ASSUMPTION]`: no staff evidence in the dossier. | Prestige as "world's largest." That is pride, not passion for the work. |

**Confirmation tests** (the evidence that would turn this hypothesis into a commitment):
1. Segment profitability shows home-state retail and small-business banking earning more than its cost of capital *at projected market deposit rates*, and international and developing-country lending earning less on a risk-adjusted basis.
2. Branch-level P&Ls show that consolidation plus ATMs can cut cost per customer meaningfully without losing primary relationships.
3. Home-state customers keep core balances when money-market funds offer higher rates, which proves the relationship is worth something beyond the rate cap.

**Uncomfortable?** Yes. It implies shrinking international reach, giving up the title of world's largest, and changing the economics of lifetime employment. It explains the past wins: the bank became great by serving the home state's little guy. It does not explain the recent growth, which is the point.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  ┌─► 1. Serve home-state households & small businesses
  │        better and more conveniently than anyone
  │                     │
  │                     ▼
  │     2. More primary relationships per customer
  │        (deposits + loans + fees)
  │                     │
  │                     ▼
  │     3. Sticky core deposits + low-loss local credit
  │        (we know the borrower)
  │                     │
  │                     ▼
  │     4. Rising RISK-ADJUSTED PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE
  │                     │
  │                     ▼
  │     5. Reinvest in automation (ATMs, processing,
  │        credit scoring); consolidate branches
  │        → lower cost to serve          ◄── WEAKEST LINK
  │                     │
  │                     ▼
  └──── 6. Better prices & convenience than rivals
```

**The loop closes.** Lower cost to serve funds better pricing and convenience, which wins more relationships.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Defuse the balance sheet first.** This is survival, not flywheel. Quantify and cap the rate gap and the concentrated credit before the transition ends.
2. **Turn link 5.** Build branch P&Ls at market deposit rates, run an ATM catch-up program, and freeze hiring through attrition. This lowers cost per customer before deposit ceilings finish phasing out over the six years.

**Doom-loop check: several signs are present.**
- Asset growth has been the goal.
- New lending categories (developing countries, farms, construction, fixed-rate mortgages) all surged at once.
- Overseas expansion went from fewer than 20 branches to more than 90.
- A venture-capital side business sits outside the core.
- The creed is "action over indecision."
- The board's "change agent who will enter new businesses" option risks a new direction meant to replace lost deposit margin. That is grasping, not flywheel.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring and rewarding asset growth.** Remove it from compensation, board reporting and public messaging. Report risk-adjusted profit per employee and ROA instead.
2. **Stop net new growth in developing-country, agricultural and construction lending** until the exposure review is complete. After that, lend only within central country and sector limits set against equity.
3. **Stop originating long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded by short deposits.** Shift to adjustable-rate products, or sell or match-fund what you originate.
4. **Stop decentralized approval of large credits.** "Entrepreneurial growth abroad" becomes freedom within limits.
5. **Stop the venture-capital direct-investment business.**
6. **Stop overseas expansion for its own sake.** Keep international services only where they serve home-state customers (trade finance, cash management) and earn a risk-adjusted return.
7. **Stop keeping branches that lose money at market deposit rates.** Consolidate where ATMs and nearby branches can serve the customers.
8. **Stop applying "action over indecision" to credit.** Speed applies to execution. In lending, a decision not to make a loan is often the right one.
9. **Stop looking for a new business to replace the lost deposit subsidy.** Refuse acquisitions or launches outside the hedgehog, however modern they look.

**Non-negotiable disciplines**
- Every credit above threshold goes to the central credit committee.
- The rate-gap report goes to the board monthly.
- Every branch has a P&L calculated at market deposit rates.
- Profit per employee and nonperforming loans are reported quarterly by segment.

**Framework boundaries (freedom within a framework)**
- **The framework:** the hedgehog, central credit and country limits, gap limits, and the scorecard.
- **The freedom:** branch and regional managers set local service, small-business lending within limits, and staffing within budget. The bureaucracy check suggests the bank has the right people. What it lacks is the framework, not more rules.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** Give ordinary people and small businesses a sound, fair bank of their own.

**Core values** (preserve these, and change practices freely):
1. Serve the little guy.
2. Stewardship of depositors' money: prudence before growth.
3. Loyalty and respect toward our people. The value is permanent. The practice of lifetime employment is not, and can change while the value holds.
4. Practical innovation that makes banking cheaper and easier for customers.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** Become the most profitable large bank in the country per employee while serving more of the home state's households and small businesses than any rival.

**Clock-building mechanisms**
- A central credit committee with country and sector limits set against equity.
- An asset-liability committee with board-set gap limits.
- Executive pay tied to risk-adjusted return, not assets.
- A standing succession process, so the next transition is not improvised.
- The quarterly scorecard reported to the board.
