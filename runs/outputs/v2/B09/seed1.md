# Good to Great: Pacifica Bancorp

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Pacifica should be the best bank in the world at serving the households and small businesses of its home state. It should run that franchise at low cost, lead with technology, and measure itself by profit per customer relationship instead of by size.

**Weakest flywheel link:** cheap, sticky deposits. Regulation has held them in place, not proven loyalty, and the rate ceilings are now being phased out.

**Top three things to stop:**
1. Measuring and rewarding asset growth.
2. Growing developing-country, farm and construction loans under decentralized approval.
3. Booking long fixed-rate mortgages that are funded by short-term deposits.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the law is removing the cheap-deposit advantage that paid for the branches, the fixed-rate mortgages and the loan growth. Record profit may be the peak of the old model.

**Numbers that would change this:** reserves and loss experience by loan category, exposure by country, the mortgage–deposit maturity gap, and expense ratio against peers.

**Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. The verdict rests on structural facts in the dossier, not on the bank's own credit figures.

Greatness potential: 30/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacifica is no longer "the world's largest bank." It is the bank that its home state's households and small businesses would hardest replace. Each customer holds several products. ATMs and automated systems carry the routine work at the lowest cost per transaction in the state. The branch network is sized to what customers use. Customers keep their deposits at Pacifica because of convenience and relationship value, since the regulated rate cap no longer holds them. Credit is centrally disciplined: every loan is priced for risk and kept within concentration limits. Interest-rate exposure is matched or hedged. International activity exists to serve home-state customers' trade needs. Success is judged by risk-adjusted profit per relationship and by return on assets, never by asset rank. This version goes back to the founder's purpose of serving "the little guy," now run as a disciplined, modern business.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the best bank in the world at serving the households and small businesses of our home state, as a low-cost, technology-led franchise, measured by profit per customer relationship.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Global lending; international branch network; consumer product innovation; home-state retail franchise; banking operations and computerization | Home-state retail and small-business franchise, with consumer product and operations innovation as the means | **International lending:** the evidence shows growth in volume, not a distinctive edge or superior returns `[ASSUMPTION: no evidence of superior international returns]`. **Venture capital:** no evidence of an edge. **Holding long fixed-rate mortgages:** this is an interest-rate bet, not a skill. |
| **Economic engine** | Assets; net income; ROA; profit per employee; profit per branch; profit per customer relationship | **Profit per customer relationship**, with profit per employee as a check | **Assets:** rewards size and risk, not returns. **Profit per branch:** too tied to the old cost structure. |
| **Passion** | Being the biggest; global reach; the founding mission of serving savers, farmers and small businesses that big banks ignored | The founding "little guy" mission `[ASSUMPTION: still alive in the culture; the dossier documents it only as history]` | **"Biggest bank":** this is pride in size, not passion for a purpose. |

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. The dominant home-state branch network, the deposit base and the consumer product firsts all sit inside it.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It rules out venture capital, international volume lending, the asset-rank goal and an unmatched fixed-rate mortgage book.
- **Uncomfortable?** Very. It shrinks the part of the bank that made it "global," and it drops the identity of being the world's largest.

**Confirmation evidence:** home-state retail should show the highest risk-adjusted return per unit of capital of any segment once credit losses and true funding costs are allocated. If international or another segment wins on that basis, revise the concept.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                              ▼
 (1) Convenient, innovative service for home-state      (2) More products per household /
     households & small businesses                           small business; deeper relationships
     (branches + ATMs + consumer products)                         │
   ▲                                                              ▼
   │                                                 (3) Sticky core deposits and fee income
 (5) Reinvest in technology that cuts                      that cost less than market-rate funding
     cost per transaction; right-size branches             │   ← WEAKEST LINK
   ▲                                                              ▼
   └──────────── (4) Rising PROFIT PER CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP ◄──────┘
                     (risk-priced lending, controlled cost)
```

**Weakest link (3):** until now, deposits stayed cheap because of the rate ceiling. Over the six-year phase-out, stickiness has to come from relationship breadth and convenience. That has not been proven. The cost side of link 5 is also strained by the high fixed costs of the branch network and the lifetime-employment tradition.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Survival first:** measure and cap the credit and interest-rate exposures before they consume the profit that turns the wheel.
2. **Then push links 5→1:** close the ATM gap and move routine transactions out of branches, while building pricing per relationship so that customers with deep relationships see value that a money-market fund can't match.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are already present.
- Growth itself is the success measure.
- The bank expanded into many loan categories at once.
- Lending authority abroad is decentralized "to encourage entrepreneurial growth."
- It runs a high-risk venture-capital side business.
- The CEO's creed favors action over deliberation.

The board's own option of a "change agent who will … enter new businesses" would add a new direction on top of all this.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop using asset growth** as the success measure, the compensation driver or a point of pride.
2. **Stop new growth in developing-country lending** until exposure by country is measured and capped. Shrink the book as loans mature.
3. **Stop letting farm and construction lending grow** beyond concentration limits set centrally.
4. **Stop booking long-term fixed-rate mortgages** for the balance sheet without matched funding or hedging. Where the market allows, originate them and sell them, or offer adjustable rates `[ASSUMPTION: secondary-market and adjustable-rate options are available]`.
5. **Stop approving loans under decentralized authority** without a central credit veto and exceptions reporting.
6. **Exit the venture-capital direct investments.**
7. **Stop adding branches,** and stop subsidizing branches that the ATM network can replace.
8. **Stop treating "mistakes of decision beat mistakes of indecision" as a credit philosophy.** It is a fine creed for service. It is a dangerous one for lending.

**Truth climate:** the risk is high. An "action" creed, compensation and pride tied to growth, and decentralized authority together mean bad credit news may not travel upward. Red-flag mechanisms needed:
- A central loan-review function that reports directly to the board.
- A monthly exceptions and concentration report.
- A quarterly repricing-gap report.
- Blameless post-mortems on every large loss.

**Charisma check:** there is no direct evidence. The "action" creed set the tone, though, and the incoming CEO should start by asking questions instead of giving answers.

**Faith statement:** *The largest branch network and the deepest customer relationships in a big, fast-growing state are an asset no rival can copy quickly; run for returns rather than size, that franchise will prevail through deregulation.*
