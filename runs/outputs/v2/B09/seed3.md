# Pacifica Bancorp: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept:** *Be the most efficient, most trusted bank for households and small businesses in our home state, and judge every decision by profit per employee.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** cost-to-serve. Branch fixed costs are high and in-state rivals lead on ATMs, just as capped-rate deposits stop subsidizing those costs.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop measuring and rewarding asset growth.
2. Stop expanding developing-country, farm and construction lending under decentralized approval.
3. Stop funding new long-term fixed-rate mortgages with short-term deposits.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the law is phasing out Pacifica's core advantage, cheap capped-rate deposits, while a large fixed-rate mortgage book is funded short.

**Missing numbers that could flip this to a turnaround:** maturity gap, country exposure against capital, reserves against problem loans by category, and expense ratio against peers.

**Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. It rests on dossier facts only, with no risk data.

Greatness potential: 25/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacifica is not the world's largest bank. It is the best bank in its home state for ordinary households and small businesses, the "little guy" it was founded to serve. It earns more profit per employee than any large peer, funds itself from loyal customer relationships rather than from a regulatory price cap, and lends mainly to customers it knows. Its credit and rate risk are set centrally and are visible to the board. It uses technology to make each transaction cheaper, keeps international operations only where they serve home-state customers, and treats size as a by-product, never the goal. It stays great after any one CEO because its scorecard, credit discipline and succession system don't depend on one person.

**Sentence:** *Be the most efficient, most trusted bank for households and small businesses in our home state, measured by profit per employee.*

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
- Does it explain past wins? Yes. The rise to the top was built loan by loan and branch by branch in-state.
- Does it rule things out? Yes: the VC partnership, the growth in developing-country loans, and branch expansion overseas.
- Is it uncomfortable? Yes. It challenges lifetime employment, the "world's largest" identity, and a ten-year record measured in assets.

**What would confirm it:** in-state segments show materially higher risk-adjusted returns than international and new loan categories, and peer expense data shows room to lead on profit per employee.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Serve in-state households & small businesses better
   │        (heritage, consumer products, convenience)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   2. More primary relationships → stable core deposits
   │        (loyalty instead of a rate cap)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   3. More products per relationship; lend mainly
   │        to known customers (fees + low credit losses)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   4. PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE rises  ◄── denominator
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   5. Reinvest in cost-to-serve: ATMs, automation,
   │        branch rationalization
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   └── 6. Lower cost + better service → competitive deposit
          pricing without margin collapse ──► back to 1
```

**Weakest link:** step 5, cost-to-serve. Pacifica trails in-state rivals on ATMs and carries a high-fixed-cost branch network.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Map profit by branch and by customer segment.
2. Start a funded ATM and automation program justified on cost per transaction.

**Doom-loop check (flagged):**
- Growth in assets has been the goal.
- Several new lending lines were added in a few years: fixed-rate mortgages, farm, construction and developing-country loans.
- A VC venture was added.
- The board is weighing a "change agent who enters new businesses."

These are the classic signs of trying to jump-start momentum with programs. Leadership turnover makes the risk worse, because a new CEO may bring a new direction.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop using asset growth** (and "world's largest") as a goal, a headline or a bonus metric.
2. **Stop adding new long-term fixed-rate mortgages to the portfolio** funded by short-term deposits. Originate adjustable-rate loans, or sell or hedge the fixed-rate ones.
3. **Stop growing the developing-country loan book.** Set country exposure limits against capital, and make no new sovereign growth until the exposure is known.
4. **Stop decentralized approval of large credits.** Above a set threshold, every loan, domestic or foreign, goes through central credit review.
5. **Exit venture-capital direct investing**, winding down over time.
6. **Stop expanding overseas branches.** Keep only the international operations that serve home-state customers' trade and finance `[ASSUMPTION: some share of international business does this]`.
7. **Stop treating every branch as permanent.** Close or merge branches that fail the profit-per-employee test once the ATM rollout is in place.
8. **Stop the concentration growth in farm and construction lending.** Cap each category as a share of capital.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to help ordinary households and small enterprises build their economic lives.

**Core values:**
1. Serve the little guy.
2. Lend only what we understand.
3. Tell the truth early, especially bad news.
4. Thrift: every dollar of cost must earn its keep.
5. Loyalty to our people, shown through redeployment rather than guarantees.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** be the primary bank for a majority of the home state's households and small businesses, with the highest profit per employee among the 20 largest U.S. banks `[ASSUMPTION: targets set without current share or headcount data]`.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- **Compensation:** tied to risk-adjusted profit and profit per employee, not assets.
- **Credit:** a central credit committee with board-set concentration limits.
- **Rate risk:** an asset-liability committee that reports the repricing gap to the board.
- **Loan review:** independent review that reports to the audit committee.
- **Succession:** a standing pipeline with two or three ready internal candidates at all times.
