## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Lindqvist is a **regional dominator, not a national generalist**. It operates only in metro areas where it is #1 or #2. Nearly every store there is a large combination store selling food, drugs and specialty departments under one roof, and each store is tuned to its neighborhood using scanner data and the company's long habit of treating purchases as shoppers' "votes." The plants exist to serve the store brand: they are sized to the stores' needs, run to a quality standard that leaves no room for another meat-hearing finding, and never act as a separate empire. Lindqvist does not try to beat Rival A on price nationally. It wins on local density, which gives it lower cost per household, more advertising reach per dollar, and more store-brand volume per plant. It uses that density to stay price-competitive where it matters. It has left the markets it could not lead, and it doesn't regret it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Expanding manufacturing capacity** beyond what the store brand needs. No plant spending without a store-volume case.
2. **Opening new family centers.** Freeze now and review the existing ones on per-household economics.
3. **Holding markets with no credible path to #1 or #2** within a set window `[ASSUMPTION: about 3–5 years]`.
4. **Copying Rival A's chain-wide discount program.** Match price perception where we lead; do not chase volume where we trail.
5. **Spending on small conventional stores** beyond basic maintenance, unless they are scheduled for conversion.
6. **Adding new thrusts** (formats, divisions, categories) before the combination-store model is proven.
7. **Exiting markets one at a time with no rule.** Every exit or stay should follow the market scorecard.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** a quarterly market scorecard; every new store or conversion clears a profit-per-household hurdle; store-brand quality audits; the shopper's vote (scanner and research data) settles merchandising arguments.

**Framework boundaries (freedom within a framework):** Corporate decides *which markets* and *what format*. Market managers have autonomy over department mix, local pricing within a band, and staffing.

**Bureaucracy check:** I can't assess this from the dossier. One thing to watch: any rule that forces stores to stock store-brand products because the plants need the volume. That would be a rule protecting the plants, not the shopper.
