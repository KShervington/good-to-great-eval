# Good to Great: Homestead Mortgage Association

## Verdict

**Homestead is good, and exceptional at credit, but it is not great. Its growth is leverage on a privilege it does not control.**

- **Hedgehog:** Homestead can be the best in the world at pricing and guaranteeing the credit risk of American home mortgages for working families. It should be judged on fair-value profit per dollar of capital, not on the size of its balance sheet.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from capital to earned trust. Capital sits only $0.9B above the legal minimum on $887B of assets, so market trust is borrowed from Washington.
- **Stop:** (1) paying executives on the EPS-doubling target; (2) growing the portfolio faster than the mortgage market; (3) leading with "operating" earnings.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** thin capital plus a −14-month duration gap brought the regulator's first-ever formal action.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the fair-value balance sheet and hedges, amortization policies, guarantee-versus-portfolio profitability, and the credit mix of recent purchases.
- **Confidence:** moderate on the diagnosis, low on the Hedgehog. The verdict rests on the dossier alone.

Greatness potential: 22/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Homestead is the mortgage-credit guarantor that markets would trust on the strength of its own balance sheet. It earns its standing through credit discipline, not through an implied federal promise. It grows at the pace of the conforming mortgage market or slower. It holds capital well above the legal floor, keeps interest-rate risk inside a tight band every month, and reports its results so plainly that a "core" earnings figure needs no explaining. It meets its affordable-housing goals through priced, data-driven underwriting, so low- and moderate-income lending becomes a proof of skill rather than a source of hidden credit risk. Its executives are paid on multi-year, risk-adjusted outcomes. Its regulator is treated as a red-flag mechanism, not an adversary. If it lost its special status tomorrow, it would be smaller but still standing.

1. Segment data show the guarantee business earns a competitive fair-value return when capital is charged at a market rate, without the funding subsidy. *(Not in dossier.)*

It rules out leverage-driven growth, EPS targets and unpriced goal-chasing. It is uncomfortable: it probably ends the 16-year streak of double-digit growth.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 │        (loan-level data, risk-based guarantee fees)
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 │   B. Lowest credit losses across the full income range
 │        (0.5 bp today; holds as the affordable mix rises)
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 │   C. Rising fair-value profit per $ of capital
 │        → capital buffer well above legal minimum
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 │   D. EARNED trust: investors, regulator, Congress
 │        (less reliance on implied federal backing)   ◄── weakest link
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 │   E. Deep, liquid MBS market → lower, steadier
 │        mortgage rates; more low/mod-income owners
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 └── F. More lenders deliver more loans → more data
```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **C → D:** build the capital buffer. Slow portfolio growth to no more than market growth, retain earnings, and set a cushion target several times today's $0.9B `[ASSUMPTION: ≥ $3–4B within 12 months]`.
2. **The truth system:** report GAAP first, keep the duration gap within a hard band, and fully clear the supervisory action. Until these are done, D cannot turn, because trust can't be earned on numbers people discount.

**Doom-loop check.** The loop that runs today is different:

> implied backing → cheap debt → buy mortgages → spread income → operating-EPS growth → stock awards tied to EPS → bigger targets → more portfolio.

This is not the classic doom loop of lurching from one direction to the next. Strategy has been very consistent. The danger is quieter: **consistent pursuit of the wrong denominator.** Signs present now:
- Growth targets set above market growth (about 15% a year versus a slower market).
- Reaction under pressure: the duration gap widened after rates fell, and the regulator acted before the company did.
- Narrative management: investors are steered to "operating" earnings, and management describes risk as "well hedged" and "minimal."

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying on EPS targets.** Retire the doubling goal as a bonus and option trigger. The target needs only about 2.4% growth this year ($6.31 to $6.46), which puts maximum pressure on precision landing just as judgmental estimates matter most.
2. **Stop growing the retained portfolio faster than the conforming mortgage market.** It has roughly doubled, from $415B to $798B, in about five years. Earnings growth has tracked balance-sheet growth, not better economics per dollar.
3. **Stop leading with "operating" earnings.** Report GAAP first and reconcile every adjustment. The gap is $1.77B (GAAP $4.62B versus operating $6.39B).
4. **Stop treating the legal capital minimum as a target.** A cushion of $0.9B is about 0.1% of assets.
5. **Stop running the duration gap outside a fixed band.** Set triggers in advance, not after the regulator acts `[ASSUMPTION: band of ±6 months]`.
6. **Stop buying loans or securities to hit goal counts unless their credit profile has been priced at loan level.**
7. **Stop arguing that the mission justifies the structure.** Let capital and candor make the argument.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A duration report to the board risk committee every month, with automatic rebalancing at the band edge.
- A capital buffer rule: portfolio growth pauses whenever the cushion falls below target.
- Every guarantee fee is risk-priced at loan level.
- GAAP-first disclosure, with critical estimates reviewed by the audit committee every quarter.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- **Inside the framework:** business units choose products, lender partnerships and affordable-lending programs freely.
- **The framework itself:** the capital, duration and pricing limits above, which no target can override.

**Bureaucracy check:** the EPS target works as a substitute for judgment. It coordinates thousands of people around one number because the organization lacks a shared, risk-aware denominator. Replace it rather than layering more controls on top of it.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make home ownership attainable and stable for working American families by keeping mortgage money flowing in good times and bad.

**Core values:**
1. Credit discipline before volume.
2. Candor: with the regulator, with investors, and inside the company.
3. Stewardship of public trust, as a privilege to be earned and not a birthright.
4. Serving the family at the end of the mortgage.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** become the mortgage guarantor whose promise the market prices on Homestead's own balance sheet, not Washington's, while financing more low- and moderate-income homeowners than anyone.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent CRO with authority to halt growth.
- A board-owned capital buffer rule.
- A duration band with automatic triggers.
- Pay based on multi-year, risk-adjusted metrics, with clawbacks.
- GAAP-first reporting.
- Audit-committee ownership of critical estimates.
