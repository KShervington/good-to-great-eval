It should measure itself by **return per dollar of stress-tested capital**, not by EPS or size.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Homestead is the country's trusted standard-setter for mortgage credit. Its revenue comes mainly from guarantee fees, which it earns by measuring and pricing credit risk better than anyone else. It does not depend on the spread between mortgage yields and its government-subsidized debt.

It keeps capital well above the legal minimum. It reports GAAP results first and explains every adjustment. It keeps interest-rate risk inside board-set limits that it publishes. It meets its affordable-housing goals with loans that perform as modeled.

Its earnings grow at the pace the mortgage market and its credit skill allow, not at a pace someone promised. Its standing in Washington rests on books that regulators and critics can verify, not on political strength. If its implied backing disappeared tomorrow, it would be smaller but still standing.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Paying executives, or vesting broad options, on EPS targets. Replace this with multi-year, risk-adjusted measures that can be clawed back.
2. Setting public EPS targets. Do not set a new one after this year's.
3. Growing the retained portfolio faster than the conforming mortgage market.
4. Operating near the legal capital minimum. The cushion is $0.9B on $887B of assets.
5. Leading investor communication with "operating" earnings. Lead with GAAP and reconcile every adjustment.
6. Letting the duration gap drift outside a published band until a regulator steps in.
7. Meeting affordable-housing goals through any purchase that counts toward them without loan-level credit discipline.
8. Treating critics and repeal bills as fights to win instead of questions to answer with data.
