# Good to Great: Homestead Mortgage Association

## Verdict

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Be the world's best at pricing and guaranteeing U.S. mortgage credit risk, earning the most profit per unit of risk borne, so that more families, especially low- and moderate-income ones, can buy homes they can keep.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** capital. Core capital is $28.1B against $887B of assets (about 32× leverage), only $0.9B above the legal minimum.
- **Top three to stop:** (1) growing the retained portfolio to produce EPS growth; (2) paying executives on an "operating" EPS figure that management defines itself; (3) running capital at the legal floor.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** bonuses, a company-wide option grant and a public promise all depend on a number management defines, and the accounting behind that number has not been independently examined.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** derivative fair values, amortization and hedge-accounting policies, the credit mix of recent purchases, and the dividend payout.
- **Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. It rests only on the dossier.

`Greatness potential: 22/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is a disciplined **credit-risk utility**, not a leveraged bond fund with a mission statement attached. It earns its returns by knowing U.S. mortgage credit risk better than anyone, pricing that risk honestly through guarantee fees, and standardizing the securities that make mortgages cheap and liquid for lenders. It deliberately holds a smaller retained portfolio, kept for liquidity and mission purposes, with tightly matched interest-rate risk. It holds capital well above the legal minimum, so that it could withstand a severe housing downturn on its own resources. It reports results investors can verify without needing management's adjusted definitions. It pays executives on risk-adjusted returns and on the safety of the franchise, not on EPS. It meets its affordable-housing mission by serving low- and moderate-income borrowers with loans that are properly priced and sustainable, not by buying volume it does not understand. The final test of greatness is endurance: its securities would stay trusted even if Congress removed the implicit government backing.

**The flywheel actually turning today** is a different loop: portfolio growth → spread income → operating EPS → bonuses, options and stock price → pressure for more portfolio growth. It closes, but only on the strength of leverage and an implicit subsidy. Every turn increases interest-rate risk and political exposure, so it compounds fragility rather than strength.

**Current push (next one to two turns):**
1. **Link 4:** keep earnings as capital instead of adding assets. Cap the retained portfolio at about its current $798B and let the surplus over minimum build.
2. **Link 5:** rebuild trust through transparency. Make GAAP the headline, publish fair-value and risk disclosures, and close out the supervisory action.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop growing the retained portfolio as the earnings engine.** Freeze it at about $798B and let it run off toward a size set by liquidity and mission needs.
2. **Stop publishing EPS growth targets**, and stop tying bonuses and options to them.
3. **Stop leading with "operating earnings."** Lead with GAAP, and add fair-value and risk disclosures rather than substituting them.
4. **Stop running capital at the floor.** A $0.9B surplus is about seven weeks of operating earnings and about 0.1% of assets.
5. **Stop treating interest-rate positioning as a profit source.** Hold the duration gap inside a tight band every month, not only on average.
6. **Stop meeting rising affordable-housing goals with loans whose risk is unpriced.** If a goal cannot be met with sustainable credit, say so to the housing department openly.
7. **Stop treating critics and the regulator as opponents to beat.** Treat them as red-flag mechanisms.
8. **Stop citing "16 straight years of double-digit growth"** as the identity of the firm.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** make home ownership attainable and *lasting* for American families.
- **Core values:**
  1. Numbers anyone can trust.
  2. Prudence before growth.
  3. Mission served through sustainable credit.
  4. Accountability to the public that granted the charter.
- **BHAG (about 15 years):** Homestead's guaranteed securities trade as the global benchmark for mortgage credit because of its own capital and record, so that removing the implicit government backing would barely move its spreads.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - An independent CRO and CAO reporting to the board.
  - A capital policy set by the board, with a buffer well above the statutory minimum.
  - A duration band with automatic rebalancing triggers.
  - Pay tied to risk-adjusted metrics with multi-year clawbacks.
  - Published stress tests.
  - A standing "red-team" review of each year's affordable-housing purchases.

**State:** good, not yet great. It is not "already great" despite the headline record, and it is not in crisis, so this is a pre-emptive run rather than a turnaround.

### Gate 10: Decline stress test

The two mechanisms most likely to bring this firm down, from its own evidence:
1. **Stage 3, denial of risk, through its own scorecard.** The firm's largest dependency is its own adjusted EPS figure. Early warnings are a widening GAAP-to-operating gap, results landing exactly on target, and outsiders (the regulator, Treasury, the auditor) finding problems before insiders do.
2. **Stage 2, undisciplined pursuit of more, through leverage.** Its most tempting move is "one more year" of portfolio growth on a capital surplus of about seven weeks of earnings. Early warnings are a duration gap beyond ±6 months and a surplus below about $1.6B.

The remaining stages:
- **Stage 1, hubris:** "16 straight years" has become the firm's identity.
- **Stage 4, grasping for salvation:** if constrained, the temptation will be riskier product lines to hit goals or growth. Watch the credit mix.
- **Stage 5, capitulation:** loss of the charter privileges, or failure to stay independent. This is avoidable if capital and transparency come first.
