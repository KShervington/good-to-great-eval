# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Calloway is already great at its core, and it is sliding into decline. This run uses stewardship mode.**

**Hedgehog:** *Calloway gets struggling six-year-olds reading through thirty minutes a day with a well-trained, closely coached tutor, in districts that renew year after year.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** tutor preparation and coaching. Training was cut from 30 to 18 hours, coaches now carry 19 tutors each instead of 12, and tutor retention is down to 68%.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. The $40M grant as written: five states in 30 months, plus three funder-nominated board seats.
2. The One Million Readers plan.
3. The math pilot and ReadAlong.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** first- and second-year schools fell from 69% to 63% to 58% at benchmark, while mature schools held at 74%. That pattern is dilution, not ramp-up.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:** tutor and coach headcounts, completion of the online modules, and results split by training cohort.

**Confidence:** moderate to high. It rests on the board's own dashboard and two independent studies. The newer study is three years old and evaluated the earlier version of the model.

Greatness potential: 62/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Calloway is one its districts can't picture operating without.

- **Every new school performs like a mature school by its second year.** In Year −2, the gap between new and mature schools was only 5 points.
- **Its effect is re-proven independently** every few years, on the model it actually runs.
- **It grows only as fast as its training and coaching system can carry quality.** It starts with the 41 schools already waiting in its own districts.
- **It manages cost per additional reader, not cost per student.** So it spends more on preparing tutors and still stays well below the sector median of about $2,600 per student.
- **Its quality system has an owner who doesn't report to growth.**
- **Its CEO is better known in its 96 schools than on conference stages.**
- **If it enters new states, it does so one at a time,** and it publishes the evidence that each entry worked.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirming the one it already lives by)

| Circle | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Daily one-to-one early-reading tutoring by trained non-teacher tutors, with close coaching, delivered inside district partnerships. | Randomized study: 0.31 SD (n=2,140). Replication: 0.27 SD. Mature schools at 74% vs. 39% for comparison students. Twelve years of renewals. |
| **Not best at** | Middle-school math, parent apps, national advocacy, and rapid multi-state launches. | There is no outcome evidence for any of them. Calloway has never operated outside its three districts, and its fastest recent entry, even inside those districts, degraded results. |

| **Passion** | The founding idea: tutors who are trained and coached well can teach a struggling six-year-old to read. | Staff engagement is 84% favorable vs. a 71% sector benchmark. Tutors go on to become teachers. |

### Denominator: cost per additional reader

This is cost per student divided by the benchmark lift over comparable students. The calculation is descriptive. The comparison group comes from district data, not a randomized control.

| | Year −2 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per student | $1,910 | $1,830 (−4%) |
| Lift over comparable students | 35 points | 31 points |
| **Cost per additional reader** | **~$5,460** | **~$5,900 (+8%)** |
| Lift in new schools / cost per additional reader | 31 pts / ~$6,160 | 19 pts / ~$9,630 `[ASSUMPTION: cost per student is the same in new and mature schools]` |

Calloway became cheaper per student and more expensive per reader it actually helps. The efficiency gains have been paid for out of the outcome.

### Confirmation tests (this is a working hypothesis)

1. Restoring training and coaching lifts new-school benchmark rates to at least 65% within one school year.
2. An independent evaluation of the *current* model finds an effect of at least 0.25 SD.
3. Districts keep renewing and keep filling the waiting list without new product lines.

If test 1 fails even with restored inputs, the cause lies elsewhere, such as which schools are selected. In that case, reopen this gate.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Well-prepared tutors (30-hr training, ~12 per coach)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   2. Daily 1:1 sessions delivered with fidelity
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   3. Children reach benchmark (lift vs. comparable students)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   4. Districts renew, superintendents cite it, waiting list grows
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   5. Funding at a low cost per additional reader
 │        │
 │        ▼
 └── 6. Reinvest in training, coaching and tutor retention
         (with tutors flowing on into district teaching)
```

**Where the loop is breaking:** between links 1 and 2.

- Training was cut by 40%.
- The coaching load rose by 58%.
- Tutor retention fell from 81% to 68%.
- Exit surveys name exactly what was cut: "felt unprepared" and "not enough time with my coach."

The new-school decline started in Year −1, before the training cut. That timing means the coaching ratio and the pace of entry are part of the cause, not training alone.

### Current push

1. Restore tutor preparation. Returning training to 30 hours costs about $410K, roughly $42 per student, which would bring cost per student to about $1,870. That is still about 28% below the sector median. Then bring the coaching ratio back toward 12. The cost of that is unknown because coach headcount isn't in the dossier.
2. Take new schools from the waiting list only when the most recent new-school cohort is within 5 points of mature schools.

## 5. Temptations to refuse (stewardship stop-doing)

1. **Five states in 30 months.** Calloway's own data shows quality falling at about 15% annual growth in districts it already knows well.
2. **One Million Readers by Year 7.** If the count starts at adoption, it requires an average of about 143,000 students a year, roughly 14 times this year's 9,840.
3. **New product lines because districts ask for them.** This covers middle-school math and ReadAlong. Districts renew Calloway for reading.
4. **Cutting tutor preparation to fund reach.** That includes training hours and the coaching ratio.
5. **Letting a dashboard stand in for evaluation.** Equally, explaining declines away ("ramp-up") without testing the explanation.
6. **The conference circuit as a strategy.**
7. **Giving up governance control to win a grant.**

### Non-negotiable disciplines

- 30 hours of pre-service training.
- A coaching ratio capped at 15, with a target of 12.
- Independent evaluation at least every three years, on the current model.
- Every outcome reported split by school age.
- A growth gate: new schools are added only when the last cohort of new schools is within 5 points of mature schools.

### Framework boundaries

Sites keep autonomy over scheduling, tutor matching and district relationships. Training content, coaching ratios and assessment are fixed across all sites.

### Bureaucracy check

No rules here are compensating for the wrong people. The risk runs the other way: quality checks are being removed.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from the CEO's letter]`:
1. Every child can learn to read.
2. Tutors deserve real training and real coaching.
3. Prove results; don't just claim them.
4. Work with districts, not around them.
5. Grow at the speed of quality.

**Core purpose:** every struggling young child learns to read.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** every struggling early reader in every district Calloway serves gets a Calloway tutor, and the model is independently shown to work at 0.25 SD or better in at least three states beyond its home.

The multi-state ambition stays. It gets earned one state at a time.

**Clock-building mechanisms** (so the system no longer depends on one person):
- Codified training and coaching standards.
- A head of program quality who is independent of growth.
- Independent evaluation on a three-year cycle.
- Board policy that writes the growth gate in.
- A board quality committee.
- A dashboard split by cohort.
