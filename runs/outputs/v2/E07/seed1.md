# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Calloway is already great, and it is in early decline.** It has sixteen years of operation, two randomized studies and twelve straight years of contract renewals. The right mode is stewardship: protect the model, and don't scale it on a funder's clock.

- **Hedgehog:** Well-trained, well-coached community tutors give struggling six-year-olds the most proven thirty minutes of their day. Calloway grows only as fast as that stays true.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** training and coaching. Pre-service hours fell from 30 to 18, and tutors per coach rose from 12 to 19.
- **Temptations to refuse:** five states in 30 months on the $40M terms; One Million Readers as a target for students reached; the math pilot and ReadAlong.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** new schools fell from 69% to 58% at benchmark while mature schools held at 74%. Ramp-up time doesn't explain a decline in what new schools achieve.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** new-school results by school type and year, online-module completion, cost by school age.
- **Confidence:** high on direction, because four indicators are moving together. Low on cause, because nobody is employed to test it.

Greatness potential: 70/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway is the organization a superintendent calls when she needs first-graders to read, because its results in a school's first year look like its results in the fifth. Every new site gets the full model: thirty hours of training, a coach with a caseload small enough to actually coach, and thirty minutes a day with every child. An independent research function tells the board the truth every year, including when the truth is uncomfortable.

Calloway grows, but expansion is earned. A new state or district opens only when the last cohort of schools has reached the bar. Funders pay for that pace. They don't set it. The CEO's visibility serves the model rather than standing in for it. The organization measures itself by additional readers, meaning children reading because of Calloway who otherwise wouldn't be, not by students "reached."

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

**Sentence:** *We turn well-trained, well-coached community tutors into the most proven thirty minutes of a struggling six-year-old's day, and we grow only as fast as we can keep that true.*

| Circle | What the evidence says | What it is *not* best at |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Daily tutoring by trained, coached community tutors for struggling early readers, at district scale. It has a randomized effect of 0.31 SD, a replication at 0.27, about twice the comparison benchmark rate, and costs $1,830 per student against a sector median of about $2,600. | Middle-school math, parent apps, national thought leadership, and fast multi-state operations, where it has no track record. |
| **Resource engine** | District contracts (12 years of 100% renewal, 41 schools waitlisted), philanthropy that follows evidence, and a tutor pipeline (22% of former tutors now teach in partner districts). | Growth grants whose conditions outrun the model. |
| **Passion** | The founding "stubborn idea," tutor development, and staff engagement of 84% against a sector benchmark of 71%. | Visibility for its own sake. |

**Denominator:** *cost per additional reader*, meaning cost per student divided by the gap in benchmark rates between Calloway students and similar students who aren't served. This replaces cost per student, the measure that made the cuts look like efficiency.

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per student | $1,910 | $1,870 | $1,830 |
| Benchmark gain over comparison group | 35 pts | 33 pts | 31 pts |
| **Cost per additional reader** | **$5,457** | **$5,667** | **$5,903** (+8%) |
| Cost per student who reached benchmark | $2,616 | $2,597 | $2,614 (flat) |

The $80 saved per student bought nothing. Cost per student reaching benchmark is flat, and cost per *additional* reader rose. The comparison group comes from district data, not the randomized studies, so treat it as a proxy `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. If restoring training and coaching moves new-school benchmark rates back toward 69% or higher within one school year, the concept holds.
2. An independent study of the *current* delivery model reproduces an effect of at least 0.25 SD.
3. District renewals hold at 100%.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  ┌─► 1. Recruit community tutors
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   2. Train well (30 hrs) + coach closely (≤14:1)   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   3. 30 minutes a day, every day, with struggling 6-year-olds
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   4. Kids reach spring benchmark → low cost per additional reader
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   5. Districts renew; waitlist grows; tutors stay and become teachers
  │          │
  │          ▼
  └── 6. Independent evidence + funding follow the results
```

The loop closes. What's broken is the input at step 2, and it is now showing up at step 5: tutor retention has fallen from 81% to 74% to 68%. Exit surveys name step 2 directly ("felt unprepared," "not enough time with my coach").

**Current push:**
1. Restore step 2 by bringing pre-service training back to 30 hours and capping the coach ratio at 14:1 or lower `[ASSUMPTION: target to be calibrated by the research director]`.
2. Rebuild step 6 by hiring the research director now and commissioning an independent study of the model *as currently delivered*.

**Doom-loop check: several signs are present.** They are:
- a big program (the $40M grant) brought in to jump-start momentum;
- a new direction (One Million Readers) adopted while the core is eroding;
- three new initiatives at once (math, ReadAlong, five states);
- inputs cut to improve a cost metric;
- a reorganization that put quality under growth.

None of these is fatal yet. Together they are the classic pattern.

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

1. **Launching five states in 30 months** to meet grant conditions.
2. **Counting "students reached."** One Million Readers needs at least 843,000 more students in seven years, which is about 120,000 a year, or 12 times today's 9,840. That holds even if every past year had been as large as this one.
3. **Cutting training or coaching to lower cost per student.** Calloway is already about 30% below the sector median.
4. **The middle-school math pilot.** It sits outside early literacy.
5. **ReadAlong.** A parent app is not the thirty-minute human model.
6. **Replacing outcome research with a dashboard.** A dashboard shows trends. It can't tell you why they are happening.
7. **Treating CEO visibility as strategy.**

**Truth climate:** weak. A fair question was answered with assertion, and the main mechanism for raising red flags was removed. **Charisma check:** the risk is real. When a confident, visible CEO's story is that "the code is cracked," that story is suppressing the new-school data.

**Faith statement:** Calloway has two randomized studies, sixteen years of renewals and a waitlist. A model that strong can afford to slow down and repair itself, and it will prevail if it does.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
- Every child can learn to read.
- Evidence over story.
- Tutors are developed, not just deployed.
- Districts are partners, not markets.
- Every dollar has to produce readers.

**Core purpose:** Make sure every struggling young reader gets daily help that actually works.

**BHAG** (one line, keeping the organization's own brand but changing what it counts): ***One Million Additional Readers*** within 25 years, meaning children reading at benchmark *because of* Calloway, measured against a comparison group. Today the figure is about 3,050 a year (9,840 students × a 31-point gain).

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An annual independent outcome study.
- A board-adopted policy of fidelity thresholds that gate any expansion (see the risk register).
- A quality function independent of growth, with the authority to stop launches.
- The training and coaching system written down so it doesn't live in one person's head.
- A CEO succession bench.
