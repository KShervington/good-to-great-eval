# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great run (stewardship mode)

**Verdict: Calloway is already great, and it is in the early stages of falling.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Calloway gets struggling early-grade readers to benchmark through thirty daily minutes with a thoroughly trained, closely coached tutor, at a cost per additional reader that districts renew year after year.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** training and coaching. Pre-service training fell from 30 to 18 hours, tutors per coach rose from 12 to 19, and tutor retention fell from 81% to 68%.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) the $40M grant as written (5 states in 30 months plus 3 funder seats); (2) the 18-hour training and uncapped coach loads; (3) the math pilot and ReadAlong.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** new schools reach benchmark at 58%, down from 69% two years ago. Ramp-up can't explain a decline among schools at the same stage. Expansion would make nearly every school a new school.
- **Missing numbers that could change this:** whether the new-school drop tracks training and coach load or site mix; completion of the online training modules; whether the funder will negotiate pace.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high. It rests on the dossier's own three-year dashboard, with no outside research.

Greatness potential: 62/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway looks a lot like Calloway five years ago, run on purpose. Every tutor gets the full 30-hour pre-service training and real time with a coach. New schools reach benchmark within a few points of mature schools. An internal research function, reporting to the board, makes bad news impossible to ignore. The organization grows into new districts and states at the pace its coaching pipeline can carry, and opens each new region only after the last one proves the model there. It says no to adjacent programs its partners ask for, however well meant, because its value comes from being the best at one thing. Its reach goal counts readers who learned to read, not students touched. Its board is independent enough to ask "can the model bear this pace?" and to wait for the answer.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

**Sentence:** *Calloway gets struggling early-grade readers to benchmark through thirty daily minutes with a thoroughly trained, closely coached tutor, at a cost per additional reader that districts renew year after year.*

| Circle | What it is | What it is *not* |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | High-fidelity, coached one-on-one early-reading tutoring delivered in district partnership. Evidence: two randomized studies (0.31 SD, then 0.27 SD), mature schools steady at 74% vs. 39% comparison, cost about 30% below the sector median. | Middle-school math, parent-facing apps, national brand building, or rapid multi-state launches. Calloway has no evidence of excellence at any of these. |
| **Resource engine** (time, money, brand) | Money: district contracts renewed for 12 straight years, 11 balanced budgets, 5 months of reserves. Time: a tutor corps, with 22% of former tutors going on to teach in partner districts. Brand: independent evidence, a 41-school waiting list, superintendents citing Calloway in their plans. | Visibility for its own sake. Conference appearances don't drive renewals; outcomes do. |
| **Passion** | "One stubborn idea" about six-year-olds learning to read; 84% staff engagement (benchmark 71%); tutors who become teachers. | Nothing in the dossier shows staff passion for math or apps. Both came from district requests. |

**Denominator: cost per additional reader at benchmark** (cost per student ÷ (Calloway benchmark rate − comparison rate)). This replaces cost per student, which has been rewarding cuts to the core.

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per student | $1,910 | $1,870 | $1,830 |
| Benchmark gap vs. comparison | 35 pts | 33 pts | 31 pts |
| **Cost per additional reader** (derived) | ≈$5,460 | ≈$5,670 | ≈$5,900 |

The "savings" made each additional reader about 8% *more* expensive. This is a descriptive estimate: the comparison group is district data, not a controlled design.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes: the RCTs, the renewals, and the waiting list all come from the original training-and-coaching model.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: math, ReadAlong, and a 30-month five-state race.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes: it means not taking $40M on the current terms.
- **What would confirm it:** restoring training and coaching brings the new-school benchmark back toward 69% or higher within one school year.
- **What would challenge it:** the decline persists after restoration, which would point to site mix or district context rather than delivery.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Thorough training (30 hrs) + close coaching (≤12–15 tutors/coach)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    2. High-fidelity daily 30-min sessions, tutors who stay all year
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    3. Struggling readers reach benchmark
 │       (denominator: cost per additional reader)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    4. Districts renew, waitlists grow, evidence earns funders,
 │       tutors become district teachers
 │            │
 │            ▼
 └─── 5. Resources reinvested FIRST in training & coaching capacity,
          THEN in new sites at the pace coaching allows
```

**Weakest link:** link 1 → 2. Year 0 cut training by 40% and pushed coach loads up 58%. Exit surveys say it directly: "felt unprepared in the first months" and "not enough time with my coach." Link 5 is also bending: money is flowing toward growth and adjacent programs while being taken out of link 1.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Restore link 1: 30-hour training and a hard cap on tutors per coach.
2. Rebuild the instrument on link 3: a research director and an annual outcome study, so that growth decisions rest on cohort-level evidence rather than a dashboard footnote.

**Doom-loop check: signs are present.**
- *Big program to jump-start momentum:* One Million Readers plus a $40M grant.
- *New directions:* the math pilot and ReadAlong, both launched in the same year.
- *Reaction without understanding:* the outcome study was dropped and the research seat left empty.
- *Leadership change that shifted focus:* the system's designer left and coaching was folded under Growth.

The loop still closes in mature schools, which is why this is not a FAIL.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: the real temptations)

1. **Accepting the $40M grant on its current terms**, meaning five new states in 30 months plus three nominated voting seats. Counter-propose instead (see the 90-day plan).
2. **The 18-hour pre-service training.** Restore 30 hours. It cost about $410K to cut, roughly 2.8% of the budget, and cost per student would still sit well below the $2,600 sector median.
3. **Letting tutors-per-coach drift.** Set a hard cap: 15 now, back to 12 as capacity allows.
4. **The middle-school math pilot.**
5. **ReadAlong.**
6. **Reporting cost per student as a success metric on its own.** Report cost per additional reader instead.
7. **Calling the new-school decline "ramp-up"** without a cohort analysis.
8. **Treating a real-time dashboard as a substitute for an outcome study.**
9. **Adopting "One Million Readers" as a reach target.** It counts students served, not readers made, and the arithmetic doesn't work (see the brutal facts).
10. **The conference circuit at its Year 0 pace.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** Every struggling young reader becomes a reader.

**Core values:**
1. Evidence over enthusiasm.
2. Tutors are trained and coached *before* children depend on them.
3. Frugality serves impact, never the reverse.
4. Districts are partners, and we owe them the truth about results.
5. Grow only as fast as quality allows.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** In every district Calloway enters, coached one-on-one tutoring becomes the standard first response to early reading difficulty. Every state Calloway operates in has independent evidence of at least 0.25 SD.

This replaces One Million Readers. It grows out of the Hedgehog Concept and counts readers made, not reach.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An annual outcome study run by a research director who reports to the board program committee as well as the CEO.
- Growth gates: no new region opens until the last new cohort is within 5 points of mature schools [ASSUMPTION threshold].
- A model owner role, independent of Growth.
- A written training and coaching standard, so the system no longer lives in one person.
- A CEO succession plan.

## 11. Risk register (decline stress test first)

**Where Calloway sits now:** past Stage 1 (hubris: "we have cracked the code," "proven in every setting"), well into Stage 2 (undisciplined pursuit of more: five states, math, an app, 1M reach), and showing early Stage 3 (denial: "ramp-up," the research seat left empty, a pace question dismissed).

**Lead mechanism 1: expansion outrunning the training and coaching system.**
- *Why it fits Calloway:* its biggest dependency is coaching capacity, and every new state is all new schools.
- *Early warnings and thresholds:*
  - New-school benchmark below 65%, or gap to mature schools above 8 points → freeze new-site openings.
  - Tutors per coach above 15 → no new sites until resolved.
  - Tutor retention below 75% → trigger a training review.
- *Current status:* all three thresholds are already breached.

**Lead mechanism 2: measurement blindness plus funder-driven governance.**
- *Why it fits Calloway:* the outcome study is gone, the evidence brand drives renewals in the home districts too, and grant terms would put a growth clock on the board.
- *Early warnings and thresholds:*
  - Any school year without an independent or internal outcome study → board escalation.
  - Any grant milestone that can't be paused for quality → renegotiate or exit.
  - Any board vote where a quality concern is overridden by a funding deadline → governance review.

**Remaining stages (brief):**
- *Stage 4, grasping for salvation:* if new states disappoint, expect a push for ReadAlong, tech, or new programs as rescue. The response should be to restore the core first.
- *Stage 5, irrelevance:* a public failure to replicate in new states could damage renewals in the original three districts.
