# Good to Great: Varden Communications

## Verdict

**Varden is already great, and it shows early signs of decline (Collins's Stages 1–2).** This run is in stewardship mode, so the job is to protect the flywheel.

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Varden is the world's best at making portable radio communication reliable at scale. It stays the best by making its own products obsolete before anyone else can.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** "first to the next standard." The flagship launch is an analog phone at the moment carriers are planning digital networks.
- **Temptations to refuse:**
  1. Defending analog.
  2. An uncapped commitment to the satellite launch phase.
  3. The target of doubling in size every five years.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Varden holds about 45% of world cell phones, mostly analog, while digital standards are forming.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:**
  - Varden's digital handset share
  - Satellite demand forecasts
  - Segment profitability
  - Carrier reaction to the distribution program
- **Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on three years of dossier financials and the stated internal positions, with no outside research.

`Greatness potential: 33/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great version of Varden is the company that leads every wireless generation, not just the analog one. It applies its old habit of self-renewal to its biggest franchise. It ships a digital successor to its own clamshell before a competitor does, and it treats carriers as partners rather than as channels to be squeezed. Its bets are judged by whether they feed the wireless flywheel, not by whether they add size. The satellite system and the PC-microprocessor fight are held as bounded options rather than as identity commitments. Growth follows from the Hedgehog Concept instead of being demanded by a doubling target. The quality discipline it pioneered (Six Sigma) and its culture of combative technical debate are aimed at the hardest current question, which is the move to digital, and not at questions the company has already won.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Varden is the world's best at making portable radio communication reliable at scale, and stays there by obsoleting its own products before anyone else can.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Portable two-way radio and cellular; manufacturing quality at scale; RF and embedded semiconductors; PC microprocessors; satellite telephone systems | Portable radio communication engineering combined with Six Sigma manufacturing quality. The proof is No. 1 positions in cellular phones, paging and two-way radio. | **PC microprocessors:** Varden is a challenger to a dominant supplier and has shown no best-in-world evidence `[ASSUMPTION]`. **Operating a global satellite telecom service:** this is a different business (capital-intensive service operation), not radio product engineering `[ASSUMPTION]`. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per employee; profit per handset; profit per wireless subscriber | **Profit per wireless subscriber served.** Handsets, infrastructure and pagers all scale with subscribers, which grew from 23M to 85M in three years. `[ASSUMPTION]`: segment profitability isn't in the dossier, so this denominator is unconfirmed. | Revenue growth itself, which is what the doubling goal measures. |
| **Passion** | Engineering, quality, self-renewal, size | Technical debate, quality, and renewal, which is "making its own products obsolete." | Size for its own sake. It appeared only after the founder's son stepped back. |

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Varden left TV and car radio, moved into wireless, and won the first national quality award.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It puts the PC-chip war, the satellite operator role and premium distribution coercion outside the core.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means obsoleting the analog franchise that produces most of today's profits.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
1. Segment profit data showing wireless earns the best returns per unit of capital.
2. Varden's share of digital handset orders.
3. A carrier survey on supplier preference for digital.

**Key insight:** the analog defense is not a new strategy. It is a departure from Varden's own Hedgehog Concept. The company that makes its own products obsolete is currently refusing to do so in its largest business.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: temptations to refuse)

1. **Defending analog as the future.** Varden should harvest analog and stop betting the flagship roadmap on it.
2. **Committing to the full satellite launch phase without** independent demand evidence and a hard cap on total exposure. That exposure includes the subcontract obligations Varden carries even if the venture can't pay.
3. **Managing to "double every five years."** It turns revenue into the goal and invites the undisciplined pursuit of more.
4. **Coercing carriers through premium distribution terms**, especially when those carriers are about to choose digital suppliers.
5. **Fighting the dominant PC chip supplier for its own sake** `[ASSUMPTION]`. Varden should keep only what feeds wireless or embedded semiconductors.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Six Sigma quality on every product.
- Funding for the next generation before the current one peaks.
- Open technical debate that must end in a dated decision.

**Framework:** divisions are free to choose products and technologies. They are not free to create unbounded corporate obligations or to override customer relationships for share.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rule in the dossier exists to compensate for wrong people. The risk is the opposite: too few gates on large commitments.

**Faith statement:** Varden has reinvented itself through every technology shift in 70 years, and it will lead digital too if it obsoletes its own analog phones first.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** (inferred `[ASSUMPTION]`):
1. Quality to Six Sigma.
2. Renewal: obsolete ourselves before others do.
3. Open, rigorous technical debate.
4. Long-range investment.

**Core purpose** `[ASSUMPTION]`: let people communicate wherever they are.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** through every wireless generation for the next 20 years, the world's leading portable phone is a Varden.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- **Annual self-obsolescence review:** each business names the product that will replace its current leader, and the date.
- **Big-bet gate:** any commitment above a set share of annual earnings needs independent demand evidence and a capped total exposure.
- **Red-flag channel:** an engineer or carrier contact can escalate a "we're wrong about the market" warning directly to the exec committee.
- **Succession built from a bench,** not from family lineage alone.
