# Varden Communications: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Varden is already great, but it shows early signs of decline. The run is in stewardship mode.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Varden is best at putting the next generation of radio communication into people's hands first, built to Six Sigma quality, and making its own products obsolete before anyone else does.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** being first to the next generation. Wireless (about 60% of sales) is defending analog while carriers plan for digital.
- **Temptations to refuse:**
  1. Defending analog as the strategy.
  2. Committing to the satellite venture's full launch phase without demand evidence.
  3. The goal of doubling every five years.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** a company famous for making its own products obsolete has stopped doing so in its biggest business. Earnings already grew only 14% on 22% sales growth.
- **Missing numbers that would change this verdict:** digital handset share, profit by segment, the satellite venture's demand forecast and break-even, and how carriers react to the distribution program.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on the dossier alone, with no segment-profit or shareholder-return data.

Greatness potential: 40/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Varden does to analog what it did to car radio and consumer TV. It treats its 45% analog share as a base to harvest, not a fortress to defend. It is shipping class-leading digital phones on whichever standards carriers adopt, before carriers have to ask twice, and the miniaturization behind the clamshell carries straight into digital. Carriers see it as a partner, not a gatekeeper. Its semiconductor arm is best in the world at the chips that make radios small, cheap and reliable, and has stopped fighting a PC-chip war it cannot win. It plays in satellite as a paid builder with capped exposure, not as an open-ended guarantor. Its goals describe generations led and profit per connection, not company size. The culture of self-renewal and open debate that brought it this far is formalized into mechanisms, so it no longer depends on one family generation remembering it.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Put the next generation of radio communication into people's hands first, built to Six Sigma quality, and obsolete our own products before a competitor does.*

This is a confirmation, not an invention. It describes the pattern of the company's 70 years: car radio, then two-way radio, then pagers, then cellular, plus exits from TV and car radio.

| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per handset; profit per R&D dollar; profit per carrier relationship; **profit per wireless connection** (handset, infrastructure, pager and two-way radio combined) | **Profit per wireless connection served** `[ASSUMPTION]`. It ties handsets and infrastructure to the subscriber growth from 23M to 85M, and it exposes the pricing squeeze. |
| **Deeply passionate about** | Engineering firsts; quality; long-range bets; open technical debate; making its own products obsolete | Self-renewal and quality. The business press describes Varden in exactly these terms. |

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes: No. 1 in cellular, paging and two-way radio, and fivefold growth in a decade.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: the PC-chip war, a guarantor role in satellite, and defending analog.
- **Would committing to it be uncomfortable?** Very. It condemns the leading position in the company's biggest business.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
- Segment profitability showing that wireless profit per connection drives company earnings.
- A digital product shipping within the next product cycle that holds share.

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

1. **Defending analog as the strategy.** Ship the analog clamshell and harvest the base, but don't let the pride of the launch set the roadmap.
2. **Committing to the full satellite launch phase without demand evidence.** No demand forecast, break-even subscriber count or contingent-liability total is in hand.
3. **"Double every five years."** From $27.0B that means about $54B by Year 4, which is adding a whole current company in five years. A size goal pulls the company toward any large opportunity, whether it fits or not.
4. **A distribution program that conditions carriers' access on share and shelf space.** Carriers are about to choose their digital suppliers, and coercion now invites them to qualify a second supplier.
5. **A PC-microprocessor war against the dominant supplier.** Keep the chips that feed the radio Hedgehog Concept and stop chasing PC dominance `[ASSUMPTION: no evidence of a winnable lead]`.
