# Halvard Strings: Good to Great (stewardship run)

**Verdict: Halvard is already great. This is a stewardship run.** Revenue has grown every year for 14 years. Operating margin was 24–29% in 10 of the last 11 years. ROIC has averaged about 34%. Share in professional orchestras rose from 9% to 31%.

- **Hedgehog:** Halvard makes the strings professional bowed-string players choose blind, and sells them at a full premium only through the specialist shops those players trust.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** winding capacity for cello and bass. Lead times hit five weeks in Halvard's strongest segment, where it holds 44% of cellists.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) Halvard Six as proposed; (2) the PE group's student brand; (3) any product the Listening Room hasn't approved.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** both items on the agenda would rebuild the business Halvard exited to become great. Halvard Six would be about 24% of revenue `[ASSUMPTION: core keeps growing ~8%/yr]` at half the gross margin.
- **Numbers that would change this:** share among advanced (non-orchestra) players, winding-machine utilization, and Halvard Six's operating margin and effect on shops.
- **Confidence:** high. The verdict rests on audited accounts and 14 years of survey data.

`Greatness potential: 78/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Halvard already is the great version. The job now is to keep it great through a CEO transition and a slowing core market.

In five years, great Halvard looks like this. It is still a single-minded maker of violin, viola, cello and bass strings. Every product passes a blind professional panel whose veto is visibly real. Its strings are on a steadily rising share of professional instruments. It sells at a premium that shops defend because it protects their margins. Cello and bass sets ship in two weeks even at the autumn peak. The COO has become CEO without the panel's authority or the channel discipline weakening.

Growth comes from deeper penetration of the bowed-string world, not from new worlds. Halvard says no, calmly and on the record, to opportunities that would have made it bigger and worse.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Halvard makes the strings that professional bowed-string players choose blind, and sells them at a full premium only through the specialist shops those players trust.**

|---|---|---|---|

| **Passion** | The acoustics engineering, the players' judgment, and the craft of winding. | **Getting the sound right for the people whose living depends on it.** Evidence: a CEO who is an acoustics engineer, 40 players who kill products, and an employee-owned plant. | Scale for its own sake. |

**Does it explain the past?** Yes. Exiting mass distribution cost about 22% of revenue, and gross margin then rose from 44% to 61%. The volume Halvard dropped never won a professional player.

**Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out both agenda items (see §5).

**Would committing to it be uncomfortable?** Yes. It means accepting a core market growing at about 2% and telling two board members no.

**Working hypothesis. It is confirmed if:**
- share among advanced players tracks pro share region by region;
- cohort retention stays at 75% or above;
- gross margin holds at 60% or above with no MAP breaks.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Listening Room blind-tests every prototype (veto is real: 3 kills / 5 yrs)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   2. Strings pros prefer (sound + durability)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   3. Orchestra adoption grows (9% → 31%; cellists 44%)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   4. Advanced players + teachers follow; specialist shops stock & recommend   [ASSUMPTION: link unmeasured]
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   5. Premium held (15–25%, MAP never broken) → shop margins steady → shop loyalty
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   6. Gross profit per pro player won & kept (GM 61%, ROIC ~34%)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 └── 7. Reinvest: panel, own winding machines, CAPACITY to deliver in 2 weeks
```

**Weakest link: step 7 into step 3, delivery capacity.** Cello and bass lead times slipped to five weeks in the autumn. That is the segment where Halvard is strongest. A professional who can't get strings before a season may switch, and switching undoes the 78% retention.

The link is under direct threat. Halvard Six asks for a second shift on two winding machines, which is the same scarce capacity.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Make two-week cello and bass lead times hold through the autumn peak. Use any second shift for the core, not for guitar.
2. Start measuring link 4, the pull-through from pros to advanced players. It is the least-evidenced link in the loop, and in-hedgehog growth depends on it.

**Doom-loop check:** there are no signs in 14 years: no new direction after new direction, no acquisitions, no discounting. This board meeting is the first real doom-loop invitation. Growth has slowed from about 12% compound to 7–9%, and two "big programs to restart momentum" are on the table at once. That pattern is what to watch for.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: temptations to refuse)

These are the real temptations. There is no padding.

1. **Halvard Six as proposed.** It fails all three circles:
   - **Best at:** a corrosion test in the lab is not tone validated by players. Halvard has no guitar panel.
   - **Engine:** 31% gross margin against 61%. At about 24% of revenue `[ASSUMPTION]`, blended gross margin would fall to roughly 54% `[ASSUMPTION, illustrative]`, below the Year −7 level of 58%.
   - **Passion:** guitar strings are not what this company's people care about.
   - **Channel:** end-caps in a mass chain are the channel Halvard left 14 years ago.
   - **Capacity:** it consumes the winding capacity cello and bass are already short of.
2. **The PE student brand at 1.1× revenue.** The low multiple reflects the business: student volume through distributors, which is the 40% of units Halvard deliberately exited. Owning it blurs the brand that pros pay a premium for.
3. **Letting the Listening Room "approve" guitar strings.** The CEO's instinct that players should decide is right. But a panel of violin-family professionals can't judge guitar tone. Using it would spend the panel's authority on a question it isn't qualified to answer. The honest reading of her principle, "who answers it," is that nobody inside Halvard can answer for guitar. That *is* the answer.
4. **Selling below MAP or listing on general online marketplaces.** Keep this rule even as shops consolidate and pressure builds.
5. **Selling winding capacity to non-core uses** before cello and bass lead times are reliably at two weeks.
