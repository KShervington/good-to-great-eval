# Good to Great: Halvard Strings

## Verdict

**Halvard is already great. Run it in stewardship mode: protect the flywheel and don't reinvent it.**

- **Hedgehog:** Violin-family strings that professional players choose in blind tests, sold only through the specialist shops that serve those players. The engine is profit per professional player.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** delivery through shops. Independent shops in North America fell about 3% last year, and cello/bass lead times hit five weeks last autumn.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) Halvard Six as proposed: mass-chain end-caps at 31% gross margin. (2) The private-equity group's student brand. (3) Using winding capacity for anything outside the core while core lead times are over two weeks.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Halvard Six would undo the founding stop-doing decision. It would also run on the same winding machines that already fell behind on cello and bass.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** winding-capacity utilisation, player share by instrument year by year, and Halvard Six's operating margin plus how shops would react.
- **Confidence:** high on "already great," which rests on 14 years of audited results and survey data. Moderate on the choice of denominator.

Greatness potential: 80/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Halvard's great version looks much like the current company, made durable. It keeps making the strings that professional violin, viola, cello and bass players pick blind. Nothing ships without the Listening Room's approval. It sells only through specialist shops at a protected price. Its growth comes from steadily taking share where it still trails: violin, viola and bass players, who lag the 44% of cellists. Margins stay in the 24–29% band and returns stay well above the cost of capital. The difference is that this greatness no longer depends on Annelie Brandt. The panel rule, the channel rule and the capacity discipline become board-level policy, and the COO proves them before she leaves. The company declines to become a bigger, ordinary string maker.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  Listening Room blind-tests every prototype
                 │
                 ▼
  Professional players adopt (31% of orchestra players)
                 │
                 ▼
  Shops and teachers recommend what pros use → advanced players follow
                 │
                 ▼
  Loyalty (78% at 3 yrs) + premium held at minimum advertised price
                 │
                 ▼
  Shops earn steady margins → stock, fit and recommend Halvard; sets delivered on time
                 │
                 ▼
  High profit per professional player (27.9% op. margin, ~34% ROIC)
                 │
                 ▼
  Funds winding R&D, own machines, the panel ──► back to the top
```

**Weakest link:** the shop and delivery step. The channel is shrinking (about 3% fewer North American independent shops), and capacity slipped to five-week lead times on cello and bass. A shop can't recommend a set it can't get.

**Current push:**
1. Protect winding capacity so cello and bass lead times stay at two weeks or less, including through the summer roof replacement.
2. Push share among violin, viola and bass players toward the cello level, through the shops.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Halvard Six as proposed.** The plan would add about $5.9M of gross profit by Year 3, but blended gross margin would fall from 61% to about 54% `[ASSUMPTION: core keeps growing ~8%/yr]`. It sells through the mass channel Halvard left, on end-caps that sit oddly beside a never-discount, no-marketplace policy. It uses a second shift on winding machines the core already strained. The evidence is a corrosion test, not a blind test by players. **If the board still wants the question answered**, send it where Brandt pointed: a blind panel of professional guitarists. Agree pass criteria first, make no channel or capacity commitment, cap the cost, and set a kill date. If it can't win blind, it isn't Halvard.
2. **Buying the student brand.** It would reverse the founding exit. A consolidator selling it quietly at about 1.1 times revenue suggests it isn't central for them either.
3. **Giving winding capacity to anything outside the core** while cello or bass lead times are over two weeks.
4. **Breaking the minimum advertised price or listing on general marketplaces** to make up for shop consolidation.
5. **Shipping any product the Listening Room hasn't approved** to meet a growth number.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION, inferred from behaviour]`:
- The player's ear decides.
- Honour the shop: one price, no shortcuts.
- Engineering rigour over marketing claims.
- Credit the players, not ourselves.

**Core purpose** `[ASSUMPTION]`: to give serious string players a sound they trust.

**BHAG (20 years):** Halvard on most of the strings played in the orchestras it surveys. That means more than 50% of players, up from 31%, which is measurable with the existing survey.
