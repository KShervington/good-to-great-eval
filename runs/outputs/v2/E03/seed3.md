# Marbury Laboratories: Good to Great run (stewardship mode)

## Verdict: already great, so protect it rather than reinvent it

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Marbury makes reliable, science-based products that let cost-squeezed health-care buyers get better care for less money, and it earns more profit per employee doing so.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** manufacturing quality. Regulators have raised concerns at the diagnostics plants for two years without resolution, and diagnostics is the business Marbury says it leads the world in.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) buying a pharmacy benefit manager; (2) merging for scale; (3) an across-the-board R&D race with the largest drugmakers.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the unresolved diagnostics quality problem. The intravenous-solution recall 25 years ago shows what a quality failure costs this company.
- **Missing numbers that could change this:** shareholder returns against the market, profit by product line, and the regulators' actual findings.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on 23 straight years of higher earnings, about 39% return on equity and almost no debt.

Greatness potential: 72/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marbury has become the supplier that health-care buyers working under fixed payments, rebates and competitive bids depend on to lower the total cost of getting people well. It leads in diagnostics, nutrition and hospital products, and it holds focused drug positions only where it can be the best. Its quality record is beyond reproach, and it runs its plants as carefully as its labs. It grows through volume rather than price. It funds its own research without debt, and it measures success by rising profit per employee, not by scale. It stays out of the consolidation wave because merging is not its game. Its earnings record continues because the flywheel keeps compounding, not because anyone is protecting a streak.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Don't buy a pharmacy benefit manager.** It is a distribution business in which Marbury cannot be best, and it would put the company in conflict with the buyers it sells to.
2. **Don't merge for scale.** Size is the drug giants' denominator, not Marbury's. Consider an acquisition only if it passes the Hedgehog test in writing and adds a best-in-world, cost-lowering position.
3. **Don't raise R&D across the board to match the largest drugmakers.** Rank projects by best-in-world potential and by cost-lowering value. Fund the top of that list fully and stop the rest.
4. **Don't treat price increases as the growth lever.** Price contributed 0.8% of growth. Plan as though that falls to zero or below.
5. **Don't let the earnings streak override quality spending.** If keeping year 24 alive requires deferring plant remediation, the streak should break.
