## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Marbury is already the company that cost-pressured health systems need. It makes the tests, formulas, IV solutions and targeted drugs that help care get done right for less money. It has grown for 23 straight years without large acquisitions, with almost no debt, by turning its own research into products buyers pick on value. The great version of the next decade looks much the same, but sharper. Quality is beyond reproach in every plant, starting with diagnostics. R&D is aimed at areas where Marbury can lead, not at matching the largest drugmakers' discovery budgets. Every product comes with evidence that it lowers the total cost of care. And the company calmly declines the consolidation wave its peers are rushing into. Its edge is what cost containment rewards: being the efficient, reliable choice.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: temptations to refuse)

1. **Don't buy a pharmacy benefit manager.** It is a distribution and data business where Marbury has no claim to being best. Rivals paying billions for PBMs is not evidence that the move creates value.
2. **Don't merge for branded-drug scale.** Thirty years of organic growth and ~39% ROE are not a strategy in need of rescue.
3. **Don't race the largest drugmakers on discovery breadth.** Increase branded-drug R&D only where Marbury can lead (for example, the antibiotic class it already dominates, and niches tied to its hospital and diagnostics franchises `[ASSUMPTION]`).
4. **Don't let the earnings streak override quality.** No quarter is worth a plant that regulators don't trust. Be willing to break the streak to fix diagnostics.
5. **Don't chase earnings growth through price or aggressive selling in government-funded channels.** Price added only 0.8% last year. Pushing harder through rebate and bid systems invites regulatory and reputational risk.
