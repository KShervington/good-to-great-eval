# Halvorsen-Pike Corporation: Good to Great

**Verdict: Good, not yet great. Commit the company to consumer paper brands and sell the commodity mills.**

- **Hedgehog (working):** Turn fiber into the leading paper-based personal-care brand in every category we choose to fight in, measured by profit per consumer brand.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** product innovation in absorbents. The diaper leaks, tampons are taking share, and the company has no nonwovens expertise.
- **Stop doing:** (1) modernizing commodity printing and newsprint mills; sell them instead. (2) Shipping a diaper that leaks; fix it to parity or exit. (3) The air-charter profit-center plan.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the company can't fund both mill modernization and a war with Rival P. It is choosing between them without being able to see segment returns.
- **Missing numbers that could change this:** segment return on capital across a full cycle, brand share trends, and realistic sale values for the mills and timberland.
- **Confidence:** medium-low. The direction rests on qualitative dossier evidence, not segment economics.

Greatness potential: 40/100

---

*Evidence note: this run had no memory, past-chat, or research access, so there was no prior baseline and no market or competitor research. Everything below comes from the dossier. Inferences are marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halvorsen-Pike is a focused consumer paper-products company, no longer a mill company that happens to own brands. It uses the fiber science it already has, plus the nonwoven capability it has to build, to make everyday personal-care products that are measurably better than parity. It turns that edge into the #1 or a strong #2 brand in each category it chooses. The capital tied up in commodity printing papers, newsprint, an air fleet, and possibly surplus timberland has been released to fund advertising, R&D, and consumer talent. Every investment is judged by one question: does it raise profit per consumer brand? The company outperforms the market over decades rather than one cycle, and the strategy outlasts the CEO who chose it.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis)

**Sentence:** *We turn fiber into the leading paper-based personal-care brand in every category we choose to fight in, and we measure ourselves by profit per consumer brand.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Low-cost commodity milling | **No.** Mills need heavy reinvestment just to reach cost parity, and Rival S is larger and more profitable. |
| | (b) Paper-based consumer products (tissue, towels, toilet tissue) | **Yes, potentially.** The company invented facial tissue and still holds the leading brand. It combines paper know-how with brand-building, which Rival P is only now entering in toilet tissue. |
| | (c) Disposable diapers / absorbents | **Not today.** Rival P created and dominates the category, the product leaks, and the company has no nonwovens skill. This is an open test. |
| | (d) Mass consumer marketing across every category | **No.** Rival P is the best consumer marketer in the world. Fight category by category, not across the board. |
| | (e) Specialty papers, air charter, forest land | **No evidence** of best-in-world potential. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per ton (cyclical, commodity); profit per mill; **profit per consumer brand** | **Profit per consumer brand.** Consumer markets grow faster and reward brand, distribution, and innovation rather than mill cost `[ASSUMPTION: branded margins are higher and steadier than mill margins; unverified because segment data is missing]`. |
| **Passion** | "We are a mill company" (existing); pride in inventing a category; craft in fiber | **Weakest circle.** Today's passion is attached to the mills. It has to be moved to *what the fiber becomes*: the products and brands. Treat this transfer as a leadership task. |

**Denominator:** profit per consumer brand. Secondary diagnostic: return on capital by segment.

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. The company's biggest success is inventing facial tissue and still leading it. The mills have delivered 20 years of below-market returns.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It excludes commodity printing papers, newsprint, the Canadian newsprint JV, specialty-paper roll-ups, air charter, and copying Rival P category by category.
- **Uncomfortable?** Very. It means giving up the namesake mill identity.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
1. Segment return on capital over a full cycle shows consumer beating mills.
2. Brand-level share data shows facial and toilet tissue leadership can be defended against Rival P's entry.
3. The diaper and feminine-care fix-or-exit reviews (see §9) come back with a credible path to parity.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  ┌─► 1. Better-than-parity products
  │      (fiber + nonwoven R&D; softness,
  │       absorbency, no leaks)
  │              │
  │              ▼
  │   2. Brand leadership: #1 or strong #2
  │      in each chosen category
  │              │
  │              ▼
  │   3. Brand premium + shelf/distribution
  │      clout → PROFIT PER CONSUMER BRAND ↑
  │              │
  │              ▼
  │   4. Capital redeployed from commodity
  │      mills / fleet / surplus land
  │      into consumer
  │              │
  │              ▼
  │   5. More advertising, R&D, and consumer
  │      talent (marketers, nonwoven scientists)
  └──────────────┘
```

**Loop closes:** yes. Step 5 funds step 1.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Turn 4:** make the capital decision. Sell the commodity printing and newsprint mills, including the Canadian JV stake, and evaluate monetizing part of the timberland `[ASSUMPTION: land is saleable at meaningful value]`.
2. **Turn 1:** close the absorbent product gap, or exit the categories where it can't be closed.

**Weakest link:** turn 1 in absorbents. Leakage complaints, share lost to tampons, and no nonwovens expertise all point here.

**Doom-loop check:** the warning signs are already present.
- A string of specialty-paper acquisitions.
- A diaper launched and then left to fall behind.
- A proposal to diversify the air fleet into a business.
- Capital spread across everything by tradition.

The mill sale is a single large move, which is a doom-loop pattern in itself. It is justified only if consistent, unglamorous pushes on this one loop follow it for years, with no further lurches.

| Classification | Person / role / commitment |

| **Keep** | The CEO (see leadership read). The facial-tissue brand team. Cellulose fiber R&D, redirected toward consumer product performance. |
| **Move seat** | Mill-operations executives who embrace the consumer direction. Move them into manufacturing excellence for consumer converting, where their skills are an asset `[ASSUMPTION: some will]`. |
| **Off this bus** | Executives whose commitment is to the mills as such. They aren't wrong people, but they belong with the mills, and they could go with them to the buyer. The air-charter proposal. Tradition-based capital allocation as a "commitment." |
| **Missing** | (1) A senior consumer marketer who has beaten a giant like Rival P. (2) A head of absorbent and nonwoven materials R&D. (3) A capital allocator or CFO function that can produce segment returns. (4) A credible successor or president, given the CEO's health (see §11). |

Use rigor, not ruthlessness. Assess each senior executive against the direction before deciding. Put the strongest people on facial and toilet tissue, the biggest opportunity, not on the failing diaper.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop modernizing commodity printing-paper and newsprint mills.** Sell them, including the Canadian JV stake.
2. **Stop allocating capital by tradition.** No business gets capital without a return-on-capital case against the denominator.
3. **Stop shipping a diaper that leaks.** Fix it to parity on a deadline, or exit.
4. **Stop the air-charter and maintenance profit-center plan.** Shrink the fleet to genuine business need `[ASSUMPTION: six aircraft exceeds need]`.
5. **Stop acquiring specialty-paper niches**, and review the envelope and cigarette paper units for sale.
6. **Stop trying to match Rival P everywhere.** Fight only where category leadership is achievable.
7. **Stop treating mill tenure as the path to the top.** Promote for consumer results.

**Truth climate:** the open debate is a healthy sign. The risk is quiet consensus from a mill-raised senior team, plus "by tradition" capital decisions that are never questioned.

**Charisma check:** low risk. The CEO is shy and self-critical, so his personality is unlikely to suppress bad news.

**Red-flag mechanism:** a quarterly segment return-on-capital report and brand-share report go to the full board, unfiltered.

**Faith statement:** *We invented a category and still own its best brand. If we focus everything on what we can lead, we will prevail, however hard the facts are today.*
