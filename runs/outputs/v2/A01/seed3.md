# Halvorsen-Pike Corporation: Good to Great Run

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Be the best in the world at branded, paper-based consumer products that people ask for by name, measured by **profit per consumer brand**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** product innovation in absorbent products. The diaper leaks, the company has no nonwoven expertise, and pads are losing to tampons.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop allocating capital to the mills by tradition.
2. Stop funding cost-parity modernization in commodity coated paper and newsprint.
3. Kill the air-fleet "profit center" idea.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** For 20 years the company has fed the most capital to businesses it cannot be best at. It cannot even see segment returns.

**Numbers that would change the answer:** segment ROC, brand-level share, and category growth rates.

**Confidence:** Moderate. The direction rests on the dossier's market structure and the facial-tissue franchise. Segment economics are unverified.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Halvorsen-Pike becomes a consumer paper products company that happens to own some mills, not a mill company that happens to own some brands. Capital, talent and research flow to a small set of categories where it can win on brand and product: facial tissue first, then toilet tissue and towels, then absorbent products if it closes the nonwovens gap. It funds this by selling or exiting commodity printing paper and newsprint, and, if the economics confirm it, the surplus timberland. It beats Rival S on sanitary paper and holds its own against Rival P category by category. The pride the company takes in craftsmanship moves from "we run great mills" to "we make the products people reach for." The result is sustained shareholder returns above the market, a brand portfolio that would leave a real hole if it disappeared, and a strategy that outlasts the current CEO.

**Tests:**

- **Rules things out?** Yes: newsprint, coated magazine paper, specialty paper roll-ups, the air fleet, and timberland as a strategy.

**Status:** working hypothesis. It is confirmed if:
1. Segment ROC shows consumer products earning above cost of capital and printing papers below it.
2. Tissue and towel share holds or grows over the next 12 months.
3. A diaper reformulation closes the leakage gap in blind testing.

2. Rebuild absorbent-product R&D, hiring or partnering for nonwovens, with diaper leakage as the first target.

**Doom-loop check:** Signs of the doom loop are already present.
- **Acquisitions without a concept:** the 20-year string of specialty paper roll-ups.

- **Drift:** an air fleet proposed as a new business.
- **Indecision:** the unresolved debate over consumer products versus mill modernization, with capital spread across both.

The coming risk is treating the mill sale as a single dramatic "transformation" and then failing to keep pushing on turns 2–6. The sale is only the first push.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop allocating capital to the mills by tradition. Every dollar must compete on profit per consumer brand.
2. Stop cost-parity modernization spending on coated papers and newsprint. Freeze it pending segment ROC.
3. Stop the air-fleet charter and maintenance "profit center" proposal.
4. Stop acquiring specialty, envelope and cigarette paper businesses. Sell the existing ones if they sit outside the Hedgehog.
5. Stop holding timberland as an identity. Keep only what consumer fiber supply needs. `[ASSUMPTION]` Market pulp can supply the rest.
6. Stop launching consumer products without a continuous improvement program behind them.
7. Stop measuring success by tons, mills or capacity.
