# Calder Business Machines: Good to Great

**Verdict: turnaround. The core is good, the company is not yet great, and profits are in crisis.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be the world's best at automating the money records of banks and the bookkeepers who serve business, sold as one system. Measure it by profit per financial-institution customer.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** profit is not feeding back into focused R&D. Margins fell from about 13% to about 2%.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:**
  1. Stop fighting Rival I across the whole general-purpose computer market.
  2. Stop acquiring for diversification's sake.
  3. Stop promising a recovery "once costs are written off" with no date attached.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** sales quadrupled while margins collapsed, and nobody can show which line makes money.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** profit by line, results for Year −3 to −1, R&D dollars by project, and bank-market share.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on Year −4 figures and a qualitative market picture.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calder is no longer a small general computer maker chasing Rival I. It is the indispensable records partner to banks and the bookkeeping trade, at home and abroad. A bank buys the whole chain from Calder: magnetic-ink checks and forms, check sorters, bookkeeping machines, and a large computer its clerks can program in near-plain business English without an expensive programming staff.

Each relationship produces rental income for years and pulls through supplies. That profit funds R&D aimed only at the next records problem those customers face. Margins are back toward their historical level. The international division sells the same system to foreign banks from plants already inside trade barriers. If Calder vanished, banks would lose the vendor that best understands how money gets recorded.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Deep bank & bookkeeper relationships (75 yrs, intl sales org)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    2. Understand their records problems first-hand
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    3. Build systems that remove clerical & programming labor
   │       (magnetic ink, sorters, bank bookkeeping machines, easy-to-program computer)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    4. Banks automate end-to-end with Calder
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    5. Recurring rentals + checks/ink/forms
   │       = PROFIT PER FI CUSTOMER  ◄── denominator
   │            │
   │            ▼   ⚠ WEAKEST LINK (2% margin; R&D spread across whole computer market)
   │    6. Profit funds R&D focused only on FI/records problems
   │            │
   └────────────┘ (better systems deepen and widen relationships)
```

**Current push, the next two turns:**
1. **Fix link 5→6.** Build line-level profitability and redirect computer R&D to financial and business-records applications. Until profit flows into focused R&D, the loop leaks.
2. **Push link 3→4.** Sell the integrated chain (checks, sorters, bookkeeping machines and computer) as a single account to the top banks, starting where Calder is already an early entrant.

**Doom-loop check.** The warning signs are present:
- **Acquisitions to jump-start momentum:** a computer maker 8 years ago and forms printers over the past decade.
- **New direction after new direction:** diversifying away from adding machines under two successive CEOs.
- **Grasping:** a computer partnership was negotiated and then lapsed without a board decision.
- **Reaction without understanding:** sales growth celebrated while margins collapsed.

The loop closes logically. It is not yet proven to close financially.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop competing across the whole general-purpose computer market against Rival I.** Do not bid outside financial and business records unless the deal clearly feeds the flywheel.
2. **Stop acquiring for diversification's sake.** Make no new lines without a direct link to FI and bookkeeping customers.
3. **Stop promising a recovery "once R&D costs are written off."** Replace it with dated margin targets by line.
4. **Stop funding R&D projects with no named FI or records customer problem.**
5. **Stop treating adding machines as a strategic franchise.** Harvest them.
6. **Stop selling separately by product line to the same bank.** Use one account and one system.
7. **Stop letting major decisions lapse at board level.** Every major proposal gets a yes or no within a set window.
8. **Stop letting defense work pull top engineers** from the bank and records program.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every investment is judged against profit per FI customer.
- Line P&L is reviewed monthly.
- The red-flag review comes before the good-news review.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Freedom:** line heads choose how they win banks and bookkeepers.
- **Framework:** they may not choose who the customer is.

**Bureaucracy check:** if layers of approval exist to manage the EVP's impatience with less-able people, the fix is better people in the seats, not more rules.
