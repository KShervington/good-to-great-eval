# Calder Business Machines: Good to Great run (autonomous)

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Calder can be best in the world at automating the money records of banks, from the ink on the check to the ledger computer, and it should measure itself by profit per financial-institution customer.

**Weakest flywheel link:** profit per bank customer funding focused R&D. Margins are about 2%, and nobody can yet see which line earns money.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop fighting Rival I across the whole general-purpose computer market.
2. Stop treating diversification as a goal in itself, including acquisitions bought for breadth.
3. Stop open-ended R&D that carries no dated profit milestones.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** net margin fell from about 13% to about 2% while sales quadrupled. Management's claim that "it recovers once costs are written off" has no timeline and no line-by-line proof.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit by line, sales and earnings for Year −3 to −1, and R&D dollars split by line.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on the dossier alone. Line-level profit could overturn the Hedgehog Concept.

Greatness potential: 22/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calder stops trying to be a smaller Rival I. It becomes the company banks and other financial institutions cannot run without. Its magnetic inks, check sorters, printed checks and forms, bookkeeping machines and easy-to-program computers form one integrated system, sold through relationships that already exist and serviced worldwide through its international organization. Every line answers one question: does this raise profit per financial-institution customer? General-purpose computing, military work and legacy adding machines are judged against that question, not against how much revenue they add. Margins rebuild because the company concentrates its spending, not because R&D costs are eventually "written off". The business is run through line-level numbers and a management bench strong enough to outlast both of its current leaders.

| **Passion** | 75 years of serving accountants and bookkeepers, plus an engineering culture that built a computer meant to be simpler to use. | **Making record-keeping accurate, fast and cheap for the people who keep the money.** `[ASSUMPTION]` The dossier shows the history but not the current staff's sentiment. |

**Hedgehog sentence:** *We automate the money records of financial institutions better than anyone, and we grow by earning more per institution served.*

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Partly. The franchise was built on bookkeepers, and banking is the fast-growing line where Calder entered early.
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It rules out the head-on computer war, diversification for its own sake, and new investment in adding machines.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It demotes the flagship large computer from "rival to Rival I" to "the bank's back-office engine".

**Confirmation evidence:**
1. Line P&L shows the banking and forms lines earn materially above the corporate 2%.
2. Profit per bank customer rises as more products are attached.
3. The large computer's ease of programming wins bank deals specifically.

**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL. This is a working hypothesis until line profit data exists.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► Long-standing bank & bookkeeping relationships
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │   Integrated systems installed (inks → sorters → ledger machines → computer)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │   Recurring rentals + consumables (checks, forms, ink) + service
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │   ↑ PROFIT PER FINANCIAL-INSTITUTION CUSTOMER
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │   Focused R&D on bank-specific systems (no broad computer war)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │   Simpler, cheaper systems that cut the bank's costs & programming staff
   │              │
   └──────────────┘ (more banks, deeper in each bank, at home and abroad)
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Measure profit per bank customer and the profit of each line. Without that, the loop cannot be steered.
2. Redirect computer R&D and sales effort toward bank and financial-institution applications built on the existing large machine's plain-English programming.

**Doom-loop check (active signs present):**
- **Acquisitions to jump-start momentum:** a computer maker, then forms and check printers. Some of these fit the Hedgehog Concept, but they were bought for breadth.
- **Repeated new direction:** the move from adding machines to "diversified business equipment" ran under two consecutive CEOs.
- **Reaction without understanding:** costs are blamed on R&D without a timeline or a line-level breakdown.
- **Inconsistency:** the board left a negotiated computer partnership to lapse.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Competing across the whole general-purpose computer market** against Rival I's scale and its air-defense lead.
2. **Diversifying as a strategy.** Any acquisition or new line must raise profit per financial-institution customer.
3. **Funding R&D without dated profit milestones.** "It will recover once written off" stops being an acceptable answer.
4. **Investing in mechanical adding machines.** Harvest the line for cash and service the installed base.
5. **Managing from consolidated numbers only.** No business line runs without its own P&L.
6. **Letting military work set the computer roadmap.** Keep it if it is profitable, and keep it separate.
7. **Settling big strategic questions by default,** as happened when the partnership lapsed. Decisions get a deadline and an owner.
