# Larkspur Financial: Good to Great

**Verdict: good, close to great, but the main gauge of its engine has never been checked.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be the primary financial home for American households and small businesses, and earn more profit than anyone per *actively used* relationship, through locally led stores.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** "products sold → deeper relationships." Cross-sell (5.92) counts products held, not products used.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) daily product-count quotas; (2) incentive pay tied to products sold; (3) running risk and HR inside the business lines.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** a regulator found that quotas and incentive pay allegedly drove misconduct in a Larkspur subsidiary. The same tools run the 9,000-store core bank.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the funded/active rate of cross-sold accounts, terminations and hotline reports about sales integrity, and branch turnover.
- **Confidence:** moderate on strategy, low on whether the metric is sound. The verdict rests on the published results and one regulatory finding.

Greatness potential: 42/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is the bank most American households and small businesses actually *use* as their primary financial home. Their paychecks land there, they pay bills from it, and they borrow from it because the local banker solved a real need. Its economic engine is profit per actively used primary relationship, not products per household. Every number about the relationship is verified: an account counts only if the customer asked for it, funded it and uses it. Decentralization stays a strength because local leaders keep autonomy over *how* they serve customers. Risk, conduct and incentive design sit centrally, under the board, so a problem in one unit cannot stay hidden there. It outperforms because its deposits are cheap and its customers stay. It endures because its reputation for trust is real, and outsiders can confirm it.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Larkspur wins by being the bank American households and small businesses actually run their financial lives through, earning more per active relationship than any rival, through locally led stores.*

|---|---|---|---|

| Economic engine (denominator) | Profit per employee; products per household (cross-sell); profit per store; **profit per active primary household** | **Economic profit per active primary household** `[ASSUMPTION: definition to be set: active checking with recurring deposits plus at least one used product]` | n/a |
| Passion | Community banking ("heart of the culture"); the vision of meeting all of a customer's needs; local autonomy | **Serving the whole financial life of local customers, with local judgment** | Hitting a sales number |

**Why this denominator:** "products per household" can be pushed up without creating any value for customers. "Profit per *active* primary household" can only rise if customers actually use the bank. That makes it the right guard against the incentive failure the regulator has already found once.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The bank came through the crisis well, which fits cheap core deposits and a retail franchise with deep relationships. `[ASSUMPTION: deposit mix not in dossier]`
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It rules out chasing mortgage share, growth led by capital markets, and product-count quotas.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It demotes the company's signature metric and the "eight" goal.

**Confirmation evidence (working hypothesis):** within 12 months, active-household data that shows (a) where profit per active household is concentrated, and (b) that primary households are more profitable and stay longer than product-heavy but inactive households.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Locally led stores; bankers rewarded for solving real needs
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   2. Customers open products they asked for and use (consent-verified)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   3. More households make Larkspur their PRIMARY bank
 │      (direct deposit, active checking, bill pay)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   4. Low-cost core deposits + lower attrition
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   5. ▲ PROFIT PER ACTIVE PRIMARY HOUSEHOLD (denominator)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   6. Reinvest in banker quality, service and digital; trust and referrals
 └────────┘
```

**Weakest link:** the step from 1 to 2. Today the store incentive rewards *opening* products. The current flywheel can spin on paper (cross-sell rising every quarter) while step 3 stalls. Nobody can currently tell which of the two is happening.

**Current push, next two turns:**
1. Measure step 2 honestly: run a funded/active/consent audit on accounts opened in the last 24 months.
2. Re-point step 1: shift store incentives from products sold to active primary relationships and customer outcomes.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Present risk:** reacting without understanding. Integration savings are ending and rates are low. The obvious lever is to raise sales goals, which presses harder on the weakest link.
- **Present risk:** a new acquisition to jump-start growth after just finishing one. Avoid it.
- **Not present yet:** a series of new strategic directions. The cross-sell strategy has been consistent. That consistency is a strength only if the metric is sound.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Daily and monthly product-count sales goals in stores.** Replace them with relationship and customer-outcome goals.
2. **Incentive pay tied to number of products sold or to cross-sell.** Tie pay to active, funded, customer-confirmed relationships, with clawbacks.
3. **Running risk, compliance oversight and HR inside the business lines** without central authority and board reporting.
4. **Leading the investor story with cross-sell and the "eight" goal** before the metric has been audited for activity and consent.
5. **Treating the $85M consumer-finance finding as a closed, legacy subsidiary matter.** Run a root-cause review of the same mechanism across every line.
6. **Chasing mortgage-origination share** as an earnings lever during the low-rate period.
7. **Pursuing a large acquisition** to replace the expiring integration savings.
8. **Selling higher-cost or riskier products to customers who qualify for better ones.** Make suitability a hard rule, not a guideline.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** help households and small businesses succeed financially.

**Core values (preserve):**
1. The customer's real interest comes first, even over this quarter's goal.
2. People are the competitive advantage. Local judgment is trusted.
3. Ethics are measured, not assumed.
4. Act like owners: low cost, long horizon.
5. What gets reported is what is true.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** be the *primary* bank, actively used, for one in two U.S. households, and be ranked the most trusted large financial company in America.

**Clock-building mechanisms:** a central CRO and conduct function reporting to the board; incentive plans that need central HR and risk sign-off, with clawbacks; customer-confirmed account opening; board-visible dashboards of hotline reports and terminations by region; a CEO succession plan with internal candidates; a lead independent director.
