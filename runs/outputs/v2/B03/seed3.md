# Larkspur Financial: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, and possibly great on paper, but the gauge that drives its engine has never been verified.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the primary financial home for American households and small businesses, and win on profit per *actively used* primary relationship rather than products sold.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** "More products sold → deeper relationship." Quotas can produce products that customers never asked for and never use.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) daily product-sales quotas tied to pay; (2) counting unfunded or inactive accounts in cross-sell; (3) letting business lines police their own sales conduct.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** A regulator has just found that incentive pay and sales quotas allegedly fostered misconduct in Larkspur's own subsidiary. The same mechanism runs 9,000+ stores.
- **Numbers that would change this:** the share of cross-sold accounts that are funded or active; hotline reports and terminations tied to sales practices; branch turnover.
- **Confidence:** Moderate on the diagnosis, low on its severity. It rests only on public results and regulatory findings.

Greatness potential: 45/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is the bank most American households and small businesses *choose* as their primary financial home. They keep their main checking account, direct deposit and everyday spending there because the advice was right, sometimes advice to buy nothing. Its cross-sell number is lower than today's headline figure, but every product in it is funded, used and wanted, and that honest number is what produces the industry's highest profit per relationship and its cheapest, stickiest deposits. Store team members are paid to be trusted, not to hit daily product counts. A central, independent risk and conduct function can see every business line. The board sees bad news early because mechanisms deliver it, not because someone chose to escalate it. The franchise that carried Larkspur through the crisis better than its rivals stays great after this CEO, after the integration savings run out, and through a long period of low rates.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Larkspur is the primary financial home for American households and small businesses, and it earns more profit per actively used primary relationship than any rival because customers freely bring it more of their real financial lives.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Largest mortgage originator; largest CRE lender; highest cross-sell; densest U.S. store network; deposit-funded, conservative relationship banking | **Being the everyday, primary bank for U.S. households and small businesses**, backed by its store density and its crisis-tested conservative balance sheet |
| **Cannot be best at** | Global investment banking, trading, capital-markets scale; market-share leadership in mortgage origination as an end in itself | Not part of the Hedgehog. These are support or parity businesses. |
| **Engine denominator** | Products per household (current); profit per customer; profit per store; deposits per household | **Profit per actively used primary-relationship household** `[ASSUMPTION: this metric is not reported today; it must be built]` |
| **Passion** | "Meeting all of a customer's financial needs" (vision and values); the community-banking culture; decentralization and ownership | **Helping customers succeed financially**, the authentic center of the Community Banking culture |

**Why the denominator changes:** "Products per household" counts *activity by Larkspur*. "Profit per actively used primary relationship" counts *choices by the customer*. Only the second compounds without inviting gaming. Products that nobody uses add cost, regulatory risk and complaints, not profit.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. Profit per primary household explains earnings across regions better than cross-sell does.
2. Households with fewer but fully used products are more profitable and more loyal than households with more, partly dormant products.
3. The concept rules things out: it rejects at least one attractive growth move (see Stop-doing) that cross-sell would have accepted.
4. It is uncomfortable to commit to. Reported cross-sell will probably fall once inactive products are removed, and that will have to be explained to investors, including the famous one.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   ▼                                                      │
[1] Needs-based advice in stores & digital                │
   (right product, or none)                               │
   │                                                      │
   ▼   ◄── WEAKEST LINK today: quota-driven selling       │
[2] Customers make Larkspur their PRIMARY bank            │
   (main checking, direct deposit, everyday use)          │
   │                                                      │
   ▼                                                      │
[3] Low-cost, stable core deposits + real product usage   │
   │                                                      │
   ▼                                                      │
[4] Higher PROFIT PER ACTIVE PRIMARY RELATIONSHIP         │
   (the denominator)                                      │
   │                                                      │
   ▼                                                      │
[5] Reinvest: store pay for service & tenure, digital,    │
    pricing, independent controls                         │
   │                                                      │
   ▼                                                      │
[6] Experienced, low-turnover store teams + earned trust ─┘
   (referrals, reputation → more customers at step 1)
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Fix the gauge:** measure active or funded cross-sell next to reported cross-sell, and expose the gap.
2. **Fix the push on link 1→2:** remove daily product quotas from pay and reward funded, used relationships, service scores and clean conduct.

**Doom-loop check:** Larkspur does *not* show the classic doom loop. Its strategy has been consistent for decades, and the crisis acquisition was integrated to completion. The risk is a quieter variant: **fanatical consistency on a component that may be partly counterfeit.** A flywheel pushed hard on "products sold" can spin faster on paper while trust, the real driver, erodes. The trigger to watch is the end of integration savings plus low rates. Together they will create pressure to push cross-sell harder exactly when it most needs checking.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop tying store incentive pay to products sold.** Pay for funded, used relationships, customer satisfaction and clean conduct, with clawbacks.
2. **Stop setting daily product-sales goals for store staff.** Replace them with relationship and service goals reviewed over longer periods.
3. **Stop counting unfunded or inactive products in cross-sell**, internally and externally. Report active cross-sell.
4. **Stop letting business lines police their own sales conduct, risk and HR.** Centralize those oversight functions.
5. **Stop treating "eight" as a number to hit.** Hold it as an aspiration that only customers can deliver.
6. **Stop chasing mortgage origination rank for its own sake.** Mortgage serves the primary relationship. Volume leadership is not the goal, and the consent orders and the reverse-redlining suit show the cost of volume without control.
7. **Stop looking for a large acquisition or a capital-markets push to replace integration savings.** Earnings growth must come from the core flywheel or be accepted as slower.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** Help customers succeed financially.

**Core values (to preserve):**
1. What's right for the customer comes before what's sold to the customer.
2. People are the competitive advantage: hire for character, keep them, and pay them to be trusted.
3. Ethics and integrity are not traded for a number.
4. Ownership and accountability close to the customer, inside an enterprise-wide framework.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Be the primary bank, chosen and actively used, for one in three American households, with the highest customer-trust scores among large U.S. banks.* `[ASSUMPTION: Larkspur "serves" one in three households today, but its primary-bank share is unknown and is likely much lower]`

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A central CRO and conduct function reporting to the board risk committee.
- A compensation design standard: every incentive plan is tested for gaming risk before launch and reviewed annually by central HR and risk.
- Enterprise ethics-hotline analytics with quarterly board reporting on sales-practice themes.
- Customer-outcome audits that randomly sample new accounts for consent and usage.
- Separation of chair and CEO, or a strong lead independent director.
- Named, developed successors for CEO and Community Banking.
