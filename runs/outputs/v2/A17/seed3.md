# Stanwick Mailing Systems: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Stanwick can be the best in the world at business mail. That means the meters, mailing machines, finance and service that get a company's mail out the door, sold through the field-force relationship that is already inside each customer's office. Success is judged by **profit per customer**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning service visits into more mail-related revenue per customer. The idea is plausible but has never been measured.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Retail electronics and point-of-sale ventures.
2. Price fights with the copier giant in its home market.
3. Treating a shrinking meter share of sales as progress.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Stanwick is deciding where to grow without profit or return figures by business line or by customer. It made the same kind of decision this year and lost $42M.

**Numbers that would change the answer:** profit and return on capital by line, customer count and profit per customer, and copier economics.

**Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on market share, recurring leases and the write-off, not on profitability.

Greatness potential: 55/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick owns the business mailroom. Postage meters are the anchor and do not need apologizing for. Around them sit the mail-handling equipment, the lease finance and the service visits that make Stanwick the obvious partner for any business that sends mail. The field force is treated as the company's most valuable asset, not as a cost center. Each visit deepens a relationship whose profit is tracked customer by customer. Electronics goes into the meter and the mailroom, where Stanwick can lead, rather than into cash registers or general copying, where others lead. The company runs the same way under the new CEO and his successors because its disciplines are written down, measured and enforced. It does not depend on the memory of a 54-year chairman.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| **Deeply passionate about** | meters and the mail; office technology broadly; growth itself | **The mail.** `[ASSUMPTION]` A 50-year meter heritage and a field force built around it suggest the pride sits there. **Tests of the intersection**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Revenue grew from $57M to $385M on the meter franchise, and share held without patent protection.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: point-of-sale systems, head-on copier competition, and retail systems built on price-marking.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It means telling the market that the "mature, regulated" meter business is the future, not the past.

**Confirmation evidence (status: working hypothesis)**
1. Per-customer profit data shows that meter customers who also lease other mail equipment are worth more, after service cost, than meter-only customers.
2. The core earns a return on capital above the non-mail lines.
3. Field-force capacity can absorb added mailroom products without service quality falling.

If test 1 or 2 fails, the denominator is wrong. Loop back to Gate 5.

**Does the loop close?** Yes. Each step causes the next, and step 6 feeds step 1.

**Weakest link:** step 3 → 4. Nobody has shown that add-ons raise per-customer profit, because per-customer profit is not measured today.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Measure profit per customer so that step 4 can be seen.
2. Run a controlled pilot in one region to test add-on mail equipment through the field force `[ASSUMPTION: region and product set to be chosen]`.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Present:** a jump-start venture (the point-of-sale joint venture, now written off). Direction-seeking through diversification (meters were down to 53% of sales five years ago). A live internal fight over strategic direction at the moment of a CEO handover.
- **Not yet present:** serial reversals. The company has had one stable steward for decades.
- **Verdict:** early doom-loop signals, which the stop-doing list addresses.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **No more retail electronics or point-of-sale ventures**, including "one more try" with a different partner.
2. **Stop the price war with the copier giant in general office copying.** Copiers stay only where data shows that field-serviced placements with existing customers earn above the company's return. Otherwise, exit.
3. **Stop treating diversification as a goal.** Do not count a falling meter share of revenue as progress.
4. **Stop using price-marking as a beachhead into retail systems.** Run it for returns, as a separate business.
5. **Stop approving investments without a per-customer return case.** Every proposal must show how it raises profit per customer.
6. **Stop lending for anything other than Stanwick hedgehog equipment** through the credit subsidiary.
7. **Stop cross-selling in ways that lean on meter dominance.** Add-ons must win on their merits. Tying them to a near-monopoly relationship invites antitrust trouble the company has already had once `[ASSUMPTION about legal exposure; confirm with counsel]`.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** keep business mail moving accurately, securely and cheaply.

**Core values `[ASSUMPTION: derived, to be validated with employees]`:**
1. Integrity with the postal system: we handle public revenue, and that trust is non-negotiable.
2. The customer's mailroom works because we showed up.
3. Engineering precision.
4. Win on merit, not on dominance.

**BHAG (horizon of about 20 years):** make metering, or its electronic successor, the way most U.S. business mail is paid for, and make Stanwick the mailing partner of most American businesses that mail regularly. For reference, metered mail was 43% of U.S. postage thirteen years ago; the current figure is unknown. `[ASSUMPTION on target level]`

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Profit per customer as the official measure in every operating review.
- An investment gate with three questions:
  - Does it serve business mail?
  - Can we be best at it?
  - Does it raise profit per customer?
- A written stop-doing list reviewed yearly by the board.
- A named successor pipeline two levels deep.
- A postal-regulatory early-warning function.
