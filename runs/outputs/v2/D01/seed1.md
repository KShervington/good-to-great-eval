# Tallypaw: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Tallypaw is good, not yet great, and the next five months are decisive.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Tallypaw is the supplier-neutral inventory brain of the independent vet clinic. It grows by earning more gross profit per clinic every year that clinic stays.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** acquisition. New clinics still come mainly from Maya's demos or from VetSource, not from a repeatable engine Tallypaw owns.
- **Stop doing:** (1) the dental pilot, (2) corporate-chain pilots, (3) Maya running most demos.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** VetSource drives 38% of ARR. It renews in five months, is building its own inventory intelligence, and hasn't answered two emails. The board filed this under "housekeeping."
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** VetSource contract terms (who owns the customer, billing and data, and what happens at exit), CAC and churn by channel, and the size of the reachable independent-clinic market.
- **Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitudes. It rests on the dossier alone.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tallypaw is the system independent vet clinics run their pharmacy and supply room on. It knows what to reorder, from which supplier, at what price, and before what expiry date, across every distributor a clinic uses. Independent clinics get purchasing intelligence that only chains used to have. Practice managers recommend it to each other unprompted, and it is the last software a clinic would cancel. New clinics come from a sales and referral engine staffed by people from the vet world, not from the CEO's calendar or one distributor's goodwill. Gross profit per clinic rises every year. No single partner can switch the company off. Its purpose is simple and lasting: **help independent clinics stay independent.**
