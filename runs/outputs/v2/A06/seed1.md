# Marlow Discount Centers: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Marlow wins when it is the lowest-price, best-known big-ticket electronics and appliance store in metros where it has advertising and buying density. It grows outward one adjacent metro at a time, paid for by profit per metro.

**Weakest flywheel link:** density leading to cheaper advertising per store. The current blitz-entry strategy skips this step.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Blitz-entering distant metros where nobody knows the name.
2. Competing on price alone in markets where Marlow has no scale advantage.
3. Counting store openings as progress.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival T is building superstores and buying its way into the largest city. Marlow has no stated format or cost advantage to answer it.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit by metro, same-store sales, ad spend per store, and the parent's long-term intent.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on the dossier's strategy facts. It has no current financials.

Greatness potential: 9/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Marlow is the price-and-selection leader for electronics, audio and major appliances across a tight cluster of metros, starting in its three-state home region. Every store sits in a market where the Marlow name is already worth something. Advertising is spread over many stores in each market. Buying volume is concentrated. Each new metro is next door to an existing one and is entered only after the current ones clear a profit-per-metro bar. It keeps the founder's original skill of making big-ticket purchases affordable to ordinary households, possibly including financing [ASSUMPTION]. It runs on a professional retail operating team, so it does not depend on one family member or on a parent that does not do retail.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   More stores in one metro (density)
              │
              ▼
   Lower ad cost per store + name recognition
              │
              ▼
   More traffic per store ──► Higher volume per metro
                                    │
                                    ▼
                        Better buying terms / lower costs
                                    │
                                    ▼
                       Lower prices + wider selection
                                    │
                                    ▼
              Higher PROFIT PER METRO ──► funds next store
              in the same metro, or an ADJACENT metro ──┐
                                                         │
              └──────────────── back to density ◄────────┘
```

**Current push:**
1. Build a profit-per-metro P&L with advertising fully allocated.
2. Redirect new-store capital into deepening the home region rather than opening distant metros.

**Doom-loop check (warning signs present):**
- **Strategy reversal after the founder died.** The founder entered markets by acquisition. The new approach is greenfield entry.
- **Big programs to force momentum.** These are the multi-store blitzes and heavy advertising.
- **Ownership change**, with growth capital coming from an owner outside retail.
- **The blitz buys name recognition with advertising** rather than with acquisitions. Nobody has shown which is cheaper.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop blitz-entering metros with no name recognition and no adjacency to existing ones.
2. Stop competing on price alone in markets where Marlow lacks volume to support it.
3. Stop measuring progress by store openings. Measure profit per metro.
4. Stop treating parent capital as free. Charge every store a cost of capital.
5. Stop advertising in markets below a density threshold. Consolidate or exit those markets instead.
6. Stop letting the parent's growth appetite set opening pace.
7. Stop re-deciding entry method by doctrine. Use the evidence on cost per point of awareness, comparing acquisition with advertising.
