# Ranvik: Good to Great

**Verdict: Ranvik is good but not yet great, and the January input-financing proposal is the wrong next push.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** "In counties where we're dense, Ranvik is the fastest, safest way to get a stranger's high-value machine into your field when the window opens."
- **Weakest flywheel link:** after the first match, the relationship leaves the platform. Only 11% of renters rebook the same owner on Ranvik, and 71% swapped phone numbers.
- **Top stop-doing:** (1) input financing in January; (2) two new states next spring; (3) counting off-platform friendships as wins.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** financing would occupy both engineers for about 5 months through planting. It is built on a $28M plan that a $6M facility can't fund in year one. The company has about 18.7 months of runway.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** same-owner repeat split by machine category; who carries damage-protection risk; the facility's recourse and first-loss terms.
- **Confidence:** moderate-low. Everything rests on one founder-written investor update.

Greatness potential: 16/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the reflex in every county it serves. When a planter breaks in a wet week, or a second combine would save the harvest, a farmer opens Ranvik and has a machine from someone they've never met in the field that afternoon. The booking is safe enough that a $2,500 rental of a very expensive machine between strangers feels routine. Ranvik makes its money on those high-stakes, time-critical first matches. It keeps the repeat business it deserves because protection, verified engine hours and clean payment are worth more than the fee. It gives up the small neighbor-to-neighbor swaps without regret. It grows county by county, and only once each county is dense. Nobody would describe it as a fintech, an input retailer or a social network.

## 3. The flywheel (Gate 8, CONDITIONAL)

```
  (1) Dense supply of high-value machines in a county
        │
        ▼
  (2) Fast confirmed match in the field window (low decline rate)
        │
        ▼
  (3) Safe stranger transaction: protection + engine-hour proof + payment
        │                                   ◄── WEAKEST LINK: deal leaves platform after first match
        ▼
  (4) Net revenue per high-value booking (+ protection margin)
        │
        ▼
  (5) Reinvest in the same counties: owner recruiting, trust tools
        │
        ▼
  (6) Owners earn more on idle iron → list more machines ──► back to (1)
```

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Make staying on-platform worth the fee for high-value repeat pairs.** That means one-tap rebook, protection and the engine-hour record available only on-platform, and a test of a lower fee on repeat pairings. Keep phone numbers unmasked: masking cut support tickets 38%, and fighting contact exchange is a losing battle.
2. **Add high-value supply in the 12 core counties before planting.** There were 1,140 declines, mostly date conflicts, which is about 17.7% of requests `[ASSUMPTION: requests ≈ bookings + declines]`. That is demand Ranvik already won and then dropped.

**Doom-loop check:** warning signs are present. Input financing, two new states and Ranvik Pro are all slated for the same spring, with 9 people and two engineers. The board is being asked to choose a new direction before the current one has been measured. The "fintech rounds of $40M+" argument is momentum borrowed from someone else's flywheel.

**Why CONDITIONAL:** the loop closes logically, but link (3) is leaking at an unknown rate. It becomes a PASS when on-platform repeat for high-value pairs is measured and rising.

## 5. Stop-doing list (Gate 6, PASS)

1. **Input financing in January.** Decline it for this year. Revisit only when all four conditions hold:
   - high-value on-platform repeat is at least 20%;
   - runway is at least 18 months after the next raise;
   - recourse and first-loss terms are reviewed;
   - the work is staffed without the current engineers.
2. **Launching two new states next spring.** The 12 core counties generate about $219k of GMV each, against about $43k in the other 29. Densify first.
3. **Treating off-platform friendships as a success metric** in investor updates or internal goals.
4. **Fighting for low-value repeat swaps between known neighbors.** No promos or features to win back a hay rake rented between friends.
5. **Ines staffing every barbecue and co-op meeting herself.** Keep the events, run them as owner-supply recruiting in the core counties, and hand them to the field reps.
6. **Blanket promotions that erode take rate** `[ASSUMPTION: promotions are broad, not targeted]`. Aim any discounts at high-value repeat pairings.
7. **Using competitors' funding rounds as a strategic signal.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every board deck shows same-owner repeat, decline rate and core-county density.
- No new product line until the core metrics hit their 12-month targets.
- Every protection claim gets a post-mortem.

**Framework:** Paul gates the roadmap; the field reps have autonomy on how they recruit supply within the core counties; Ines owns the hedgehog and the story.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rule currently exists to compensate for wrong people. Don't let a compliance apparatus arrive with a business line you don't need.
