# Ranvik: Good to Great

**Verdict: good, not yet great. The community Ranvik is proudest of is where its revenue leaks out.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Ranvik makes renting a high-value machine from a neighbor faster and safer than a handshake, on the first rental and every one after, built one dense county at a time.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** repeat rentals staying on Ranvik. Only 11% of renters rebooked the same owner in the app, while 71% exchanged phone numbers.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) launching input financing in January; (2) entering two new states next spring; (3) treating "a neighbor I can call" as a success metric.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** with about 18 months of cash, next season has to prove that pairings keep paying. Today they probably pay once.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the off-platform repeat rate; GMV per county outside the founding 12; whether the $512k of net revenue includes protection premiums.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on one investor letter, with no cohort-level or county-level data.

Greatness potential: 14/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the default place where a farmer finds a planter, combine or baler they don't own, in the week it matters. It also stays the easiest way to keep renting from that neighbor afterward. It does not try to replace the relationship. It makes the relationship's paperwork, payment, protection and hour logging easier than a phone call and a handshake, and it charges a fair price for that. It grows county by county. It moves into a new county only after the last one reaches density: most peak-week requests get filled, and most repeat pairings stay on the platform. It runs on field reps and a county playbook, not on Ines's personal reach. It does not lend money, sell inputs or chase national coverage. If it disappeared, farmers in its counties would lose their fastest route to idle iron nearby, along with the protection and records that make renting to a near-stranger feel safe. Today, farmers would mostly keep the phone numbers and carry on without it. That gap between the two pictures is the work.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

This is the uncomfortable part: the passion as currently framed celebrates the leak. |

**Denominator: net revenue per pairing over its lifetime.** It is the single number that shows whether Ranvik is a utility that gets paid on every rental or a matchmaker that gets paid once. Revenue per booking hides leakage. Revenue per county hides it too.
- Current proxies: about $96 net revenue per booking ($512k / 5,310) and about $212 per active renter ($512k / 2,416). True per-pairing value is unknown `[ASSUMPTION: requires cohort analysis Ranvik can run from its own data]`.

**Tests the concept passes:**
- *Explains past wins:* the "aha" moments are urgent, high-value first pairings.
- *Rules things out:* input financing, new states, generic subscriptions.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to:* it says no to the board member's proposal and to the founders' favorite story.

**Confirmation tests (by late February, then next harvest):**
1. When one-tap rebooking and repeat-pair pricing ship, the same-owner on-platform repeat rate rises well above 11%.
2. In a survey, the share of pairings that renewed off-platform falls.
3. Non-core counties that reach a supply threshold show fill rates and GMV per county approaching the founding counties.

If repeat pairings won't stay on the platform at any reasonable price, the hedgehog changes to **"paid matchmaker"**: a higher one-time match fee and no expectation of repeats. That is a smaller, different business. It should be decided on data, not on hope.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   ▼                                              │
1. Dense county supply of high-value machines     │
   (owners with offset planting/harvest windows)  │
   │                                              │
   ▼                                              │
2. Urgent requests filled fast                    │
   (peak-week fill rate; today ~82%)              │
   │                                              │
   ▼                                              │
3. A trusted first rental                         │
   (payment, damage protection, hour meters)      │
   │                                              │
   ▼                                              │
4. Repeat rentals stay on Ranvik  ◄── WEAKEST LINK│
   (rebook, repeat pricing, records, protection)  │
   = net revenue per pairing grows                │
   │                                              │
   ▼                                              │
5. Owners earn more per machine and list more;    │
   neighbors in the county hear about it ─────────┘
```

**Does the loop close?** Only if link 4 holds. Today, link 3 produces a phone-number exchange, and link 4 mostly happens off-platform. The visible result is the Ostrander–Kemp shared baler. The loop is generating goodwill rather than revenue.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Link 4:** make the second rental with the same neighbor easier and cheaper on Ranvik than off it. Do this before planting.
2. **Link 1:** concentrate field reps on supply density in the best 8–10 of the 29 non-core counties. Recruit owners whose windows are offset from local demand, for example different crops or staggered planting.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs present.**
- Three new directions are proposed for one year: financing, two new states, and Ranvik Pro.
- One "big program" is pitched as a revenue jump-start: about $1.1M of modeled financing revenue against $512k of core revenue.
- There is hype pressure: two ag-fintech companies raised $40M+.
- There is reaction without understanding: the leakage that masking was guarding against has not been measured since masking was removed.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the January input-financing launch.** Defer it, with explicit re-entry conditions (see the Risk Register).
2. **Stop the two-state expansion planned for next spring.** Densify the existing 29 counties first.
3. **Stop reporting phone-number exchange and "neighbor I can call" stories as success.** Report on-platform repeat rate and net revenue per pairing instead.
4. **Stop running community events in the already-dense home counties.** Hold events only in thin counties, built around listing sign-ups and rebooking setup.
5. **Stop using survey intent as demand evidence.** "52% would likely use" covers about 200 people out of 388 respondents. It is not a forecast.
6. **Stop blanket promotions.** Spend them only where they buy peak-week supply or keep a repeat pair on-platform `[ASSUMPTION: promotions currently reduce take; size unknown]`.
7. **Stop building Ranvik Pro as a general subscription.** It exists only as repeat-renter tooling for fleet owners.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A weekly leakage and fill-rate dashboard.
- No new county until the current target counties hit their density thresholds.
- Every new product line answers three questions in writing: Does it raise net revenue per pairing? Does it make us better at the trusted rental? Does it use engineers needed for link 4?

**Framework boundaries:**
- Field reps are free to choose tactics within their assigned counties, but they are measured on fill rate and on-platform repeat.
- Engineers decide how to build repeat tooling, but the roadmap stays inside the hedgehog.
- The board advises on new lines, but adoption requires the hedgehog test and Paul's risk memo.

**Bureaucracy check:** nothing excessive is visible. With 9 people, the risk is the opposite: too little measurement.
