# Ranvik: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great.** The growth is real. But part of the "community" the letter celebrates is business leaving the platform, and the January lending proposal steps outside what Ranvik can be best at.

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Ranvik makes the first high-stakes rental between farmers who don't yet know each other safe and fast in the week it matters, one dense county at a time.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** repeat business staying on Ranvik. 71% of renters swapped phone numbers, but only 11% rebooked the same owner through the app.
- **Top three stop-doing:** (1) launching input financing in January; (2) opening two new states next spring; (3) counting number swaps and "a neighbor I can call" as success.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the lending plan assumes $28M of loans in year one. The bank has committed $6M. The build takes both engineers for about 5 months, and Ranvik has about 19 months of runway.
- **Numbers that would change this:** the off-platform repeat rate among pairs Ranvik introduced, economics by county cohort, and who carries the damage-protection risk.
- **Confidence:** moderate-low. It rests on one investor letter.

`Greatness potential: 17/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the default way a farmer in its counties gets a planter, combine or baler they don't own, in the narrow window when it matters. It is especially the default when the owner is a stranger. Ranvik fills about 9 in 10 peak requests within hauling distance. It wins those requests by being safer and easier than a phone call: guaranteed payment, damage protection, hour-meter billing and condition records. That is why repeat rentals between people who already know each other stay on Ranvik instead of drifting to cash and handshakes. It grows by making each county dense before adding the next. It measures itself on net revenue per county, not on GMV headlines. Its county playbook runs without Ines at every barbecue. It turns down adjacent businesses like input lending until the core engine pays for itself.

**Evidence that it explains past wins:**

**What it rules out:** input lending, new states before existing counties are dense, Ranvik Pro as a separate revenue line, and optimizing for low-value repeat rentals.

2. On-platform repeat among high-value pairs rises measurably after repeat-pair pricing and post-booking number reveal.
3. Peak fill rate reaches 90% or more.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Find and plug the leak at link 4.** Measure how often pairs Ranvik introduced transact again off-platform. Test lower fees for repeat pairs, make damage protection available only on-platform, and reveal phone numbers only after a booking is confirmed. Keep phone calls at the field edge; that problem was real.
2. **Close the gap at link 2 before planting.** There were 1,140 declines, mostly date conflicts: 17.7% of requests. When an owner declines, offer the renter the nearest available alternative automatically. At the $727 average booking, that is up to about $0.83M of GMV (upper bound, derived). Pair this with field reps signing up planter and combine owners in the non-home counties.

**Doom-loop check:** Ranvik is at the edge. Three new directions are proposed at once for a 9-person team: lending, two states, and Pro. Part of the justification is that "two ag-fintechs raised $40M+," which is hype-driven reasoning. A large program is being used to manufacture a bigger story ahead of the next raise `[ASSUMPTION about motive]`. It is not in a doom loop yet, because the core is still compounding.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the January input-financing launch.** Defer it. Revisit only once three things hold: (a) net revenue per county doubles outside the home cluster, (b) runway exceeds 24 months after a raise, and (c) a version exists that needs no more than 1 engineer-month.
2. **Stop the two-state launch next spring.** The 29 existing non-home counties run at about 1/5 the density of the home cluster.
3. **Stop treating number swaps and "a neighbor I can call" as success metrics** until off-platform repeat is measured.
4. **Stop Ines personally running all six barbecues.** Field reps run them, and each one doubles as a supply sign-up and leakage survey.
5. **Stop Ranvik Pro as a separate revenue line.** Fold large-owner pricing into the repeat-pair and volume pricing test, where it works against leakage instead of adding fees.
6. **Stop investor updates that lead with GMV.** Lead with net revenue per county, fill rate and runway.
7. **Stop spending acquisition money on low-value categories,** where going direct is natural and fees per booking are about $73 (derived).

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every declined request gets an alternative offer.
- Density by county is reviewed weekly during the season.
- Every damage claim gets a blameless autopsy.
- Nothing gets engineering time unless it moves fill rate or on-platform repeat.

**Framework boundaries:**
- Field reps choose freely *which* owners and co-ops to pursue within their assigned counties.
- No one opens new geography or a new business line without the board-level criteria above.

**Bureaucracy check:** no compensating rules found. The team is small. The risk here runs the other way: too few mechanisms, too much reliance on the founders.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** help farmers get the right machine at the right moment from the people around them.
- **Core values:**
  - Neighbors first.
  - Trust is earned on every rental.
  - Practical at the field edge.
  - Honest numbers, especially the bad ones.
- **BHAG (15-year horizon):** every farm in Ranvik's region is within a short haul of the machine it needs in its critical week, and Ranvik is how it gets there.
- **Mechanisms that keep this working without the founders:**
  - A written county launch playbook.
  - A dashboard tracking fill rate, leakage and density by county.
  - Claims autopsies.
  - A new-business-line rubric: "Does it raise net revenue per county?"
  - The board bad-news page.
