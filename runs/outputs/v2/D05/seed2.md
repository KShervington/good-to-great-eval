# Good to Great: Tessel

## Verdict

**The run stopped at Gate 1. Tessel is *not yet good*, so it gets no Hedgehog Concept or flywheel yet.** Its core does not yet work without the founder's network and manual effort. Most early users came from the founder's own circles. Only 7 of 41 care circles are active weekly, and one of those is the founder's own family. The most-wanted feature, refill sync, works at one pharmacy because the founder loads a spreadsheet by hand every Monday.

**Hold until "good" is proven:** (1) Android, (2) the employer features (SSO, dashboard, staffed concierge), (3) a hire.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the founder names pharmacy access as the biggest risk. The bigger unknown is whether strangers keep using Tessel at all.

**Missing numbers:** weekly-active circles by origin (network vs. stranger), retention with vs. without refill sync, and who the 3 payers are.

**Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on the founder's own figures.

Greatness potential: 8/100

### Draft definition of great (product / app vocabulary)
1. **Superior performance:** families using Tessel lapse on refills and miss doses measurably less than families coordinating by group chat.
2. **Distinctive impact:** discharge planners and primary caregivers would feel a real hole if it vanished. The social worker's card is the first hint of this.
3. **Lasting endurance:** it works without the founder hand-loading data, without one friendly pharmacist, and without the founder's personal network.

### Candidate scorecard outputs (for the next run)
- **Weekly active circles from outside the founder's network** (current: unknown, ≤6)
- **Refills tracked automatically, with zero founder touch** (current: 0. All refill data is loaded by hand.)
- **Paying circles from strangers** (current: ≤3, origin unknown)

### Why `FAIL` rather than `CONDITIONAL`
The skill describes "not yet good" with three markers. Tessel shows all three:
- **Most users are friends.** The founder says the first users came from the support group and old colleagues, who were "incredibly generous."
- **Retention is unproven outside that circle.** There are 7 weekly circles, including the founder's family, and nothing is split out by origin.
- **The key feature is hand-operated.** Refill sync exists because one pharmacist emails a file and the founder loads it every Monday.

Tessel has never been good and then declined, so it doesn't qualify for the turnaround path. Its core has not yet worked on its own. Running Gates 2–10 now would mean designing a flywheel on top of a product nobody has proven sticks, and that would be building on sand.

This failure is a statement about the product's stage. It says nothing negative about the founder or the problem. The problem is real, the founder-problem fit is unusually strong, and there is real pull (the newsletter, the social worker, the interview quotes). What's missing is proof that the **app** holds strangers without hand-holding.

## What "good" would look like

Tessel is good when **a family who has never met the founder sets up a circle, keeps using it every week for months, and pays, and no part of that requires the founder to do anything by hand.**

### Three milestones that would prove it
All thresholds are `[ASSUMPTION]`, set as reasonable pre-seed bars. Adjust them if better benchmarks exist.

1. **Stranger retention.** At least 20 weekly active circles where no member knew the founder beforehand, with week-8 retention of at least 40% for circles that complete setup.
2. **Stranger payment.** At least 25 paying circles from non-network sources, with monthly churn measured over at least 3 months.
3. **No hand-operation.** One of two outcomes must hold, and the result is useful either way:
   - (a) Refill status flows in with zero founder action, through the aggregator or an automated feed from the existing pharmacy.
   - (b) Retention data shows circles without refill sync stick just as well. Then the reminder-and-log core is the product, and refill sync is an accelerator.

When all three hold, re-run from Gate 1. Gate 5 will then have real evidence to work from.

## The direction question on the table: the employer channel

The skill's process is to resolve the Gate 1 issue before anything else, so this section isn't a full Hedgehog analysis. The decision is live this week, though, so here is the honest read.

- **It doesn't fix "not yet good."** Selling to employers changes who pays. It doesn't prove that families keep using the app.
- **The pilot's requirements would consume the founder.** An admin dashboard, SSO and anonymized reporting are months of B2B infrastructure for one self-taught developer `[ASSUMPTION]`. A staffed "care concierge" turns a software product into a services business with headcount.
- **It cuts against the founder's best insight.** "Caregivers don't want another app; they want fewer phone calls." A concierge chat adds another channel to answer.
- **The economics aren't verified.** Graham's figures ($3–5 per employee per month, about 1 in 5 employees caregiving) come from an advisor with a channel interest and haven't been checked. `[ASSUMPTION: plausible but unconfirmed. Research couldn't be done this session.]`
- **Recommendation:** decline building for the pilot now. Keep the HR head and Graham warm. Bring the employer question back as a Gate 5 candidate once Milestone 1 is met. At that point, employer distribution could be a legitimate accelerant for a product that already works.
