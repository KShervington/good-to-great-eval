# Good to Great: Tessel

## Verdict

**Tessel is not yet good, so the run stops at Gate 1.** That is not a judgment on its future. It means the core doesn't yet work without you.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** none yet. Building one now would rest on 7 active families.
- **Weakest link:** families stop using it. Only 7 of 41 care circles are active weekly, and one of them is your own.
- **Not yet:** (1) building single sign-on (SSO), an admin dashboard and a staffed concierge for an employer pilot; (2) a full native Android port before you know why circles stall; (3) a hire.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the risk isn't pharmacy access. It's that families outside your circle don't stay. The feature they say they'd pay for, refill sync, is hand-loaded from one pharmacy's Monday email.
- **Numbers that would change this:** retention by acquisition channel; how many of the 7 active circles and 3 payers come from outside your network; newsletter-to-circle conversion; sign-ups from the discharge card.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high on "not yet good." It rests on the application's own numbers.

Greatness potential: 8/100

### Why this is "not yet good" and not "good, not yet great"

The skill defines "not yet good" as a core that doesn't work without the steward's personal effort, network or manual workarounds. The skill's own example is: most users are friends, retention is unproven outside that circle, and the key feature is hand-operated. Tessel matches all three:

1. **Network-sourced users.** The first users came from your Wednesday support group and former colleagues. The application doesn't say how many of the 7 active circles come from outside that circle.
2. **Unproven retention.** 41 circles were created and 7 are active weekly. Even counted generously, about 5 of every 6 circles have gone quiet.
3. **A hand-operated key feature.** Refill sync depends on one pharmacist's weekly email and on you loading it. The chain-pharmacy route has been "under review" since August.

This was never a core that worked on its own and then broke, so it isn't a turnaround either. This gate stays a `FAIL`, not `CONDITIONAL`. A Hedgehog Concept, flywheel or employer strategy stacked on 7 active circles would be built on sand.

### Draft definition of great (kept for the re-run)

1. **Superior performance:** families using Tessel miss measurably fewer refills and make fewer "who's handling this?" calls than families coordinating by group chat.
2. **Distinctive impact:** discharge teams and primary caregivers would feel a real hole if Tessel vanished, because nothing else gets the right reminder to the right family member at the right time.
3. **Lasting endurance:** it keeps working without you loading data, without one friendly pharmacist and without one social worker.

### Scorecard outputs (proposed)

1. **Weekly active circles from outside your network.** Current: unknown, at most 6.
2. **8-week circle retention by acquisition channel.** Current: unknown. 7/41 weekly active is only a rough proxy, not true cohort retention.
3. **Paying circles from outside your network still paying in month 2.** Current: unknown, at most 3.

## What "good" looks like, and the milestones that would prove it

**Good means** families who have never met you set up a circle, still use it two months later, and some of them pay. All of that happens without you loading data by hand.

Three milestones would justify re-running Gates 2–10. All thresholds are `[ASSUMPTION]`: they are reasonable pre-seed bars for this stage, not industry figures.

1. **Retention outside your circle.** At least 25 weekly active circles, at least 20 of them with no personal tie to you, and at least 40% of circles still active at week 8.
2. **Refill value without hand-loading.** Refill reminders run for every circle with no weekly manual step. They can come from the aggregator, from automated feeds from several pharmacies, or from a version that needs no pharmacy at all (see action 2 below).
3. **Strangers pay.** At least 20 paying circles from outside your network, with at least 70% renewing in month 2.

### The facts behind the verdict

These are not a full Gate 4 register, but they explain the FAIL.

- **You've named the wrong biggest risk.** The application says the biggest risk is pharmacy access and that "everything else I can build." The numbers say the risk is use, not build: 34 of 41 circles have stopped engaging.
- **"I'd pay anything" is not payment.** You have 3 subscribers. The paid tier hasn't really tested refill sync either, because almost nobody can use refill sync yet.
- **The product's premise conflicts with your own finding.** Tessel is pitched as a way to *share* care work. Your strongest insight is that one exhausted daughter carries most of it and wants *fewer phone calls, not another app*. Find out which of these is true for the circles that stayed.
- **Device mix may be quietly breaking circles.** About a third of your waitlist uses Android. If one sibling can't join, the shared log stops being shared. `[ASSUMPTION: this is a hypothesis to check, not a known cause of churn]`
- **There are single points of failure.** One pharmacist supplies all refill data. One social worker supplies the discharge channel. If either retires or changes jobs, that capability is gone.

## The employer pivot (Graham's proposal)

This is the most tempting move on the table, and the money is real if it materializes. Using Graham's figures (unverified), 1,800 employees at $3–5 per employee per month comes to about $5,400–9,000 a month. That is more than your entire monthly burn. Even so, don't build for it now. There are four reasons:

- **The offer is conditional.** "Would pilot next spring *if*" you add four things is not a commitment.
- **One of the four things is a staffing business.** A human-staffed care concierge is a services company. With one founder and a weekend designer, it would absorb you.
- **Employers renew on usage, and the reports you'd send would show your weakest number.** You'd ship anonymized usage reporting to an HR head while weekly activity is around 17% of circles.
- **It's a different subject:** a B2B benefit with a different customer, sales cycle and product. If you choose it, choose it on purpose once the core has proven it retains families. Don't drift into it because a pilot is on offer.

**Cheap move now:** send Graham and the HR head a warm reply. Ask the HR head two questions: (a) Would you run a *paid* pilot with no SSO and no human concierge? (b) What usage would you need to see to renew? Their answers price the opportunity without costing you a quarter of building.

## Next 90 days (all aimed at "good")

All actions are owned by the founder unless another owner is named.

1. **Find out why circles go quiet (weeks 1–3).** Tag all 41 circles by origin (network, newsletter, forum, discharge card) and by device mix. Interview at least 15 of the 34 inactive circles. Ask what they went back to: the group chat, a whiteboard, or one person doing everything.
2. **Separate refill value from pharmacy access (weeks 2–8).** Ship "refill due" reminders that need no pharmacy feed: fill date and days' supply taken from a bottle photo or a quick entry, a reminder before the medication runs out, and a one-tap "picked up." Your aunt reviews the clinical wording. In parallel, set a firm date to escalate or drop the aggregator application, and line up a second independent pharmacy so one pharmacist isn't the only source.
3. **Let every family member participate before porting to Android (weeks 4–10).** Use SMS or a web link for non-iPhone members, with help from the weekend designer. This tests whether complete circles retain better. It also tests your own insight: the app should "disappear into a reminder."
4. **Make the strongest channels measurable (weeks 1–4).** Give the newsletter and the discharge card unique links or codes, working with the social worker on the card. Define activation as a circle with at least one medication and one reminder set in the first session, and track it.
5. **Reply to Graham and the HR head with the two pricing questions above (week 1).** Hold off on SSO, the admin dashboard and the concierge.

**For the accelerator application:** consider rewriting question 11. "The biggest risk is retention outside my own circle, and here's my 90-day plan to prove it" is truer than "pharmacy access." Founders who can see their weakest number tend to read as stronger, not weaker.
