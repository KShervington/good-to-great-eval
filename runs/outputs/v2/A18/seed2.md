# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great Run

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Duplicon should be the equipment, supplies and know-how partner that keeps the independent quick printer profitable, and it should measure itself by profit per installed print-shop system.

**Weakest flywheel link:** recurring supplies and service profit per installed press. It has never been measured, and plain-paper copiers may already be taking short-run work away from those presses.

**Top 3 stop-doings:**
1. No bet-the-company push into "electronic office" against much larger firms.
2. No head-on copier fight with Rival X.
3. No growth capital for plate addressing. Run it for cash.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Both founding franchises are mechanical technologies that newer technology is replacing. Duplicon does not lead in either replacement.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Current profit and return on capital by business line.
- Quick-print market share.
- How fast computers are replacing plate addressing.

If credit cards turn out to be the most profitable line, the Hedgehog Concept may move there.

**Confidence:** Low to moderate. The verdict rests on a qualitative dossier with no current financials.

Greatness potential: 15/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Duplicon is smaller and sharper. It no longer tries to be an office-equipment conglomerate of 27 subsidiaries. Instead, it is the company the independent quick printer cannot do without. That means the press, the prepress and typesetting, the plates, inks and supplies, the service network and the business training that make a small print shop profitable. Most of its profit comes from the recurring stream on each installed system, not from one-time machine sales. The old addressing franchise is run openly for cash, and that cash funds the core. Units that don't serve the print shop are sold to owners who value them more. Duplicon competes with no one on plain-paper copying. Where copiers do win short runs for its customers, it helps those shops adapt instead of pretending the shift isn't happening. It is great if quick printers would struggle to replace it and if its return on capital beats the office-equipment sector over a full decade. `[ASSUMPTION: this definition is not yet validated against numbers]`

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Duplicon is the indispensable equipment-and-supplies partner to the independent quick printer, earning profit per installed print-shop system.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Small offset presses for quick print, where one model made Duplicon "a leading name"; (b) embossing and imprinting, which links addressing plates to credit cards; (c) office copying, *ruled out* because Rival X invented it and dominates; (d) electronic office systems, *ruled out* because much larger firms compete there; (e) large-organization plate addressing, *ruled out for the future* because computers are displacing it. | (a) **Systems for the small commercial printer.** (b) stays alive as an alternative until the numbers come in. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per machine sold; **profit per installed system** (supplies, plates, service); profit per customer account; profit per square foot of plant | **Profit per installed print-shop system** `[ASSUMPTION: that supplies and service carry most of the margin]` |
| **Passion** | Printing craft; precision mechanical engineering; serving small business owners | **Making small printers succeed** `[ASSUMPTION: no evidence on what the workforce cares about]` |

**Why this one:**
- It explains Duplicon's clearest recent win, the popular quick-print model.
- It rules a lot out: copiers, the electronic office, addressing growth and engineering graphics.
- It is uncomfortable, because it means shrinking a company that is proud of its $400M+ peak.
- It matches the dossier's third option, which is the least glamorous of the three.

**Confirmation tests. Promote this from hypothesis only if all three hold:**
1. Quick-print and offset supplies earn the highest return on capital of any line.
2. Profit per installed press is stable or rising. Supplies volume per press is the early read on copier erosion.
3. Duplicon's share of equipment in new quick-print shop openings is #1 or #2.

**Kill test:** If credit-card and data-recording products beat quick print on all three measures, re-run Gate 5 with candidate (b), "best at embossing and personalizing transaction cards."

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Reliable, easy-to-run press + prepress built for the small printer
 │         │
 │         ▼
 │   2. Shops open or expand on Duplicon systems (installed base grows)
 │         │
 │         ▼
 │   3. Recurring plates, ink, supplies, service per installed system  ◄── DENOMINATOR
 │         │
 │         ▼
 │   4. Profit per installed system funds dealer/service network + focused R&D
 │         │
 │         ▼
 │   5. Better prepress, training, uptime → shops earn more per job
 │         │
 └─────────┘  (profitable shops buy more and recommend Duplicon to new owners)

   Fuel input: cash harvested from addressing systems
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
- **Turn 1: Measure link 3.** Find out what Duplicon actually earns per installed press, and whether that number is falling.
- **Turn 2: Strengthen link 5.** Bring better typesetting and prepress into the quick-print shop. The typesetting unit belongs here, so this effort folds it into the core.

**Weakest link:** Link 3. If shops buy their supplies from third parties, or if copiers are taking the short runs that keep presses busy, the loop leaks.

**Doom-loop check:** Several warning signs are present.
- **Sprawl:** 27 subsidiaries.
- **Drift:** growth has stalled since the late 1960s, and three competing strategies are still open.
- **A peer warning:** Rival S just wrote off a large loss on a venture far from its core.
- **A big-program temptation:** the "invest heavily in electronic office" option is exactly the kind of program Collins warns about.

No evidence yet of lurching from one direction to the next, but the open strategy question is the setup for it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** pursuing leadership in general electronic office technology against much larger firms.
2. **Stop** competing with Rival X in plain-paper copiers.
3. **Stop** putting growth capital, R&D or new models into large-organization plate addressing. Price it for cash, and support existing customers.
4. **Stop** selling duplicators into general offices, where copiers win short runs. Concentrate on print shops.
5. **Stop** running 27 subsidiaries as separate fiefdoms. Consolidate or divest whatever doesn't serve the print-shop customer.
6. **Stop** using acquisitions or large ventures to bring back top-line growth. Rival S's point-of-sale write-off is the cautionary tale.
7. **Stop** treating total sales against the $400M+ peak as the scoreboard. Track profit per installed system and return on capital instead.
