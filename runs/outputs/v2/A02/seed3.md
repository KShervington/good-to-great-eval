## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Corwin is a focused household paper company again. It owns two or three brands that households choose on preference, not price, and those brands are made in integrated mills and forests that give it the lowest cost per ton in the category. It has sold or closed the films, furniture and teaching-aid businesses. Capital goes to softness, absorbency and cost. It does not buy growth. It defends share region by region with product and advertising, not blanket list-price cuts. One accountable chairman leads it and has a successor already developing. It earns the highest return in the U.S. paper industry and keeps earning it after the people who rebuilt it have left.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all acquisitions outside household paper.** A hard freeze, effective immediately.
2. **Retire "diversification into growth industries" as the stated strategy.**
3. **Exit technical films, casual furniture and AV teaching aids.**
4. **Stop holding fine printing papers by default.** Sell it unless it clears the hurdle on its own capital.
5. **Stop funding the disposable diaper** beyond a minimal test until blind tests show it wins.
6. **Stop using across-the-board list-price cuts** as the main weapon. They cut profit per ton on all volume to defend a few regions.
7. **Stop refusing promotion on principle.** Replace the blanket rule with targeted, measured regional tests.
8. **Stop spending mill capex on tonnage** that neither raises softness nor lowers cost per ton. Pollution compliance is the exception, because it is mandatory.
9. **Do not revive committee leadership** at the top.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. A few products, made excellently.
2. The lowest cost through careful stewardship of mills and forests.
3. Our own brands, and earning the consumer's preference rather than renting it with price.
4. Face the facts early.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** Become again the most profitable paper company in the U.S., with the towel and tissue brands most American households choose.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A written succession plan with at least two internal candidates.
- A capital rule: no investment unless it raises branded profit per ton (compliance spending excepted).
- Quarterly blind tests against the top rival, reported to the board.
