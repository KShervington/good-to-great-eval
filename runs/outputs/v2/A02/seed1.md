## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Corwin is a focused household-paper company. It turns its own timberland and modernized mills into a small set of tissue, towel and napkin products. Those products match or beat Rival P in blind softness tests and cost less to make per ton. They are sold under Corwin's original brands, including the inventor of the paper towel and one of the country's oldest tissue names, and backed by sustained advertising. Every dollar of capital goes to mills, product quality or brands. A single accountable chairman runs it, and a management team agrees on what the company is. Its denominator, profit per ton of branded household paper, rises year after year. Greatness here would not be a new direction. It would be a disciplined return to the formula that made Corwin the most profitable paper company in the country, updated for a world where softness is the battleground.

**Baseline (Gate 1):** The company is *good, not yet great*, in the turnaround variant. It was demonstrably great for decades: it led the industry in profits and growth in the 1950s and had the best margins and return on investment a decade ago. It is now declining. Return on equity fell from about 12% to about 9.5%, earnings fell 18%, and about $200M of acquisitions are unprofitable. The core still works without heroics: record sales two years ago and heritage brands. The brutal facts and survival moves therefore come first.

**Definition of great for Corwin:**
- **Superior performance:** returns back above the paper industry and the household-products peer set.
- **Distinctive impact:** the brands that set the national quality-per-dollar standard for household paper.
- **Endurance:** performance that outlasts the next chairman.
