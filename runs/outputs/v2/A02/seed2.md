## Verdict: Corwin Paper Company

**Good but declining. This is a turnaround case: once near-great, not great now.**

- **Hedgehog (working):** Make the household paper Corwin pioneered (towels and bath tissue) the kind families prefer, under its own brands, at the industry's lowest cost per ton.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** product superiority. Corwin has no answer to Rival P's softness.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:** (1) treating diversification as the strategy, and keeping the non-paper acquisitions; (2) running the company by committee; (3) launching or buying anything outside towels and tissue until the core is defended.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival P went national this year with a heavily advertised premium soft tissue. Corwin is losing on the attribute shoppers can feel.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** current share by category and region, the acquisitions' P&L, and blind-test softness results against Rival P.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on the financial trend and the competitive facts. It does not rest on share or consumer data.

`Greatness potential: 20/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corwin is a focused household-paper company again. It wins blind tests against Rival P on softness and absorbency in the two categories it created. It funds that product lead and the industry's heaviest advertising out of the lowest cost per ton in the business, which comes from an integrated mill and timberland base that rivals can't easily copy. It has sold or closed everything that doesn't feed that loop. One accountable chairman leads it. Every year it reports share by category and region, blind-test results and profit per ton, so that management can't be comfortable with past dominance again. If it disappeared, households would lose the brands that defined the category, and the industry would lose its cost benchmark.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Corwin wins by making paper towels and bath tissue that households prefer, sold under its own brands and made at the lowest cost per ton in the industry.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) branded household paper; (b) low-cost bulk/private-label tissue; (c) a diversified growth portfolio; (d) a broad packaged-goods marketer | **(a), narrowed to towels and bath tissue.** The evidence: it invented the paper towel, owns one of the oldest tissue brands, held 38% share vs 11% for the nearest rival about 16 years ago, and was the most profitable paper company a decade ago. **Cannot be best at:** technical films, furniture, AV teaching aids, fine printing papers, or out-marketing a giant packaged-goods company across many categories. |
| **Economic engine** | profit per ton; profit per household; profit per advertising dollar | **Profit per ton of branded household paper.** It connects the mill cost advantage to the brand price premium in a single number. |
| **Passion** | brands; mills and forests; "growth industries" | **[ASSUMPTION]** The real passion is the brands and the mill craft. That is the company's own history: a family member pushed it to drop private label and sell under its own name. The insiders who are split toward the "portfolio" view are evidence against this, so it needs to be tested. |

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. Blind tests reach softness parity or better against Rival P's premium tissue.
2. Profit per ton on branded towels and tissue beats every other business line.
3. Share stops falling in Rival P's launch regions.
4. Management can name at least five attractive things it turned down because of this sentence.

**What would kill the hypothesis:** if Corwin can't close the softness gap at a cost below Rival P's, the "best at" circle becomes "low-cost value tissue." That is a different and smaller Hedgehog, and it should be chosen deliberately rather than slid into.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                              ▼
 Lowest cost per ton              Profit per ton of branded paper
 (integrated mills + timber)                       │
   ▲                                              ▼
   │                          Reinvest: product superiority (softness,
   │                          absorbency) + heaviest advertising in category
   │                                              │
 Volume through modernized                        ▼
 tissue/towel mills  ◄──────── Household preference & regional share
```

- **Weakest link:** product superiority. The loop closes on paper but has a broken link. Advertising can't carry a product that loses on feel.
- **Current push (next 1–2 turns):**
  1. Put the best engineers and product people on a softness program.
  2. Send the cash freed by exiting the acquisitions into it, and into the modernization of the tissue mills that make the branded product.
- **Doom-loop check: present.** The signs are:
  - about $200M of acquisitions made to manufacture growth;
  - direction after direction (diversify, then an office of the chairman, then dissolving it);
  - a diaper launched without a clear Hedgehog fit;
  - 14 years of slow reaction to Rival P in the north-central states.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop treating "diversify into growth industries" as the strategy. Withdraw it publicly.
2. Stop owning the plastic-coating/technical-films, furniture and AV teaching-aid businesses. Start sale processes.
3. Stop holding fine book and printing papers. They are paper, but outside the consumer Hedgehog.
4. Stop all acquisitions and new-category launches (including another diaper attempt) until towel and tissue share is stabilized.
5. Stop leading by committee. One chairman is accountable.
6. Stop spending modernization capital on mills that don't make branded towels and tissue, beyond the environmental minimum.
7. Stop treating the no-coupon policy as doctrine without evidence. Keep it, but run a contained regional test so the decision rests on data and not on pride. **[ASSUMPTION]** The policy may be costing share against a promotion-capable rival.
