# Good to Great: Wexley Medical Corporation

## Verdict

**Weakest flywheel link:** Selling. The company has virtually no effective sales force, so new products cannot reach customers.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop paying generalist salespeople flat salaries.
2. Stop routing every decision through the founding family.
3. Stop IPO preparation until a budget and goal system has run for two cycles.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The company has no sales engine and no management system, and it has just lost its operating leader.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** net margin, sales history, share by product line, and the actual growth of instruments.

**Confidence:** Low to moderate. The verdict rests on the product heritage and the dossier's qualitative facts. No profit data is available.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Wexley is a clinician-driven device company built from several focused specialty businesses: patient handling, powered orthopedic instruments, and later others. Each business has its own P&L, its own sales force paid on results, and its own direct line to the surgeons and nurses whose problems it solves. The founder's design rule (every product must make caregivers more efficient or cut the cost of treatment) becomes a written gate that every product passes, so it no longer depends on one man's judgment. Growth comes from repeated turns of the same loop: hear a clinical problem, build the device, place it through specialists, reinvest. It does not come from a big acquisition or an IPO. If Wexley vanished, the specialties it serves would lose the people who understand their workflow best.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying generalist sales staff flat salaries.** Move to specialty-focused, results-based pay.
2. **Stop routing all decisions through the founding family.** Decisions go to the CEO and then to the business-unit heads.
3. **Stop treating the IPO as a near-term goal.** Defer it until the budget system has run two cycles and there is an earnings trend to show.
4. **Stop considering an entry into implants** (by building or buying) for at least three years. It is outside the best-at circle and would be a classic grab for salvation.
5. **Stop launching products that fail the founder's rule** (caregiver efficiency or treatment cost). Write the rule down and use it as a formal gate.
6. **Stop spreading the international subsidiary across every market.** Focus it on the one or two regions where the specialty products already sell `[ASSUMPTION]`.
7. **Stop competing on price in commodity beds and stretchers.** Compete on features that save caregiver time, or accept a smaller share.
