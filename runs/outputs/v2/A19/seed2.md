# Marlowe Drug Co.: Good to Great

**Verdict: good, not yet great. Marlowe needs to narrow its focus; it does not need a survival turnaround.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be America's most convenient drugstore, with the pharmacy at the center, and grow profit per customer visit.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** profit per visit. Sales are up 2.3× over the nine-year table. Net margin fell from 2.2% to 0.8% reported, or about 1.4% before LIFO.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:**
  1. Opening restaurants. Freeze the 22 planned units.
  2. Investing in the 25 discount department stores. Prepare an exit.
  3. Expanding side businesses: the second lab, the container plant, the travel agency.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** EPS barely moved in seven years while sales nearly doubled. Growth is not creating value.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** profit and return on capital by division, and profit per store and per visit.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on consolidated figures only. Division data could reorder the stop-doing list. It is unlikely to change the Hedgehog.

Greatness potential: 55/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marlowe is a drugstore company and nothing else. Its stores are the most convenient place in each neighborhood to fill a prescription and pick up health, beauty and everyday needs. Stores are dense, well-sited and fast, and the pharmacist is at the center of the store. Every store, site and capital dollar is judged by one number: profit per customer visit. Scale (already about double the nearest competitor) turns into buying power, house-brand margin and location density that regional chains can't match. Earnings grow faster than sales, year after year. Capital that used to go to restaurants, discount department stores, a travel agency and factories now goes to more and better drugstore locations, starting in the Sun Belt where Rival E is advancing. It lasts because the system decides (division P&Ls, a per-visit hurdle for every site, independent board oversight), not because a member of the founding family is in the chair.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Marlowe will be the best in America at running convenient, pharmacy-centered neighborhood drugstores, and it will measure itself by profit per customer visit.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Selected | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Large drugstore chain operations; (b) convenient pharmacy-centered neighborhood stores; (c) house-brand health/beauty products; (d) family restaurants; (e) general-merchandise discount | **(b).** It is the largest chain, about 2× the nearest competitor, pharmacist-founded, has 75 years of practice, and management already calls the stores "community centers for health and beauty." | (d): dedicated restaurant chains will outrun a drugstore company. (e): its 25 stores are a regional sideline that the recession hit hardest. (c): valuable as *sourcing for the stores*, but not a thing to be best at as a manufacturer. |
| **Economic denominator** | Profit per store; per sq ft; per prescription; **per customer visit** | **Profit per customer visit** `[ASSUMPTION: not measured in the dossier]`. Convenience produces frequent, repeat visits, and pharmacy visits recur. Each visit can carry front-store and house-brand margin. | Sales growth. It doubled while EPS stayed flat, so it is the wrong denominator. |
| **Deep passion** | Drugstores and pharmacy; growth itself; restaurants (heritage since the 1920s soda fountains) | **Drugstores and pharmacy.** The founder was a pharmacist, and management "holds fast to its basic identity as drugstores." | "Growth across all divisions" reads as ambition for size. It is not a passion for any single thing. |

**Tests of the intersection:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Mostly yes. Self-service, low prices and steady store openings built the drugstore lead. The diversified businesses came during the years when margins fell `[ASSUMPTION: causality not proven without division P&L]`.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: restaurants, discount stores, the travel agency, factory expansion and the Mexico stake.
- **Would it be uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means unwinding food service, which has been part of the company's identity for 50 years, plus 339 units and a new restaurant chain management just called "positive."

**Evidence that would confirm it (next 6–12 months):**
1. The drugstore division earns a materially higher return on capital than restaurants and discount stores.
2. Profit per visit varies widely across the 616 stores, and location or convenience explains most of the variance.
3. Pharmacy customers visit more often and spend more per visit than non-pharmacy customers.

If (1) turns out false, revisit Gate 5. If (2) is false, the denominator may be profit per store or per square foot instead.

## 3. The flywheel

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                          │
 [1] Dense, convenient, pharmacy-centered          │
     locations (open/relocate the best,            │
     close the marginal)                           │
        │                                          │
        ▼                                          │
 [2] More customer visits (prescriptions           │
     recur; convenience wins the quick trip)       │
        │                                          │
        ▼                                          │
 [3] More margin per visit (front store +          │
     house brands + low shrinkage)                 │
        │                                          │
        ▼                                          │
 [4] PROFIT PER CUSTOMER VISIT ↑                   │
        │                                          │
        ▼                                          │
 [5] Scale buying power + cash freed from          │
     non-core exits                                │
        │                                          │
        ▼                                          │
 [6] Capital into the next best sites ─────────────┘
```

**Weakest link:** [3]→[4]. Margin per visit is thin. Reported net margin is 0.76%, and at least ~1.4% before LIFO (derived from management's claim that earnings would have beaten the $13.7M record). Shrinkage is a named problem, and nobody measures per-visit profit.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Make [4] visible.** Build store-level and per-visit profit reporting, and use it to rank all 616 stores.
2. **Feed [6] from the exits.** Redirect the capital planned for 22 restaurants, the second lab and the container plant into drugstore sites in the Sun Belt markets Rival E is targeting.

**Doom-loop check: the signs are present.**
- Acquisitions used to buy momentum: discount stores 12 years ago, a travel agency 3 years ago, 29 stores from another chain last year.
- A new restaurant chain started during a margin squeeze.
- A stated aim of growth in *every* division at once.
- The earnings shortfall explained by accounting (LIFO) and the economy rather than by economics.

None of these is fatal yet. Together they describe pushing several flywheels at once, none of them hard enough to turn.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop opening restaurants.** Cancel or freeze the 22 planned units, and stop building the new family-restaurant chain.
2. **Stop investing in the discount department stores.** No new capital. Commission exit options (sale or conversion).
3. **Stop expanding manufacturing.** Pause the second lab and the plastic-container plant until a make-vs-buy review is done. Keep house-brand *products*; question owning the *plants*.
4. **Stop holding the travel agency.** Sell it.
5. **Stop adding to the Mexico stake.** Review whether to hold or sell it.
6. **Stop using "growth across all divisions" as the goal,** and stop reporting success mainly as sales volume. Report earnings growth versus sales growth, and profit per visit.
7. **Stop buying store chains for footprint alone.** Any acquired store must clear the same per-visit hurdle as a new site.
8. **Stop explaining earnings shortfalls with accounting and the economy** in internal reviews. Use LIFO-adjusted *and* reported figures, and say what the business itself did.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Pharmacist-grade trust: health comes first, and the pharmacist's word is good.
2. Convenience for the customer: near, open, quick.
3. Thrift: low prices made possible by low costs.
4. Stewardship of the long term over the size of this year.

**Core purpose:** to make staying healthy easy for every community Marlowe serves.

**BHAG (20–30 years):** *A Marlowe pharmacy within a few minutes of most Americans' homes, and the place most of them choose to fill their prescriptions.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A per-visit profit hurdle for every new, relocated or acquired store, applied by the system rather than by one person's judgment.
- Annual "prune" reviews that close or relocate the bottom-ranked stores, continuing the practice behind last year's 51 closures.
- Division and store P&Ls reviewed by the independent directors.
- A succession plan that keeps non-family candidates on the slate.
- Capital allocation by Hedgehog fit first and financial return second.
