# Good to Great: Marlowe Drug Co.

## Verdict

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Marlowe becomes America's most convenient drugstore, the pharmacy and health-and-beauty stop closest to people's daily lives, judged by **profit per customer visit**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** profit per visit. It isn't measured, and net margin is under 1% (about 1.4% before LIFO).

**Top three stop-doing:**
1. Discount department stores. Open no new units and prepare to sell all 25.
2. Free-standing family restaurants. Hold the 22 planned units.
3. Stop treating "growth in every division" as the goal. Sell the travel agency and review the side plants.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** sales doubled in nine years, but earnings per share (EPS) rose only from $1.30 to $2.11 at best. Growth isn't turning into profit.

**Missing numbers that could change this:** profit and return on capital by division, profit per store and per visit, and regional share against Rival E.

**Confidence:** moderate-to-low. The verdict rests on consolidated totals only.

`Greatness potential: 40/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marlowe is a drugstore company and nothing else that matters. It is the pharmacy and health-and-beauty stop people pass most often. It is dense in the markets it chooses to win and absent from those it can't. Its house-brand labs exist only to raise the profit of each drugstore visit. It has sold its discount department stores, travel agency and other side businesses. Food survives only where the numbers prove it raises drugstore profit per visit. Every store, site and capital decision is judged on one number, profit per customer visit, and the chain prunes stores that fall short as steadily as it opens new ones. The founder's pharmacist identity, "community centers for health and beauty service", becomes an operating rule rather than a slogan. The company outperforms because of what it refuses to do, not because it is bigger.

**Confirmation tests (6–12 months):**
1. Drugstore operating profit is clearly the majority of corporate profit and earns the highest return on capital. If discount stores or restaurants earn more per dollar invested, revisit the concept.
2. Profit per visit varies meaningfully by store, and the variance is explained by convenience factors (location, density, pharmacy share, house-brand share).
3. Denser markets show higher profit per visit than scattered ones.
4. In-store lunch counters either raise profit per visit for the store as a whole or they don't. This decides their fate.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Convenient, dense drugstore sites in chosen markets
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   2. More customer visits per store (frequency: pharmacy refills, daily needs)
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   3. Higher PROFIT PER CUSTOMER VISIT
 │      (pharmacy + house brands + lower shrinkage)
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   4. Cash freed from non-core exits + store earnings
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   5. Density economics: distribution, advertising, supervision, brand
 │          │
 └──────────┘  (funds more convenient sites; prunes marginal ones)
```

**Weakest link:** step 3. It is unmeasured, and the evidence (a margin that fell from about 2.2% seven years ago to about 1.4% before LIFO) suggests it is eroding.

**Current push:**
1. Build the measurement for step 3.
2. Stop the capital leak to non-core divisions so step 4 actually funds steps 1 and 5.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Open no new discount department stores. Prepare to sell all 25.
2. Hold the free-standing family restaurant pipeline, including the non-drugstore share of the 22 planned units.
3. Stop "growth across all divisions" as a stated aim, and stop treating the $1B sales mark as a milestone worth celebrating.
4. Sell the travel agency. Make no further non-drug acquisitions.
5. Expand manufacturing (labs, container plant) only as far as Marlowe's own stores need. Keep house brands in, and keep third-party ambitions out.
6. Exit the coffee and ice cream plants unless captive economics beat outside supply.
7. Stop opening stores in markets where Marlowe can't reach density, and keep pruning marginal stores (51 closed in two years is the right reflex).
8. Stop expanding the 1,800 agency franchise as a growth line. It is a house-brand outlet, not the Hedgehog.
9. **In-store lunch counters (288)** go to a 12-month test, not an automatic keep. Keep each one only where it raises the store's profit per visit.
