# Kessling Company: Good to Great Run (Autonomous)

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Kessling can be the best in the world at discovering, proving and economically making medicines for endocrine and inflammatory disease, built on its steroid-hormone chemistry, and measured by profit per research dollar.

**Weakest flywheel link:** owned, well-proven new medicines. The next growth engine is a licensed compound that belongs to another firm.

**Stop doing:**
1. Trying to match the largest drug companies' research budgets across every field.
2. Diversifying outside human medicine, starting with a review of the seed business.
3. Choosing top leaders only from the family.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** regulators now remove drugs that lack strong evidence. One removal cost about 12% of domestic gross income, and the diabetes franchise is under a safety cloud.

**Numbers that would change this:** 10-year return on equity against peers, research spending as a share of sales, non-drug share of sales, and family ownership.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. This rests on history and qualitative facts, with no current financials.

Greatness potential: 12/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kessling is no longer a scaled-down copy of the largest drug firms. It is the leader in a deliberately narrow field: hormone-based and anti-inflammatory medicines. In that field, its fermentation and steroid chemistry give it a cost and know-how edge, and its research dollars produce more profit than anyone else's. Every product it sells is backed by evidence strong enough that regulators and doctors don't question it. Licensed products fund the bridge, but owned follow-on compounds replace them. The company is run by the best available leader, family or not, under an independent board. Non-drug ventures have been sold or walled off. The founder's values (medicines that work as promised, care for employees, roots in the home city) are kept as the core. Everything else is open to change.

**Hedgehog sentence:** *Kessling can be the best in the world at discovering, proving and economically making medicines for endocrine and inflammatory disease, built on its steroid-hormone chemistry, measured by profit per research dollar.*

**Why this candidate passes the three tests:**
- **It explains past wins.** The adrenal-hormone first, the steroid fermentation route and the oral diabetes drug all sit inside it.
- **It rules things out.** Seeds, broad antibiotic research, and racing the giants on total research spend all fall outside it.
- **It is uncomfortable.** It means leaving the self-image of being "one of the big research houses," and putting the seed acquisition under review.

The licensed arthritis compound sits inside the inflammation circle. It should be used as a bridge, not treated as the Hedgehog itself.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis, not yet settled):**
1. Kessling's market share and cost per unit in steroid/hormone categories are top-tier against peers.
2. More than half of current research projects can credibly be placed in endocrine or inflammation `[ASSUMPTION: unknown]`.
3. Historically, Kessling's profit per research dollar in these areas beats its own average elsewhere.
4. Inflammation specialists respond to the arthritis launch, which shows a channel exists for owned follow-ons.

If tests 1 and 3 fail, the honest conclusion may be that Kessling can be a good mid-sized drug company but not a great one.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop benchmarking total research spend against the largest firms.** Benchmark profit per research dollar inside the focus areas instead.
2. **Stop diversifying outside human medicine.** Make no further agricultural or non-drug acquisitions. Put the seed business through a sale or spin-off review.
3. **Stop starting new research programs outside endocrine and inflammation.** Existing out-of-focus programs get a fixed review date.
4. **Stop in-licensing compounds that fall outside the focus areas.** No license is signed without a funded plan for an owned follow-on.
5. **Stop using litigation as the main answer to regulatory challenges.** Answer with controlled evidence, or withdraw the product.
6. **Stop filling the top seat only from the family.**
7. **Stop marketing claims that run ahead of the evidence**, especially for the diabetes drug while its safety questions are open.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every marketed product has an evidence file that would survive today's regulatory standard.
- Run an annual portfolio prune against the Hedgehog Concept.
- Track profit per research dollar every quarter.

**Framework boundaries:** scientists have full freedom in *how* they pursue a target, as long as the target sits inside endocrine and inflammation. Bureaucracy check: approval layers that exist only because leaders are picked for kinship rather than ability should go once the selection process changes.
