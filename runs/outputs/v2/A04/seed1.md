# Good to Great: Kessling Company

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great, and on a turnaround footing.**

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Kessling will be the best in the world at discovering, and making affordably, drugs in a few chosen areas of hormone, inflammation and metabolic medicine, judged by profit per research dollar.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning its own discovery into new drugs. The newest in-house "first" in the dossier is 17 years old, and the next growth product is licensed from another firm.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Non-drug acquisitions (seeds and agriculture).
2. Trying to match the giants' research budgets across every area.
3. Fighting regulators in court instead of answering with safety data.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Kessling cannot outspend the largest drug firms, and its future profits may depend on a compound another firm owns.

**Numbers that could change this:** sales, earnings and return-on-equity trend; research spending as a share of sales versus peers; non-drug share of sales; share of steroid and hormone markets.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on the dossier's history only. There are no current financials.

`Greatness potential: 22/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Kessling is a mid-sized, research-based drug company that has stopped measuring itself against the giants on total spending. It wins on a narrower field instead. It owns a few therapeutic areas built on its historical strengths: hormones and steroids, inflammation, and metabolic disease. In those areas it discovers its own drugs and makes them at lower cost than anyone, using its fermentation and process-chemistry skill. It earns physicians' trust by producing the best safety evidence in those categories. It licenses products only inside its chosen areas, and only as a bridge to its own follow-on compounds. Its board and its leader are chosen for merit, not family ties. Every dollar that leaves drugs, whether for seeds or anything else, has to earn its way back against the Hedgehog Concept.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Focused research in a few chosen areas
   │         (hormone / inflammation / metabolic)
   │                     │
   │                     ▼
   │   2. Own first-in-class or best-in-class drugs,
   │      made at low cost (fermentation, process chemistry)
   │                     │
   │                     ▼
   │   3. Best-in-category safety and outcome evidence
   │      (meets regulators ahead of time, not in court)
   │                     │
   │                     ▼
   │   4. Physician trust → prescription share in chosen areas
   │                     │
   │                     ▼
   │   5. PROFIT PER RESEARCH DOLLAR  (denominator)
   │                     │
   │                     ▼
   └── 6. Reinvest in focused research + targeted licensing
          that feeds own follow-on compounds
```

**Weakest link:** step 1 to step 2. The dossier shows no major in-house first since the oral diabetes drug, about 17 years ago. The record may be incomplete `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Current push (the next one or two turns):**
1. Launch the licensed arthritis compound with a proactive safety-monitoring program. This builds step 3 instead of repeating the court fight.
2. Redirect research toward a short list of chosen areas, starting with inflammation, so that Kessling owns a follow-on to the licensed compound.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are already present.
- **Diversification to find growth:** the seed acquisition six years ago.
- **Litigating instead of understanding:** the antibiotic court fight.
- **Looking for an outside product to restart momentum:** the licensed compound.
- **A strategy that keeps oscillating:** the open question "match research spending, or lean on licensing and diversification?"

None of these is fatal yet. Together they look like the start of a doom loop.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop making non-drug acquisitions,** and stop putting new capital into seeds and agriculture until a divestiture review is complete.
2. **Stop trying to match the largest firms' total research budgets** across every therapeutic area.
3. **Stop litigating regulators on the science.** Answer safety questions with better data, and do it sooner.
4. **Stop defending weakened products,** such as the diabetes drug, with marketing instead of outcome studies.
5. **Stop licensing compounds outside the chosen areas,** however attractive the near-term profit.
6. **Stop treating family connection as a path to the top seat** or to the board.
7. **Stop leaving the "licensing vs. research vs. diversification" question open.** Answer it once, using the Hedgehog Concept.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Science first.
2. Patient safety over product defense.
3. Care for employees.
4. Commitment to the home community.
5. Medicine that is easier and cheaper to take (the founder's pill).

**Core purpose:** to make medicines that work better and reach more people, through Kessling's own science.

**BHAG** (20-year horizon): become the world's leading company in hormone, inflammation and metabolic medicine by share of prescriptions in those areas, with most of that share from drugs Kessling discovered itself.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A board with an independent majority.
- Merit-based CEO succession with internal and external candidates.
- An annual portfolio review that cuts any research program outside the chosen areas.
- A safety-signal system that reports directly to the CEO and the board.
- A licensing policy restricted to the chosen areas and requiring an in-house follow-on plan.
