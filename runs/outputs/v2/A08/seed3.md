# Pacific Crest Financial Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be the best in [home state] at turning household savings into small, conservatively underwritten adjustable-rate home loans, measured by profit per unit of interest-rate risk.

**Weakest flywheel link:** profit → capital. The old fixed-rate book is funded by deposits that reprice, so it drags earnings, and its size is unknown.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. No further capital into brokerage and real-estate services.
2. None of the riskier assets that deregulation now permits.
3. No thrift acquisitions or out-of-state expansion judged by size.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the company still carries fixed-rate mortgages funded by market-rate deposits, in a rate squeeze that is costing even Rival K money.

**Numbers that would change this:** three years of net income, ROE and net worth; the size and yield of the fixed-rate book; the earnings of the new non-lending unit; home-state deposit and mortgage share.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on a strong qualitative record (60+ years, early ARM pioneer, conservative lending) with no financials.

`Greatness potential: 30/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Pacific Crest is not the biggest thrift. It is the one that never needs rescuing. It gathers household savings through the densest, lowest-cost office network in [home state]. It lends those savings back as small, carefully underwritten adjustable-rate home loans whose yields move with its funding costs. It keeps credit losses far below the industry's and earns a steady spread through rate cycles that break competitors. It refuses the deregulated buffet of brokerage, development and high-yield assets, and acquires only in-state thrifts that improve profit per unit of risk. Its reputation as the safest place to save and the most trustworthy place to get a mortgage compounds for decades. That reputation does not depend on one CEO, one spokesman or one rate environment.

**Baseline sheet (Gate 1)**

- **Evidence of good:**
  - More than 60 years of continuous operation.
  - Survived an early-1960s credit crisis. Foreclosures peaked at $23.2M about 20 years ago, then fell back.
  - Built a statewide presence in nearly every major metro area.
  - Pioneered variable-rate mortgages in the 1970s. About 60% of its mortgages were variable-rate by the end of that decade, and 100% of new loans have been adjustable since regulators approved ARMs.
  - Known for conservative, small-balance residential lending and for avoiding the riskier assets peers are chasing.
- **What is not evidenced:** any earnings, ROE or net worth figure. "Good" here is established qualitatively, not numerically.
- **State: Good, not yet great.** The core works on its own and at scale. There is no evidence of superior performance, and size is not performance. If the missing numbers show losses that are eroding net worth, the run stays in this state but becomes a **turnaround**: rate-gap reduction and capital preservation come before everything else.
- **Draft definition of great:**
  1. *Superior performance:* return on assets and net-worth growth that beat peer thrifts across a full rate cycle, not only in good years.
  2. *Distinctive impact:* the home state's households would lose their most trusted savings-and-mortgage institution, one that kept lending conservatively while others blew up.
  3. *Lasting endurance:* performance holds through at least two rate cycles and a CEO succession.

## 5. Stop-doing list (Gate 6)

1. **Stop putting capital into brokerage and real-estate services.** Freeze it now, review it within 90 days, and sell it if it can't beat the mortgage book on profit per unit of risk.
2. **Stop considering the high-risk assets that deregulation now allows**, however strong peers' reported growth looks.
3. **Stop adding fixed-rate loans to the portfolio** unless they are matched-funded, hedged or sold.
4. **Stop expanding out of state** until the hedgehog is proven at home.
5. **Stop acquiring thrifts for size.** Acquire only in-state institutions with conservative books that improve the denominator.
6. **Stop citing "second-largest in the nation" as a goal or a success measure.**
7. **Stop bidding up deposit rates or buying volatile funding to keep pace with fast-growing peers** `[ASSUMPTION: this pressure exists]`.
8. **Stop paying for celebrity advertising that isn't tied to deposits per office.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines**
- Every new loan is adjustable-rate or matched-funded.
- Underwriting standards stay small-balance and residential, with no exceptions for volume.
- Every capital-allocation decision is scored on profit per unit of interest-rate risk.
- The repricing gap and the delinquency rate go to the board every month.

**Framework boundaries:** branch and regional leaders have autonomy over service, staffing and local deposit tactics, within fixed rules on rate risk, credit standards and pricing floors.

**Bureaucracy check:** any approval layers added to control the acquired businesses or the merged offices should be reviewed. If they exist because the wrong people are in the seats, fix the seats instead.

**Verdict: PASS.**
