## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is the home lender that is still standing, and still profitable, after every rate cycle and every real-estate downturn. That includes the ones that wreck the competitors who used deregulation to chase size. It does one thing: it turns everyday household savings into small, carefully underwritten, adjustable-rate home loans. Its balance sheet is matched, so a rate spike costs it little. It sells or swaps what it cannot match, through the new securities channel. It uses its huge branch network and its trusted brand to keep funding cheap and loyal. It grows only as fast as discipline allows. It measures itself on risk-adjusted profit per dollar of mortgage assets, not on asset rank. Its edge compounds quietly: lower losses, steadier earnings, stronger capital, cheaper deposits, better borrowers, and round again.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Buying non-lending businesses.** Freeze the brokerage and real-estate services unit. Within 12 months, keep it only if it shows a measurable reduction in deposit cost or a lift in mortgage households. Otherwise, divest it.
2. **Buying high-risk assets because deregulation now allows them.** This covers commercial real-estate development, equity stakes in real estate, high-yield securities, and large-ticket loans.
3. **Holding new fixed-rate loans unhedged.** Originate them only to sell or swap.
4. **Acquiring thrifts for size.** Every deal must pass written criteria: accretive to risk-adjusted spread, credit quality at or above ours, and funding cost at or below ours.
5. **Expanding out of state for its own sake.** Enter only if a market passes the same criteria and the home state is saturated [ASSUMPTION: no share data].
6. **Bidding up deposit rates or buying "hot" money to fund growth.** Accept slower growth instead.
7. **Funding celebrity advertising on faith.** Test it against deposit cost per dollar. Keep it if it works and cut it if it doesn't.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every loan is small and residential.
- Every new portfolio loan is adjustable.
- There is a monthly repricing-gap report with hard limits.
- Executive pay is tied to risk-adjusted profit, never to asset size.

**Framework boundaries:**
- Branches and loan officers have freedom on service, local marketing and customer relationships.
- They have no freedom on credit standards, rate risk or product scope.
