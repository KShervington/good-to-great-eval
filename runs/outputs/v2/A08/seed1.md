## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is the dullest strong balance sheet in American home finance. It is great because it never has to be rescued, not because it is big. It makes small, carefully underwritten adjustable-rate home loans across its home state. It funds them with a loyal statewide deposit base. It keeps the rate risk on its books close to zero by matching loan repricing to deposit repricing and moving anything it can't match into the secondary market. While deregulated competitors chase yield and blow up, it compounds steady spread income. It gains share and deposits from their failures, and it acquires only distressed franchises that pass its own underwriting test. Rate cycles, a CEO change and a state housing downturn would all hurt it without breaking it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **No further non-lending acquisitions.** Freeze investment in the brokerage and real-estate unit pending a decision to sell or wind it down.
2. **No high-risk assets**, even where deregulation now permits them. Examples: speculative development lending, direct real-estate equity, and high-yield securities `[ASSUMPTION: the specific asset classes peers are entering]`.
3. **No thrift acquisitions for size or rank.** Only distressed or bargain franchises that pass the underwriting and deposit-cost test.
4. **No new fixed-rate loans held in portfolio.** Originate fixed-rate loans only for sale.
5. **No deposit-rate wars to fund growth.** Don't buy volatile, rate-shopping money to keep up with aggressive peers.
6. **No out-of-state expansion** until home-state risk-adjusted returns and the rate gap hit target. After that, expand only into markets where the underwriting edge provably transfers.
7. **Stop measuring the company by national rank or asset size** in internal and investor communication.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:**
  - Prudence over growth.
  - Match every risk you take.
  - Treat the saver's dollar as a trust.
  - Tell the board the bad news first.
- **Core purpose:** help ordinary households save safely and own their homes.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** be the safest major home lender in America. In numbers: no loss year and no dividend cut through two full interest-rate cycles.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A credit committee with written, board-approved underwriting limits.
  - An asset-liability committee with hard gap limits.
  - Independent risk reporting to the board.
  - Executive pay tied to risk-adjusted return, not asset growth.
  - An acquisition test (underwriting plus deposit cost) that any deal must pass in writing.
