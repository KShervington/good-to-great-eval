# Good to Great: Heart Failure Transitions Program (HFTP), St. Aldric

**Verdict: good, not yet great. The headline number is weaker than it looks.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Keep heart-failure patients home past day 90, not just day 30, by finding and fixing the medication and home-support gaps that send them back.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** holding patients after the day-30 "graduation." The 90-day readmission rate is 32.7% for enrolled patients and 33.9% for those not enrolled.
- **Top three to stop:** (1) taking the lead on Hospital at Home now; (2) presenting enrolled vs. not enrolled as proof of effect; (3) ending follow-up automatically at day 30.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the 5.9-point gap at 30 days shrinks to 1.2 points by day 90. The comparison group also includes nursing-facility discharges and patients who couldn't be reached by phone.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** a risk-adjusted comparison, 90-day mortality, readmissions to other hospitals, and Finance's value per readmission or bed-day.
- **Confidence:** moderate-low. It rests on one year of unadjusted internal data.

Greatness potential: 30/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great HFTP is the team St. Aldric could not cut without a measurable rise in heart-failure hospital days. It reaches the highest-risk patients, including those without a phone, before they leave. Its pharmacists untangle medication regimens that span several prescribers. It keeps patients enrolled until they meet stability criteria, not until a calendar date, and then hands them to a named clinician. Every quarter it reports risk-adjusted 90-day results and mortality. Finance has signed off on a value figure, so the budget conversation becomes arithmetic rather than advocacy. It refuses growth that would dilute this, Hospital at Home leadership included, until the core is proven.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *We keep heart-failure patients home past day 90, not just day 30, by finding and fixing the medication and home-support gaps that send them back.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out (cannot be best at) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best in the world (narrow world: HF patients in St. Aldric's region) | Reliable 30-day execution (82% seen within 7 days vs. 46%); pharmacist untangling of multi-prescriber regimens (Mr. D.); in-home reach (CHWs, home visits) | Medication and home-support gap-fixing, held past day 30 | Acute inpatient-level care at home; generic care management for every condition; volume enrollment |
| Resource engine (budget and headcount leverage) | Penalty reduction; readmissions avoided; bed-days freed | Value Finance will sign off on | The federal penalty alone (it covers several conditions, so attribution is shared) [ASSUMPTION: general policy knowledge, not verified this session] |
| Passion | "Make it home and stay home" (the memo's own mission); patient stories; low turnover; CHWs delivering 214 donated scales | Staying home, which the 90-day data says isn't yet happening | A new department title |

**Denominator:** risk-adjusted **90-day hospital days avoided per $100K of program cost**.
- Unadjusted proxy today, counting readmissions rather than days: about **0.9 per $100K at 90 days**, against about **4.6 per $100K at 30 days**. Both are derived from the dossier as 1,146 × gap ÷ $1.46M.
- The derived cost is about **$1,274 per enrolled patient**.
- That implies about **$21.6K per naively avoided 30-day readmission**, but about **$106K per naively avoided 90-day readmission**.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. A risk-adjusted 90-day comparison, excluding nursing-facility discharges, shows a meaningful gap.
2. Patients who received a pharmacist review show a larger effect than those who didn't.
3. A pilot extension for high-risk patients narrows the day 31–90 cliff.
4. Finance converts the result into dollars.

**Does it pass the three tests?**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Intensive 30-day support explains the 30-day gap and the 7-day visit rate.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: Hospital at Home leadership, volume-first targets, and a fixed endpoint at day 30.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It says the headline metric is the wrong one, and it means deferring a promotion.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** accepting the Hospital at Home lead now. Recommend that the hospital staff it as its own team, with HFTP as referral and consult partner.
2. **Stop** presenting enrolled vs. not enrolled as the program's effect. The comparison group includes decliners, phone-unreachable patients and nursing-facility discharges.
3. **Stop** automatic graduation at day 30. It is the federal penalty window, not a clinical endpoint.
4. **Stop** chasing 75% enrollment as the headline goal. Depth past day 30 comes before breadth. Do keep the weekend shift if it is cheap and targets high-risk patients [ASSUMPTION: cost unknown].
5. **Stop** treating "can't be reached by phone" as grounds for exclusion. These are likely among the highest-risk patients [ASSUMPTION].
6. **Stop** giving every enrolled patient the same six touches regardless of risk.
7. **Stop** crediting the drop in the federal penalty to HFTP without an attribution analysis.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every enrolled patient is tracked to day 90, including mortality.
- Every patient gets a pharmacist medication review before handoff.
- A monthly 90-day report goes to the VP.
- Every new request is tested against the Hedgehog sentence.

**Framework:**
- *Fixed:* bedside enrollment, a 7-day visit, a pharmacist review, daily weights and stability criteria.
- *Free:* navigators, as RNs, set touch frequency and channel by risk.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** Help people with heart failure live well at home.
- **Core values:**
  - The patient's home is the goal.
  - Tell the truth with data, especially when it's unwelcome.
  - Fix root causes, starting with medications.
  - Reach the hardest-to-reach first.
- **BHAG (10 years, one line):** No heart-failure patient in St. Aldric's region spends a hospital day that the right medication or support at home could have prevented. Measure it as the lowest risk-adjusted 90-day HF bed-days in the peer group.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A written protocol and stability criteria.
  - A monthly 90-day dashboard.
  - A value formula agreed with Finance.
  - A named deputy, so the program survives Colette's promotion or departure [ASSUMPTION: no deputy today].
