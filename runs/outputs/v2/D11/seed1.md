# HFTP Good to Great: Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** HFTP is the team at St. Aldric that adds the most *days at home* in the 90 days after a heart-failure discharge. It does this by untangling medications and catching decline early, and it proves the result with fair comparisons.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** At 90 days, readmission rates for enrolled and non-enrolled patients are almost equal: **32.7% vs 33.9%**. That is true even though the comparison is tilted in the program's favor. The 30-day win may be readmissions pushed past day 30 rather than prevented.

**Weakest flywheel link:** There is no fair measure of effect, so the program has no verified value to bring to the budget cycle.

**Stop:**
1. Leading Hospital at Home now.
2. Citing enrolled vs not-enrolled as proof.
3. Graduating every patient at day 30.

**Numbers that would change this:**
- Risk-adjusted 90-day readmissions and days alive and out of hospital.
- 90-day mortality.
- Finance's value per heart-failure readmission and per bed-day.

**Confidence:** Moderate on the stop-doing list. Low on the Hedgehog Concept, which rests on one memo, one attachment and one patient story.

Greatness potential: 27/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, HFTP is deeper rather than bigger. It knows, from a fair comparison, how many days at home it adds per dollar spent. It follows its highest-risk patients to day 90, not just past the edge of the 30-day penalty window. Its pharmacists sit at the center of the protocol and catch problems like two diuretics from two specialists before they cause the next admission. Its budget is defended by a number Finance has validated, not by thank-you letters. When the hospital starts a new home-based venture, HFTP serves as its referral partner and evidence base. It does not absorb that venture before its own core is proven. The team is already good at *reaching* patients (82% seen within 7 days, versus 46% of non-enrolled patients). The great version proves that reaching them *changes what happens to them*.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *We add more days at home for St. Aldric's heart-failure patients in the 90 days after discharge than anyone else could, by fixing their medicines and catching decline early, and we prove it.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** (the narrow world: heart-failure patients after a St. Aldric discharge) | Reliable post-discharge contact; medication untangling across specialists; practical home support (scales, community health worker visits); acute care at home | **Reliable contact** (82% vs 46% seen within 7 days, the strongest evidence in the dossier) plus **medication untangling** (pharmacist-led; plausible but rests on one story) `[ASSUMPTION]` | Acute inpatient-level care at home; remote-monitoring technology; general care management for all conditions |
| **Resource engine** | Federal penalty avoidance; enrollment volume; 30-day readmissions avoided; 90-day home days | **90-day home days per dollar** | Penalty dollars (they cover several conditions, are lagged and are easy to game at day 30); enrollment volume (more patients is not the same as more effect) |
| **Passion** | Home visits; individual patients like Mr. D; building new services | **Getting patients home and keeping them there.** Low turnover and Ana's licensure suggest a committed team. | Colette's energy for Hospital at Home is a real passion signal. It should be pointed at the core work first `[ASSUMPTION]` |

**Denominator:** *verified 90-day home days gained per $100k of program cost.* A home day is a day alive and out of hospital, measured against a fair comparison group. Finance converts that figure into dollars. This measure also captures death, which is a competing risk the current readmission numbers ignore.

**Confirmation tests (each one has to pass before this stops being a hypothesis):**
1. A risk-adjusted 90-day comparison of eligible home discharges, excluding nursing-facility patients, shows a meaningful gap in home days.
2. Patients whose pharmacist review happened by day 10 do better than comparable patients whose review was late or missed.
3. A high-risk tier followed to day 90 has fewer readmissions in days 31–90 than the same tier under the current protocol.
4. Finance values a home day at more than it costs HFTP to produce one.

**Does it rule things out?** Yes: Hospital at Home, enrollment volume as a goal, and day-30 design. **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It commits the team to a metric that might show the program does less than everyone believes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop: leading Hospital at Home now.** Recommend that another leader own it, or defer HFTP's involvement about 12 months until confirmation tests 1 and 4 have results. Offer HFTP as the heart-failure referral partner instead.
2. **Stop: reporting enrolled vs not-enrolled as the program's effect.** The not-enrolled group includes nursing-facility discharges, patients who can't be reached by phone, and patients who declined. It is not a comparison group.
3. **Stop: graduating every patient at day 30.** Keep day 30 for low-risk patients and follow the high-risk tier to day 90.
4. **Stop: chasing 75% enrollment as a target.** Don't launch the weekend shift as a straight expansion. Roll it out in steps and measure it (see §9).
5. **Stop: claiming the drop in the federal penalty as HFTP's result.** The penalty covers several conditions and is calculated from earlier years (general knowledge of the federal program, not verified in this session).
6. **Stop: using stories and the 94% "would recommend" score as evidence of value.** Keep them as meaning, not proof. The survey had a 34% response rate (391 of 1,146), which tilts it positive.
7. **Stop: giving every patient the same protocol intensity.** Tier patients by risk.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- **The framework:** 90-day home days is the outcome; the pharmacist review by day 10 is non-negotiable; every outcome claim uses a fair comparison.
- **The freedom:** navigators, social workers and community health workers decide the contact mix within a patient's tier.

**Bureaucracy check:** None evident. A team with low turnover doesn't need compensating rules.

**Fanatical consistency (non-negotiable disciplines):**
- Contact within 48 hours.
- Visit within 7 days.
- Medication review by day 10.
- A quarterly 90-day review run by someone outside the program.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** Heart-failure patients live longer and better at home.

**Core values:**
- The patient's 90 days matter more than the hospital's 30.
- Honest numbers, especially the uncomfortable ones.
- No patient goes home on a medication list nobody has reconciled.
- Reliability over heroics.

**BHAG (one line, because this is a team with a years-long horizon):** Become the state's proof case that transitions care adds days at home rather than shifting readmissions past day 30.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A quarterly 90-day dashboard, with an adjusted comparison, owned by Quality rather than by HFTP.
- A written risk-tiering rule.
- A documented protocol.
- A budget tied to Finance-verified value per home day.
