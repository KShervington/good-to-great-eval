# Keystone Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the best capital-markets operator in residential mortgages, earning a disciplined profit on every unit of risk Keystone keeps, so that more families can get a home loan.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** matching funding to the duration of the assets Keystone holds. This is the link that broke, and nothing in the evidence shows it has been fixed.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt.
2. Stop measuring success by volume or portfolio size.
3. Stop taking guarantee credit risk that hasn't been priced, reserved for and measured.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The old business loses money whenever rates rise, and it still does. No one has stated net worth at market value, so Keystone could be underwater.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** market-value net worth, portfolio size, yield versus cost of funds, duration gap, and credit losses by loan type.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on a dossier that contains no financial statements.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone makes money in any rate environment, because it no longer bets on rates. It buys and guarantees residential mortgages at scale. It turns most of them into securities for investors and keeps only the risk it can price, fund at matching maturities and reserve against. Its edge is that it is the most skilled and most trusted operator between lenders and the capital markets. When credit tightens and others pull back, it keeps buying, and it can do so because it is well capitalised. It meets its affordable-housing mission through an explicit, funded and measured program, not through deals struck under political pressure. It runs as if the Treasury backstop did not exist. It opens its books to its overseers, and it would survive the departure of the current CEO.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Selected |

| **Passion** | Housing access for underserved households (the CEO's background) · Being the market's buyer of last resort · Engineering the financial structure | Opening homeownership to more families, including the higher-risk and lower-income households the CEO has watched being left out `[ASSUMPTION: that the staff share this]` |

**Denominator:** profit per unit of risk. This is the right denominator because risk, not volume, is what nearly broke the company.

**Confirmation tests** (this is a hypothesis, not a finding):
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Keystone was most valuable when it supplied liquidity in credit squeezes.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: unhedged holding, non-residential lending, and volume for its own sake.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It means a smaller retained portfolio, lower headline earnings in the near term, and more capital than the law requires.
- **Evidence that would confirm it:**
  - a positive margin on new business through a rate move;
  - a guarantee business that stays profitable after actual credit losses;
  - risk-adjusted return improving for four or more consecutive quarters.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt. Every new purchase is match-funded or hedged.
2. Stop measuring success by purchase volume or portfolio size. Measure profit per unit of risk instead.
3. Stop guaranteeing securities without credit pricing, reserves and loss data by product.
4. Stop giving the audit office selected data. Give it full access by default.
5. Stop expanding multifamily lending until single-family credit discipline is proven and an expert underwriter is in the seat. Cap it at its current level.
6. Stop settling the tension between mission and shareholders deal by deal under political pressure. Set an explicit mission budget and goals.
7. Stop treating the Treasury backstop as a substitute for capital.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to bring capital-markets money to home lending, so that more families can own homes.

**Core values:**
- Liquidity in every market.
- Prudence before growth.
- Openness with those who oversee us.
- The mission is funded, not improvised.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** A fairly priced home loan for every creditworthy household, in every rate environment, without ever needing the Treasury.

**Mechanisms (clock building):**
- Board-approved limits on duration gap and credit exposure.
- An independent risk function that reports directly to the board.
- An internal capital standard set higher than the legal minimum.
- An open-book agreement with the audit office.
- A monthly report on profit per unit of risk.
- A succession plan for the CEO and the head of risk.
