# Good to Great: Keystone Mortgage Association

## Verdict

**State: good but bleeding. Run as a turnaround.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Keystone should be the best in the world at pricing and managing the interest-rate and credit risk of home mortgages. It should measure itself by profit per unit of mortgage risk, and do this in service of wider home ownership.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Keystone funds long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter borrowing. Its interest margin has been negative since Year −3, when it was losing about $1M a day.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** risk measurement. There is no market-value net worth and no credit-loss data by loan type.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Funding long fixed-rate assets with short money and no hedge.
  2. Guaranteeing mortgage-backed securities without priced fees and loss reserves.
  3. Buying volume or mission loans that aren't priced to their risk.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** market-value net worth; portfolio size, yield and cost of funds; credit-loss rates.
- **Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. It rests on the dossier's qualitative facts. No financial figures were available.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone no longer makes money by betting on interest rates. It makes money by measuring, pricing and moving mortgage risk better than anyone else. Its retained portfolio is funded to match the life of its assets, so a rate spike causes only a dent. Its mortgage-backed securities business earns guarantee fees that exceed credit losses through a regional downturn. Lenders bring it their loans because its prices and processing beat the alternatives, not only because its borrowing costs are low. It remains the buyer of last resort when credit tightens. It widens access for moderate- and low-income households, but only through loans it can price honestly. Its overseers can see everything. It stays profitable across a full rate cycle and through a change of chief executive. That last test matters most, because Keystone's past "goodness" rested heavily on a federal backstop and on a falling-rate world. Neither is a skill.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Keystone will be the best in the world at pricing, distributing and managing the interest-rate and credit risk of home mortgages, measured by profit per unit of mortgage risk, so that more households can get a home loan through every rate cycle.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Selected | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Largest holder of mortgages; cheapest borrower; mortgage capital-markets risk management; multifamily lending | **Capital-markets management of residential mortgage risk** | Scale, charter, lender relationships and funding access are unmatched in this narrow world. Holding mortgages is not a skill, and its own losses prove it isn't best at that. Multifamily is too new and too different to claim. |
| **Not best at** | Originating loans; forecasting rates; commercial real estate; out-lending thrifts on adjustable loans | Explicitly ruled out | Rival G already leads on adjustable-rate portfolio lending. The charter bars non-residential work. |
| **Economic denominator** | Profit per mortgage; profit per dollar of assets; **profit per unit of mortgage risk** | **Risk-adjusted profit per unit of risk taken** (rate risk plus credit risk) | Profit per mortgage rewards volume, and volume is the trap. | **Passion** | Home ownership for families shut out; liquidity for lenders; financial engineering | **Widening home ownership**, grounded in the CEO's career-long focus on the affordability gap | Staff passion is unknown `[ASSUMPTION]`. |

**Tension to manage:** the passion circle (higher-risk borrowers) pulls against the denominator (risk-adjusted profit). The Hedgehog holds only if mission lending is priced and capped like every other risk.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. New business earns a positive risk-adjusted spread for at least four consecutive quarters.
2. Guarantee-fee income exceeds credit losses on guaranteed pools through at least one regional housing downturn.
3. The board turns down at least one profitable-looking but unhedged or under-priced deal, on the record.
4. It explains the past: Keystone won in credit squeezes through capital-markets access and lost when it carried unpriced rate risk.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt** unless they are hedged or match-funded.
2. **Stop guaranteeing securities** without a guarantee fee priced from loss data and a reserve behind it.
3. **Stop buying for volume or market share.** Every purchase must clear a risk-adjusted hurdle.
4. **Stop letting mission purchases bypass credit pricing.** Mission loans get priced, capped and tracked separately, not waved through.
5. **Stop treating the Treasury backstop as free capital.** Cheap borrowing is a privilege to protect, not a reason to grow the balance sheet.
6. **Stop expanding multifamily lending** until it has its own credit data and limits. It is a different risk from single-family loans.
7. **Stop showing overseers only chosen data.** Default to full access for auditors.
8. **Stop deferring the legacy-portfolio decision.** Set a rule for how much to keep, how to fund it, and when to sell.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to keep mortgage money flowing so that more households, in every town and every rate cycle, can own a home.

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION]` (to be confirmed with leadership):
1. Price risk honestly.
2. Candor with those who oversee the public trust.
3. Access: widen the door to ownership.
4. Prudence. The backstop is borrowed trust, not owned capital.

**BHAG (one line):** *Become the safest, lowest-cost channel of mortgage money in the financial system, and never post a loss year through a full rate cycle while widening access to home ownership.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Board-approved limits on the duration gap, credit concentration and capital.
- A risk and audit function independent of the business lines.
- Market-value net worth reported every month.
- A capital buffer set above the regulatory minimum.
- A documented succession plan.
