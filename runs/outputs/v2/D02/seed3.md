# Corvane: Good to Great

## Verdict: good, not yet great

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Corvane keeps independent restaurants' dish lines running faster than anyone else in its metros, and it wins by packing more subscribed machines into every technician's route.

**Weakest flywheel link:** cheap machines. 71% of units came from Harrow's Grill remodels at $1,140 landed, and that program ends within about a year. The next-best source costs about $3,150, which roughly doubles payback from about 12 months to about 24.

**Stop:** opening a third metro, signing Larchmont as offered, and building the $64k second bay before supply is secured.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the Harrow's supply cliff. None of the four offsite agenda items addresses it.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** churn, fully loaded technician cost, and Delmar's remaining or next-chain volume.

**Confidence:** moderate to low. It rests on one advisor memo, and technician labor is excluded from the reported margin.

Greatness potential: 18/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is the company an independent restaurant owner calls before anyone else. Its machine never costs them a service, and its technician is in the kitchen before lunch turns over. It earns that in two metros first, at a route density where speed is cheap to deliver. It does not spread across three metros or into hotel conveyor lines. Every placement has a payback that works without Harrow's Grill. Prices reflect what speed is worth. Sales run on a playbook that does not need Dev in the room. A new metro opens only after an existing one has earned it on density and response time, so expansion is a reward for discipline and never a way to restart growth.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Low-cost refurbished machine placed (incl. redeploys)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   2. Fast, reliable service (4-hour response, <3% rework)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   3. Chef word of mouth + "fast" reviews
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   4. New accounts INSIDE existing routes (density)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   5. Gross profit per technician ↑, drive time ↓, response faster
   │          │
   │          ▼
   └── 6. Cash + slack to source, price and place the next machine
```

**Weakest link:** link 1. The low-cost part of "low-cost machine" is about to break.

| Source | Landed cost | Placed cost (+ $610 refurb + $380 install) | Payback on gross profit of $172.60/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delmar / Harrow's | $1,140 | $2,130 | ~12 months |
| Auction or closure (midpoint) | ~$3,150 | ~$4,140 | ~24 months (21–27) |
| New machine | $7,650 | ~$8,030 | ~47 months |

These paybacks are before technician labor. At $289 a month, an auction-sourced unit still pays back in about 22 months. About **$331 a month** would bring it to 18 months, assuming chemicals and maintenance stay at about $101 per machine per month `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Link 1.** Secure the remaining Harrow's units, find the next source, and reprice.
2. **Links 4 and 5.** Add new accounts only inside current routes, and lift the east to the home metro's standard.

**Doom-loop check:** three signs are present.
- **A third metro funded by Series A cash** is a big program to jump-start momentum.
- **Larchmont** is a new direction: hotels, conveyor machines and service-only work.
- **East expansion** has already outrun the service standard, at 81% against 94%.

None is fatal yet. All three are "more" before the engine is proven.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop planning a third metro** until the east holds 92% or better within 4 hours, and placements outside Harrow's supply pay back in 18 months or less. Neither is true today.
2. **Decline Larchmont as structured.** The deal is three years, with a 2-hour SLA and service credits, on conveyor machines, across both metros. It puts the scarcest resource, fast-response technician time, behind a different customer with penalties attached, while the east misses its own 4-hour promise. A narrow alternative is optional: 4-hour service on Larchmont's non-conveyor units at home-metro properties, 12 months, no credits.
3. **Stop treating capex as the bottleneck.** Defer the $64k bay until a supply plan shows throughput, and not cores, is the constraint.
4. **Stop reporting 63% as the unit economics.** Report contribution per machine and gross profit per technician, with technician labor included.
5. **Stop adding accounts outside a technician's response radius**, east included. Pursue density, not footprint.
6. **Stop pricing new placements at $274.** Approve $289 now, and price toward the true replacement cost of a machine.
7. **Stop buying auction units without a payback check.** Set a ceiling on placed cost per unit.
8. **Stop routing most large closes through Dev.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** keep independent kitchens open and serving.

**Core values (proposed `[ASSUMPTION]`):**
1. Speed is a promise, not a target.
2. The kitchen's service comes first.
3. Fix it right the first time.
4. Waste nothing: refurbish, redeploy, reuse.
5. Say the hard number out loud.

**BHAG (about 15 years, full version):** *No independent restaurant Corvane serves loses a service to a dead dish machine, in every metro Corvane has earned the right to enter.*

**Mechanisms (clock building):**
- An expansion gate: a new metro opens only when existing metros hit the density and response thresholds.
- Routing and dispatch as institutional memory, so the system does not depend on Callum personally.
- Teodor's on-call rota.
- A written sales playbook.
- A sourcing pipeline that does not depend on one chain.
- A monthly unit-economics dashboard.
