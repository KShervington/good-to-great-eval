# Corvane: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Corvane is good, not yet great. The plan is to narrow and deepen in the two current metros, not to expand.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working):** Never-down dish rooms for independent restaurants, on routes dense enough to answer any call within hours. The economic denominator is *fully loaded contribution per machine-month*.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** 71% of refurbished units came cheaply through Delmar, from Harrow's Grill remodels. Harrow's says that program finishes next fiscal year. After it ends, a placed machine costs about twice as much: roughly $4,140 against $2,130 all-in `[ASSUMPTION: auction midpoint]`.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** affordable machine supply.
- **Top three to stop:**
  1. The Larchmont contract as offered.
  2. A third metro this spring.
  3. Quoting a 63% margin that leaves out field labor.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:** loaded technician cost, account churn, and how many units Delmar still has to sell.
- **Confidence: medium-low.** It rests on one advisor memo, and unit economics after labor have not been measured.

`Greatness potential: 20/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is the obvious answer for any independent restaurant in its metros that cannot afford a dead dish machine on a Friday night. It answers 95%+ of emergencies within four hours in *every* territory, not only at home. That speed is cheap to deliver because the routes are dense: many machines per technician in tight clusters, so drives are short and idle time is the buffer that keeps the promise. Its machines come from several refurb pipelines that it controls, bought under a landed-cost ceiling. Its contribution per machine-month is positive, measured monthly, and rising with density. It enters a new metro only after the last one has proved the model. It says no to hotel conveyor contracts, new-machine sales and chemical-only deals, however good the revenue looks.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't sign Larchmont as offered.** The terms are 3 years, 2-hour response with service credits, on conveyor units Corvane doesn't yet service. Reasons are in Gate 6.
2. **Don't open a third metro this spring.** Gate it on the east proving out (see Gate 10 thresholds).
3. **Stop reporting 63% gross margin as the unit economics.** Field labor sits in opex. Report fully loaded contribution per machine-month by territory.
4. **Stop treating 37% technician idle time as waste.** Some of it is the buffer that buys the 4-hour promise `[ASSUMPTION]`. Use it for density and preventive maintenance, not for third-party SLAs.
5. **Stop buying machines without a landed-cost ceiling.** Proposed rule: all-in cost must be at most 18 months of gross profit `[ASSUMPTION: payback target]`. At $289 × 63% = $182, that gives about $3,277 all-in, so landed cost can be at most about $2,287. Most auction units ($2,700–3,600) fail this test.
6. **Stop signing accounts far outside existing clusters.** Each one dilutes density.
7. **Don't sell new machines, service conveyor units, or take service-only contracts.**
8. **Dev stops personally closing most large accounts.** The hand-off begins this quarter.

**Gate 6: Discipline (PASS).**

*The Larchmont reasoning.* The contract either uses idle hours or it adds dedicated staff:
- **If it uses idle hours,** a credit-backed 2-hour SLA takes dispatch priority over restaurant calls and erodes "fast," the word the brand is built on.
- **If it adds 3 dedicated techs,** the main rationale for it disappears and Corvane has started a second business: hotel conveyor service. That business doesn't use the refurb edge, serves a different customer, and needs a different certification.

Its revenue per machine (about $837 a month) is attractive, which is exactly why it is the temptation.

*Framework boundaries:*
- Two metros only.
- Independent restaurants only.
- Undercounter and door-type machines only.
- The landed-cost ceiling.
- Cluster-first selling.

*Freedom inside the framework:* technicians decide on-site fixes, Marguerite owns refurb standards, and dispatch owns prioritization.

*Fanatical consistency:*
- Report 4-hour response weekly by territory.
- Never skip quarterly preventive maintenance.
- Check every machine against the cost ceiling.

*Bureaucracy check:* no compensating rules were found.
