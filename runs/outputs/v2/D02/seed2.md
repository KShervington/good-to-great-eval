# Corvane: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept:** Corvane keeps independent restaurants' dish lines running faster than anyone else in a dense metro, and earns more per technician with every account it adds to a route.

**Weakest flywheel link:** cheap machines. About 71% of units came from Delmar's pulls out of Harrow's Grill, and Harrow's says its remodel program finishes by the end of its next fiscal year.

**Stop doing:**
1. Opening a third metro in the spring.
2. Taking Larchmont as offered (2-hour conveyor-unit service with credits).
3. Quoting a 63% "gross margin" that leaves out technician labor.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** once Delmar's supply is gone, putting a machine in service costs about $4,140 instead of $2,130. Payback before labor roughly doubles, from about 12 months to about 24.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** loaded technician cost, churn and contract length, and refurb-bay capacity.

**Confidence:** moderate. It rests on a single advisor memo. The labor and supply-runway figures are inferred.

Greatness potential: 22/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Corvane is the dish-machine utility for independent restaurants in a few dense metros.
- Every account is a short drive from a technician carrying the right parts.
- The 4-hour promise is met almost every time in every metro, and 2 hours is becoming normal.
- Machines come from a pipeline: several chain-remodel sources, trade-ins, and Corvane's own redeployed fleet. One lucky contract no longer carries supply.
- A service-only plan covers owners who already have a machine. It fills technician hours without tying up capital.
- The company knows its fully loaded contribution per technician every month.
- It opens a new metro only after the existing ones clear a written bar.
- Chefs recommend it without Dev in the room.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  ┌─► 1. Fastest response (4-hr met; "fast" in reviews)
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   2. Reviews + chef word of mouth (4.8 / 212)
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   3. More independents signing in the SAME zones
  │          │            (+ service-only plan for owned machines)
  │          ▼
  │   4. Route density ↑ (machines per technician)
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   5. Contribution per technician ↑   ◄── denominator
  │          │
  │          ▼
  │   6. Reinvest: sourced machines, parts stock,
  │      deliberate slack in technician hours
  └──────────┘
```

**Weakest link:** step 6. Machine supply is about to get twice as expensive. Step 5 is also unproven, because labor isn't measured.

**Current push:**
1. Secure the rest of the Harrow's/Delmar supply and open two new sourcing channels.
2. Fill east to density and get its response time from 81% to ≥90%.
3. Pilot a service-only plan, which adds density without needing a machine.

**Doom-loop check:** this is a real risk. The offsite agenda pulls four ways at once: a new metro, a new segment (hotels), new capital spending (the bay), and a new price. A third metro and Larchmont are both large programs meant to jump-start momentum before the existing flywheel is proven in east.

**Truth climate:** good signs include Dev and Callum disagreeing openly, and an advisor who writes candid pre-reads. The red flag is in that same memo: Delmar appears in §3 and the end of Harrow's remodel program appears in §6, and nobody connected them. A monthly "supply runway in months" figure would make that connection impossible to miss.

**Charisma check:** two forces could drown out the labor number. One is Dev's relationships with chefs. The other is board enthusiasm for "the best-quality revenue on the table."

**Larchmont, the honest counter-case:** this deal is not foolish. It brings $412k a year (about $837 per machine per month), needs no machines, and Larchmont's ops lead is asking for Corvane's people by name.

The case against it:
- A 2-hour SLA with service credits will push hotel calls ahead of restaurant calls in both metros, including east, which already misses its 4-hour promise one time in five.
- It uses up the slack that produces "fast."
- It puts about 25% of revenue in one customer, on a machine type where the manufacturer has the edge.
- It lands in the same year as the supply cliff.

**What would flip the call:** if the unit-economics work shows that restaurant subscriptions can't reach positive contribution after Delmar, then Corvane's real hedgehog may be service, not equipment. Larchmont would then become a deliberate test of a pivot, not a deal taken because of an LOI deadline.

**Faith statement:** independent restaurants are opening (+6%), the alternatives are slow or lock customers in, and the word customers use for Corvane is "fast." That promise, delivered on dense routes, can outlast the cheap machines that started it.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. The kitchen doesn't stop.
2. Count honestly: every late call, every cost.
3. Fix it right the first time.
4. Reuse before buying new.

**Core purpose:** keep independent restaurants open, clean and ready for inspection.

**BHAG (about 15 years):** no independent restaurant in any Corvane metro loses a dinner service to its dish machine.

**Mechanisms that work without the current stewards:**
- A response dashboard by metro, visible to the whole company.
- A monthly fully loaded contribution report per technician.
- Written criteria for entering a new metro or segment.
- A sourcing pipeline that always shows at least 12 months of supply.
- A sales playbook that doesn't depend on Dev.
