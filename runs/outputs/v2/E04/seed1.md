# Northfield-Brandt Corporation: Good to Great run

**Verdict: the consumer core is close to great; the company as a whole is not yet great.** The branded consumer business behaves like a winner. The airline, the commodity mills and an untested CEO succession are what hold the whole company back.

- **Hedgehog (working):** Out-invent larger rivals to own the leading brand in each everyday absorbent-care need, from infancy to old age, measured by profit per consumer brand.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** reinvestment. Capital is going to the airline and the mills while Rival P outspends Northfield-Brandt on R&D and advertising.
- **Stop doing:** (1) funding the $120M airline expansion; (2) owning newsprint and printing-paper mills; (3) building European plants without a path to brand leadership.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** a 64-year-old chief executive with 20 years in the role is about to hand a three-business conglomerate to a president who has been in his job for two months.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** profit and return on capital by segment and by brand, current category shares, and European returns.
- **Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on market positions and company-level returns. No segment profits were available.

Greatness potential: 58/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Northfield-Brandt is a focused branded consumer company. It owns the leading brand in a small number of everyday absorbent-care categories: facial and bath tissue, diapers and training pants, feminine care, and adult incontinence. It wins against a larger rival by inventing better products for each stage of life, not by outspending that rival. Every dollar of capital and every hour of senior attention goes to the categories where it can be #1. The airline, aviation services and commodity paper operations have been separated into independent companies, where they can each pursue their own path. A new chief executive runs the company on the same discipline the outgoing one used when he sold the mills 20 years ago. The test that it has become great is that this discipline survives his departure.

**Weakest link: (6), reinvestment.** Rival P has deeper R&D and advertising budgets. Northfield-Brandt cannot close that gap while it funds a $120M airline expansion and recurring reinvestment in the mills.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Free up capital by separating the airline and the commodity paper businesses, and redirect it into absorbent-materials R&D and launch support.
2. Choose European categories by their path to leadership, not by plant capacity.

**Doom-loop check:** the core loop has been consistent for 20 years, so it passes. There are three doom-loop signs at the edges:

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop funding the airline expansion.** Freeze the uncommitted part of the $120M and separate the airline (spin-off or sale) [ASSUMPTION: some of the spend may already be contractually committed].
2. **Stop owning aviation services.** Separate them together with the airline.
3. **Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.** Exit them.
4. **Stop housing tobacco, technical and business papers inside a consumer company.** Spin them out so they can be run for their own markets.
5. **Stop approving European plants on capacity logic.** Each plant must show a credible path to #1 or #2 brand position in its category and geography.
6. **Stop launching premium-priced niche products for limited uses.** This is the lesson of the anti-viral tissue.
7. **Stop turning support functions into profit centers.** The airline began exactly that way.
8. **Stop fighting Rival P on advertising volume.** Fight on product superiority and patents.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Review profit by brand every quarter.
- Approve no capital outside absorbent personal care.
- Every launch must answer: is this an everyday mass need, is the product better, and is the result patentable?

**Framework boundaries:**
- *Freedom:* brand teams own innovation, pricing and launches within absorbent personal care.
- *Responsibility:* each brand owns its profit per brand and its share target.

**Bureaucracy check:** the finance staff's "profit center" logic was undisciplined thought dressed up as rigor. Replace it with return-on-capital discipline measured against the hedgehog.
