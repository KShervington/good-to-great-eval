## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tolliver is a single-format company. It runs one superstore system, and that system is repeatable to the point of boredom. It enters a region only when it can open several stores together and dominate local share, advertising and distribution there. It funds each new region from the profits of the last one instead of borrowing to keep up with Rival S. It no longer runs leased departments, mail-order, audio boutiques, or acquired chains it can't convert. It knows the profit of every store and every region monthly, and its chief executive keeps asking hard questions but now acts on the answers. On the three tests of great, Tolliver would show superior returns, would leave a regional hole if it vanished, and would keep working after the founder's family steps back. `[ASSUMPTION: the superstore format is economically superior; unproven until format P&Ls exist]`

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop buying chains** for their tax credits or for instant presence in a market. Any acquisition must be convertible to the superstore format, or it doesn't happen.
2. **Stop operating the West Coast leased departments.** Wind them down on a defined schedule.
3. **Stop entering a market with one or two stores.** Set a minimum cluster size per region `[ASSUMPTION: e.g., 3–5 stores]`.
4. **Stop opening new mid-sized stores** pending the format P&L. Treat existing ones as conversion candidates, not as a growth vehicle.
5. **Stop funding the big-city chain open-endedly.** It gets a fix-or-exit test with a fixed deadline and a fixed capital ceiling. Get tax counsel first on whether exiting affects the use of the $36.5 million in tax-loss credits.
6. **Stop expanding faster than internal cash flow allows,** even when Rival S moves first.
7. **Stop leaving the format question open.** Asking questions has done its job; the board should commit in writing.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** a monthly store and region P&L reviewed by the CEO; one written superstore playbook; a written cluster-entry checklist (minimum store count, capital source, payback); and a written debt ceiling.

**Framework boundaries:** the format, the brand and region selection are set centrally. Store managers have freedom on staffing, local merchandising and service within the playbook.

**Bureaucracy check:** this is unknown from the dossier. Watch for approval layers created to supervise the acquired chain. Those layers are a symptom that the wrong people are in the seat.
