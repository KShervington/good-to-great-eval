# Kestrel Stores: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Kestrel becomes the place Americans buy complex, high-ticket technology with confidence. That means expert advice backed by best-in-class store systems, measured by operating profit per store, region by region.

**Weakest flywheel link:** whether customers will pay for expert advice. Rival B's self-service model has been winning share, so this link is unproven.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Chasing size targets ($15B, 800 stores).
2. Stand-alone appliance stores and appliance price wars.
3. Side ventures that pull management attention. Separate the used-car business.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival B leads Kestrel on sales, earnings, profit per store and share. Kestrel is no longer best at its own core.

**Numbers that would change this:** electronics profit per store and margin trend versus Rival B, appliance margins, store age and remodel cost, and used-car profitability.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on headline public figures only, with no margin or per-store profit data.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kestrel stops trying to be the biggest electronics chain and becomes the most trusted and most profitable per store. It sells the purchases where advice matters most: computers, home theater, and new digital categories. Its counselors are paid to get the customer the right solution, not just to close a sale. Its distribution and store-operations systems keep those counselors stocked and supported better than anyone's. It wins region by region on profit per store, not nationally on store count. Capital goes into remodeling the existing base, not into inventing formats or opening categories where it cannot be best. The used-car business runs as its own company with its own leaders and its own Hedgehog Concept. The board and the new CEO run Kestrel on a short scorecard that shows the gap to Rival B honestly every quarter.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Kestrel is where people buy complex, high-ticket technology with confidence, because expert advice runs on top of the best store systems in the industry. It measures success by operating profit per store in every region it serves.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Largest electronics chain; lowest prices; proprietary formats; big-box retail of any considered purchase; expert-led selling of complex tech | **Expert-led selling of complex technology, supported by distribution and store-ops systems that are well regarded in the industry** | *Largest chain*: Rival B already leads. *Lowest price / self-service*: copying Rival B makes Kestrel a permanent #2. *Format invention*: the disc-rental venture showed Kestrel cannot get other retailers to adopt its formats. *Appliances*: home-improvement chains are attacking the category on price. |
| **Economic engine** | Sales per store; comp growth; total EPS; profit per store; profit per region | **Operating profit per store, tracked by region** | Revenue and store count are the metrics behind the missed $15B / 800-store goals. |
| **Passion** | Growth and scale (the historic culture); retail operations excellence; helping customers through complex purchases | **Operational excellence in service of the customer's purchase** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from the systems reputation and commissioned-expert model; not directly evidenced]` | Passion for growth itself, which has been driving the doom-loop behaviors. |

**Denominator:** operating profit per store, by region. It is chosen because it is the metric on which Rival B leads, so it measures the real gap directly.

**Why this is uncomfortable:**
- It rules out the 800-store story.
- It probably rules out appliances.
- It forces the used-car business out of the parent.
- It requires redesigning the commission model rather than defending it or abandoning it.

**Confirmation tests (12 months):**
1. In the 30–35 remodeled test stores, does expert-led selling produce higher profit per store than matched control stores? (Counselor pay will be redesigned to reward customer outcomes and margin, not just volume.)
2. In those stores, do customers pay for advice? Look at measurable attach, margin, or repeat-purchase premiums.
3. Does Kestrel's profit-per-store gap to Rival B narrow in at least one region?

If test 1 fails, this Hedgehog Concept is wrong. Kestrel would then have to choose between being a disciplined #2 on Rival B's model and finding a different narrow world to be best in.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Expert counselors who understand complex products
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    2. Confident customers buy the full solution
   │       (product + accessories + installation/service) and come back
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    3. Higher operating PROFIT PER STORE (denominator)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    4. Reinvest in counselor skill/pay and remodels of the
   │       densest, best regions (not new store count)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    5. Regional density + distribution systems → best in-stock
   │       and assortment in complex categories at lower cost
   │              │
   └──────────────┘  (draws more customers to the experts)
```

**Weakest link:** step 1 to step 2. No evidence yet shows customers prefer Kestrel's commissioned advice to Rival B's self-service. Rival B's share gains suggest the opposite for at least part of the market.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Turn the planned remodel program into a real format experiment, with control stores and a decision date.
2. Remove the attention drag: separate used cars, and freeze stand-alone appliance stores.

**Doom-loop check:** several signs are present.
- **New direction after new direction:** the disc-rental format, the used-car chain, now stand-alone appliance stores.
- **A big program to jump-start momentum:** about $233M spent on disc-rental.
- **Goals set by size:** $15B revenue, 800 stores.
- **Reaction without understanding:** appliance price battles started by others.
- **The concern dismissed with a growth number:** the CEO's "great shape" answer.
- **The next likely doom-loop move:** a panicked, wholesale copy of Rival B's self-service format.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop setting size goals** (revenue, store count). Replace them with profit per store and return on capital.
2. **Stop inventing proprietary formats and products.** The disc-rental venture cost about $233M in development and promotion, and more than $200M in losses and charges across two years. Kestrel is a retailer, not a format owner.
3. **Stop opening stand-alone appliance stores, and stop matching home-improvement chains' appliance price cuts.** Default to shrinking or exiting appliances unless floor-space profitability proves otherwise.
4. **Stop running the used-car business on the same leadership's attention.** Separate it.
5. **Stop treating comparable-store and EPS growth as proof of health.** Report the profit-per-store gap to Rival B instead, internally and to investors.
6. **Stop deferring the commission / self-service decision.** Decide it through the test, with a date, not by drift or by sudden imitation.
7. **Stop giving floor space to low-advice commodity categories** where Kestrel adds nothing over a self-service rival `[ASSUMPTION: category margins not in dossier; music and video are likely candidates]`.
