# Good to Great: Bellmoor 250

## Verdict

**Gate 0 FAIL. Bellmoor 250 is a one-off event, so this run produces no Hedgehog Concept and no flywheel.**

- **Why:** The charter dissolves the commission about 90 days after its final audit, and every staff contract ends with it. Without a horizon of years, nothing can compound.
- **Hedgehog Concept:** none. Forcing one onto a ten-day event would be theater.
- **Weakest link (in delivery, not a flywheel):** Calder Mills owes $160,000 in April. Its champion has retired, and its Bellmoor operations are "under review."
- **Stop now:** (1) moving toward the $96,000 app without full funding and a permanent owner; (2) planning around 180,000 visits, a figure with no method behind it; (3) debating "Bellmoor Days" inside the commission.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the tall-ship final deposit (March) and the end of loan cover (May) fall on either side of an unconfirmed $160,000.
- **What would change the answer:** a council decision to charter a standing events body, which would be a new subject the framework can work on. Also Calder's written confirmation of the second installment.
- **Confidence:** high on the FAIL, because it rests on the dissolution clause. Medium on the risk ranking.

`Greatness potential: 5/100`

---

## 1. Why the run stops at Gate 0

Collins built his framework for endeavors that last. The Hedgehog Concept is a commitment you keep for years. The flywheel produces nothing until it has been pushed through many turns. Built to Last and How the Mighty Fall both assume the subject keeps going. Bellmoor 250 has one turn of the wheel: ten days in June. Its steward is chartered to stop existing shortly after.

That is not a flaw. A commemoration is supposed to end. It does mean that the third test of great, **lasting endurance beyond one person, one product or one lucky break**, is ruled out by the charter. The chair asked for the framework to be applied "properly." Applying it properly means saying it is the wrong instrument for this subject. Producing a Hedgehog sentence, a BHAG and a 10–30-year flywheel for a body that dissolves within about two years would distract from the decisions that actually decide whether June succeeds.

## 2. What to use instead: a delivery-and-risk plan

The better-fitting method is ordinary project delivery discipline: a cash-flow scenario plan with go/no-go checkpoints, tiered scope (must / should / could), a pre-mortem, and a legacy hand-off plan. Some Collins ideas still carry over to a one-shot project, and they are used below: confronting brutal facts, keeping a stop-doing list, and being rigorous about who fills key seats.

**Faith statement (Stockdale, adapted):** Bellmoor 250 can deliver a fine commemoration and leave real assets behind, provided it plans as though the second Calder installment may never arrive.

### Stop-doing list (project scope)

1. **Stop treating 180,000 visits and 25 million impressions as targets.** Replace them with a documented counting method and a low/medium/high range. Budget on the low case.
2. **Stop deferring the app.** Set the decision rule now: the app goes ahead only if (a) outside money fully funds it and (b) a body that will outlive the commission commits in writing to maintain it. Otherwise decline it in January. `[ASSUMPTION]` A cheaper alternative worth pricing: audio clips from the oral-history archive at QR-coded street markers, hosted by the library, which will still exist after the commission.
3. **Stop debating Bellmoor Days inside the commission.** Give the council a hand-off package instead (see below) and leave the decision with the council.
4. **Stop waiting until April to learn Calder's position.** Go to Calder and its parent now.
5. **Stop paying non-refundable deposits ahead of confirmed revenue** without first knowing the cancellation terms.

### Legacy hand-off (the honest answer to "Bellmoor Days")

The things that could last are the volunteer list, the sponsor relationships, the oral-history archive, the symphonic work, and the lessons learned. Package them for the council and the library before dissolution: contact lists (with consent), a delivery post-mortem, actual attendance figures with the method used, and a cost breakdown. That gives a future standing body a real Gate 1 baseline and avoids a second commission starting from a workshop guess the way this one did.
