## What "great" can honestly mean here

The commission cannot meet Collins's third test, lasting endurance. It can still do something better than "good": **deliver the ten days excellently, close the books cleanly, and hand its durable assets to permanent stewards.** Those assets are the archive, the symphonic score, the time capsule, and real attendance records. Notably, real records are exactly what the 200th anniversary failed to leave behind.

Some of the Collins habits the chair values still transfer here as tools, but not as a framework run:

- **Brutal facts.** The Program chair asked where the 180,000 target came from. That is exactly the behavior to reward.
- **Stop-doing discipline.** It applies directly to the app and to the Bellmoor Days proposal.
- **"First who."** It shows up in the Calder relationship, which ran through a single person.

Recommendation: spend the next meeting on the scenario budget below rather than on the Hedgehog chapter.

### Facts the review must face

| Fact | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Calder's $160k is at risk | The champion has retired and no successor is named. The parent is consolidating and Bellmoor operations are "under review." Calder's pledge is $270k, which is 63% of all sponsorship and 20% of the budget. The outstanding $160k is 12% of the budget. | **Critical** |
| Cash commitments come due before the cash arrives | The tall-ship final deposit is due in March, the Calder installment in April, and loan coverage ends in May. `[ASSUMPTION]` These all refer to the months just before opening. | **Critical** |

| The legacy is real but incomplete | 141 of 240 oral-history interviews done (59%). The library will host the archive permanently. | Medium (on track) |

### Next 90 days

1. **Secure the Calder money.** *Owners: chair and ED.* Meet Calder's current senior leader now rather than waiting for a successor to be named. Ask for written reconfirmation of the $160k and request early payment. *Owner: treasurer.* Confirm whether the pledge is a signed agreement.
2. **Build a three-scenario budget.** *Owners: treasurer and ED.* Model Calder paying in full, paying half, and paying nothing. Agree the order of cuts in advance. Set a go/no-go rule before the tall-ship deposit. Ask the town whether the loan can be extended past May. Start a replacement-sponsor search now.
3. **Rebuild the attendance estimate from the bottom up.** *Owners: Program chair and ED.* Estimate event by event (pageant seats, parade route, harbor viewing, fireworks), and separate unique attendees from repeat visits. Restate the target with its method, then re-derive the ticket and merchandise projections.
4. **Decide the app in January, with conditions.** *Owners: steering committee; youth chair to secure the owner.* Recommendation: decline it as specified unless two conditions hold:
   - a permanent owner, such as the library or school district, commits in writing to host and maintain it;
   - the $76k gap is new money, not the contingency that the Calder risk requires.

   If the app goes ahead, make the decision after the Calder status is known. Schedule and ticketing can run on existing off-the-shelf tools.
5. **Hand off the legacy.** *Owners: chair and Legacy chair.* Formalize the library agreement for the archive. Recommendation: decline Bellmoor Days as commission work. Instead, prepare a handover package for the council: volunteer contacts (with their consent), sponsor relationships, vendors, an after-action report, and measured attendance data.
