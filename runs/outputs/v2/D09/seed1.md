# Larder Line: Good to Great

**Verdict: Larder Line is good, not yet great, and its biggest source of food may be shrinking.**

- **Hedgehog (working):** Larder Line is Corbridge's cold chain for rescued food. It moves the dairy, meat and fresh produce no one else can, the same day, to agencies that will get it onto plates.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Freshway Eastside's pounds per pickup fell 36% (412 → 263) after the markdown app arrived. Freshway supplies 44% of all pounds, and the app goes chainwide next year.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** there is no proof the food is eaten. Pounds are counted at pickup, households aren't counted, and agencies compost surplus.
- **Top three to stop:** the café grant application; buying the third van now (fix van #2 instead); the composting route and backpack pilot.
- **Numbers that would change this:** Eastside month-by-month yields and pickup times against the app's start date; agency use rates; whether any other Corbridge group runs refrigerated vans.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on five interviews and one treasurer's table.

Greatness potential: 38/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larder Line is the cold chain that Corbridge's food system can't do without. When a grocer, wholesaler or institutional kitchen has dairy, meat or fresh produce it can't sell in time, including whatever is left after Freshway's markdown app has had its turn, Larder Line's trained drivers and refrigerated vans move it the same day to the agency that told them that morning it can use it. Larder Line knows, and can show funders, how many households that food reached and how little of it was wasted at the other end. No single donor supplies more than about a third of its food. Its operations and donor relationships run without depending on the founder. It says no to good ideas that don't use the cold chain, and it can name an organization that would do them better.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Reliable same-day cold-chain pickup
   │       (trained drivers, reefer vans, on time)
   │                    │
   │                    ▼
   │    2. Donors trust us with high-value perishables
   │       (dairy, meat, quality produce) ◄── SUPPLY AT RISK
   │                    │
   │                    ▼
   │    3. Matched same-day to agencies that can use it
   │       (agency tells us what it can move today)
   │                    │
   │                    ▼
   │    4. More USABLE PERISHABLE POUNDS PER ROUTE-HOUR
   │       reach households            ◄── DENOMINATOR
   │                    │
   │                    ▼
   │    5. Proof of impact: households reached,
   │       % used, cost per usable pound ◄── WEAKEST LINK
   │                    │
   │                    ▼
   └─── 6. Funding, donor pride and volunteer loyalty
           → more cold-chain capacity and trained drivers
```

The loop closes. Today it turns on pounds at pickup and skips steps 3 and 5. That is why the household field went blank and why Agnes composts produce "nobody asks" about.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Secure step 2.** Find out the real cause of the Eastside drop. Get ahead of the chainwide app rollout with Freshway, and start adding cold-chain donors.
2. **Build step 5 by way of step 3.** Ask agencies each day or week what they can use and what they composted. Report households reached.

**Doom-loop check:**
- Three new programs in two years plus a café proposal follow a "the answer should be yes" pattern, which is undisciplined pursuit of more.
- If pounds fall next year, the temptation will be to jump to a big new program for a new funder story. That is the classic doom-loop move.
- The third van could be a well-meant attempt to buy momentum before the supply question is answered.
- No acquisitions, no repeated reversals of direction.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the café grant application** as a Larder Line program.
2. **Stop the third-van purchase for now.** Fix van #2 and decide on a third van at a set checkpoint.
3. **Stop the composting route.** It uses refrigerated capacity on work outside the Hedgehog Concept.
4. **Stop running the backpack pilot** after the school year ends.
5. **Stop the gleaning crew** until agencies show they need more produce.
6. **Stop treating pounds at pickup as the measure of success.** Keep reporting it, but stop leaving the household field blank and calling pickup weight "the industry standard."
7. **Stop explaining away the Eastside drop.** It should be tested, not assumed.
8. **Stop the automatic yes.** New asks pass a filter: does this move usable perishable pounds per route-hour? If not, refer the asker to someone who does it better.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Same-day delivery, every time.
- Drivers text the agency before arrival (already practiced, so keep it).
- Temperature integrity on every cold load.
- Monthly yield by donor site reviewed by the board, with any site down more than 15% flagged.
- Agencies are asked what they can use before the vans are loaded.

**Framework:** Kofi and the drivers have full autonomy over routes, timing and agency matching, inside three fixed rules:
1. Cold chain first.
2. Same day.
3. Deliver what the agency says it can use.

**Bureaucracy check:** none found. The training system is what lets drivers work without tight supervision.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** get good food that would be thrown away onto Corbridge tables.
- **Core values:**
  1. No edible food wasted at either end.
  2. Reliability: same day, and we text before we come.
  3. Bring what recipients can use, not what is easy to weigh.
  4. Volunteers are partners, not labor.
  5. Every donated dollar and hour is accounted for.
- **BHAG (10–20 years):** *Every pound of edible dairy, meat and fresh produce that Corbridge food businesses can't sell reaches a household within a day.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Monthly yield-by-site report.
  - Agency use-and-households check-in.
  - A written driver training curriculum.
  - A named deputy who holds donor relationships at the operations level.
  - The Hedgehog filter written into board policy for new programs.
  - A concentration cap: no single donor above about 35% of pounds within two years [ASSUMPTION target].
