## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Larder Line is the organization Corbridge's grocers, wholesalers and processors trust with their most perishable surplus. That includes milk, meat, eggs and produce, and it includes whatever Freshway's markdown app fails to sell by day's end. Larder Line moves that food on a documented, temperature-safe cold chain to agencies that have asked for it and can distribute it. It knows, and can prove, how many unduplicated households that food reaches and how little of it ends up in an agency's compost bin. That proof funds the next van and the next driver. It says "no" more often than it does today. It no longer runs a café, a school backpack route or a compost run. Its donor base is broad enough that no single chain's business decision can halve its supply. And it would keep running if Teddy stepped away tomorrow, because the donor agreements, the data and the routes are institutional rather than personal.

**Denominator:** *usable perishable pounds delivered per route-hour.* "Usable" means requested and distributed, not composted at the agency. This makes the van and driver hour the unit of scarcity, which is the true constraint, and it penalizes moving bread nobody asked for.

**Why it would be uncomfortable:**
- The headline pounds figure will likely fall.
- Three programs end.
- The board chair's café proposal falls outside the Hedgehog.
- "Yes" stops being the default answer.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. A scan of Corbridge confirms no other rescue group runs refrigerated pickup. If one does, the "best at" circle narrows further.
2. An agency survey confirms dairy and protein are the largest unmet need. Agnes's "more dairy, less bread" is one data point so far.
3. Cold-chain pounds reach more households per route-hour than dry pounds.
4. At least one funder responds to the household-and-usable-pounds story with renewed or larger support.

**Current push (next two turns):**
- **Turn 4:** start measuring what agencies actually distribute and how many households they serve. Without this, turn 5 runs on the pounds story, which the county fund has already stopped rewarding.
- **Turn 2:** secure perishable supply against the markdown app. Ask Freshway for its leftovers and recruit new perishable donors.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are present.
- **New directions in quick succession:** gleaning, backpacks and composting were added in two years, and the café would make a fourth.
- **A big program to jump-start momentum:** the café is framed as "the next chapter."
- **A capacity purchase as the answer:** a $71,000 van, at about 11% of the budget, approved before anyone has diagnosed the supply dip.
- **Reaction without understanding:** the Eastside dip was attributed to construction with no test.

No single sign is fatal, but together they look like the start of a doom loop.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't apply for the $150,000 café grant as a Larder Line program.** It sits outside the Hedgehog. Its best comparable broke even only in year 3. And it would draw on the same rescued supply that is shrinking. Instead, tell the city's economic development office and Renata that Larder Line will supply surplus to a separately run café.
2. **Don't buy the third van ($71,000) now.** Fix van #2's transmission ($6,800). Revisit the third van once the Freshway question is answered: a chainwide app may cut volume, not raise it.
3. **End the Monday composting van route.**
4. **Hand off the weekend backpack pilot** at the end of the school year.
5. **Pause the gleaning crew** until agencies confirm they can move more produce.
6. **Stop leading with pounds at pickup** as the headline. Report usable pounds and households instead.
7. **Stop chasing bread volume** beyond what agencies request.
8. **Stop saying yes by default.** Every new ask gets a written answer to one question: *does this add usable perishable pounds per route-hour?*

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Temperature-safe handling every time.
- Drivers text agencies before they arrive.
- The donor-site table goes to the board quarterly.
- Agencies report monthly.

**Framework, and the freedom inside it:**
- Kofi and the route captains can change routes, timing and donor mix freely, as long as it serves the denominator.
- Nothing new that uses a van, a driver or staff time starts without passing the Hedgehog test.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rule in the dossier compensates for having the wrong people. The gap runs the other way: there are too few rules on what gets started.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Food safety and reliability, every run.
2. Dignity and respect for agencies and the families they serve.
3. Drivers and volunteers are the asset.
4. We measure what reaches people, not what we pick up.
5. We say no to protect the yes.

**Core purpose:** to make sure good food that would be wasted feeds the people of Corbridge.

**BHAG (10–15 years):** no edible perishable surplus from a Corbridge food business gets thrown away while a Corbridge pantry is short of protein and dairy.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Written donor agreements, so relationships aren't personal to Teddy.
- A quarterly board dashboard with red-flag thresholds (see the Risk Register).
- A monthly agency reporting loop.
- Volunteer route captains under Kofi.
- A written Hedgehog test for every new program.
- A named successor path for both the ED and the operations lead.
