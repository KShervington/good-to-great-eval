## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Larder Line is the cold-chain backbone of Corbridge's food safety net. Its reefer vans and veteran volunteer drivers move the hardest food to rescue (meat, dairy, fresh produce) from wherever it is surplus to the agency that has asked for it, the same day. Its supply no longer depends on one grocer's shrink. It pulls large perishable loads from wholesalers, distributors and processors, and it takes whatever Freshway's markdown app can't sell. It measures what agencies actually serve and how many households that reaches, not just what it lifts off a dock. It reports those numbers to funders before they ask. It does one thing, and no one else in the city can replace it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't apply for the $150,000 café grant as a Larder Line program.** The grant would expand the budget by 24% into a new business: food service plus workforce development. Larder Line has no best-in-world claim there. If Renata pursues it independently, offer a supplier agreement.
2. **Don't buy the third van ($71,000) this year.** Fix Van #2's transmission ($6,800). Without it, half the cold chain is down. Revisit the third van once used-pounds data and new upstream donors justify it.
3. **Stop running the Monday compost route** in Larder Line's van.
4. **Stop the Friday backpack route** after this school year, with a handoff.
5. **Stop the gleaning crew** after this season.
6. **Stop reporting pounds-at-pickup as the headline** and stop leaving "unduplicated households" blank.
7. **Stop taking every bread pickup by default.** Thin out the lowest-yield bakery stops and send bread only to agencies that want it.
8. **Stop treating "yes" as the default.** New asks must pass three questions:
   - Is it perishable?
   - Does it need the cold chain?
   - Will the receiving agency actually serve it?

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** good food shouldn't go to waste while neighbors go without.

**Core values:**
1. Same day, every time.
2. Food goes where it will be eaten.
3. Cold chain is sacred. Food safety is never traded for volume.
4. Drivers and agencies are partners, not inputs.
5. Tell the truth with numbers.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** no edible perishable surplus in Corbridge's food supply chain reaches a landfill while a Corbridge household goes without protein, dairy or fresh produce.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Written cold-chain and driver-training standard operating procedures.
- Monthly yield dashboard (Harold).
- Agency usage log (Kofi).
- Three-question program filter.
- Board conflict-of-interest policy.
- ED succession plan.
