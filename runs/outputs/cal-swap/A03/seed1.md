# Keystone Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Keystone will be the best in the world at pricing, managing and passing on the risks of residential mortgages through the capital markets, and it will measure success as profit per unit of mortgage risk, not per loan bought.*

Greatness potential: 35/100

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** credit-risk pricing. Keystone is moving its risk from interest rates (a risk it understands and is now shedding) to credit (guarantees, adjustable-rate loans, apartment loans). It has no loss-rate data by loan type, and its overseer cannot fully see its books.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter-term debt.
  2. Stop measuring success by purchase volume or "largest buyer" status.
  3. Stop scaling any loan type or guarantee before its credit losses are modeled and priced.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the core model, borrowing short to hold long fixed-rate loans, has lost money since Year −3. At the CEO's arrival it was losing about $1M a day, or roughly $365M a year. Nobody in the dossier knows whether Keystone's market-value net worth is positive.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  1. Market-value net worth.
  2. Remaining fixed-rate portfolio size, with its yield and cost of funds (the duration gap).
  3. Credit-loss rates by loan type.
  4. Net income for the last 3 years.

  If market-value net worth is deeply negative, Gate 1 drops to FAIL and the work becomes survival work, not greatness work.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone no longer bets on interest rates. Each mortgage it touches is either match-funded or securitized, and any rate risk left over is held within hard limits that are published. It makes money as the most trusted pricer and distributor of mortgage risk: it guarantees and packages loans for lenders, it charges for credit risk according to measured loss experience, and it holds only the risk it can price better than anyone else. Its earnings stay steady through rate cycles. When lenders can't sell loans anywhere else, Keystone is still buying, and that is its distinctive role. Its low-income lending is deliberate, priced and reported separately, so it is not an unpriced political chore. Its overseers can see everything, and it is strong enough on its own capital that the Treasury backstop is a convenience rather than a crutch.

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. Keystone's best moments came when it was the dependable buyer during credit squeezes, which is a risk-and-liquidity role.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out the buy-and-hold carry trade, chasing volume, and any unpriced credit expansion.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes, in two ways. It means giving up "largest buyer" status as a goal. It also creates a real tension with the passion circle, because lending to higher-risk borrowers is only on-hedgehog if it is priced. That tension is the core management problem.

**Confirmation evidence (12–36 months):**
1. Interest-rate sensitivity falls inside a published limit.
2. New business (fees plus spread on new purchases) is profitable on its own, separate from the legacy book.
3. Actual credit losses on guaranteed and adjustable-rate books come in at or below priced losses through at least one housing downturn.

**Weakest link:** the step from [6] to [2]. Keystone has no credit-loss data by loan type, yet it is already guaranteeing securities, buying adjustable-rate loans, and (since last year) buying apartment loans. If pricing is wrong, step [4] reports profit that is really unreserved future loss.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Shrink the legacy duration gap at [3], so that the old book stops draining capital at [1].
2. Build the loss database that powers [6], before scaling new loan types at [2].

**Doom-loop check:**
- *Many new directions at once.* In about three years Keystone sold $10B of loans and started adjustable-rate purchases, MBS, swaps and apartment loans. Each move is defensible, but together they show signs of a doom loop. → Risk.
- *Reacting to rivals.* The adjustable-rate shift follows Rival G `[ASSUMPTION]`. That is fine as long as Keystone prices the payment-shock credit risk of these loans. → Watch.
- *Big one-off moves.* The $10B sale was a cleanup, not a cure. The underlying business still has to be rebuilt. → Acceptable.

**Verdict:** the loop closes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** funding long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter-term borrowing. Match-fund, securitize or sell them.
2. **Stop** using purchase volume, market share or "largest buyer" as a success measure.
3. **Stop** scaling any product before a credit-loss model and a loss-history plan exist. Keep apartment loans at pilot size until losses are measured.
4. **Stop** taking rate views. No positions based on a forecast of falling rates.
5. **Stop** curating what overseers see. Share complete data with the audit office voluntarily.
6. **Stop** treating mission purchases as unpriced obligations. Price them, report them separately, and debate them openly.
7. **Stop** letting the Treasury backstop stand in for capital. Don't grow faster than equity grows.
8. **Stop** competing with your own lender-customers. Stay on the secondary side of the market. The charter requires this, so keep the discipline even if someone lobbies to loosen it.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every purchase or guarantee carries an explicit risk price.
- A rate-shock report goes to the board on a fixed cycle.
- A post-mortem is held whenever credit losses exceed priced losses.

**Framework and freedom:** business units are free to design products, but only inside board-set limits on rate and credit risk.

**Bureaucracy check:** the regulator's approval steps are fixed and outside Keystone's control. Internally, any approval layer that exists because risk people are missing should be replaced by hiring the right risk people.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Price every risk honestly.
2. Keep mortgage money flowing in every market.
3. Full candor with owners and overseers.
4. Capital first, growth second.

**Core purpose:** to widen access to homeownership by making mortgage money dependable and affordable, in good markets and bad.

**BHAG (20 years):** *Earn a profit in every year of every rate and housing cycle while financing more lower- and moderate-income homes than any other institution, without ever needing the Treasury line.* I wrote this from the Hedgehog Concept; it has no numeric target because the dossier provides none.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A board risk committee with independent experts.
- Published limits on rate and credit risk.
- Separate, priced mission reporting.
- Standing full data access for auditors.
- A CEO succession plan with an internal candidate.
