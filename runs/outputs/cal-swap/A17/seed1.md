# Good to Great: Marlowe Drug Co.

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the most convenient, pharmacy-led drugstore in every market where Marlowe can be the densest, and judge every decision by profit per customer visit.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning customer visits into profit. Net margin has fallen from about 2.2% seven years ago to roughly 1.4–1.5% last year even after adding back the LIFO effect. Profit per visit is not measured at all.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop "growth across all divisions." Stop treating sales volume, including the $1B milestone, as the goal.
  2. Cancel the 22 planned restaurant openings and stop expanding food service.
  3. Stop investing in the 25 discount department stores, and prepare an exit.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** sales more than doubled in nine years, but reported EPS fell from $1.30 to $1.15. Even on the best reading, EPS from two years ago ($2.11) was barely above the figure from seven years ago ($1.95). Growth is not compounding into earnings.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Profit and return on capital by division. If restaurants or discount stores earn well above the drugstores, Gate 5 must be redone.
  - Profit per customer visit and per store, segmented by market density.
  - Regional market share. This decides where density is winnable and where Rival E already owns the ground.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marlowe is a drugstore company and nothing else. Its pharmacy-led stores sit so densely in its chosen markets that a Marlowe is the easiest place to fill a prescription and pick up everyday health and beauty items. Capital that now goes into lunch counters, family restaurants, discount department stores, a travel agency and a coffee roaster goes instead into denser clusters, better sites, systems that link pharmacies, and lower shrinkage.

Every store, remodel and product line is judged by one number: profit per customer visit. House-brand products stay only where they raise that number. Leadership comes from a deep bench, not just the next family member, and the board can say no.

The result would be earnings that grow faster than sales for a decade or more. That would be the reverse of the last nine years.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the most convenient, pharmacy-led drugstore in every market where Marlowe can be the densest, and judge every decision by profit per customer visit.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Largest U.S. drugstore operator; convenient neighborhood pharmacy; house-brand health and beauty; low prices; restaurants; discount general merchandise | Convenient pharmacy-led drugstores in dense local clusters. It already runs about twice the volume of its nearest drugstore competitor and has 75 years of pharmacist heritage. | **Restaurants:** 8% of sales, with no evidence of advantage over dedicated chains. **Discount department stores:** 25 regional stores that were hit hardest by the recession and have high shrinkage. **Travel, coffee roasting, ice cream:** no route to best-in-world. **Low prices alone:** this cannot be a lasting advantage on a 1–1.5% margin. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per store; per sq ft; per prescription; **per customer visit** | **Profit per customer visit** `[ASSUMPTION]`. On thin margins the levers are visit frequency (convenience, density, pharmacy refills) and the profitability of each visit (pharmacy mix, house brand, low shrinkage). Profit per store hides the density effect. Profit per prescription ignores front-of-store sales. | Sales volume. Nine years of doubling sales without rising EPS shows that volume is not the engine. |
| **Passion** | Pharmacist founder; "community centers for health and beauty service"; "holds fast to its basic identity as drugstores"; restaurants (management calls the outlook "positive") | Health and beauty service, drugstore identity `[ASSUMPTION: shared beyond the letter]` | Restaurants. There may be real affection for them, going back to the soda fountains, but affection is not a Hedgehog circle. |

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Drugstores are about 80% of sales, average over $1.3M a store, and are the source of the scale lead. The store-pruning record (51 marginal stores closed, 94 opened) already behaves like a density-and-quality discipline.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: restaurants, discount stores, travel, coffee, ice cream, the Mexico stake, and scattered single-store markets.
- **Uncomfortable?** Very. It means exiting a 12-year-old division and the lunch counters, which are heritage dating back to the 1920s.

**Confirmation tests (status: working hypothesis):**
1. Division ROC shows drugstores earning at or above every other division.
2. Store-level data show profit per visit rising with local density.
3. Pharmacy customers visit more often and generate more profit per visit than non-pharmacy customers.

If test 1 fails, redo the engine circle. If test 2 fails, drop "densest" from the sentence.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop "growth across all divisions"** as the corporate aim. Stop celebrating sales milestones such as $1B.
2. **Stop opening restaurants.** Cancel the 22 planned units and the new family-restaurant chain.
3. **Stop investing in discount department stores.** No new stores and no remodels. Prepare a sale or closure.
4. **Stop the travel agency, coffee roasting and ice cream businesses.**
5. **Stop expanding manufacturing capacity** (second lab, plastic-container plant) until make-vs-buy shows a gain in profit per visit.
6. **Stop treating the Mexico stake as strategic.**
7. **Stop opening isolated single stores** in markets where Marlowe cannot become the densest.
8. **Stop explaining earnings through accounting** ("would have beaten the record but for LIFO"). Report the operating truth.
9. **Stop answering Rival E with bidding wars** for Sun Belt acquisitions outside Marlowe's clusters.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every capital request states its expected effect on profit per visit.
- Every store is reviewed yearly. Closing a marginal store is routine, not a failure.
- Shrinkage is reported store by store, every period.

**Framework boundaries:**
- *Fixed:* drugstores only, density-first site selection, profit per visit as the test.
- *Free:* store managers and pharmacists decide merchandising, staffing and local service within that framework.

**Bureaucracy check:** if the planned "cost control and productivity" program adds approval layers in every division, it is compensating for a portfolio that is too wide. Narrowing the portfolio first will remove the need for much of it.
