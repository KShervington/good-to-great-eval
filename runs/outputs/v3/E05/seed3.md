# Ashby & Crane: Good to Great

**Verdict: good and close to great. Concentrate on what works and don't buy the soft-drink company.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Turn a few challenger brands into the defining badge of adult consumer categories where image drives choice and the company makes the product itself. Then carry those brands worldwide, maximizing profit per major brand.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the cash engine. U.S. cigarette profits fund every other push, and federal anti-smoking pressure is rising. Nothing in the evidence shows that beer can replace those profits yet.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) the soft-drink purchase at about $500M; (2) treating diversification away from tobacco as a goal in itself; (3) adding beer capacity before returns are proven.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** this month the government made smoking its top public-health target.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:** profit and return on capital by business, the soft-drink target's earnings and deal terms, and shareholder returns against the market.
- **Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on 18 years of share gains and two proven brand turnarounds. It does not rest on financials.

Greatness potential: 60/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ashby & Crane is a disciplined brand-building company. It owns the best-selling cigarette brand in the United States and grows that brand country by country until it leads the world. It runs its brewer for profit per barrel, not just rank, and earns its capital back before it builds more. It enters a new category only when three things are all true:

- the category is adult and image-driven;
- an incumbent leads it complacently;
- the company would control how the product is made.

It does not chase categories because they are "away from tobacco." It deals with the health question openly, at board level, with a named owner, instead of leaving it to the lawyers. It has a successor generation ready before the chairman leaves, so the next 20 years do not depend on two men who have worked together for 20 years.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the soft-drink purchase at about $500M.** That price is several times the brewer stake. Two giant colas lead the category, and their bottler networks control distribution that the company would not control. A lemon-lime brand sold as "the alternative" is a niche fighting the leaders' own ground. Revisit only if the target's numbers show a distribution path the company can control.
2. **Stop treating "diversify beyond tobacco" as a goal in itself.** Diversify only into categories that pass the hedgehog test.
3. **Stop adding beer capacity ahead of proven profit per barrel.** Stage each addition against demand and returns.
4. **Stop small unrelated acquisitions.** The razor and gum lesson is already paid for.
5. **Stop running packaging and paper as a strategic growth business.** Keep it only as a captive cost advantage.
6. **Stop spreading the top marketing talent across more categories.** Concentrate it on the global flagship.
7. **Stop handling the health question as a legal and public-relations matter only.** It is a strategic fact and needs a strategic owner.
