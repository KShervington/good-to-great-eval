# Eastbank Housing Defense Collaborative: The Great Version

**Verdict: Good, not yet great. The headline number hides a retreat.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Every Marlow County tenant facing eviction gets fast, courthouse-based help at the level that changes their outcome. Success is counted per eviction filed, not per case taken.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Filings rose 22% while full representation fell 25%. Tenants kept housed through full representation fell from about 1,334 to about 1,121, even though the rate rose to 88%. The 93% mostly reflects screening for winnable cases.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** measurement. Outcomes for the 5,930 advice-desk contacts are unknown, and the county renewal is next year.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) screening by winnability; (2) Homeownership Pathways; (3) doubling the policy unit for a statewide campaign.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** advice-desk outcomes; Year −1 attorney headcount; cost per program.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on the dossier alone, and the housed counts assume the reported rates apply to all closed cases `[ASSUMPTION]`.

Greatness potential: 38/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great EHDC is Marlow County's eviction-defense system, not a boutique that wins the cases it picks. Every filing on the clerk's docket gets reached within days. Each tenant is triaged by one question: *will counsel change this outcome?* That includes negotiated move-out time and record protection, not only winnable defenses. Tenants then get full representation or a structured advice-desk path with a tracked result. EHDC reports one honest number to the county, funders and itself: tenancies preserved per eviction filed. Rising filings become the argument for a bigger county contract rather than a quiet erosion of coverage. The organization runs at full attorney strength without a hidden deficit, and it refuses programs that pull it out of housing court. Its board decides strategy on the merits, free of members' institutional interests. Its practices are written down, so they would survive the founder's departure.

## 5. Stop-doing list (and the disciplines that replace it)

**Stop doing:**
1. **Screening full representation by winnability or rental-assistance match.** Screen by whether counsel changes the outcome, which includes negotiated time and record protection.
2. **Leading reports with the win rate.** Lead with tenancies preserved per filing and with coverage.
3. **Homeownership Pathways.** Decline it. The $735K and about $37–44K a year in indirect costs is a salve for the deficit, not a strategy. Refer interested clients to existing homebuyer counselors, and invite Granite Mutual to fund defense or rental arrears instead `[ASSUMPTION the bank is open to this]`.
4. **Doubling the policy unit for a statewide campaign.**
5. **Letting partners, sponsors or board members set operating priorities.** That includes intake design, new programs and scope.
6. **Budgeting on vacancy savings.** Present a full-staffing budget and close the gap it reveals.
7. **Paper logging at the advice desk.** Use an interim digital intake form now and the case-management system in the spring.
8. **Meeting clients in the lobby when the elevator fails.** Secure an accessible, private meeting space.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- Every docketed filing gets an outreach attempt within a set number of days `[ASSUMPTION: about 5]`.
- Every contact, in either program, gets an outcome attempt at 60 days.
- Every new initiative must pass one test before it reaches the board: *does it raise tenancies preserved per filing in Marlow County?*

**Framework and freedom:** management fixes the triage rubric, the metrics and the scope. Attorneys keep full freedom over case strategy.

**Bureaucracy check:** the winnability screen is a rule that compensates for missing capacity. The fix is more capacity and honest triage, not a tighter screen.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Every tenant facing eviction deserves a defense.
2. Count honestly: success is measured against need, not against the cases we chose.
3. Speed matters, because days are housing.
4. Client dignity: accessible, private, respectful.
5. The mission is set by the mission, not by funders or partners.

**Core purpose:** keep Marlow County families housed and make eviction court fair.

**BHAG (about a 15-year horizon):** Marlow County guarantees counsel-level help to every tenant facing eviction, with EHDC as its backbone. The county's eviction-judgment rate falls to half of today's `[ASSUMPTION: baseline judgment rate not in the dossier]`.

**Clock-building mechanisms (so it works without Dana):**
- A written triage rubric
- The quarterly coverage dashboard
- A board conflict-of-interest policy, with recusal
- The hedgehog test for new initiatives
- A designated deputy or successor
- A county contract with coverage-based deliverables
- Program-level cost accounting
