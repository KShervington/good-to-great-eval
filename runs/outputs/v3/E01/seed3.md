# Brennard Steel Corporation: Good to Great run (stewardship mode)

## Verdict

**Already great. Run in stewardship mode.** Brennard has compounded for thirty years and earns a 21.9% ROE on almost no debt. It also invented minimill flat-rolled sheet. The live test is endurance: the first CEO handover in three decades, arriving just as rivals copy the model.

- **Hedgehog (confirmed, working hypothesis):** Make steel at the lowest cost per ton in its home market by pairing new-technology greenfield mills with small, lean, output-paid teams.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** metal inputs. Scrap costs are rising, competition for prime scrap is growing, and the hedge (iron carbide) is unproven.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) scaling iron-making before the first plant proves out; (2) building capacity to match rivals' +40% or to protect the historic growth rate; (3) "professionalizing" the culture under the new CEO.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** about 10 copycat mills (+40% capacity) are due within roughly three years, and sheet prices are already falling.
- **Numbers that would change this:** cost and profit per ton by mill versus rivals; actual iron-carbide cost and output.
- **Confidence:** moderate. It rests on four years of financials and the 30-year history, with no per-ton cost data.

`Greatness potential: 72/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard is the same company, deliberately kept that way under a new CEO. It produces steel at the lowest cost per ton in the country. It does this because small, highly paid, self-directed teams run the newest mills, and because a headquarters of fewer than 25 people refuses to add layers. Its edge no longer depends on being the only company using thin-slab casting, since rivals can buy that equipment. It depends on running every mill, including the third sheet mill, at a cost the copycats can't match, and on securing metal inputs before scrap gets scarce. It grows only where a new mill will be the lowest-cost mill in its market. It stays profitable and keeps its no-layoff record through the coming capacity glut, and it comes out of that glut larger, as the producer the weaker newcomers couldn't outlast. Its board and succession mechanisms are strong enough that the culture doesn't depend on the chairman's presence.

**Confirmation tests:**

**Current push (next one to two turns):**
1. **Make the third sheet mill the lowest-cost mill in the industry from start-up.** Staff it with proven people from the first two sheet mills.
2. **Secure metal inputs.** Prove or kill iron carbide against hard criteria, and build a broader scrap and scrap-substitute plan before the new minimills bid up prime scrap.

**Doom-loop check:** there are no doom-loop signs yet. The strategy has been consistent for 30 years, growth has been organic, and there has been no acquisition binge. Three yellow flags: the "how far into iron-making" question; capex doubling to more than $500M, about 1.8× last year's earnings; and pressure to hold a growth rate the market may no longer allow.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship: temptations to refuse)

1. **Committing to a second or larger iron-making plant** before the Caribbean plant hits rated output (about 320k t/yr) at a cost below scrap-equivalent for a sustained period.
2. **Building capacity because rivals are, or to keep the historic growth rate.** Build only mills projected to be the lowest-cost producer in their product and region.
3. **Going overseas or beyond steel to rescue growth.** Both are outside the Hedgehog Concept and both break current policy.
4. **"Professionalizing" under the new CEO**: adding layers, growing headquarters above about 25 people, or diluting the output bonus or the no-layoff practice to look more like peers.
5. **Keeping fabrication businesses out of habit.** Fasteners and bearings should earn their place on the same per-ton/return test as the mills. This item is about the decision rule, not a verdict to exit.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values (do not change):**
1. Pay for performance, shared by everyone from the shop floor up.
2. Egalitarianism: no class distinctions between management and workers.
3. Shared pain before layoffs: cut hours, not people.
4. Lean and decentralized: few layers, with decisions made at the plant.
5. Safety as a precondition of output. This is proposed for explicit addition, given the fatality and the bonus design.

**Core purpose:** to show that trusted, fairly paid working people with the best tools can out-produce anyone, and to keep their jobs secure while doing it.

**Practices, not core (keep, but they may evolve):** greenfield-only growth, home-country-only operation, steel-only scope. These have served the Hedgehog Concept, but they are means, not values. Writing that down now stops a future leadership team from confusing loyalty to a practice with loyalty to the core, in either direction.

**BHAG (20-year):** *Become the largest and most profitable steel producer in our home country, without a single layoff.* `[ASSUMPTION: scale and timeframe chosen to follow from the Hedgehog Concept]`

**Clock-building mechanisms:** weekly output bonuses; the no-layoff and shared-hours practice; the headquarters headcount cap; plant GM autonomy. **To add:** independent directors, a written CEO and GM succession process, a cost-per-ton capital hurdle, and a safety measure built into the bonus system.
