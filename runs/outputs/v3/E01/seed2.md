## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Brennard stays the lowest-cost steelmaker in its home market through the coming capacity wave because of how it runs, not because of any single technology. Production teams earn far above industry pay by out-producing everyone. Plant general managers run their mills like owners. Headquarters stays tiny. Every new mill is built from scratch around the newest technology that has actually been proven to work. Brennard grows only as fast as it can staff new mills with general managers who carry its culture and only where it can beat new rivals on cost per ton. When rivals copy the minimill model, it is already applying the next proven advance. It treats iron-making as a disciplined experiment, not a crusade. The first change of chief executive in 30 years shows that the culture is a system, not one man's personality.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Do not build more iron-making capacity** until the island plant runs near its rated 320,000 tons a year for two consecutive quarters, at a cost per ton below the equivalent of scrap. [ASSUMPTION: the threshold is about 80% of capacity.]
2. **Do not buy old integrated mills or foreign plants** to keep up the growth rate. The greenfield-only and home-country policies are part of the Hedgehog. The island iron plant is already one exception, so do not let it become a precedent.
3. **Do not approve capacity only because rivals are adding it.** Every project in the $500M+ plan should show a cost per ton below the incoming minimills'.
4. **Do not add headquarters staff, management layers or "professional" process** during the succession.
5. **Do not cut base pay or lay people off** in the sheet downturn. Keep shortening hours instead.
6. **Do not let output bonuses outrun safety.** Put a safety floor inside the bonus system rather than beside it.
