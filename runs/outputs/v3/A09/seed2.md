# Ashcombe Company: The Great Version

## Verdict

**Ashcombe is good, not yet great.** It is a shaving company with other businesses attached, and its greatness depends on accepting that.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** *Engineer a measurably better shave, protected by technology, and earn the return on the refills each shaver keeps buying.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** shavers trading *up* to premium systems instead of *down* to disposables.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:**
  1. Fighting Rival B's lighter price war.
  2. Pushing the disposable for unit share.
  3. Entering categories outside shaving.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** every disposable sold in place of a refill lowers profit, and disposable demand is beating forecasts.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - per-unit profit of a disposable versus a refill
  - disposables' share of U.S. shaving
  - ROE trend
  - profit by line
- **Confidence:** moderate. The verdict rests on the profit mix (blades ≈71% of pretax profit) and on durable share leadership, not on total financials.

`Greatness potential: 45/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ashcombe is a global shaving-technology company that happens to own a few cash-generating side businesses. Every few years it launches a shaving system that is clearly better, and shavers pay more for it. That expands an installed base of handles, and the refills those handles consume earn most of the company's profit. It treats disposables as a fence that keeps low-price rivals out of its customer base, not as the field it plays on. It has left the businesses where Rival B's low-price model wins. It measures itself on **profit per shaver**, not unit share. Its shaving R&D budget is protected from quarterly pressure, and its next shaving leader is already in place.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Ashcombe engineers a measurably better shave, protected by technology, and earns its return on the refills each shaver buys again and again.*

This is a working hypothesis.

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Shaving systems; low-cost disposables; consumer products generally; deodorant; drugstore distribution | **Precision shaving technology made at mass scale.** The evidence is decades of U.S. blade leadership (60–65% share) and two successive twin-blade systems. | Low-price disposables, where Rival B's model is built for this and has already won in lighters. Luxury goods, electronics and diversified consumer products, where the previous era's failures and shrinking margins count against them. U.S. electric shavers, which are legally blocked. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per blade; per razor handle; per shaver; per unit sold | **Pretax profit per shaver per year.** This is the denominator. | "Total razor unit share." Disposables can raise it while lowering profit. |
| **Passion** | Shaving; consumer marketing; "growth" | **The shave itself**, the business the company was founded on 80 years ago. `[ASSUMPTION]` Its engineers and its brand identity still center on it. | Diversification for its own sake. |

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes: blade leadership, the twin-blade systems, and the share recovery after the stainless-steel shock.
- **Explains past losses?** Yes: watches, calculators, perfumes, smoke alarms and lighters all sat outside shaving and competed on someone else's terms.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: lighters, new categories, and a volume push on disposables.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It implies capping toiletries and pens, and it means accepting some loss of total unit share.

**Confirmation tests, to run over 1–3 years:**
1. Users of the newest system buy refills at stable or rising rates and don't defect to disposables.
2. Profit per shaver holds or rises even as disposables grow.
3. The next premium system wins share at a higher price per cartridge.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Shaving R&D: measurably better shave
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    2. Premium system launch, priced up
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    3. Shavers trade UP and install handles   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    4. Repeat refill purchases (profit per shaver ↑)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    5. Manufacturing scale lowers cost per cartridge
   │              │
   │              ▼
   └─── 6. Cash funds more R&D, advertising and global reach
```

**Current push, the next two turns:**
- **Turn 1:** fund and accelerate the next premium system (link 1→2) with a protected budget.
- **Turn 2:** strengthen link 3. Use the disposable as an entry point that recruits users into systems, rather than as an endpoint.

**Doom-loop check:** the previous chairman's era shows classic doom-loop signs: one new direction after another, and acquisitions bought to jump-start growth. The current CEO is reversing that. The live danger now is **reacting to Rival B without understanding**: pouring resources into disposables because a rival did, which turns the flywheel backwards.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop funding the lighter price war.** Exit the lighter business.
2. **Stop pushing the disposable for volume.** No promotions or advertising that move refill buyers into disposables.
3. **Stop entering new categories.** That includes hair coloring, perfume, electronics and safety products, by acquisition or by launch.
4. **Stop competing with Rival B on price anywhere.** This applies to pens and to razors.
5. **Stop launching new toiletries outside deodorant.** Shampoos and hair products already failed once. `[ASSUMPTION: this reflects their current state]`
6. **Stop investing management time in U.S. electric-shaver entry** while the antitrust suit stands.
7. **Stop reporting total razor unit share as the headline shaving metric.** Replace it with profit per shaver.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every new shaving system must beat its predecessor in blind shave tests before launch.
- The shaving R&D budget is ring-fenced as a fixed share of shaving sales. `[ASSUMPTION: percentage to be set]`
- Profit per shaver is reviewed quarterly at board level.
- Every acquisition must pass a written Hedgehog test.

**Framework boundaries:**
- Shaving teams have wide autonomy *inside* shaving.
- Non-shaving divisions run autonomously for cash, but get no growth capital.
