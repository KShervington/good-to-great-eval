# Ashcombe Company: The Great Version

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept:** Ashcombe makes the world's best shave. It sells technologically superior, premium cartridge systems that shavers everywhere buy again and again, and it earns its profit per shaver on the refills.

**Weakest flywheel link:** trade-up. Each disposable sold in place of a refill lowers profit, and disposable demand is running ahead of plan.

**Stop doing:**
1. The lighter price war with Rival B.
2. Judging shaving by total razor share instead of profit per shaver.
3. Promoting the disposable as a growth product.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the disposable Ashcombe is pushing to defend share eats into blade profits. Blades produced about 71% of pretax profit in the late 1970s.

**Numbers that would change the answer:** disposables' share of U.S. shaving, profit per shaver by system, and line-level profit and ROE.

**Confidence:** moderate. It rests on the dossier's share and profit-mix data. There are no total financials.

Greatness potential: 60/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ashcombe is a shaving company first. It brings out a measurably better cartridge system on a steady cadence. Each one is premium-priced, made in precision plants at a scale no rival matches, and sold through the worldwide distribution it has run for most of its history. It measures success by profit per shaver per year, not by how many razors leave the shelf. The disposable is a defensive flanker that keeps Rival B from owning the low end, and it is never the growth story. Toiletries stay only where they pass the same test the new deodorant passed: real technology, premium price, repeat purchase. The lighter price war is over, the low-price pens are gone, and there is no fresh diversification wave. Blade cash pays for the next shaving system, not the next unrelated acquisition. If it vanished, the world's shavers would notice within a week.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Ashcombe makes the world's best shave. It sells technologically superior, premium cartridge systems that shavers everywhere buy again and again, and it earns its profit per shaver on the refills.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Chosen | Ruled out |

It still includes lighters and pens, where Rival B's low-cost model wins. Ashcombe cannot be best in the world at low-cost disposables, lighters, commodity pens, small appliances in general, fashion or perfume, or electronics. |
| **Economic engine** | profit per razor sold; total razor share; **profit per shaver per year** | **Profit per shaver per year.** The handle is sold once and the cartridges are bought for years. | Unit share rewards the disposable, which "costs more to make than a refill and sells for less." |
| **Passion** | the founding business (~80 years); engineering pride in the shave; the deodorant team's two-year development push | **Making a visibly better shave** `[ASSUMPTION]`: the dossier gives no direct evidence on what the staff care about | Passion for being a diversified conglomerate ended when that strategy shrank margins. |

**Why this intersection holds:**
- **It explains past wins:** blades (60–65% share), two successive twin-blade cartridge systems, and the new deodorant (about a quarter of its market after two years of development).
- **It explains past failures:** watches and calculators were technology without repeat purchase. Lighters and pens are commodities under price attack. Perfume and hair coloring are fashion, not technology.
- **It rules things out:** lighters, low-price pens, any toiletry that is "me-too," and a full-throttle disposable push.
- **It is uncomfortable to commit to:** it means accepting lower total razor unit share if needed. It means writing off the lighter fight. It means betting R&D money on trade-up while a cheaper product is selling better than expected.

**Status:** working hypothesis. **Confirmation tests:**
1. Profit per shaver-year is higher for cartridge-system users than for disposable users, and by how much.
2. Disposable buyers can be traded up to a cartridge system at a measurable rate.
3. The next premium system, when launched, raises average profit per shaver within 18–24 months.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Fund the next premium system.** Fix a launch target and protect its budget from every other line.
2. **Reposition the disposable as a flanker.** Price it for margin, give it no growth advertising, and use it as a sampling route into the cartridge handle.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Past:** the previous era shows classic doom-loop signs. It took one new direction after another (watches, calculators, smoke alarms, perfume, greeting services). Margins shrank, and the lighter acquisition turned into a price war.
- **Present risk:** "do both" disposables and premium *with equal weight* would mean reacting to Rival B without understanding the economics. Do both, but unequally. Premium leads and the disposable defends.
- **Positive sign:** the current CEO's divestitures (leather goods, greeting service, plant food, pencils) are flywheel behavior, not doom-loop behavior.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the lighter price war.** Sell the business or wind it down. Decide within 12 months.
2. **Stop measuring shaving by total razor or unit share.** Report profit per shaver-year and the premium mix instead.
3. **Stop advertising the disposable as a growth product.** Keep it priced for margin and use it as a flanker.
4. **Stop competing with Rival B on price anywhere.** That covers pens, lighters, and the bottom of the razor market.
5. **Stop acquiring outside the Hedgehog Concept.** Don't follow Rival W into unrelated businesses such as bakery.
6. **Stop launching me-too toiletries.** If a product can't be premium-priced on demonstrable performance, don't launch it.
7. **Stop treating the German subsidiary as an appliance company.** Its job is shaving technology.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** make a daily routine measurably better for people everywhere.
- **Core values:**
  1. Technological superiority: sell only what is demonstrably better.
  2. Quality without compromise in manufacturing.
  3. Earn the repeat purchase.
  4. Global by default.
  5. Disciplined capital: cash goes back into the Hedgehog Concept.
- **BHAG (20-year):** *By Year +20, most of the world's blade shavers shave with an Ashcombe system, at premium prices.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A product-generation roadmap with a fixed cadence that outlives any one leader.
  - A profit-per-shaver test in every capital decision.
  - An annual board review of the stop-doing list.
  - At least two internal successor candidates in development.
