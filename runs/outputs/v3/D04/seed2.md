# Linewright: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Linewright is good, not yet great, and the feature most buyers pay for is about to become free.**

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** *Linewright is the subtitler-built desktop tool that makes a file pass the client's delivery spec the first time, paid for by seats that renew every year.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning renewal revenue into product progress. The founder is the whole engine: 50 h/week, ~30 tickets/week, and two bug-fix releases in a row.
- **Top three stop-doing:** (1) Linewright Lite, (2) the Linux port, (3) answering install and licensing tickets by hand.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** renewals fell four quarters in a row (64% → 52%). That is below the 58% the price rise explained. Meanwhile the #1 purchase reason, auto-align (69%), ships free in Lantern's stable release "this winter."
- **Numbers that would change this:** studio vs. individual renewal split, studio seats a year ago, and why the Q2–Q3 non-renewers left.

**Confidence:** moderate. It rests on the dossier's own figures. The Lantern → churn link is `[ASSUMPTION]`.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Linewright is the tool a studio *specifies*. When a broadcaster or streamer publishes delivery rules, a Linewright profile for them exists within weeks. Studios put every freelancer on a seat because "passes Linewright" means "passes QC." Auto-align is still in the product but is treated as table stakes. The reason people buy and renew is spec compliance, shared style profiles, and a fast keyboard-first editor built by someone who did the job for 11 years. Most revenue renews. The founder spends their week on spec intelligence and the product, not the licensing queue. A second person can ship a release and run support for a month without them. That is the only form of "lasting endurance" a solo product can credibly claim.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. Studios renew for the spec checker and profiles, and two managers report rejections cut "by more than half."
- *Rules things out?* Yes: Lite, Linux, racing speech models, enterprise workflow.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It turns down 2,310 waitlist signups and a friend's offer. It also concedes that the most-cited purchase reason is no longer a moat.

**Confirmation evidence (working hypothesis until then):**
1. The studio renewal rate, split out, is ≥85–90% and holding.
2. Individual non-renewers cite auto-align or Lantern, not spec checks.
3. At least 3 studios confirm the rejection reduction with their own numbers.
4. A spec-focused release is followed by stable or rising renewals for two quarters.

If studio renewals turn out to be falling too, the hedgehog is wrong or late, and the run becomes a turnaround.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Founder's subtitling expertise + studio feedback on delivery specs
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   2. Spec-checker presets & shared style profiles that make files pass first time
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   3. Studios standardize on Linewright, put freelancers on seats (fewer QC rejections)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   4. Freelancers learn Linewright as "the tool whose files get accepted" → buy & renew
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   5. RECURRING REVENUE PER RENEWING SEAT rises (denominator)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 └── 6. Revenue buys capacity (support help, 2nd engineer) → more spec profiles, faster releases
```

**Weakest link: 5 → 6.** Revenue is not turning into product progress. Every turn of the wheel passes through one person who is spending capacity on install/licensing tickets and bug fixes.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Free founder capacity by fixing licensing and adding part-time support.
2. Ship a spec-focused release, with new presets and a per-studio QC report, *before* Lantern's stable auto-align lands.

**Doom-loop check: at risk, not yet in it.**
- Warning sign: four options being circled at once, which is reaction without a settled direction.
- Warning sign: Lite would be a "big program to jump-start momentum."
- Warning sign: the price rise was decided without measuring renewals by cohort.
- In its favor: there is no history of serial new directions yet.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Linewright Lite / creator product.** It means six months of the only developer, a different customer, a different platform, and a product built on the commoditizing feature. The 2,310 signups came through Marisol's network: a waitlist, not paying users. The "50x" market claim is an unsourced deck figure. Illustration: a 10% conversion at $8/month would be about $22k/yr gross before revenue share `[ASSUMPTION]`.
2. **The Linux port.** 46 forum requests do not make a strategy.
3. **Answering install and licensing tickets by hand.** Fix the activation flow, add a self-serve license portal and an install FAQ, then hand the remainder to the support seat.
4. **Leading with auto-align.** Keep it good enough, and consider adopting an open speech model. Stop racing it and stop marketing on it.
5. **"Clear the backlog" as a strategy.** Doing nothing new is not neutral while the moat moves.
6. **Reading renewals as one blended number,** and changing prices without cohort data.
7. **Hiring sales before capacity.** Sequence: capacity, then spec release, then console, then sales.
8. **Chasing enterprise localization workflow** to match the cloud platform.
