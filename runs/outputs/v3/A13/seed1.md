# Good to Great: Kestrel Industries

## Verdict

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Kestrel makes simple steel (bars, light shapes, joists) at the lowest cost per ton in America. It does this by pairing minimills that anyone can buy with a culture nobody can buy: lean, egalitarian, and paid for output.

**Weakest flywheel link:** output → pay. Recession short weeks shrink bonuses just as the Texas mill starts up.

**Top 3 stop-doing:** (1) any diversification outside steel; (2) committing to a fourth mill before Texas runs at standard; (3) any plan that depends on import protection.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the recession hits hardest at the incentive-pay system, which is Kestrel's real moat.

**Numbers that would change this:** cost per ton against integrated mills and rival minimills; profit per ton for the last two years; current joist share.

**Confidence:** moderate. It rests on strong strategy and culture evidence. There is no cost data proving "lowest cost."

`Greatness potential: 60/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kestrel is the steel company that integrated mills and importers cannot match on cost per ton in the products it chooses to make. It still runs from a tiny headquarters. Its plants are rural, nonunion, and paid by team output, and its people earn far above local wages because they produce far more per hour. In downturns everyone shares the pain, from the top down, and nobody is laid off. It adds minimills one at a time. Each new mill reaches standard output before the next is committed, and each is located near scrap and customers. Its joist plants give the mills a base load. It does not drift back into conglomerate habits. It watches equipment makers closely, so that when new casting or rolling technology lets a minimill make products that used to require integrated mills, Kestrel is the first to apply it at scale. It stays great because the culture, not the CEO, produces the cost advantage.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **No diversification outside steel.** That means no instruments, chemicals, electronics or energy. The company has already lived this failure.
2. **No fourth mill commitment until Texas runs at standard output** for a sustained period `[ASSUMPTION: define "sustained" as two consecutive quarters]`. One start-up at a time.
3. **No strategy that depends on import protection.** A low-cost producer should win on cost. Kestrel may take a public position on trade, but its plans must work without protection. `[ASSUMPTION: that Kestrel's cost is competitive with imports; unverified]`
4. **No flat-rolled or specialty-grade push on the current route.** Kestrel is not best-in-world there. Revisit only if the equipment changes.
5. **No downstream products that don't consume Kestrel steel or can't be made at lowest cost.** This settles the expansion debate. More regional minimills near scrap and customers are inside the Hedgehog. Downstream fabrication beyond joists is inside only if it passes both tests.
6. **No layoffs as a cost lever.** Short hours, shared top-down, remain the downturn tool.
7. **No added management layers or HQ staff** to manage growth. Grow GMs instead.
8. **No acquisitions of old high-cost plants** whose culture or cost structure can't be converted.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** pay by team output against standards; profit sharing; cost per ton measured monthly at every mill; shared downturn hours; safety stop authority on every floor.

**Freedom within a framework:** plant GMs run their plants. They set crews, methods and local hiring. The center sets the Hedgehog, the capital allocation, pay design, safety standards and the per-ton scorecard.

**Bureaucracy check:** the dossier shows almost none. Keep it that way by hiring right rather than adding rules.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values (3–5, permanent):**
1. **Shared fate:** no layoffs, pain shared from the top down, the same hard hat for everyone.
2. **Pay for what we produce:** the rewards follow output and profit.
3. **Lean by design:** few layers and a small center; decisions are made at the plant.
4. **Everyone goes home safe:** this value was implicit before. It is made explicit after the fire.
5. **Straight talk:** facts about cost and safety go up the line unfiltered.

**Core purpose:** to prove that American steel can be made at world-competitive cost by people who share fully in what they produce.

**BHAG (horizon about 25 years):** *Be the most profitable steelmaker in America on profit per ton across a full cycle, without ever laying anyone off.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- the output-based pay formula
- profit sharing
- the no-layoff, shared-hours rule
- the monthly per-ton scorecard at every mill
- the one-start-up-at-a-time rule
- GM rotation for succession
- safety stop authority
