# Ironbridge Steel Corporation: Good to Great (autonomous run)

## Verdict

**Weakest flywheel link:** the cost per ton at the plants it keeps. Nobody has shown that it can match imports, and the 3%-a-year wage floor pushes cost up whatever happens to demand.

**Top three stop-doing:**

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Ironbridge is smaller, plainer and much more profitable per ton. It no longer tries to be a steel empire. It concentrates its capital and its best people on two or three modern flat-rolled operations: the Lake Michigan plant beside Midwest auto and appliance customers, and the tidewater plant `[ASSUMPTION: these are the lowest-cost sites]`. It wins long-term contracts on delivered cost, quality and reliability instead of on price increases. It exits commodity bars, sells or spins off shipbuilding, and closes or sells capacity that cannot earn its cost of capital through a full cycle. It treats mines, ships and railroads as cost tools, not as identity. Executives are paid on return on capital across the whole cycle, not on volume or peak-year profit. The company shares real cost data with the union and with its board. It treats the minimills as teachers, not as an annoyance.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Confirmation tests.** Each must come out yes. If any comes out no, Ironbridge may only be able to stay good, or survive smaller.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop raising prices to protect profit.** Every profit plan should start from cost.
2. **Stop defending commodity bars** against minimills. Exit or sell.
3. **Stop funding capacity that can't earn its keep.** That means no new capacity, plus a close-or-sell plan for high-cost plants once the audit is done.
4. **Stop building ships** as part of the core. Sell or spin off.
5. **Stop treating vertical integration as identity.** Judge every mine, ship and railroad on what it contributes to delivered cost.
6. **Stop status spending.** End perks, monogrammed-doorknob symbolism and bonuses that aren't tied to through-cycle return on capital.
7. **Stop treating import protection as the strategy.** Lobbying can buy time at most. It is not a hedgehog.
8. **Stop reading peak-year profits as health.** Report normalized earnings to the board.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:** cost honesty; quality customers can count on; safety and respect for the people who make the steel; long-term thinking over peak-year thinking.
- **Core purpose:** to make the steel North American industry builds with, better and more cheaply than anyone can bring it in.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** by Year 20, be the world's lowest delivered-cost producer of flat-rolled steel to North American manufacturers.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Executive pay tied to through-cycle return on capital.
  - Outside directors.
  - A public cost-per-ton report by plant.
  - A succession pipeline that includes outside hires.
  - Capital approvals that require a profit-per-ton-of-capacity test.
