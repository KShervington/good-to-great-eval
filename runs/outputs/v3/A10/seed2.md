# Good to Great: Harwell-Brandt Company

## Verdict

**Harwell-Brandt is a good company caught in a doom loop. It must commit to one Hedgehog Concept and divest everything outside it.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Be the best in the world at leading everyday mouth-and-throat consumer brands (mouthwash, gum, lozenges, antacids), measured by *profit per category-leading brand*.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** direction. Management has announced two strategies in about a year, so no push gets a chance to compound.
- **Stop doing:** (1) the bakery; (2) the optical business; (3) the distant-second shaving fight against Rival A.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** margins fell about 40% and return on equity (ROE) is about 9%, while regulatory, legal and criminal matters pull management away from the business.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** segment profit and capital employed, prescription-drug (Rx) pipeline and R&D spend, and gum market share. If Rx earns the best returns *and* has a real pipeline, the Hedgehog Concept flips to pharma.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. The verdict rests on the dossier alone, with no segment economics.

Greatness potential: 12/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Harwell-Brandt is a narrower, quieter and far more profitable company. It owns the leading brands in a small set of mouth-and-throat categories and defends them country by country with substantiated claims, steady product renewal and concentrated marketing. It has sold the bakery, optical and shaving businesses to owners who can do better with them. It has stopped making strategy announcements to the press. It has stopped using acquisitions to manufacture momentum. Every major decision is tested against one question: does this raise profit per leading brand? Its plants are known for safety, and its overseas dealings are clean. Its returns stay above its peers' long after the current leadership team has gone. `[ASSUMPTION]` This picture depends on the segment audit confirming that the consumer mouth-and-throat brands, not prescription drugs, are the true economic engine.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *We will be the best in the world at building and defending category-leading mouth-and-throat brands that people use every day, and we will judge everything by profit per leading brand.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) research-based prescription drugs; (b) broad consumer products; (c) mouth-and-throat consumer brands; (d) global distribution in about 140 countries | **(c)**. The mouthwash brand holds a large share of its market, and the gum, cough-drop and antacid lines sit next to it. On (a), the dossier says larger drug companies set the pace, and regulators forced divestitures that shrank the drug unit. On (b), "broad" is not a world anyone can be best in. | Shaving (a distant #2), bakery, optical, and broad-line research pharma |
| **Economic engine** | profit per employee; per country; per brand; per advertising dollar | **Profit per category-leading brand** `[ASSUMPTION: needs segment data to confirm]` | Brands with no credible route to a #1 position in a category or country |
| **Passion** | Unknown from the dossier | `[ASSUMPTION]` Pride in household-name brands people trust. This must be tested with the brand teams, not assumed. | — |

**Does the candidate pass the tests?**
- *Explains past wins?* Partly. The most durable asset in the company's history is the leading mouthwash brand, acquired about 25 years ago.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: bakery, optical, shaving and broad pharma.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It contradicts the new team's stated aim of reviving pharmaceuticals.

**What would confirm it:** (1) the mouth-and-throat brands earn returns on capital above company average; (2) gum and cough-drop brands hold #1 or #2 share in their categories in major markets; (3) the Rx business shows no pipeline that could put it ahead of the larger drug companies.

**Alternative Hedgehog Concept (B):** be the best at a narrow set of prescription-drug therapeutic areas. Choose B only if the audit shows a superior pipeline and better economics. If B wins, the consumer brands become the businesses to sell. Either way, choose once and hold the choice for a decade.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Narrow portfolio of category-leading mouth & throat brands
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    2. Concentrated marketing + distribution, every claim substantiated
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    3. Share and pricing power → higher PROFIT PER LEADING BRAND
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    4. Cash reinvested in product renewal and clinical proof of efficacy
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    5. More categories × countries where we are #1 (organic, one at a time)
 │            │
 └────────────┘  (retailers give shelf space to leaders, which strengthens step 1)
```

**Current push (the next one or two turns):**
1. **Narrow the portfolio.** Sell the bakery and optical businesses, which fit neither proposed direction, and decide on shaving.
2. **Rebuild claim credibility.** Make every flagship claim clinically substantiated, so the corrective-advertising episode becomes a turning point instead of a lasting scar.

**Doom-loop check (found):**
- New direction after new direction: "leading consumer-products company" last year, "revive pharma" now.
- Acquisitions used to jump-start growth: about 35 years of mergers, including a $243 million cash purchase of a bakery chain two years ago.
- Reaction without understanding: the new direction was set before any segment economics appear in the record.
- Fighting the leader on its own ground: shaving against a dominant Rival A in the middle of a price war over disposables.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop owning the **bakery chain**. It sits outside both drugs and personal care.
2. Stop owning the **optical-products business**, for the same reason.
3. Stop fighting **Rival A in shaving** from a distant second place while disposables erode pricing.
4. Stop **announcing strategy to the business press** before the facts are in.
5. Stop **using acquisitions to manufacture growth**. Antitrust history (the challenge recommended against the drug merger and the forced divestitures) also makes this path expensive.
6. Stop making **advertising claims that cannot be substantiated** before they air.
7. Stop **treating 140 countries as equal priorities**. Concentrate on markets where a leading brand can win `[ASSUMPTION: country-level profit data needed]`.
8. Stop **trying to be a broad-line research drug company** against larger rivals, unless the audit reverses the Hedgehog Concept.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values (proposed, to be tested with the company's people):**
  1. Every claim is true and proven.
  2. Every plant is safe.
  3. We deal cleanly in every country.
  4. We steward brands rather than trade companies.
  5. We report results, not announcements.
- **Core purpose:** helping people feel healthy and confident every day.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Be the brand people around the world reach for first for a fresh, healthy mouth and throat: #1 in every core category in our top markets.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A Hedgehog council that meets quarterly.
- A substantiation gate that every advertisement must pass before it runs.
- A capital-allocation rule: no acquisition outside the Hedgehog Concept, and any acquisition inside it needs a credible path to #1 or #2.
- A compliance hotline that reports directly to the board.
- Board-level safety reporting.
- Two internal CEO successors kept in development at all times.
