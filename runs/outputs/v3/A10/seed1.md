## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Harwell-Brandt is a narrower company than today's. It is a consumer-health company built around products people use in their mouth and throat every day: the leading antiseptic mouthwash, cough drops, and gum, plus any adjacent remedy that can lead its category. Its old drug-house science stops trying to out-research much larger pharmaceutical companies. Instead, it becomes the engine that proves every product claim before the claim is made, which turns the trust lost in the corrective-advertising case into the company's distinguishing asset.

It uses its presence in about 140 countries to carry a small number of category-leading brands everywhere, instead of carrying many second-place businesses. It owns no bakeries and no optical shops, and it does not fight Rival A for blades. It runs its plants safely, operates cleanly in every country, and has stopped announcing a new identity every year.

**Denominator:** profit per category-leading brand. This means operating profit divided by the number of brands that are number one, or a clear number two, in their category. Brands that lead nothing push the ratio down, which builds the "sell or fix" discipline into the measure itself.

**Shared economics that make the intersection plausible `[ASSUMPTION]`:**
- Gum and cough drops can share confectionery-style manufacturing.
- Mouthwash, cough drops, gum and antacids share drugstore, grocery and checkout distribution.
- All of them benefit from drug-house credibility.

**Does it pass the tests?**
- **It explains past wins.** The mouthwash, the company's strongest asset, is squarely inside it.
- **It rules things out.** It excludes the bakery, optical, blades, and broad prescription-drug research.
- **It is uncomfortable.** It contradicts the new team's public talk of reviving pharmaceuticals.

**Confirmation tests, to run within 90 days:**
1. Segment data shows the mouth-and-throat units earn above-company return on capital.
2. Gum and cough drops lead or co-lead their categories in major markets.
3. An outside review finds no narrow therapeutic area where the prescription-drug pipeline could be best in the world. If one exists, re-run this gate. The Hedgehog may become "narrow Rx niche plus consumer health," but never "broad Rx revival."

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.**
- **Condition:** no segment economics and no pipeline data.
- **What turns it into a PASS:** tests 1–3 above.

- **Reaction without understanding:** the corrective-advertising order was fought all the way to the Supreme Court instead of the claim being fixed.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.**
- **Condition:** the loop closes logically, but every link is unverified, and the doom loop is active.
- **What turns it into a PASS:** segment data confirming link 5, plus four quarters with no new strategic announcement and no new outside acquisition.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop announcing strategic identities** to the press before the portfolio review has concluded.
2. **Stop acquiring outside the Hedgehog.** No more bakeries, optical shops or other unrelated businesses.
3. **Stop owning the bakery and optical businesses.** Run a sale process for each.
4. **Stop fighting Rival A head-on in razor blades.** Sell or harvest the business `[ASSUMPTION: confirm with data]`.
5. **Stop broad-front prescription-drug research meant to match bigger drug companies.** Keep only a niche that has been validated, plus the work of substantiating claims.
6. **Stop making efficacy claims that aren't clinically proven,** in every country.
7. **Stop litigating regulators over claims instead of fixing the claims.**
8. **Stop tolerating "local custom" payments.** Apply one standard of integrity in all 140 countries.
9. **Stop measuring success by size** (revenue, number of countries, number of businesses). Measure profit per leading brand instead.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Proof before claims.
- Plant safety audits.
- Quarterly review of profit by brand.
- Any new business must fit the Hedgehog and be able to lead its category.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- Country and brand managers have full autonomy over execution in their markets for the core brands.
- They have no authority to enter new categories or to make unproven claims.

**Bureaucracy check:** if compliance and safety rules multiply because local managers can't be trusted, that points to wrong-seat people. Fix the people, not the rulebook.

**Verdict: PASS.**
