## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halden is the company physicians trust most when a new medicine reaches millions of chronic-disease patients. That trust comes from two things: its science is first or best in class, and its evidence on benefit and harm is the most rigorous and most fully disclosed in the industry. It treats the patent cliff as a known, temporary dip and does not hide it. It tells investors plainly that growth will slow while new breakthroughs mature, and it refuses to buy or market its way to a growth rate its science has not yet earned. It runs the COX-2 program as a model of disciplined proof: large outcome trials, safety endpoints set in advance, independent monitoring, and promotion matched to the evidence. It reinvests in research breadth so that no single drug class carries the company. The PBM stays only if it demonstrably increases profit per breakthrough medicine. The founding line, "medicine is for the people, not for the profits, and profits follow," is again the operating rule and not just heritage.

## 5. Temptations to refuse (stewardship mode)

1. **Making growth rate the #1 objective.** Restore purpose first; growth is an outcome, not a goal.
2. **Promoting the COX-2 drug beyond its evidence.** That means no broad claims and no guidance that depends on it before the long-term outcome data are in.
3. **A mega-merger to fill the patent gap.** The CEO already refuses this; keep refusing it.
4. **Using PBM revenue to flatter headline growth.** Report pharma growth excluding the PBM separately and lead with it.
5. **Treating patent counts as a success measure.** Patents are inputs. Approved breakthroughs with proven outcomes are the output.
