# Good to Great: Halberd Tobacco Company

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Halberd wins by putting nearly all its brand money behind a few premium tobacco brands, led by Ridgeway, keeping each brand's image unchanged for decades, and carrying them abroad. The measure is **profit per advertising dollar**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** advertising → share gains. Ridgeway's image runs on mass advertising, including the new TV campaign, which regulators are now debating limiting.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop publicly disputing the Surgeon General's findings.
2. Stop buying small, unrelated businesses (razors, shaving cream, gum).
3. Stop spreading ad money across the whole portfolio.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the government has just concluded that Halberd's product causes lung cancer, and the CEO is publicly denying it.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** current share by brand, profit per ad dollar by brand, return on capital, and post-report consumer behavior.

**Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on Ridgeway's ten-year climb, with no financials.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halberd is no longer the sixth-place company with ten brands and a drawer of unrelated side businesses. It is a focused brand house whose flagship, Ridgeway, has one image that has stayed the same for decades. That image is strong enough to survive advertising restrictions, and its licensing and export partners abroad reproduce it faithfully.

Every advertising dollar goes where it earns the most. Secondary brands are funded only if they earn their place. Management runs on brand-level profit numbers and real consumer research, not instinct alone.

It treats the health evidence as a fact to plan around, not an argument to win in public. It complies early with labeling, funds independent product science, and keeps its credibility intact for the regulatory decades ahead.

**Frank caveat:** this skill measures *business* greatness, meaning sustained outperformance of the market. For a product just officially linked to lung cancer, commercial greatness and social good point in different directions. The plan below does not resolve that, and the board should not pretend it does.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Publicly disputing that smoking causes disease.** The government's expert committee has spoken. Denial destroys credibility with regulators and the public just as the rules are being written.
2. **Acquiring unrelated small businesses** as a "hedge." They are too small to hedge anything, and they consume management attention.
3. **Funding brands evenly.** Rank them by profit per ad dollar and starve the tail.
4. **Matching rivals' new-brand launches.** Halberd can't outspend a brand flood. Let the rivals scatter.
5. **Changing Ridgeway's image.** No new figures, no repositioning in a panic. The cowboy stays.
6. **Making TV the spine of the brand.** Use it while it is allowed, but never let the brand depend on it.
7. **Protecting the namesake brand for sentiment.**
8. **Me-too menthol or filter variants** unless they pass the Hedgehog test (Rival R owns that ground).
