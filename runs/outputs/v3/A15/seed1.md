# Good to Great: Halberd Tobacco Company

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Halberd puts its money behind a few iconic brands, led by Ridgeway, gives each one image that never changes, and carries them into every market where they can legally be sold. It judges success by profit per advertising dollar.

**Weakest flywheel link:** advertising reach. Halberd is outspent, and TV and print ads may soon be restricted.

**Top three stop-doing items:**

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Halberd has stopped trying to be a smaller copy of its rivals. It no longer runs a long line of brands or a scattered set of side businesses. It is the company that builds one or two cigarette brands with a single image that never changes, then takes those brands abroad faster and further than anyone else, earning more profit per advertising dollar than companies twice its size. It gets there by facing the health findings openly rather than fighting them. It complies early with warnings and advertising limits. It refuses any marketing aimed at minors. It keeps a standing science-and-regulation function that tells the board the truth. That honesty is what lets it survive the regulatory decades ahead without its brand becoming a liability. It outperforms because it concentrates where others scatter, and it lasts because the brand discipline lives in rules and people rather than in one CEO's taste.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop spreading advertising money across many brands.** Fund Ridgeway first, one premium brand second, and let the rest harvest.
2. **Stop buying small, unrelated businesses.** Freeze them. If the board wants a hedge against the health issue, it should be one deliberate, adequately sized decision with stated criteria. A collection of small purchases is not a hedge.
3. **Stop publicly disputing that smoking causes disease.** It erodes credibility with the regulators who will set advertising rules, it creates legal exposure `[ASSUMPTION]`, and it breaks the truth climate inside the company.
4. **Stop changing Ridgeway's image.** No new figures, no new positioning, no reaction to each rival campaign.
5. **Stop matching rivals' new-brand launches.** Their stream of new brands is their strategy, not Halberd's.
6. **Stop judging the company by the size of its ad budget.** Judge it by profit per advertising dollar.
7. **Stop any advertising or placement that reaches under-age audiences,** and review the TV schedule and imagery against that standard.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION]`, to be validated with leadership:
1. Craft in the product: blend, pack and quality matter.
2. One image, kept consistently, for the long run.
3. Concentration over scatter.
4. Honesty about the product and its risks. This value is new, and the company needs it.
5. Sell only to adults, by law and in spirit.

**Core purpose (beyond money):** to make the best-crafted tobacco products for adults who choose to smoke, and to sell them honestly.
*Note: this purpose is in real tension with the health findings. The company should state that tension openly, not paper over it.*

**BHAG (25-year horizon):** make Ridgeway the best-selling cigarette brand in the U.S. and the most widely sold brand outside it, while the company stays in good standing with the regulators of every market it sells in.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A written Ridgeway brand charter: image, pack, blend standards, and what may never change.
- Brand-allocation rules that tie the advertising budget to profit per ad dollar.
- A standing science-and-regulation seat that reports to the board.
- A licensing playbook, so international expansion does not depend on one executive.
- A formal CEO succession plan with an internal candidate.
