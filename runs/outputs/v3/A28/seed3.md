# Corvane Systems: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Corvane is good, but early and thinly evidenced.** Its strategy as stated (buy companies "across a wide range of fields" and pay with stock) is the classic path to the doom loop. The great version would be narrower.

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Corvane is best at turning its own semiconductor know-how into compact, rugged digital computers and control systems for defense and space, and it judges everything by profit per dollar of capital employed.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning design wins into profitable production. One ~$5M program carries that proof, and there is no unit-level return on capital in the evidence.
- **Top stop-doing:**
  1. Acquisitions outside semiconductors and digital systems.
  2. Paying with stock for deals that fail a written per-share return test.
  3. Diversifying away from defense by buying unrelated industries.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Corvane is buying businesses with stock and debt, and there is no evidence of the return each one earns.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** return on capital by unit against purchase price, the split between organic and acquired revenue, and program concentration.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on one profitable year, the backlog, and the dossier's description.

Greatness potential: 20/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Corvane is the independent company that brings solid-state electronics into military and space digital systems before the giants do. It is small enough to move fast and narrow enough to be the best at it. Every business unit reports its profit per dollar of capital employed every month. Acquisitions happen only inside the circle, only at prices that raise per-share returns, and only to speed up a flywheel that is already turning. Its devices division feeds its systems divisions, and its systems win production programs that larger rivals cannot match on size, weight or reliability. It outlasts its brilliant founder because capital decisions follow written criteria and are audited afterward. It reduces its dependence on defense by selling the same proven solid-state systems to adjacent space and industrial customers, not by buying unrelated industries.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Corvane is best at turning its own semiconductor know-how into compact, rugged digital computers and control systems for defense and space, and it judges everything by profit per dollar of capital employed.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | Ruled out (what it can *not* be best at) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world** | (a) Solid-state digital systems for defense and space; (b) capital allocation across technology businesses; (c) broad electronics and controls conglomerate | **(a)**, in the narrow world of compact, rugged digital computing and control where semiconductors give a size, weight and reliability edge | General defense prime work, commodity semiconductors (established makers are bigger), antennas and RF, unrelated industries. Candidate (b) is unproven: no acquisition prices or returns are in evidence. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per engineer; profit per program; profit per $ capital employed; profit per share | **Profit per dollar of capital employed**, by unit, rolled up per share `[ASSUMPTION: best denominator for a company growing by acquisition]` | Sales, backlog and headcount as headline measures |
| **Passion** | Founders' stated drive to "apply digital technology" broadly; engineering culture; R&D organization as a stated priority | Digital and solid-state engineering that matters in the field `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from founder backgrounds and the R&D priority]` | Running a portfolio of unrelated businesses for its own sake |

**Confirmation tests** (working hypothesis; Collins's companies took years to find theirs):
- **Does it explain past wins?** Partly. The artillery fire-direction computers, navigation systems, satellite data processors and devices division all sit inside it.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: wide-industry acquisitions, antenna work and open-ended research.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It contradicts the founders' stated breadth ambition and accepts defense dependence for now.
- **Confirmed if,** within 12–24 months:
  - systems that use in-house solid-state devices win against larger rivals;
  - production programs earn clearly higher return on capital than study contracts;
  - in-circle units out-earn any out-of-circle unit.

**Why not the capital-allocation candidate:** it may turn out to be the founder's real edge. But it does not narrow scope, and nothing in the evidence shows the price paid or the return earned on any of the five deals. It can be promoted if the unit return data from the first 90 days shows the acquisitions earning well above their cost. Until then, capital allocation is a **discipline inside** the hedgehog, not the hedgehog itself.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. Deliver the ~$5M artillery fire-direction computer contract on time and at a measured profit. This is the proof that step 3 leads to step 4.
2. Put devices-division solid-state parts into at least one in-house system program, so that step 1 actually feeds step 2.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring outside semiconductors and digital computing and control.** No deals justified by "applying digital technology to a wide range of fields."
2. **Stop using stock as currency for any deal that fails a written per-share return test**, including a dilution cost.
3. **Stop trying to cut defense dependence by buying unrelated industries.** Diversify later by selling proven solid-state systems to adjacent space and industrial customers.
4. **Stop taking study and research contracts that do not lead toward a production program inside the circle.** This includes air-defense and anti-submarine studies with no follow-on path.
5. **Stop bidding new antenna-design work** beyond current commitments `[ASSUMPTION: RF/antenna work is outside the digital and semiconductor core]`.
6. **Stop funding the adaptive "learning" computer beyond what customers pay for** `[ASSUMPTION: some internal funding exists]`. Keep it as contract-funded research only.
7. **Stop measuring success by sales, backlog and headcount growth.** Report profit per dollar of capital employed first.
8. **Stop making capital decisions without a written rationale and a 12-month post-audit.**
