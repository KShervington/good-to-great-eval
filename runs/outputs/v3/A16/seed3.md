## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Carrow does three things. It is the company that is first and best when smokers shift to a new kind of cigarette, as it already was with blended, filter and king-size menthol. It runs that discipline at home and abroad with an unusually low cost per unit from one consolidated plant. It does not try to buy its way out of tobacco's problems. It tells the truth about its product's risks, stops spending credibility on denying the science, and treats warnings and advertising limits as conditions to operate under rather than wars to fight. It judges itself by profit per thousand cigarettes and by share of each growth category, not by total size or by how many businesses it owns. Its leaders, its research staff and its capital decisions are all organized so that bad news reaches the top quickly.

**A caveat stated plainly:** "great" here means Collins's business definition, sustained outperformance of the market. Carrow's product causes the disease its customers die of. On the social-sector test of *distinctive impact*, its disappearance would not leave a hole that society needs filled. This run does not pretend otherwise.

**Tests**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes, all three.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: the fruit punch business, me-too launches, and defending a declining brand at any cost.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It commits Carrow to tobacco while the industry is under attack, and it requires the company to be honest about the risks that come with that commitment.

**What would confirm it:**
1. Return on capital shows tobacco well ahead of any available alternative.
2. Post-report consumer data shows smokers shifting between categories rather than quitting in large numbers.
3. Carrow's filter and menthol brands hold or gain share in the 12 months after warnings arrive.

**What would break it:** a sustained, steep fall in total consumption. In that case the "only good" conclusion, or a measured exit, becomes the honest answer.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop publicly disputing that smoking causes disease.** Carrow's own research said so a decade ago. Continued denial piles up legal, regulatory and credibility risk. It is also the "denial of risk" stage of decline.
2. **Stop further unrelated acquisitions** in foods, beverages or other fields. Run the fruit-punch business as a single, time-boxed test with hard numbers (see Section 9).
3. **Stop ceding overseas markets** to Rival H.
4. **Stop launching me-too brands** to keep pace with the fast-launching rival. Launch only into a real category shift.
5. **Stop defending the plain brand's former #1 position** beyond the point where it earns its profit per thousand. Harvest it rather than rescue it `[ASSUMPTION: that it is declining as filters gain]`.
6. **Stop making health-implying claims** for filters or any product without independent evidence. Do not aim marketing at young people. This is preventive: the dossier does not show either practice, but the pressure to do both will rise.
7. **Stop filling the top only from inside.**
