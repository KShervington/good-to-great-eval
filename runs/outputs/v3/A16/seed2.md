# Good to Great: Carrow Tobacco Company

## Verdict

**Carrow is good, and arguably was great for decades. It now faces an industry-wide crisis. Run this as a turnaround of focus, not an escape from the core business.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Carrow builds America's leading filter and menthol cigarette brands at the industry's lowest cost, and grows profit per thousand cigarettes rather than size.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** brand momentum. The plain brand has lost #1, Rival H's cowboy filter brand is growing, and heavy advertising, which drove every past win, is what regulators now want to limit.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) publicly disputing what your own researcher wrote; (2) buying unrelated businesses to escape tobacco; (3) copying the rival's rapid brand launches.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** ten years ago Carrow's own research called tobacco an important cause of lung cancer. The industry still denies this in public.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** profit per thousand cigarettes by brand, the share trend against Rival H, and consumer research done since the report.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on the dossier's history, with no current financials.

Greatness potential: 28/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Carrow is narrower and more honest than today's. It treats cigarettes as a mature, regulated, shrinking-risk business. It runs that business for profit per unit, not volume, and stops pretending it is something else. It concentrates on the filter and menthol segments, where it pioneered and where demand is moving. It harvests the plain brand instead of defending its past. It squeezes cost from its single large plant while removing that plant's single-point-of-failure risk. It stops saying in public what its own research contradicts, because that gap is its largest legal and credibility exposure. It allocates capital under a hard rule. Tobacco cash goes back into the core or to shareholders unless a non-tobacco business proves that Carrow's brand-building skill transfers to it. Size for its own sake is never a reason.

One plain caveat about the framework itself: the "distinctive impact" test is hard for a company whose core product causes disease. If Carrow vanished, rivals would fill the gap, and society would not mourn the gap. Carrow's achievable greatness is therefore sustained financial outperformance with integrity in how it operates. It is not the irreplaceable-impact kind. That ceiling is part of why the score above is low.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Consumer research on the health report's effect.** This is link 1. Right now nobody knows how far sales will fall.
2. **Defend and grow the filter brand against Rival H's cowboy brand** with a research-backed plan that does not depend on advertising freedoms likely to disappear. This is link 2.

**Doom-loop check:** warning signs are present.
- A diversification committee studied the question for seven years and then made one unrelated acquisition. That is drift followed by a lurch.
- One rival is launching brands at a fast pace. Matching it would be reaction without understanding, and Carrow already lost about $30M on a rejected brand.
- Public denial of causation is reaction to a threat, not understanding of it.
- No big jump-start program has started yet. That is the window to act.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop publicly disputing what your own research found.** Say nothing beyond the evidence. The internal memo makes denial a legal and credibility liability, not a defense.
2. **Stop buying unrelated businesses for size or as an escape.** Freeze acquisitions until the fruit-punch business proves that Carrow's skills transfer.
3. **Stop copying the rival's rapid launch cadence.** Launch only with consumer evidence, and not to fill shelf space.
4. **Stop funding growth for the plain nonfilter brand.** Harvest it for cash and profit. Do not try to win back #1.
5. **Stop implying that filters or menthol are safer.** No evidence in hand supports that claim, and making it would repeat the denial problem in reverse.
6. **Stop any advertising that reaches under-age audiences** `[ASSUMPTION: some current placements do]`. It is the most likely first target for regulators, and the right thing to do in any case.
7. **Stop running strategy by standing committee.** Seven years of study is how the doom loop starts.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values (proposed):**
1. Tell the truth about our products, inside and outside the company.
2. Craft and quality in what we make.
3. Cost discipline: every thousand cigarettes earns its keep.
4. Loyalty to our hometown and workforce.

**Core purpose (contested):** *To give adult smokers well-made products they freely choose, and to be honest with them about what those products are.* The framework asks for a purpose beyond money. For a product shown to cause cancer, the most defensible purpose is honest service of informed adult choice. Any purpose that depends on concealment will not endure.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Be the tobacco company whose word on its own product regulators and smokers believe, while earning the industry's highest profit per thousand cigarettes.*
