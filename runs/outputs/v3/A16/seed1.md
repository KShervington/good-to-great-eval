# Carrow Tobacco Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. Whether it can endure is in serious doubt.**

- **Hedgehog (working):** Carrow wins by being first and biggest in each new format adult smokers move to, made at the lowest cost per thousand. The next move is toward demonstrably lower harm.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** reading the shift honestly. Its own scientist named the cause a decade ago, while the industry's public line denies it.
- **Stop:** (1) publicly disputing the causal science; (2) fear-driven purchases outside tobacco brand-building; (3) brand launches that aren't a real format shift.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the internal memo, set against public denial, is a growing legal and credibility exposure.
- **Numbers that would change this:** share by brand, profit per thousand, return on capital, weekly post-report consumption, the punch business's P&L.
- **Confidence:** low to moderate. It rests on 50 years of launch history, not current financials.

Greatness potential: 25/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Carrow is the company that has led every format shift in American smoking: the modern blend, the mass filter, the king-size menthol. It decides to lead the shift the Surgeon General's report has just started, instead of denying it or running from it. It tells the truth about its product: no public statement contradicts what its own laboratory knows. It puts its best scientists and brand builders on developing and launching products with independently demonstrated lower harm. It keeps a few strong brands running through one low-cost plant. It refuses to spend tobacco cash on businesses it can't be best at, and it returns surplus cash to shareholders rather than buying its way out of fear.

One caveat sits underneath all of this. The framework measures business performance. Carrow's product has just been officially found to cause lung cancer. Any greatness here is bounded by that fact, and the plan below doesn't treat the product's harm as anything other than real.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Carrow wins by being first and biggest in each new format adult smokers move to, made at the lowest cost per thousand, and the next format is demonstrably lower harm.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Chosen | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best in the world at | (a) mass consumer brand-building in general; (b) cigarette brand-building; (c) **leading cigarette format shifts at scale** | (c): it explains the blend, filter and menthol wins | General consumer goods: no evidence yet. One punch acquisition proves nothing. |
| Economic engine | Profit per thousand cigarettes; profit per brand; **profit per brand-loyal adult smoker** | Profit per brand-loyal adult smoker `[ASSUMPTION]`, because launches are costly and loyalty is the asset | Profit per acquisition. Revenue growth for its own sake. |
| Passion | Tobacco craft; the home town; winning from the smallest seat | Winning launches and the home-town company identity `[ASSUMPTION]` | Running a beverage conglomerate |

**Denominator:** profit per brand-loyal adult smoker `[ASSUMPTION]`. Profit per thousand cigarettes is the measurable proxy until loyalty data exists.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. Every big win was a format first; the $30M failure wasn't one.
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It excludes unrelated diversification, brand proliferation, and marketing that relies on doubt.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It commits the company to building its next growth on admitting the product is harmful.

**Confirmation evidence needed:** current share and profit by brand showing that format leaders earn more per smoker; consumer tracking showing smokers moving toward perceived lower-harm formats after the report; R&D evidence that lower harm is technically achievable. If the science can't deliver lower harm, the concept collapses into "harvest the cigarette business with discipline," and Carrow can at best stay good.

**Guardrail:** "lower harm" must be independently demonstrated. Filters spread on health worry, but the dossier contains no evidence that they reduce disease. Implying a health benefit without proof is the same denial under another name.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 [1] Read smokers' next shift early
     (own health science + consumer panels, reported straight)
        │
        ▼
 [2] Launch the leading brand in that format: first, and at scale
        │
        ▼
 [3] Heavy, consistent advertising behind FEW brands
        │
        ▼
 [4] Brand-loyal adult smokers / share
     ◄── denominator: profit per brand-loyal smoker
        │
        ▼
 [5] Volume through one plant → lowest cost per thousand
        │
        ▼
 [6] Cash → funds health research and the next launch ──► back to [1]
```

**Weakest link:** [1]. The company has the signal (its own 10-year-old memo, and now the Surgeon General's report), but its public stance is to dispute it `[ASSUMPTION that Carrow shares the industry line]`. A flywheel can't turn on a reading of the market it refuses to believe.

**Current push:**
1. Make [1] honest and fast: a health-science function with board access, plus weekly consumer tracking.
2. Brief [2] for the first lower-yield product, held to a strict evidence standard.

**Doom-loop check:** warning signs are present.
- A diversification committee formed under health pressure, followed by a first acquisition in an unrelated category (fruit punch). This looks like reaction without understanding.
- A large rival is launching many brands quickly. Carrow should not copy that; its own failure shows how expensive launches are.
- Public denial of the science is itself reaction without understanding.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Publicly disputing that smoking causes disease** while internal research says otherwise.
2. **Buying businesses outside tobacco brand-building out of fear.** No acquisition goes ahead without passing a written Hedgehog test.
3. **Launching brands that aren't a genuine format shift.** Don't match the rival brand-for-brand.
4. **Implying health benefits without independent evidence**, through filters, "mild" language or anything similar.
5. **Fighting warning labels as strategy.** Comply early and spend the effort on products instead.
6. **Marketing aimed at, or effectively reaching, people under smoking age** `[ASSUMPTION: no current evidence either way, but this is where advertising limits will land first]`.
7. **Defending the plain brand's former #1 position with spending.** Let it age gracefully and move investment to filters and new formats.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** (proposed; hold leadership to them, don't just print them):
1. Tell customers and regulators the truth about the product.
2. Be first and best, not most.
3. Lowest cost per thousand, always.
4. Stewardship of the home town.

**Core purpose:** *Make the tobacco products adult smokers choose as well, as cheaply and as honestly as they can be made, and lead the move to less harm.* A brutal note: a purpose beyond money is thin for this subject. That thinness is itself a threat to how long the company can last.

**BHAG** (25-year horizon): *By Year 25, most of Carrow's volume is in a product with independently demonstrated lower harm, and Carrow sold it first.* `[ASSUMPTION: the science may never deliver such a product; if it doesn't, retire this BHAG rather than fake it.]`

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Health science reports to the board, not to marketing.
- No public claim may contradict internal research; general counsel signs off.
- A written Hedgehog test for every acquisition.
- A launch gate: a real format shift plus consumer evidence, before any national launch.
- Succession bench for chairman and president, including at least one outside perspective.
