# Good to Great: Tolland Stores, Inc.

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great, and drifting off its Hedgehog Concept.**

**Hedgehog Concept:** *Tolland wins by being the everyday-low-price discount store that Northeast small towns choose over any national chain, measured by profit per store.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** expansion. New stores now come from debt-funded distressed chains in 14 new states. They no longer come from profitable small-town stores near existing operations.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Buying distressed chains outside the core region.
2. Running variety-store formats.
3. Borrowing to buy growth (debt is about 80% of net worth).

**Most urgent brutal fact:** no one has shown that converted acquired stores earn what core stores earn. Meanwhile Rival W, which runs the same model, advances store by store.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** profit per store after conversion, comparable-store sales, shrinkage, full-year Year −1 earnings, and the debt schedule.

**Confidence:** moderate on direction, low on magnitude. It rests on a strong long-term record, with no store-level data on the new footprint.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tolland is smaller in geography and larger in dominance. It runs one format, the everyday-low-price discount store, in dense clusters of small and rural towns from northern New England to the mid-Atlantic. In each of those towns it is the store people choose first, even after a national chain opens nearby. Every store is judged on the same Monday scorecard and earns its keep in profit per store. Growth comes from steady store-by-store openings into adjacent towns, funded from cash flow. Acquisitions are rare, small and inside the region. The balance sheet is conservative enough to survive a price war with Rival W. The company no longer depends on the founding family to make it work.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Denominator:** four-wall profit per store, reported separately for core, converted and variety stores.

**Tests:**

- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It probably means a smaller footprint, dropping the 12% growth target, and possibly selling part of the Year −1 acquisition.

**What would confirm it:**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop buying distressed chains outside the core region.** Allow only small, contiguous additions that meet the store-profit hurdle.
2. **Stop running variety-store formats.** Sell or convert them, including the Florida variety stores.
3. **Stop financing growth with debt.** No new borrowing for expansion until leverage falls (see the scorecard).
4. **Stop publicly committing to 12% annual growth.** It is a sales target that rewards size, not profit per store.
5. **Stop operating in states where the company cannot reach distribution density** within a set period `[ASSUMPTION: 3 years]`.
6. **Stop running more than one pricing and merchandising model.** Every store keeping its banner runs pure everyday low pricing, or exits.
7. **Stop counting tax credits as a reason to buy.** Judge every deal on its pre-tax store economics.
