# Good to Great: Pacifica Bancorp

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Be the best-run, lowest-cost bank for the households and small businesses of our home state, measured by **profit per employee**, not by being the biggest bank in the world.

**Weakest flywheel link:** cost-to-serve. A high-fixed-cost branch network and lagging ATMs both look worse once deposit rates float.

**Top three stop-doing:**
1. Stop treating asset growth as the measure of success.
2. Stop new lending to developing countries.
3. Stop decentralized credit approval without central limits.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The cheap, rate-capped deposits that fund everything are being deregulated within six years. Meanwhile the bank holds long fixed-rate mortgages and fast-grown risky loans whose losses it has not measured.

**Numbers that would change this answer:** loan losses and reserves by category, exposure by country, the mortgage–deposit maturity gap, and expense ratios against peers.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacifica stops competing to be the world's largest bank and becomes the indispensable, lowest-cost bank for ordinary people and small businesses in its home state. That is the "little guy" mission it was founded on.

- **Funding and rate risk:** It earns its deposits in a free-rate market by being cheaper to bank with, not by relying on a regulatory ceiling. It matches the maturity of its assets to its funding.
- **Credit:** Every loan meets one central credit standard.
- **Technology:** It uses its computing heritage to automate routine transactions, so the branches carry relationships rather than paperwork.
- **The rest of the business:** Its international and venture businesses shrink to what directly serves home-state customers.
- **Results:** It earns top-quartile returns among large U.S. banks through the deregulation transition, and that performance outlasts any one CEO.
- **Leadership:** It is led by someone the board picks for humility and resolve. That person is neither a caretaker nor a diversifier.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Measuring success by total assets or by "world's largest bank."
2. New lending to developing countries. Manage the existing book down.
3. Approving credit through decentralized authority without central limits and review.
4. Originating long fixed-rate mortgages to hold on short-term deposits. Sell them, hedge them, or move to adjustable-rate products `[ASSUMPTION: those options are available]`.
5. Direct venture-capital investments in high-risk technology companies.
6. Expanding overseas branches. Keep only what serves home-state customers' international needs.
7. Growing agricultural and construction loans at the recent pace until credit quality is measured.
8. Applying "mistakes of decision beat mistakes of indecision" to credit and capital decisions. Keep the bias for action in service and operations.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- One credit standard, applied everywhere.
- A monthly asset-liability review.
- Profit per employee and the expense ratio reported to the board every quarter.
- Every new product or market must pass the Hedgehog Concept test in writing.

**Framework:**
- **Freedom inside it:** branch and small-business managers decide on service, local relationships and the customer experience.
- **Set centrally:** credit limits, pricing floors and the capital plan.

**Bureaucracy check:** Decentralized authority was a growth tool, not a sign that the right people were in place. Central credit policy is discipline, not bureaucracy. Add rules only where people in the wrong seats make them necessary, and fix those seats instead.
