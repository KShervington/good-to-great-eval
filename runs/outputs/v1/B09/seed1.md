# Pacifica Bancorp: Good to Great

**Verdict: good, with a great past, but that greatness rested on capped deposit rates that the law is now phasing out.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the best bank in the world at serving the households and small businesses of its home state, measured by profit per customer relationship, not by size.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cheap, stable funding. Deregulation removes the rate cap, and only deeper relationships and a lower cost to serve can replace it.
- **Top stop-doing:** (1) chasing asset growth and the "world's largest" title; (2) new developing-country and decentralized concentrated lending; (3) holding long fixed-rate mortgages funded by short deposits.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** record earnings may be hiding losses. Fast-grown risky loans and a rate mismatch are both still unmeasured.
- **Numbers that would change the answer:** reserve adequacy by loan type, country exposure, the maturity gap, expense ratio against peers, and home-state retail profit at market deposit rates.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacifica goes back to what it was founded to be: the bank of the little guy, now run with modern discipline. It is the default bank for the households and small businesses of a large, fast-growing state. It earns more per customer relationship than any large bank anywhere, because its cost to serve is the lowest in the industry (automation and a right-sized branch network) and because its customers keep their deposits there for service, products and trust rather than a legal rate cap. It no longer measures itself by assets. It lends only what a central credit function understands, matches the maturities of its funding to its loans, and keeps its international business to the work that serves its home-state customers. It is smaller on the balance sheet, possibly no longer "the world's largest," and much more profitable and resilient.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Pacifica will be the best bank in the world at serving the everyday households and small businesses of its home state, and will judge every decision by profit per customer relationship, not by size.**

*Working hypothesis. Collins's companies often took years to find their Hedgehog Concept. Confirmation tests are below.*

|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | World's largest bank; international lending; home-state consumer and small-business banking; consumer banking technology; venture investing | **Home-state consumer and small-business banking.** It has the densest branch network, the largest consumer deposit base, an origin as the little guy's bank, and a record of pioneering consumer products and early account computerization. | Global wholesale and sovereign lending to developing countries; venture capital; warehousing long fixed-rate mortgages; concentrated agricultural and construction lending. Profit per employee is the secondary diagnostic. | Asset growth, the current implicit denominator. It rewards exactly the lending that creates the risks in §6. |
| **Passion** | Founding mission ("the little guy"); being the biggest; banking innovation | **Serving ordinary customers well, through innovation that reaches them.** `[ASSUMPTION]` Long-tenured staff identify with the founding mission. To be tested with staff, not assumed. | "Being the biggest" is pride, not passion for the work. |

**Tests of the intersection**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes: the founding growth model, the deposit base, the consumer-product pioneering.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: developing-country lending expansion, venture capital, decentralized international growth, rate-mismatched mortgages, asset-size targets.
- *Would it be uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It likely means shrinking the balance sheet and possibly giving up the "world's largest" title.

**Confirmation tests (needed for PASS)**
1. Segment profitability with deposits repriced at market rates shows home-state consumer and small-business banking at or above the bank's average return on assets.
2. Measured relationship depth (products per customer, deposit retention when rates rise) shows deposits staying for reasons other than the rate cap.
3. Cost to serve per relationship, compared with in-state rivals, shows a gap that automation can close.

If test 1 fails, the home-state franchise is only profitable because of the cap. The Hedgehog Concept would then need rework, starting with a return to Gate 4.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Turn [4] first: stop the bleeding.** Centralize credit authority, true up reserves, and stop adding rate mismatch. Nothing else compounds while unmeasured losses sit on the balance sheet.
2. **Then turn [1]→[3]:** deploy ATMs and branch automation to cut cost to serve, and build relationship depth before deposit ceilings fully phase out over the six-year window.

**Doom-loop check:** The *current* pattern already looks like the early doom loop: asset growth bought by moving into new loan categories and countries, a venture fund, and expanded authority. The risk under a new CEO is a jump-start: one big transformational program or acquisition to restore growth, followed by a new direction every few years. The rule is to push the same wheel, every quarter, for years.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring and rewarding asset growth.** Retire "world's largest" as a goal, internally and externally.
2. **Stop new lending to developing countries.** Freeze new commitments pending the country-exposure review, then run the book down to what capital can absorb.
3. **Stop originating long fixed-rate mortgages to hold on the balance sheet** with short-term funding. Match-fund them, sell them, or shift to adjustable-rate products.
4. **Stop decentralized authority for large or concentrated credits.** Local officers originate; a central committee approves.
5. **Stop growing agricultural and construction exposure** until loss experience and reserves are reviewed by category.
6. **Stop new venture-capital direct investments.**
7. **Stop treating the branch network as fixed.** Consolidate overlapping branches on branch-level profitability at market deposit rates.
8. **Stop the creed that action beats indecision.** Replace it with: facts first, then decisive action.
9. **Stop expanding the international network for its own sake.** International work must serve home-state customers or a clearly profitable niche.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency)**
- A monthly ALM review with board-set limits on the maturity gap.
- Concentration limits by country, sector and borrower, enforced centrally.
- Every product, branch and initiative judged on profit per relationship.
- A quarterly red-flag report to the board on problem loans, reserve coverage and deposit outflows, produced independently of the line businesses.

**Framework (freedom within boundaries):** Branch and relationship managers have broad freedom over service, pricing within bands, and local customer decisions. They work inside fixed credit, rate-risk and cost boundaries they cannot override.

**Bureaucracy check:** `[ASSUMPTION]` Decades of lifetime employment have probably produced layers that exist to manage around people in the wrong seats. Audit management layers before adding any new controls, and make the new risk controls few and hard rather than many and soft.

- An ALM committee with board-set gap limits.
- Compensation tied to profit per relationship and ROA, never to asset growth.
- A small board committee that runs continuous succession planning.
