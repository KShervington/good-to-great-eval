# Western Commonwealth Bank: Good to Great

## Verdict

**A good franchise that is sliding. It needs to narrow hard and stop chasing size.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Be the lowest-cost, most convenient bank for the households and businesses of its home state, run like a business and measured by **profit per employee**.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** *low cost*. The bank carries reportedly the industry's highest overhead just as its cheap-deposit advantage is being deregulated away.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Growing assets for the sake of size.
  2. Writing new energy, shipping and developing-country loans.
  3. Buying or building new businesses, including expanding the brokerage, to grow out of trouble.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** bad loans are emerging in four sectors at once, and their size relative to reserves and capital is unknown.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - non-performing loans vs. reserves and capital, by sector and country
  - profit by business line
  - three years of net income and return on assets (ROA)
  - costs as a share of revenue

Greatness potential: 15/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller in assets and far larger in earnings power. It is the bank of its home state. It serves households, small and middle-market businesses, and home-region farms through a dense, low-cost network of branches and machines. It leads the industry in profit per employee instead of total assets. It prices deposits at market rates and still wins, because its cost per transaction is the lowest in the state. It underwrites only credits it understands, in a region it knows. It keeps the founder's innovation instinct (the credit card, computerized records) and points it at one goal: making banking cheaper and easier for ordinary customers. It does not try to be a global money-center bank, a financial supermarket, or the world's largest anything.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the lowest-cost, most convenient bank for the households and businesses of our home state, run like a business and measured by profit per employee.*

### Three circles

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out (and why) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Global corporate and international lending; (b) a full-service financial supermarket; (c) home-state consumer and middle-market banking, delivered at scale through technology | **(c).** It is the dominant statewide franchise, it has a history of consumer-banking innovation, and retail produced about half of profits for a decade. | (a): the bank is a lender there, not a leader, and the loans are going bad. (b): it has no edge in brokerage or other new lines, and they add cost. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per employee; profit per customer relationship; profit per branch | **Profit per employee.** It attacks the highest-overhead problem directly and cannot be gamed by growing assets. | Total assets, the current implicit denominator, rewards exactly the behavior that created the problem loans. |
| **Passion** | Founder's mission of banking for ordinary people; being a first mover in consumer innovation; global prestige | Banking for ordinary people, plus consumer innovation `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from founding story and innovation record; must be tested with staff]` | Global prestige. If that is the real passion, it conflicts with the hedgehog and should be named as such. |

**Open question: agricultural lending.** The bank is the largest agricultural lender in the world, and farm loans are now souring. Being largest is not the same as being best. Home-state farm lending fits as middle-market banking only if underwriting is rebuilt. Agricultural lending outside the home region falls outside the hedgehog.

### Confirmation tests (it stays a hypothesis until these are met)

1. Profit-by-line data shows home-state retail and middle-market banking earn above the bank's cost of capital *after* deposits are priced at market rates.
2. Retail and middle-market lines have a lower loss rate than the international, energy and shipping books.
3. Branch and staff consolidation raises profit per employee without net loss of core relationships over 12 months.

**Does it pass the hedgehog tests?**
- *Explains past wins:* yes. The credit card, computerization, the statewide network and retail profits all fit.
- *Rules things out:* yes. International expansion, energy, shipping and the brokerage push all fall outside.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to:* yes. The bank would give up its "one of the largest in the world" identity.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  ┌──► 1. Disciplined cost & credit (fewer, better branches; machines; sound home-region underwriting)
  │            │
  │            ▼
  │    2. Lowest cost per customer → rising PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE
  │            │
  │            ▼
  │    3. Competitive deposit pricing + convenience, even after rate ceilings vanish
  │            │
  │            ▼
  │    4. More and deeper home-state relationships (deposits, cards, loans, business accounts)
  │            │
  │            ▼
  │    5. Greater density per branch and system (more volume over fixed cost)
  │            │
  │            ▼
  └─── 6. Reinvest in technology that cuts cost & improves convenience
```

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Link 1, credit.** Get the true size of the bad-loan problem and reserve for it honestly. Every other push depends on knowing the capital base.
2. **Link 1, cost.** Run a funded, specific overhead program covering branch consolidation, a hiring freeze and executive-perk cuts, with profit per employee as the stated target.

**Weakest link:** link 1. On cost it is currently the industry's worst. On credit, four portfolios are deteriorating at once.

- **Momentum by acquisition:** the brokerage, for about $55M.
- **Pursuit of scale as strategy:** 15% annual asset growth, with "more assets, more branches, more countries, more lines of business."

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring and rewarding asset growth.** Compensation, internal targets and public messaging should shift to ROA and profit per employee.
2. **Stop new energy, shipping and developing-country lending** beyond existing commitments and workouts.
3. **Stop buying or launching new businesses to grow out of problems.** Freeze brokerage expansion and treat it as a possible source of capital.
4. **Stop expanding into new states or countries** until the home-state engine is fixed.
5. **Stop holding new long-term fixed-rate mortgages on the balance sheet.** Originate adjustable-rate loans or sell fixed-rate ones `[ASSUMPTION: secondary-market sale is feasible]`.
6. **Stop opening branches by default.** Every branch has to justify itself on profit per employee.
7. **Stop relying on below-market deposit costs.** Price for a world without rate ceilings now, not in six years.
8. **Stop executive perquisites that signal size over discipline** `[ASSUMPTION: specific perks unknown]`.

### Disciplines and framework (Gate 6)

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every new loan, branch or product is tested against the hedgehog sentence.
- Quarterly profit per employee is reported to all managers.
- Credit officers hold an independent veto.

**Freedom within the framework:**
- Branch and regional managers set local service, staffing mix and small-business lending within cost and credit limits.

**Bureaucracy check:**
- If credit approvals need layers of committee review, ask whether that compensates for lenders who lack judgment. Fix the people rather than adding layers.
