# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

## Verdict

**State: a good bank that has started to decline. It needs to narrow now, not grow.**

**Hedgehog (working):** *Be the lowest-cost, most credit-disciplined consumer and middle-market bank in our home state, run to maximize profit per employee.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** cost-to-serve. The bank has the highest overhead in the industry, and that overhead sits on a cheap-deposit advantage the law is now removing.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop growing assets for the sake of size.
2. Stop new international, energy, shipping and developing-country lending.
3. Stop adding businesses, states and countries. Run the brokerage at arm's length, and sell it if capital is needed.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the cheap-deposit engine is closing on a fixed legal schedule. About three years of rate ceilings remain, and money-market deposit accounts are already live. At the same moment, bad loans of unknown size are surfacing.

**Numbers that would change this answer:** non-performing loans against capital and reserves; net income and ROA for the last three years; profit per employee and cost-to-revenue; profit by business line.

Greatness potential: 18/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller in assets and far larger in earning power. It returns to what its founder built: a bank for the ordinary people and businesses of its home state `[ASSUMPTION: founder ethos inferred from "immigrant financier" and "statewide branch bank"]`. It is the lowest-cost operator in that market because it judges every branch, product and person by profit per employee. It lends only where it knows the borrower: consumers, the state's middle market, and the state's agriculture, all underwritten conservatively. It has stopped lending to distant energy companies, shipping lines and sovereign borrowers, and it has stopped buying businesses it does not understand. It turns its history of innovation (first successful bank credit card, early computerization) toward one goal: cutting the cost of serving a customer. Its stock is valued for consistent returns, not for being the largest. Its rivals fear its cost base more than they fear its size.

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The statewide branch network, the credit card and early computerization all served the in-state retail customer. Retail produced about half of profits.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: international, energy, shipping and sovereign lending, new states, new businesses, and asset growth as a goal.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means giving up the "one of the largest banks in the world" identity.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
- Profit by business line shows in-state consumer and middle-market banking earning above the bank's cost of capital once deposits are priced at market rates.
- Profit per employee in the best-run branches beats the bank's average by a wide margin, which would show the model is reachable.
- Staff interviews confirm the passion circle.

**Current push (the next one or two turns):**

**Weakest link:** link 2, cost-to-serve. The bank has had the highest overhead in the industry, and the deposit subsidy that once offset it is being dismantled.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring success by asset size or rank.** Remove asset growth from every plan, bonus and press release.
2. **Stop new international, energy, shipping and developing-country lending.** Put the existing exposure into managed run-off.
3. **Stop entering new businesses.** The brokerage gets no further capital or integration. Hold it as a financial asset and sell it if capital is needed.
4. **Stop expanding into new states and countries** to "grow out" of problems. Growing out of problems is grasping for salvation.
5. **Stop writing long fixed-rate loans funded by short deposits** without a matched-funding or sale plan.
6. **Stop keeping branches open for coverage or prestige** when they fail the profit-per-branch test.
7. **Stop reporting credit quality optimistically.** Reserves follow the facts, not the earnings target.
