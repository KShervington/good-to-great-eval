# Western Commonwealth Bank: Good to Great

**Verdict: a good franchise that is already sliding. Stop growing and start narrowing.**

- **Hedgehog (working):** Western Commonwealth will be the lowest-cost, most-trusted bank for the households, farms and mid-sized businesses of its home state, run to maximize profit per employee.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cost to serve. By some accounts its overhead is the highest in the industry.
- **Top three stop-doing:**
  1. Managing for asset size.
  2. Making new energy, shipping and developing-country loans.
  3. Buying or launching businesses to grow out of trouble.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** nobody has measured the bad-loan book, and deregulation is removing the cheap deposits that would absorb the losses.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:**
  - Net income and return on assets for the last three years.
  - Non-performing loans against reserves, by sector and country.
  - Profit by business line.
  - Costs as a share of revenue.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller in assets and larger in earnings. It is the bank of its home state: the place ordinary families, farmers and mid-sized companies keep their money and get their credit. It serves them at a lower cost per customer than any rival, because it uses technology in operations the way it once pioneered the credit card and computerized records. It lends only where it knows the borrower and the local economy. It recognizes problem loans quickly and pays nobody for volume. Its scoreboard is profit per employee, not rank by assets. It no longer depends on regulated cheap deposits for its margin. It earns its keep through efficiency, credit judgment and deep customer relationships that rivals cannot copy quickly.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Concentrate on home-state households, farms & mid-sized firms
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │   2. Cut cost to serve (branch consolidation, automated operations)
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │   3. Price competitively on deposits and loans, lend only where
 │      the bank knows the borrower (tight, independent credit)
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │   4. Lower costs + lower losses → higher PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │   5. Retained earnings rebuild capital; reinvest in tech & service
 │              │
 │              ▼
 └── 6. Better, cheaper service wins more products per household
```

**Weakest link:** step 2, cost to serve. Overhead is reportedly the highest in the industry. If cost doesn't fall, step 3 can't price competitively against deregulated deposit rates, and the wheel stalls.

**Current push** (next two turns):
1. **Recognize and ring-fence the bad loans.** This clears step 4, so losses stop silently eating capital.
2. **Start branch and headcount rationalization with a published cost-per-customer target.**

**Doom-loop check.** Present now, on four counts:
- Assets grew 50% in two years, and the bank grew 15% a year while its peers did not.
- It keeps adding countries and business lines.
- It has just bought a brokerage.
- Management is openly considering "expanding into new businesses and states to grow out of the problems."

That last option is a classic grasp for salvation through a big program. It is the doom loop, not the flywheel.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop managing for asset size and league-table rank.** Drop asset growth from every executive goal.
2. **Stop new lending to energy, shipping, developing-country borrowers and international corporates.** Only existing workouts continue.
3. **Stop acquisitions and new ventures.** Make no further deals like the brokerage. Within 12 months, decide whether the brokerage is a core consumer product or a source of capital.
4. **Stop interstate and new-country expansion** until home-state profit per employee leads the peer group.
5. **Stop running branches that don't earn their cost.** Consolidate them.
6. **Stop holding new long fixed-rate mortgages funded by short-term deposits.** Price new mortgages as adjustable, or sell them.
7. **Stop paying lenders for volume.** Pay them for risk-adjusted profit.
8. **Stop "extend and pretend."** Don't roll over bad loans to avoid recognizing the loss.
