# Good to Great: Wexley Medical Corporation

## Verdict

**Wexley Medical is good, not yet great.** It has a strong founder-built foundation, and that founder era has just ended, leaving a vacuum.

- **Hedgehog Concept (working):** *Wexley turns clinicians' frustrations into specialty devices that make care faster or cheaper, and it competes only in niches it can lead.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** getting devices in front of clinicians. The company has effectively no sales force.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Salary-only sales pay.
  2. Routing every decision through the founding family.
  3. Answering Rival H with implants or an early IPO.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Wexley has no working sales engine and no budget. The man who generated its products is in his 80s.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:** margins and profit by product line, the multi-year sales trend, and market share in each niche.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Wexley is a specialty medical-device company that carries the founder's discipline in its process rather than in his head. Its rule stays the same: every product must make caregivers more efficient or cut the cost of treatment. Surgeons and nurses bring it problems. Small product-line teams turn those problems into devices. Commission-paid specialist reps demonstrate the devices at the bedside and in the operating room. The company enters only niches where it can be #1 or #2.

Patient-handling equipment funds the business. Orthopedic powered instruments become the growth spearhead, riding the rise in joint-replacement surgery without trying to make implants. Each product line has a budget, a goal and an accountable manager. Growth comes from steady turns of the flywheel, not from a public offering or an acquisition.

**Current push:**
1. Rebuild a commission-based specialist sales force, starting with powered instruments, where the CEO's experience applies directly.
2. Replace the founder's personal idea flow with a formal surgeon and nurse input channel and a product-review gate that applies the founder's rule.

**Doom-loop check:**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying the sales force salary-only.** Move to commission or a heavily variable plan tied to niche results.
2. **Stop routing every decision through the founding family.** Give each product line its own budget authority.
3. **Stop running without a budget or goals.** No unbudgeted spending after the first annual plan.
4. **Stop considering implants as a response to Rival H.** Wexley has no capability there and faces a pharma-backed giant. Serve the surgeries implants create instead.
5. **Stop treating an IPO as a near-term fix.** Defer it until there are 2–3 years of budgeted, audited results `[ASSUMPTION: no urgent capital need]`.
6. **Stop launching products that fail the founder's rule** or that can't reach #1 or #2 in their niche.
7. **Stop international expansion into new countries** until the domestic flywheel turns.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Every product makes caregivers more efficient or treatment cheaper.
2. Listen to clinicians first.
3. Each person owns a number.
4. Integrity with hospitals and surgeons.

**Core purpose:** help caregivers deliver better care at lower cost.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** become the instrument and patient-handling brand surgeons and nurses name first in every niche Wexley enters, the way the trade already names turning frames after its founder.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An annual budget and goal-setting cycle.
- Product-line managers with their own P&L.
- A clinician advisory panel and a product-review gate that applies the founder's rule.
- A commission sales structure.
- A documented CEO succession plan.
