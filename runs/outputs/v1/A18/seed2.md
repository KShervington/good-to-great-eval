# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict

**The business is good and fading. It has to narrow hard or it will keep sliding.**

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** *Duplicon will be the best in the world at equipping the short-run printer (the quick-print shop and the in-plant print room) and will earn its profit per installed press through plates, supplies and service.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** offset's cost-per-page edge on short runs, which plain-paper copiers erode every year.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Chasing leadership in the electronic office against much larger firms.
  2. Putting new development money into plate-based addressing. Harvest it instead.
  3. Running 27 subsidiaries and five lines as a growth portfolio.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** both founding franchises are mechanical, ink-based technologies, and both are being replaced by technologies Duplicon does not lead.
- **Numbers that would change this answer:**
  - profit and return on capital for each line;
  - supplies and service profit per installed press;
  - the run length at which copiers beat offset;
  - growth and market share in the credit-card line.

`Greatness potential: 14/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Duplicon is smaller, perhaps around half its peak sales `[ASSUMPTION]`, and much more profitable per dollar of capital. It is the default supplier to the world's short-run printers. A new quick-print shop opens with a Duplicon press, a Duplicon platemaker and a Duplicon service contract, and it buys plates, masters, ink and parts from Duplicon every week for twenty years.

It wins because it gets a shop from customer original to finished job faster and more cheaply than any copier or rival press, for runs of a few hundred copies and up. Engineering graphics and the general-office ambitions are gone. Addressing is run for cash and wound down in an orderly way. Credit-card embossing has been either confirmed as part of the core or sold at a good price. Management talks about profit per installed press, not total sales.

```
   │              │

   │              │
   │              ▼
   └── 6. Reinvest in press/platemaking engineering and shop-owner support
```

**Current push:**
- **Turn 1:** measure profit per installed press and the copier crossover point. You can't push a wheel you can't see.
- **Turn 2:** cut make-ready and platemaking time so the cost-per-page edge moves to shorter runs, where copiers are attacking.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Inconsistent direction:** five lines and 27 subsidiaries suggest it has already happened `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Live danger:** a big electronic-office program or an acquisition meant to restart growth.
- **Peer precedent:** Rival S's point-of-sale write-off shows what this kind of leap can cost.
