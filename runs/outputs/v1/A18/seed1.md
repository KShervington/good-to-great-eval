# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great

**Verdict:** Duplicon is a former franchise in structural decline. It is still *good* by installed base and name, but the core technology is being replaced by plain-paper copying and computers. The only credible great version is a **smaller, narrower one**.

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** *Be the world's best supplier of short-run offset printing systems to quick-print shops and in-plant print rooms, earning on every press installed.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** shop economics. Offset must stay cheaper per job than plain-paper copying at the run lengths shops actually print.

**Top stop-doing:**
1. Don't try to become an electronic-office leader against much larger firms.
2. Don't build a plain-paper copier to fight Rival X head-on.
3. Stop putting growth capital into plate addressing. Harvest it.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** sales peaked six years ago, and the core technologies are being displaced by technologies Duplicon doesn't lead.

**Numbers that would change the answer:** profit and return on capital by line, quick-print market share, card-systems economics, and supplies/service revenue per installed press.

`Greatness potential: 14/100`

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Duplicon is perhaps half its current breadth and noticeably more profitable on what remains. It owns the short-run printing room: the neighborhood quick-print shop, and the school or corporate in-plant shop. It sells and services the presses, platemakers and consumables that let those shops turn jobs around fast at a lower cost per copy than any copier above a known run length. Most of its profit is a recurring annuity of plates, masters, ink, parts and service from a large, dense installed base. It runs plate addressing for cash and sells or closes the lines it can't lead. It treats Rival X's copiers as a fact to design around, not a market to invade. It doesn't stake the company on an electronic-office leap. It wins by being the obvious, cheapest-per-job, best-serviced choice in a narrow world it can dominate.

**Current push (next 1–2 turns):**
