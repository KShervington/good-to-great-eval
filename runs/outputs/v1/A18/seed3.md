# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Duplicon will be the best in the world at keeping the professional short-run printer profitable, through the press, the supplies and the service, and it will earn on profit per installed press.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** whether a better press still wins shops' work as plain-paper copiers get cheaper at short run lengths.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop considering a large bet to lead the electronic office against much larger firms.
2. Stop fighting general office copying head-on.
3. Stop funding engineering graphics and plate-addressing as growth lines. Harvest or sell them.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Duplicon's core is being replaced by a technology it does not lead, sales peaked six years ago, and the most tempting response is the one most likely to sink it.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** profit and return on capital by line, quick-print market share, and the run length at which copiers beat offset on cost per page.

`Greatness potential: 12/100`

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Duplicon is smaller, focused and profitable. Its people wake up thinking about one customer: the professional short-run printer, meaning quick-print shops, in-plant print rooms and franchise chains. It sells that customer the most reliable, lowest-cost-per-impression system for turning an original into hundreds or thousands of copies. That system includes the press, fast platemaking, in-shop composition, supplies, financing and service, and it uses whichever technology serves the printer best. Its profit comes mainly from the recurring supplies and service attached to a growing installed base. The addressing-plate business is harvested for cash with dignity. Peripheral subsidiaries are sold to owners for whom they would be core. The electronic-office arms race is left to the giants. If it disappeared, independent printers would lose the partner that built their trade.

**Denominator:** profit per installed press.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The popular quick-print model is the clearest recent win in the dossier.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It rules out the electronic office, engineering graphics, general office copying, and growth investment in addressing.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It means shrinking the company, harvesting the founding franchise, and saying no to the "transformation" option.

**Confirmation evidence:**
1. Quick-print or offset lines earn above the cost of capital.
2. Duplicon holds #1 or #2 share in that niche.
3. Quick-print shop count is growing `[ASSUMPTION]`.
4. Offset keeps a cost-per-page advantage at shop-typical run lengths.

If tests 1 or 4 fail, the hedgehog is wrong. Duplicon may then only be able to be good, and should be managed for cash and an orderly sale of its lines.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Best short-run press + fast platemaking
 │        (lowest cost per impression, highest uptime)
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    2. More presses installed in quick-print shops & chains
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    3. Recurring supplies + service revenue
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    4. ↑ PROFIT PER INSTALLED PRESS (denominator)
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    5. Reinvest: press/platemaking R&D, dealer & service density,
 │       shop-owner success (financing, training, site selection)
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    6. Shop owners earn more → open more shops / add presses
 └────────────────────┘
```

**Current push:**
- **(5→6)** Measure and raise shop-owner profitability before adding anything new.
- **(1)** Close the convenience gap with copiers: fast plates from any original, and simpler operation.

**Weakest link: 1→2.** If copiers win the run lengths shops actually print, a better press does not grow the base.

**Doom-loop check:**
- The 27 subsidiaries are a red flag for past pursuit of growth beyond the core.
- The three competing strategic options are a red flag for direction-seeking.
- The "invest heavily in electronic office" option is the classic jump-start program.
- There is no current evidence of disciplined, consistent turns.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop considering a heavy investment to become an electronic-office leader against much larger firms.
2. Stop competing for general office copying against plain-paper copiers.
3. Stop growth spending on plate addressing. Harvest it for cash and supplies.
4. Stop funding engineering graphics. Sell it.
5. Stop running 27 subsidiaries as separate empires. Any line that cannot show a link to the hedgehog gets a sale date.
6. Stop acquisitions of any kind until the hedgehog is confirmed.
7. Stop treating every line as equally worth defending (the "defend and improve everything" option).
8. Stop judging units on sales volume. Judge them on profit per installed press and return on capital.
