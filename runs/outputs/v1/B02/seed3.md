# Good to Great: Homestead Mortgage Association

## Verdict

**Homestead is good, and its numbers look great, but they rest on a fragile base. It is not great yet, and it shows early signs of decline.**

- **Hedgehog:** Be the best in the world at pricing and managing American mortgage credit risk. Earn a fee per dollar of risk managed, not a spread on money borrowed cheaply.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** investor trust rests on an *implied government guarantee*, not on Homestead's own capital and transparency.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Managing the company, and everyone's pay, to the EPS-doubling goal.
  2. Growing the retained portfolio faster than the mortgage market.
  3. Running capital only $0.9B above the legal minimum.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the capital cushion is $0.9B on $887B of assets, about 0.1%. This coincides with a −14-month duration gap and the regulator's first-ever formal action.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:**
  - Profit split between the guarantee and portfolio businesses.
  - Fair values of the derivatives.
  - The premium and discount amortization policy.
  - The credit mix of recent purchases.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is the institution that makes American mortgage credit safe, cheap and liquid, and it would stand on its own merits even if nobody assumed Washington stood behind it.

- **How it makes money:** mainly from guarantee fees priced to the credit risk it takes. It does not make its money by holding an ever-larger portfolio funded by its cheap borrowing.
- **Capital:** it holds well above the legal minimum, so a rate shock or a housing downturn is an earnings event, not a solvency event.
- **Reporting:** GAAP results come first, and it explains volatility instead of defining it away.
- **Incentives:** pay rewards risk-adjusted economic profit and safety, not a per-share number chosen years ago.
- **Mission:** it meets its affordable-housing commitments through loans it can price and understand, with credit performance published by segment.
- **Critics:** it answers them with voluntary capital and disclosure standards rather than with political muscle, so its privileges look earned rather than defended.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Homestead will be the best in the world at pricing and managing the credit risk of American home mortgages, and will make its money per dollar of credit risk it manages, not per dollar of cheap debt it holds.**

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Interest-rate spread investing; mortgage credit underwriting and guarantee; MBS standardization and liquidity; Washington relationships | **Conforming mortgage credit risk** (0.5 bp losses across a $1.82T book) and **the standardized MBS that carries it** | **Rate arbitrage:** the edge is funding cost from the charter, not skill, and the −14-month gap shows it is not best-in-world at hedging. **Political influence:** it is a dependency, not a capability. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per share; profit per dollar of portfolio; profit per dollar of capital; profit per dollar of credit risk guaranteed | **Economic profit per dollar of mortgage credit risk managed**, measured on GAAP and capital-at-risk | **EPS:** it can be met by leverage. **Profit per dollar of portfolio:** it rewards balance-sheet growth funded by the implied guarantee. |
| **Passion** | Growth record; the mission of home ownership; being Washington's essential institution | **Expanding sustainable home ownership** (the $2T commitment, $670B delivered in Year −1) | "16 straight years" of growth. That is pride in a streak, not in a purpose. |

**Tests of the intersection:**

- *Explains past wins?* Mostly. The low credit losses and the mission record are real. The earnings growth is only partly explained, because much of it is portfolio spread.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: portfolio expansion beyond market growth, rate bets, and credit it cannot price.
- *Uncomfortable?* Very. It implies slower EPS growth, the end of the streak, and loss of the doubling-goal narrative.

**Status: working hypothesis.**

- **What would confirm it:**
  - Segment data showing the guarantee business earns a higher return on *market-required* capital than the portfolio does.
  - Guarantee fees holding when Homestead prices risk more strictly.
  - Affordable-goal loans performing close to model through a full credit cycle.
- **What would refute it:** if the guarantee business is uneconomic without cross-subsidy from portfolio spread, the honest conclusion is that Homestead can be good but not great in its current structure.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Disciplined credit underwriting & risk-based pricing
 │        (loan-level data on a quarter of U.S. mortgages)
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │     2. Low, predictable credit losses (0.5 bp)
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │     3. Investor trust in Homestead MBS — earned by OWN capital
 │        + transparent GAAP reporting   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │     4. Deep, liquid MBS market → lower mortgage rates for lenders & families
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │     5. More lender volume, incl. affordable-goal loans it can price
 │              │
 │              ▼
 │     6. Guarantee-fee economic profit per $ of credit risk  (denominator)
 │        → retained capital
 └──────────────┘
```

**Weakest link (3).** Today investors trust Homestead paper mainly because they assume the government stands behind it. Its own capital is $28.1B against a $1.82T book, about 65:1. Until trust rests on Homestead's own balance sheet and disclosures, the flywheel turns on borrowed momentum that Congress or Treasury could revoke.

**Current push, the next two turns:**

1. **Rebuild link 3.** Set a capital-surplus target well above the minimum, lead reporting with GAAP, and commission an independent review of hedge and amortization accounting.
2. **Re-weight link 6.** Cap retained-portfolio growth at or below mortgage-market growth. Publish guarantee and portfolio segment returns so the denominator becomes visible.

**Doom-loop check.** Homestead is not flailing: there are no serial reorganizations or rescue acquisitions. But a second loop is competing with the flywheel:

> *Public EPS goal → grow retained portfolio (+92% in five years, $415B to $798B) → spread income funded by implied guarantee → "operating" EPS on target → pay vests → bigger political and rate exposure → critics → defend the franchise politically → repeat.*

This is a growth treadmill: momentum is manufactured from leverage and a funding subsidy. It is the "undisciplined pursuit of more" pattern dressed up as consistency.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** The loop closes, but link 3 is unproven on Homestead's own merits.

- **Gaps:**
  - The CEO publicly set the EPS-doubling goal and tied his own pay, executive pay and a company-wide option grant to it. That points ambition at a number, and at the record of hitting it, rather than at the institution's safety. `[ASSUMPTION]`

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop managing to a public EPS target.** Let the doubling goal lapse and announce no successor target.
2. **Stop tying pay to EPS**, including the company-wide option grant structure for future grants.
3. **Stop growing the retained portfolio faster than the mortgage market.** Cap growth at market growth, and let it shrink as a share of earnings.
4. **Stop running capital near the legal minimum.** A $0.9B cushion is not a strategy.
5. **Stop leading investor communication with self-defined "operating earnings."** Lead with GAAP and reconcile to operating.
6. **Stop defending the "16 straight years" streak.** Treat a down year as acceptable.
7. **Stop buying loans or securities to hit housing-goal counts when Homestead cannot price their credit risk.** Meet the goals through products it understands.
8. **Stop answering structural critics mainly through political leverage.** Get ahead of them with voluntary standards instead.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**

- A hard duration-gap band, reported monthly to the board. Example: ±6 months `[ASSUMPTION: band to be set by the CRO]`.
- A capital surplus target, also reported monthly.
- Accounting-policy changes approved by the audit committee, never by the earnings owner.
- Credit performance published by segment, including the affordable-goal books.

**Framework boundaries.** Business units have full freedom on products, lender service and pricing *inside* the risk limits and the Hedgehog Concept. Nobody has freedom over the measurement system.

**Bureaucracy check.** Much of the compliance machinery likely exists because incentives pull against prudence. Fixing the incentives (Gate 3) should allow fewer, sharper rules. `[ASSUMPTION]`

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** make sustainable home ownership more affordable and more available to American families, especially those the market underserves.

**Core values:**

1. Safety and soundness come before growth.
2. Candor: report the hard number first.
3. Earn our privileges; never assume them.
4. Stewardship of a public trust.

**BHAG, 20-year horizon:** *Homestead manages American mortgage credit risk so well, with so much of its own capital, that the market would price its securities the same with or without any assumed government backing.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**

- An independent CRO with a veto on growth.
- A controller who reports to the audit committee.
- A board-set capital floor above the statutory minimum.
- Compensation formulas based on risk-adjusted economic profit.
- Published stress tests (rate shock and house-price shock) every quarter.
- Segment-level credit disclosure.
- Blameless post-mortems on every limit breach, starting with August Year −1.
