# Homestead Mortgage Association: The Great Version

## Verdict

**Homestead is good but not great.** It is highly profitable and its credit losses are tiny. That record, though, rests on a government privilege that can be taken away. The privilege funds a portfolio that is growing fast on thin capital, and the company's incentives point at an EPS number.

- **Hedgehog:** Be the best in the world at pricing and guaranteeing American mortgage credit, and earn a fair fee per dollar guaranteed, on capital that would stand without Washington.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** capital and earned trust. The capital cushion is $0.9B above the legal minimum, on $887B of assets.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) paying people for hitting an EPS target; (2) growing the portfolio faster than the mortgage market; (3) leading with "operating" earnings.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** last year's hedge losses, which operating earnings excluded, came to $1.77B. That is about twice the capital cushion, and the regulator has already acted for the first time ever.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** a fair-value balance sheet, the hedge positions, the amortization policies, and the credit mix of recent purchases.

Greatness potential: 20/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is the institution that makes American mortgage credit cheaper, steadier and more durable, and it gets there by being better than anyone at knowing which loans will perform and what that risk is worth. It earns its money mainly from guarantee fees, net of credit losses, not from a leveraged spread built on borrowing at near-Treasury rates. It keeps enough capital that the market would trust its guarantee even if the implicit federal backstop disappeared. It publishes numbers that show its risks rather than smooth them away. It meets its affordable-housing mission with loans families can actually keep. Its growth tracks the mortgage market and the quality of its underwriting, not a public EPS promise.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Homestead is the best in the world at pricing and guaranteeing the credit risk of American home mortgages. It earns a fair fee per dollar guaranteed on capital that would stand without a government backstop, so that homeownership, especially for low- and moderate-income families, is affordable *and* durable.**

This is a working hypothesis.

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Partly. Credit losses of 0.5 bp on a $1.82T book is the strongest real evidence of skill in the dossier.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It rules out portfolio growth for its own sake, EPS targets, and holding capital at the legal minimum.
- **Uncomfortable?** Very. Capping and shrinking the retained portfolio will slow operating-earnings growth, possibly for years.

**Evidence that would confirm it:**
- A segment split showing guarantee economics vs. portfolio economics on a fair-value basis (not in the dossier).
- The credit performance of recent goal-qualifying purchases.
- Whether guarantee fees stay adequate once capital is raised.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Best-in-class credit data & underwriting
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   2. Low, predictable credit losses
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   3. Lower, stable guarantee fees → cheaper, reliable credit
   │      for lenders & families (incl. LMI borrowers who can keep the loan)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   4. More conforming volume + deeper, more liquid MBS market
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   5. ★ Net guarantee income per $ guaranteed  +  more loan-level data
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   6. Strong capital + transparent numbers → earned trust
   │      (regulators, investors, policymakers)
   │          │
   └──────────┘  (trust lowers funding cost and secures the license to operate;
                  data and income fund better underwriting)
```

**Weakest link: component 6.** Capital is 3.2% of assets, about 31× leverage. The cushion over the minimum is about 14% of one year's operating income. Trust is eroding: there is a first-ever supervisory action, Treasury officials are critical, and there was a bill to repeal the credit line.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Rebuild capital and transparency at component 6.
2. Cap portfolio growth so that earnings growth has to come from components 1→5.

**Doom-loop check.** The loop actually running today is a leverage loop, not a flywheel:

> implicit backing → cheap debt → portfolio growth (about 14–18%/yr) → spread income → EPS growth → bonuses and options → more portfolio growth

It does close, but it compounds risk and political exposure along with earnings. Other doom-loop signals:
- Redefining earnings in response to an accounting change, rather than changing the risk.
- A single big public numeric target.
- Growth that accelerated (+21%, +19% operating income in the last two years) as the target deadline approached. [ASSUMPTION] that the timing reflects target pressure.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying anyone on EPS targets.** For future grants, replace them with multi-year, risk-adjusted measures with clawbacks.
2. **Stop growing the retained portfolio faster than the mortgage market.** Cap it, then let it run down toward the size actually needed for market liquidity.
3. **Stop leading with "operating" earnings.** Headline GAAP, a fair-value balance sheet, and a reconciliation. The excluded swings *are* the main risk.
4. **Stop running capital near the legal minimum.** Set an internal target well above it. [ASSUMPTION] Start by doubling the cushion within 12 months.
5. **Stop defending the charter with "scale lowers costs."** Earn legitimacy with numbers that would satisfy a skeptic at the Treasury.
6. **Stop buying goal-qualifying loans that wouldn't pass credit on their own.** Meet the goals through underwriting innovation, not looser standards.
7. **Stop letting the duration gap drift.** Set a hard band [ASSUMPTION ±6 months], with automatic rebalancing and escalation to the board.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Safety and soundness before growth.
2. Numbers that tell the truth, especially the bad ones.
3. Homeownership that lasts, not just loans that close.
4. Earn the privilege every year.

**Core purpose:** to make it possible for American families, especially those with modest incomes, to buy homes they can afford and keep.

**BHAG (25 years):** *Become the mortgage guarantor whose promise the world trusts on its own capital, so that the implicit government backstop no longer matters to anyone.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent CRO and a board risk committee.
- Incentives with clawbacks, based on risk-adjusted results.
- A monthly published fair-value balance sheet and duration gap.
- Annual stress tests with severe rate and credit scenarios, published.
- An audit committee that owns accounting-policy judgment.

| 1. Hubris born of success | Treating 16 years of double-digit growth and its charter as entitlement | Dismissing the regulator or critics as political rather than substantive |

| 4. Grasping for salvation | When growth stalls: riskier loan purchases, new product lines, aggressive lobbying, earnings management | Credit losses rising from the 0.5 bp base; surprise restatements; sudden strategic pivots |

**Root problems.** More than half the gates (7 of 11) came out CONDITIONAL. Two shared root problems explain them:

1. **An incentive system aimed at an EPS number.** It drives leadership focus, the bus, the truth climate and portfolio growth.
2. **A business model whose returns depend on a revocable government privilege funding a leveraged, opaque portfolio.** It drives the brutal facts, the weak flywheel link and the decline risk.

Fixing these two problems resolves most of the register.
