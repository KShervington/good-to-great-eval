## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Harlan Pacific is the bank that treats deregulation as its opening, not its threat. Its rivals lose the margin that rate ceilings used to protect. Harlan has already cut its cost base so far that it can pay market rates for deposits and still earn more per employee than anyone in the West. It has stopped trying to be a global bank and stopped trying to out-branch Rival B. Instead it is the dominant, efficient lender to its home state's households, mid-sized companies and property market. It underwrites with a conservatism that lets it come through credit cycles that hurt others. It also buys in-state banks and removes their excess cost. Its earnings come from discipline rather than size, and the discipline is built into systems and culture, so it does not depend on one chief executive.

2. **No new lending to Latin American sovereign or quasi-sovereign borrowers.** Work out the existing book (it cannot simply be exited) and reserve for it honestly.
3. **No branch-for-branch competition with Rival B.** Close or consolidate branches that cannot earn their keep.
4. **No "new financial ventures" opened just because deregulation permits them**, unless they fit inside the Hedgehog Concept.
5. **No large-corporate lending done for relationship prestige** `[ASSUMPTION]`, unless line-of-business data shows it clears the bar.
6. **No executive perks or head-office overhead that a lean regional bank would not carry.** Cost discipline has to be seen starting at the top.
7. **No loan growth paid for by loosening underwriting**, especially in real estate.
