# Harlan Pacific Corporation: Good to Great run (autonomous mode)

## One-screen verdict

**Good, not yet great. It can get there only by narrowing.** Harlan Pacific is a capable bank spread across five businesses. Deregulation is now removing the cheap deposits that paid for that sprawl.

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** *Be the best-run, lowest-cost bank in the American West, lending to the home-region businesses, property and households it knows best, and judge everything by profit per employee.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** credit discipline. A cost advantage tempts the bank to price loans too cheaply and to load up on real estate.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) overseas expansion, foreign joint ventures and foreign bank stakes; (2) building branches to match Rival B; (3) deregulation side-ventures outside the core.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** deposits are moving to market rates, and the bank's cost base was built for a regulated era that is ending.
- **Missing numbers that could change this:** profit by business line, Latin American exposure against capital, and cost-to-revenue and ROA against peers.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Harlan Pacific has stopped trying to be a smaller Rival B or a global bank. It runs like a business owned by its shareholders. Every branch, office and product line has to earn its keep. Overhead is cut to the bone, starting with the executive floor. Overseas, it keeps only what serves its home-region clients. It lends to the businesses, developers and households of its home state and the West, which it understands better than anyone. It prices those loans keenly because its costs are the lowest in its peer group. It turns down credit it doesn't understand. Each cycle produces more earnings per employee, and those earnings fund automation that lowers costs further. In a deregulated market where every bank pays market rates for money, the cheapest well-run operator wins. In that version, Harlan Pacific is that operator.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the best-run, lowest-cost bank in the American West, lending to the home-region businesses, property and households it knows best, and judge everything by profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Selected | Ruled out |

| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per branch; profit per loan; ROA; profit per employee | **Profit per employee** `[ASSUMPTION: pending line-of-business data]` | Asset size or national rank (11th-largest is a vanity metric) |
| **Passion** | Banking prestige; global reach; building things; running a tight, profitable shop | **Disciplined, owner-minded banking in its home region** `[ASSUMPTION: the dossier does not document culture. Inferred from the new CEO having built real estate lending into a strong profit contributor.]` | Global prestige: this passion produced the overseas losses |

**Why this denominator:** once deposit ceilings are gone, every bank pays roughly the same for money. Funding stops being the place to win. The lever the bank still controls is operating cost per dollar of revenue, and with people as the largest cost `[ASSUMPTION]`, profit per employee captures that lever directly.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Partly. Real estate lending was a strong profit contributor. The 1970s growth came before deregulation squeezed spreads.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: international expansion, branch build-outs, new deregulation ventures, and chasing rank.
- *Would it be uncomfortable?* Yes. It means closing offices, shrinking a century-old institution's footprint, and conceding the consumer crown to Rival B.

**What would confirm it within 12 months:**
1. Line-of-business data shows that domestic commercial, real estate and middle-market lending earn above the bank's cost of capital, and that international does not.
2. Cost-to-revenue falls by at least 5 points without a rise in credit losses `[ASSUMPTION target]`.
3. Profit per employee rises faster than at peer banks.

**What would refute it:** if consumer banking turns out to be the main profit pool, the Hedgehog has to be rebuilt around a cost-advantaged consumer model, not a business-lending one.

## 3. The flywheel

        ▼                                          │
 [6] Reinvest in automation (and, later, in-region │

**The loop closes.** Step 6 lowers unit costs further, which feeds back into step 1.

**Current push (the next one or two turns):**
1. **Turn 1:** a visible cost cut that starts at the top, together with full line-of-business profitability reporting. Until the bank knows where it makes money, it can't cut the right things.
2. **Turn 2:** shrink international to what serves home-region clients, and size and reserve the Latin American book.

**Weakest link, step 4:** a low-cost lender is always tempted to win volume by cutting prices or loosening credit. Real estate is both the proven earner and the CEO's home ground, which makes concentration risk easy to rationalize. That is why credit discipline needs an independent owner (see the Bus).

**Doom-loop check:**

| Sign | Present? |

| Big programs to jump-start momentum | **Yes**: the statewide branch expansion hurt earnings |
| Reaction without understanding | **Likely**: deregulation is "drawing many banks into new financial ventures." Harlan is at risk of following. |

The doom loop is the current state. The plan above is meant to replace it, not to add to it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop overseas expansion.** No new offices or JVs. Wind down any office that doesn't serve home-region clients profitably.
2. **Sell the West German minority stake.** Make no new foreign equity stakes.
3. **Stop new Latin American and foreign-sovereign lending.** Cap the book, reserve against it, and shrink it.
4. **Stop building branches for coverage.** Rank every branch by profit, then close or consolidate the bottom tier.
5. **Stop entering deregulation ventures** (new financial products or businesses) unless they directly raise profit per employee in the home region.
6. **Stop competing with Rival B customer-for-customer in statewide consumer banking.** Serve consumers profitably; don't chase share.
7. **Stop using asset size or "11th-largest" as a goal** in any internal or public communication.
8. **Stop overhead that doesn't earn.** Executive perks, head-office layers and committees get justified from zero, starting at the top `[ASSUMPTION: scale of such overhead unknown]`.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:** (1) Run it like it's our own money. (2) Face the facts: bad news travels fast and upward. (3) Lend only what we understand. (4) Integrity in every transaction. (5) Earn your place: every unit pays its way.
- **Core purpose:** to help the businesses and households of the West prosper through sound, efficient banking.
- **BHAG (15 years):** *become the most profitable large bank in America per employee.*
- **Mechanisms (clock building):** monthly unit P&Ls for every branch and lending group; an independent credit veto; zero-based overhead budgeting every year; executive pay tied to profit per employee and credit quality, not to asset growth; a named succession bench for CEO, COO and CCO.
