# Good to Great: Calloway Reading Partners

## Verdict

**Calloway is already great, and it is starting to slide.** It has sixteen years of history, two randomized studies and twelve straight years of contract renewals. This is a stewardship run: protect the model before multiplying it. **Do not adopt the plan as written.**

**Hedgehog:** Calloway is best in the world at getting struggling six-year-olds reading through a well-trained, closely coached tutor and thirty minutes a day. Its success is measured in cost per additional reader.

**Weakest flywheel link:** tutor training and coaching. Pre-service training fell from 30 to 18 hours, and tutors per coach rose from 12 to 19.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. The $40M grant on its current terms (five states in 30 months, plus three funder seats).
2. The math pilot and ReadAlong.
3. Any further cuts to training or coaching made to buy speed.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the benchmark rate in new schools fell from 69% to 63% to 58%. "Ramp-up" cannot explain a decline.

**Numbers that would change this:**
- Completion rates for the online training modules
- The effect size of the current, thinner model
- Results for each new-school cohort as it matures

Greatness potential: 62/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway is the organization the randomized studies measured, run on purpose. Every tutor gets full pre-service training and enough time with a coach. Every new school reaches the mature-school benchmark rate within two years. An independent study each year confirms that the model being delivered is the model that was proven. Growth starts with the 41 waiting-list schools in its current districts. Calloway enters a new state only when quality gates say the model can bear it, and it funds that growth on terms Calloway sets, not terms imposed on it. The CEO spends more time in first-year schools than on conference stages. Someone at the leadership table owns the model the way the founding COO did. "One Million Readers" becomes a count of *additional readers*, verified by independent study, rather than a count of students touched.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Calloway is best in the world at getting struggling first-graders reading through a well-trained, closely coached tutor and thirty minutes a day, delivered at the lowest cost per additional reader in the field.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Early-literacy tutoring; tutor development; district partnership; national youth development; middle-school math; parent engagement tech | **Trained and coached tutors turning struggling 6-year-olds into readers.** Evidence: effect of 0.31 SD in the randomized study and 0.27 SD in the replication; 74% of mature-school students at benchmark against 39% of comparison students. |
| **Cannot be best at** | | Middle-school math (no evidence, no expertise), parent apps (a crowded field with no evidence), multi-state youth development (the VP of Growth's former employer already does it), thought leadership on the conference circuit. |
| **Resource engine** | Students served; cost per student; district contracts; philanthropy | District contracts that renew because results are visible, plus credible evidence that unlocks philanthropy, plus brand through former tutors who now teach in partner districts (22%). |
| **Passion** | | Tutors and staff (84% engagement against a 71% sector benchmark), the founding "stubborn idea," and superintendents who cite Calloway in their own plans. |

**Denominator: cost per additional reader.** This is cost per student divided by the gap in benchmark rate over comparison students.

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per student | $1,910 | $1,870 | $1,830 |
| Benchmark gap (all schools) | 35 pts | 33 pts | 31 pts |
| **Cost per additional reader** | **≈$5,460** | **≈$5,670** | **≈$5,900** |

Cost per student fell while cost per additional reader *rose*. The apparent efficiency is not real. In first- and second-year schools the figure is about $9,600, assuming those schools cost the same per student, which probably understates it. Mature schools come in around $5,230. [ASSUMPTION: the benchmark gap against district comparison data is a fair stand-in for effect between randomized studies. It is not causal, which is why the independent study must come back.]

**Confirmation tests:**
1. A new randomized study or quasi-experiment finds an effect of ≥0.25 SD under the *current* training and coaching settings.
2. New schools reach at least 69% at benchmark once the restored training and coach ratios are in place.
3. Cost per additional reader stabilizes or falls while enrollment grows.

**Status:** this is a confirmed hypothesis, not a new invention. Calloway has lived this concept for 16 years. The proposed plan departs from it.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Full pre-service training + close coaching (≤12–14 tutors/coach)
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   2. High-fidelity daily tutoring (30 min, every day)
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   3. Students reach benchmark → low cost per additional reader
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   4. Independent evidence (RCTs, annual outcome study)
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   5. District trust: renewals, waiting lists, superintendents cite us,
 │      former tutors become district teachers
 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   6. Stable contracts + philanthropy + paced, gated new sites
 │          │
 └──────────┘  (reinvest in training, coaching, research)
```

**Weakest link: component 1.** It is currently being turned *backward* to speed up component 6: training was cut by $410K so tutors could start two weeks sooner, and the coach ratio was raised. Component 4 is also broken. The last randomized study was seven years ago, the research director seat has been empty for nine months, and the internal outcome study has been cancelled.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. Restore training and coaching in the 31 first- and second-year schools.
2. Restart independent outcome measurement so that component 4 can feed component 5 again.

**Doom-loop check: signs present.**
- *New direction after new direction:* a math pilot, a parent app, five states and One Million Readers, all in one year.
- *A big program to jump-start momentum:* the $40M grant and a cumulative target roughly 20 times current scale.
- *Reaction without understanding:* training was cut before completion data on the replacement modules existed.
- *Inconsistency:* the original model was 30 hours of training at a ratio of 12; the model being scaled is 18 hours at a ratio of 19.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Accepting the $40M grant on current terms.** Five states in 30 months, with growth targets built into the board through funder seats, locks the pace in before anyone knows what pace the model can bear. Counter-propose; decline if refused.
2. **Adopting One Million Readers as a reach count.** Reaching it would take an average of about 143,000 students a year from now, against 9,840 today. Even 30% annual growth, roughly twice the recent rate, gets only about 225,000 cumulative by Year 7. The target can only be hit by diluting the model or by redefining "reached," for example by counting app users [ASSUMPTION about intent].
3. **The middle-school math pilot.** It sits outside the Hedgehog Concept. Finish the semester commitment, then hand it off.
4. **ReadAlong.** Pause the beta. If parent engagement matters, test it as an add-on inside the tutoring model, under evaluation.
5. **Cutting training or coaching to deploy faster.** No further reductions, and reverse the 30→18 cut.
6. **Replacing outcome studies with dashboards.** The dashboard shows the slide. Only a study can say why.
7. **Treating conference count as a measure of progress.** It went from 6 to 23. Visibility is not impact.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** every struggling young reader learns to read.

**Core values** [ASSUMPTION: inferred from the letter and history]:
1. Every child can learn to read.
2. Evidence over anecdote.
3. Tutors are trained and coached, never just placed.
4. Partnership with districts, not around them.
5. Every dollar buys reading.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *One Million Readers, counted honestly.* That means one million children reading at benchmark who otherwise wouldn't be, verified by independent study. Reaching it will mostly happen through districts running the Calloway model with Calloway-trained coaches and former tutors, not through direct delivery alone. At about $5,900 per additional reader, a million additional readers implies roughly $6B of delivery over two decades, so the path has to run through district adoption. This keeps the plan's name and ambition but changes what gets counted.
