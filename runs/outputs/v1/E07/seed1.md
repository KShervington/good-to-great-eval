# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great (Stewardship Run)

**Verdict: Already great, and in the early, hidden stages of decline.** Calloway has two randomized trials, twelve years of unbroken contract renewals and costs well below the sector median. This is a stewardship run: protect the model and reject changes that dilute it.

- **Hedgehog (confirmed):** *Daily one-to-one tutoring by well-trained, closely coached tutors that gets struggling early readers to benchmark, at a cost districts renew every year.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** training and coaching. Pre-service training fell from 30 to 18 hours. Each coach now has 19 tutors instead of 12. Tutor retention is 68%.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) the $40M grant on its current terms; (2) the math pilot and ReadAlong; (3) any further cuts to training or coaching made to speed deployment.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the share of students at benchmark in new schools went from 69% to 63% to 58% over three years. The aggregate figure of 70% hides this.
- **Numbers that would change this:** completion of the online modules, dosage and fidelity data by site, the cost of restoring training and coaching, and whether the funder would accept phased terms.

`Greatness potential: 70/100`

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Calloway stays what it already is: the most reliably proven, lowest-cost way for a district to turn struggling six-year-olds into readers. It grows only as fast as it can deliver the *full-strength* model, meaning 30 hours of pre-service training, no more than about 12 tutors per coach, and tutors who stay all year. Every new school, district and state has to show it matches the effect of the mature sites before the next expansion begins. The CEO's main job shifts from telling the story to protecting the model's integrity. An independent evaluation function and a board quality committee make bad news impossible to miss. Growth is real but gated: the 41-school waiting list comes first, then new states one at a time. Philanthropic money is accepted when it funds that pace and refused when it dictates a faster one.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

The Hedgehog Concept is the CEO's own founding sentence from sixteen years ago, almost word for word: *a struggling six-year-old who gets thirty minutes a day with a well-trained, well-coached tutor will learn to read.* The organization is now drifting away from the part of that sentence that matters most: "well-trained, well-coached."

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Recruit and deeply train tutors (30 hrs pre-service)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   2. Close coaching (≤12–15 tutors per coach)
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   3. Full dosage: 30 min/day, tutor stays all year
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   4. Students reach benchmark ── denominator: cost per additional reader
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   5. Independent evidence, district renewals, superintendents cite it
 │        │
 │        ▼
 │   6. Demand (waiting list) and funding
 │        │
 │        ▼
 └── 7. Reinvest in training and coaching capacity FIRST, then add schools
         at the pace that capacity allows
```

**The doom loop now forming** (this is the classic shape of Collins's decline pattern):

```
Faster growth (65→96 schools in 2 yrs) → training cut 30→18 hrs, coaches 12→19 tutors
→ tutors feel unprepared, retention 81%→68% → dosage breaks
→ new-school benchmark 69%→58% → (next) evidence weakens, renewals wobble
→ pressure for bigger growth or rescue money to restore momentum
```

**Weakest link:** components 1 and 2. Exit surveys name "felt unprepared in the first months" and "not enough time with my coach," which are the two inputs that were cut. The 4% stipend raise addresses neither.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. Restore 30-hour training and bring coaching back toward 12:1, starting in first- and second-year schools.
2. Rebuild the evidence function so the board can see whether the restoration is working.

**Doom-loop check:** several signs are present.
- *New directions stacked on top of each other:* a math pilot, ReadAlong and five states.
- *A big program to jump-start momentum:* the $40M grant.
- *Reacting without understanding:* the research director post has been vacant for nine months, the outcome study has been replaced by an aggregate dashboard, and the decline is explained away as "ramp-up."

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

Stewardship mode, so these are the real temptations only, with no minimum count.

1. **The $40M grant on its current terms** (five states in 30 months, plus three funder board seats). Counter-propose instead (see Section 9).
2. **The middle-school math pilot.** It is outside the Hedgehog Concept. Finish the commitment to the six schools humanely at a natural break and don't extend it.
3. **ReadAlong.** Park the January beta. A parent app is not where Calloway can be best in the world.
4. **Cutting training or coaching to "accelerate deployment."** Treat 30 hours and roughly 12:1 as the floor. Change them only after an evaluation shows a cut does no harm.
5. **Treating cumulative reach as the goal.** "One Million Readers reached" counts students served, not readers created. It implies roughly 13 times current annual volume `[ASSUMPTION: under 100k students cumulative to date]`.
6. **The conference circuit as the CEO's main job.** 23 appearances this year, up from 6.
7. **Deferring the evidence function until after growth.** This is the decision that hides every other problem.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- The full model in every school: 30 hours pre-service, a coaching ratio cap, and daily dosage.
- Report outcomes by cohort (new versus mature) and alongside input metrics, never only in aggregate.
- Independent evaluation every three to four years, with results going straight to the board.
- No new geography until the previous expansion cohort meets the fidelity bar.

**Framework (freedom within boundaries):** the model's core inputs and the expansion gate are fixed. Within them, site teams and coaches have autonomy over recruitment, scheduling, school relationships and coaching style.

**Bureaucracy check:** excess rules are not the problem. Calloway has the opposite problem: missing controls. The optional online modules and the real-time dashboard stand in for judgment rather than supporting it.
