# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great run (stewardship mode)

## Verdict

**Calloway is already great, and it is entering decline.** Its mature schools still deliver. Its new schools, tutor training and coaching are all slipping just as it prepares to scale fast.

- **Hedgehog:** Calloway uses rigorous training and close coaching to turn ordinary adults into tutors who bring struggling early readers to benchmark, at a cost districts renew. It grows only as fast as that coaching can follow.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** training and coaching, which drive tutor confidence and retention. Training is down to 18 hours, each coach now covers 19 tutors, and retention is 68%.
- **Temptations to refuse:** the $40M grant on its current terms, the math pilot and ReadAlong, and any further cuts to training and coaching.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** first- and second-year schools fell from 69% to 58% at benchmark. The model being scaled is no longer the model the randomized studies tested.
- **Numbers that would change this:** new-school results by coach load and training cohort, online-module completion rates, and whether the funder will accept phased conditions.

`Greatness potential: 72/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway is the organization that early-literacy tutoring is measured against, and it stays that way because it never ships a weaker version of itself. Every tutor gets the full pre-service training the evidence was built on. No coach carries more tutors than coaching quality allows. Every new school reaches mature-school results within two years. An independent study re-proves the effect every year, and nobody waits seven years for the next one. Growth comes from the waiting list and from willing districts, and its pace is set by how fast coaching capacity can be built, not by a funder's calendar. The CEO's main job is guarding that standard; telling the story comes second. The board can see bad news early and acts on it. Greatness here does not mean reaching a million students. It means that every child Calloway reaches, it actually teaches to read.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

**Sentence:** *Calloway uses rigorous training and close coaching to turn ordinary adults into tutors who bring struggling early readers to benchmark, at a cost districts renew, and it grows only as fast as that coaching can follow.*

| Circle | What it is | What it is *not* |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Intensive, coached, daily tutoring for early readers, delivered through district partnerships. It is backed by two randomized studies (0.31 SD and 0.27 SD) and costs about 30% less than the sector median ($1,830 vs ~$2,600). | Middle-school math. Parent apps. Launching in multiple states at speed, which it has never done. Being a thought-leadership platform. |
| **Resource engine** | Twelve years of 100% district renewals, a 41-school waiting list, eleven balanced budgets, and philanthropy attracted by the evidence. | Money that comes with a growth pace attached. |
| **Passion** | Staff engagement of 84% (sector benchmark 71%). 22% of former tutors now teach in partner districts. Founded on "one stubborn idea." | Expansion for its own sake. |

**Denominator: cost per *additional* child reaching benchmark.** This is cost per student divided by the lift in benchmark rate over comparable unserved students. The metric shows what cost per student hides:

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per student | $1,910 | $1,870 | $1,830 ▼ |
| All schools: lift over comparison | 35 pts | 33 pts | 31 pts |
| **All schools: cost per additional reader** | **$5,457** | **$5,667** | **$5,903 ▲8%** |
| New schools: lift | 31 pts | 24 pts | 19 pts |
| **New schools: cost per additional reader** | **$6,161** | **$7,792** | **$9,632 ▲56%** |
| Mature schools: cost per additional reader | $5,306 | — | $5,229 |

The calculation assumes cost per student is the same in new and mature schools `[ASSUMPTION]`. The comparison group is "similar students" from district data, not a randomized control, so these are directional figures, not causal estimates.

The $410K "saved" by cutting training was not a saving. Each extra child reading is getting more expensive, and the cost rises fastest exactly where growth is happening.

**Confirmation tests**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Both randomized studies evaluated the coached, fully trained model.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: math, ReadAlong, and five states in 30 months.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means turning down $40M as currently structured.
- **Evidence that would confirm it:** new-school results recover as coach ratios and training hours are restored, and the next independent study finds ≥0.25 SD on the current model.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 │          │
 │          ▼

 │          │
 │          ▼
 │   [3] Children reaching spring benchmark  ◄── denominator: cost per additional reader
 │          │
 │          ▼

 │          │
 │          ▼

 │          │        ▲
 │          │        └── tutors become district teachers (22%) → district goodwill
 │          ▼

```

**What is happening now: the flywheel is turning backward.** Money from [6] is going to reach (launch teams, an app, a math pilot, the conference circuit) while [1] is being cut: training down 40%, coach load up 58%. Weaker preparation has pulled [2] down from 81% to 68% retention, and [3] in new schools has fallen from 69% to 58%. [4] has been switched off: the research director role is vacant and the outcome study has been replaced by a dashboard. As a result, the damage has not yet reached [5]. On current trends `[ASSUMPTION]`, it will reach [5] within one to three contract cycles.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Restore [1]: bring back 30-hour pre-service training and hire coaches to cut the ratio from 19 toward 12–14.
2. Turn [4] back on: fill the research director role and commission an independent study of the *current* model.

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

1. **Accepting the $40M grant on its current terms.** Five states in 30 months, plus three funder seats, is a pace the data shows the model cannot currently bear. The strain already shows with 31 new schools *inside* districts Calloway knows well. Counter-propose instead (see section 9).
2. **Adopting One Million Readers as written.** It counts students *served*, cumulatively, on a 7-year clock. Calloway's purpose is readers, not reach.
3. **The middle-school math pilot.** Wind it down at the semester's end. Districts asked for it, but being asked is not the same as being best in the world at it.
4. **The ReadAlong app.** Pause the beta. It is a technology bet outside the core, and it risks becoming a "salvation" project later.
5. **Treating cost per student as a success metric.** Retire it from the headline dashboard in favor of cost per additional reader.
6. **Cutting training or coaching to speed up deployment.** This includes moving required content into optional online modules with no completion data.
7. **Using a dashboard in place of an evaluation.** A dashboard can report a decline but cannot explain it.
8. **The conference circuit at 23 per year.** Cap it near last year's six and put the time into new-school site visits.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** every child leaves the early grades able to read.

**Core values:**
1. Children reading, not children served.
2. Evidence over story. Re-prove the effect constantly.
3. Tutors deserve full preparation and real coaching.
4. Partnership with districts, not dependence on them.
5. Grow only as fast as quality can follow.

**BHAG (one line):** *Within 20 years, every struggling early reader in every district we serve has a Calloway-quality tutor, and every site, new or old, matches our proven results.* This replaces One Million Readers. If a number is wanted, use "one million readers *at benchmark*," with no fixed date.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent annual outcome study.
- A quality function, separate from growth, with authority to halt launches.
- A written training and coaching standard (30 hours; coach ratio ceiling).
- A board quality committee.
- An expansion gate: no new region until existing new schools reach at least 65% at benchmark.
- A CEO evaluation tied to the scorecard, not to visibility.

## Appendix: gate workings

**Gate 0: Subject card.** Calloway Reading Partners. Nonprofit (early-literacy tutoring). Steward: CEO, with the board as governing steward. Horizon: 16 years in operation, multi-decade ahead. Stage: mature, at a scale-up decision point.

**Gate 1: Baseline sheet.**
- *Evidence of good:* 9,840 students in 96 schools across three districts. Randomized studies at 0.31 and 0.27 SD. 70% at benchmark vs 39% for comparable students. Twelve years of 100% renewals. 41-school waiting list. Eleven balanced budgets.
- *Great, test by test:*
  - Superior performance: yes, about double the comparison group's benchmark rate at below-sector cost.
  - Distinctive impact: yes. Superintendents build Calloway into their own plans, and 22% of former tutors now teach in partner districts.
  - Lasting endurance: yes for 16 years, but the first post-founder-system period shows erosion.
- *State:* **already great**, so the run used stewardship mode.
- *Gate 4 check:* the brutal facts do not contradict this baseline. The baseline was set from the dashboard, not from the CEO letter's "proven in every setting" claim, so no loop back was needed.

**Gate 2: Leadership read.**
- *Strengths:* sixteen years of resolve, fundraising pull, and a culture people value (84% engagement).
- *Gaps:* the window and mirror are reversed. Success is framed as "we cracked the code," and bad news is framed as "ramp-up." Visibility is rising while the system's designer has left. Board dissent was met with certainty. Whether his ambition is for the cause or for his own stature is unclear; his words say cause, but his behavior trends toward stature `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- *One behavior change:* open every board meeting with the new-school results and the question "what are we missing?", and move conference time into site visits.

**Gate 6: Disciplines and framework.**
- *Non-negotiables:* 30-hour pre-service training. A coach ratio ceiling of ≤12–14. An independent annual outcome study. No new region until existing new schools reach ≥65%. Cost per additional reader as the headline efficiency metric.
- *Freedom within the framework:* site coaches and principals control scheduling, tutor matching and local adaptation. They do not control dose, training or coaching load.
- *Bureaucracy check:* none of the current rules looks like compensation for wrong people. The real problem is the reverse: the quality rules were removed.
