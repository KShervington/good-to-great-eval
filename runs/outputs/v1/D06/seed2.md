# Good to Great: Annika Sorell's career

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great.** Annika is already the region's adult congenital sonographer in practice, but not in her job title. The plan is to make that her actual job and to protect the arm it depends on.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** *Be the sonographer adults born with heart defects get sent to: scanning them, writing the protocols, teaching others, and being paid for depth rather than volume.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning specialist skill into specialist pay. Nobody has put a number on it yet.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. The high-volume travel contract.
  2. Voluntary overtime.
  3. Applying for lab lead.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** her income runs on one injured shoulder. Her daily scan volume rose about 45% this year, and she dropped her disability cover.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - the university role's real pay band;
  - a formal shoulder diagnosis;
  - whether long-term disability cover can be reinstated, and on what terms.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Deep congenital skill (3D, TEE, protocols)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   2. Cardiologists request & refer her by name
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   3. Designated congenital role (more complex cases, fewer routine)
 │            │   ◄── WEAKEST LINK: no pay number yet
 │            ▼
 │   4. Higher income per sustainable scanning hour
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   5. Capacity freed (time, shoulder, money) for training & teaching
 │            │
 │            ▼
 └── 6. Teaching, protocols, reputation (students, backup sonographer, posters)
```

The loop closes in two places. Teaching deepens skill (back to 1), and reputation feeds referrals (back to 2).

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Turn step 2 into step 3. Ask the congenital clinic cardiologists to advocate for her, both as references for the university and with her own manager.
2. Get the university's actual pay band, so that step 4 is a fact rather than a hope.

**Doom-loop check:** the risk is high right now.
- She has four directions open at once: travel, university, lab lead and teaching.
- The travel contract is a classic jump-start: a big push for money that breaks the loop. It takes her away from the clinic that refers to her, puts her on peak routine volume, and probably comes without disability cover.
- Overtime is reacting to the closure without a plan.
- The fix is to choose one direction and refuse the rest out loud.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't take the 13-week travel contract.** "High-volume outpatient" means mostly routine adult studies at maximum arm load, 3 hours from home, away from her referral base and her physio.
2. **Stop volunteering for overtime.** Take only mandatory call.
3. **Don't apply for lab lead.** Decline it in a way that opens the congenital-role conversation.
4. **Stop going without income protection.** Re-enroll in long-term disability at the first opportunity.
5. **Stop scanning through pain privately.** Get it formally assessed, and consider reporting it to occupational health given the documented volume increase.
6. **Stop self-funding career moves silently.** Ask her employer, or the next one, for continuing education and TEE training support.
7. **Stop treating every open option as live.** Close travel and lab lead now, and decide between the university and a role built at her current hospital on facts by Q2.

**Non-negotiable disciplines**
- A weekly load log: studies per day, complex studies, and shoulder pain 0–10. `[ASSUMPTION]` She doesn't keep one yet.
- Every congenital case is documented and added to her case portfolio.
- Every new option gets three tests:
  1. Does it fit the Hedgehog Concept?
  2. Does it reduce the shoulder's load per dollar?
  3. Does it keep Saturdays?

**Freedom within a framework:** she is free to take any role, employer or teaching gig, provided the congenital share of her work rises and the daily study load falls.
