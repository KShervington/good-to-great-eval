# Ravenhill Drug Company: Good to Great Run

## Verdict

**Already great (provisionally), so this is a stewardship run.** Ravenhill has 21 straight record years, a 19.1% ROE, no debt, and two decades of disciplined exits. The job is to protect the flywheel, not reinvent it.

- **Hedgehog:** Ravenhill runs the best, most convenient pharmacy-led drugstores in America and earns its living on rising profit per customer visit.
- **Weakest link:** Profit per visit. Payers are squeezing margins on prescriptions, which are 43% of sales and the fastest-growing part.
- **Temptations to refuse:**
  1. A big chain acquisition to match the rival merger.
  2. Prescription contracts below the profitability floor.
  3. A standalone mail and benefit-manager business that pulls visits out of stores.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Ravenhill is becoming more of a pharmacy just as payers gain the power to set pharmacy prices.
- **Numbers that would change this:** shareholder return against the market, profit per visit by store format, same-store sales, and profitability of mail service and the managed-care subsidiary.

Greatness potential: 65/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ravenhill looks like Ravenhill today, only with sharper focus. It keeps opening freestanding corner pharmacies with drive-throughs, clustered densely enough that one is always the easiest stop. Each store makes more on every visit than any rival, because the pharmacy is efficient, prescriptions follow the customer to any store, and front-end items and photo add to the basket. All of that growth is paid for out of its own cash. It treats payers as partners above a profitability floor and walks away below it. It grows by building, not buying. It ignores the consolidation race, because a rival with twice the stores and similar total sales is not a better business. And it shows the record streak does not depend on one family chairman, because a successor runs the same system just as well.

**Confirmation tests:**

2. Profit per visit rises in markets where store density rises.
3. Profit per visit holds even as the prescription mix grows.

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

1. **No large chain acquisition to keep pace.** Acquired chains bring mostly older strip and mall stores, which Ravenhill is already closing (88 last year). It makes sense to buy individual sites or small clusters that fit the corner, drive-through format.
2. **No prescription contracts below the profitability floor,** even when walking away costs volume.
3. **No push of mail service and benefit management into a standalone business.** Mail removes store visits, the very thing the denominator counts. Keep these only where they protect in-store prescription access, until their profitability is known.
4. **No re-entry into non-drugstore businesses** (restaurants, optical, discount general merchandise, international). The exits were right.
5. **No opening stores just to hit 3,000.** The target is a by-product of good sites. It is not the goal.
6. **No acquisition debt.** The negligible-debt balance sheet is a strategic weapon and should not be spent on a defensive deal.
