# Ravenhill Drug Company: Good to Great

**Verdict: already great, so this is a stewardship plan.** Ravenhill has 21 straight years of record sales and earnings, a 19.1% return on equity, negligible debt and the industry lead. The job is to protect the flywheel, not reinvent it.

**Hedgehog:** Be the most convenient pharmacy-led drugstore on the best corner in every market served, and grow profit per customer visit.

**Weakest flywheel link:** prescription profit per visit. Prescriptions are 43.4% of sales and rising, while third-party payers squeeze the margin. Gross margin fell from 28.4% to 28.0%.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. A big chain acquisition to match the consolidating rival.
2. Growing mail and PBM into a business that competes with the stores.
3. Payer contracts below a profit floor, or weaker sites opened to hit 3,000 stores.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the fastest-growing line is now the one whose margin outsiders set.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit per customer visit, store returns by format, shareholder returns versus the market, and mail/PBM profitability.

Greatness potential: 76/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ravenhill keeps doing what it has done for two decades: it exits everything that is not a drugstore and compounds the drugstore it is. It holds a dense, self-funded network of freestanding corner stores with drive-through pharmacies, all linked so a prescription follows the customer anywhere. Each new store is chosen and owned by Ravenhill, not inherited through a merger. The company runs on one number, profit per customer visit, so the prescription margin squeeze shows up early and is negotiated against. It is never hidden by volume growth. When the industry consolidates around it, Ravenhill refuses to buy size for its own sake. It gets payer leverage from being the store patients won't give up, and it builds a successor bench so the streak survives the current chairman.

**Why it passes the tests:**
- **It explains past wins.** Every exit (restaurants, optical, department stores, Mexico) moved Ravenhill toward the convenient pharmacy, and earnings set records throughout.
- **It rules things out.** It excludes transformative acquisitions, price leadership and mail-first strategy.
- **It is uncomfortable to commit to.** It means watching the largest rival double in size without responding in kind.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. Profit per customer visit is higher, and growing faster, at freestanding drive-through stores than at older strip and mall stores.
2. Visit frequency rises with local store density.
3. Customers who refill across stores have higher annual profit than single-store customers.

If test 1 fails, the store-format strategy needs rethinking before the 3,000-store push.

## 5. Stop-doing list (temptations to refuse)

In stewardship mode this list covers only the real temptations, not a quota.

1. **No transformative chain acquisition** to keep pace with the rival's roughly 4,500-store combination. Acquired chains bring legacy strip-center sites that contradict the format strategy, and integration at scale is unproven. Buy single stores or prescription files only if they pass the site test.
2. **Don't grow mail service or the PBM into a second business** that pulls prescriptions out of the stores. Keep them defensive and capped.
3. **Don't accept payer contracts below the profit floor** to protect volume. A prescription that loses money per visit pushes the flywheel backward.
4. **Don't let the 3,000-store target override site quality.** The count is an output, not the goal.
5. **Don't chase price leadership** against discounters and supermarkets. Compete on convenience first.
6. **Don't run indefinite tests.** The mini-store has been tested for six years and has about two dozen units. Decide whether to scale it or stop it, based on profit per visit.
