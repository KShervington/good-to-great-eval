# Ravenhill Drug Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Already great. Stewardship mode.** Ravenhill has 21 straight record years, a 19.1% return on equity and almost no debt. Over two decades it has exited every business that wasn't the drugstore.

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Own the most convenient pharmacy corner in every market, and earn more profit per customer visit than anyone.

**Weakest flywheel link:** visits → profit per visit. Payers are squeezing prescription margins while the prescription share of sales keeps rising.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. A big chain acquisition made to match a consolidating rival.
2. Growing pharmacy-benefit and mail service as a business in its own right instead of a feeder to the stores.
3. Third-party contracts below minimum profitability.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The payer, not the patient, increasingly sets the prescription price.

**Numbers that would change this:** profit per visit, same-store sales, shareholder returns versus the market, profitability of the benefit and mail businesses, and returns on freestanding stores versus strip-center stores.

`Greatness potential: 78/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Ravenhill stays what it has quietly become: the drugstore that is easiest to reach and easiest to use. It is a freestanding corner store with a drive-through window. Your prescription is on file at every location, and the pharmacist's time goes to the patient, not to paperwork. Each new store is self-funded, self-sited and clustered densely enough that the next store is never far away. Pharmacy brings the customer in, and photo and everyday merchandise make each trip worth more. The company measures itself on profit per customer visit, not store count or sales rank. It deals with payers from strength because it is too convenient to leave out of a network, and it walks away from contracts that don't pay. It does not chase consolidating rivals by buying store count. It does not turn its benefit arm into a competitor of its own stores. It does not drift back into restaurants, optical, discounting or foreign retail. Greatness here means doing the same coherent thing for another 20 years, with the next generation of leaders running it.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   │                                                         ▲

```

Read it as a loop: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → back to 1. Visits (3) also feed profit per visit (5) directly through the front-end basket.

**Weakest link:** 3/4 → 5. Prescriptions are 43.4% of sales and rising, while third-party margins keep falling, and gross margin has already slipped 0.4 points. If payer pressure grows faster than visit frequency and the front-end basket, the cash that funds new corners starts to thin.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Measure the denominator.** Profit per visit is not tracked in the evidence. Build it by store type and by payer.
2. **Turn the benefit subsidiary into a leverage tool for the stores, not a separate growth story.** Its network should route prescriptions into Ravenhill stores at or above the minimum profitability standard.

**Doom-loop check: clean.** The company has made few acquisitions (24 stores in five years, the largest ever only 66 stores). It holds its exit deadlines and has pursued one consistent direction for two decades. **The live doom-loop risk is external:** a large rival merger could provoke a reactive, jump-start acquisition.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Don't buy a large chain to keep pace in store count.** Older-format stores in weak sites would dilute profit per visit. Tuck-ins are acceptable only when the sites meet corner-convenience standards.
2. **Don't grow the benefit and mail businesses as ends in themselves.** If they compete with Ravenhill's own counters for the same customer, they turn the flywheel backward.
3. **Don't accept third-party contracts below minimum profitability** just to protect volume.
4. **Don't plant scattered flags.** Enter new metros only at a density that makes the next store close by. Store count is not the goal.
5. **Don't fight discounters and supermarkets on front-end price.** Compete on convenience and earn a fair price for it.
6. **Don't return to formats outside the drugstore.** Restaurants, optical, discount stores and foreign retail stay closed.

## Appendix: Gate artifacts

**Subject card (Gate 0):** Ravenhill Drug Company. Type: business (listed retail drugstore chain). Steward: chairman and CEO, with the board. Time horizon: decades, with a five-year growth plan. Stage: mature, industry-leading, organically growing.

**Baseline sheet (Gate 1):** Net sales $10.4B (from $6.7B four years earlier). Net earnings $321M (+13.8%), the 21st consecutive record year. ROE 19.1%. Long-term debt about $2.4M. 2,085 stores. Prescriptions 43.4% of sales.
- *Superior performance:* strong on records and industry leadership; not verified against the market.
- *Distinctive impact:* the leader in sales, profit and technology, with refill-anywhere access across 2,085 stores. A real hole if it vanished, though rivals could fill part of it.
- *Endurance:* the record streak (21 years) predates the chairman's tenure (19 years), so it already extends beyond one person in part.
- **State: Already great → stewardship mode**, conditional on market-relative returns.

**Leadership read (Gate 2):**
- *Strengths:* fierce resolve for the cause. He made hard exits from businesses the family built and kept a self-imposed deadline.
- *Gaps:* there is no evidence on window-and-mirror behavior or on succession. Severance protections after an investor approach are a mild entrenchment signal.
- *One behavior change:* publish a succession plan and let the president lead visible decisions this year.

**Culture of discipline (Gate 6):**
- *Framework:* the Hedgehog Concept, the stop-doing list, the contract profitability floor and the site density rules.
- *Freedom inside it:* store and regional managers decide service, staffing mix and local merchandising.
- *Bureaucracy check:* no evidence of compensating rules.
- *Fanatical consistency:* self-funded corner stores, prescriptions refillable at any store, and steady store opening and closing cadence.
