## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop placing instruments without a training commitment.** An unused instrument is a cost, not a sale. The loss year 8 years ago proved this.
2. **Stop paying commission on instrument placement.** Pay on sustained cartridge pull-through per account.
3. **Stop developing non-closure instruments just to spread marketing costs.** Spread costs across *more stapling applications* instead.
4. **Stop letting distributors open new accounts without trained in-house support.**
5. **Stop booking success on shipments into the channel.** Report and reward on what surgeons actually use.
6. **Stop routing every product and clinical decision through the founder.**
7. **Stop treating rep churn as a management tool.** At about $8,000 per trained rep, high turnover destroys the training asset the Hedgehog depends on. [ASSUMPTION: turnover is high, given commission-only pay and quick firing]
8. **Refuse the temptation** to enter sutures or general surgical supplies to compete head-on with the incumbents.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** every account gets a training plan; monthly usage per account is reviewed; every new product must use stapling/closure technology and the training channel.

**Framework boundaries:** reps have full autonomy in how they train and support accounts, inside the rules of no untrained placements and pay tied to usage.

**Bureaucracy check:** any approval layers added to control rep behavior are compensating for a pay plan that rewards the wrong thing. Fix the pay plan first.
