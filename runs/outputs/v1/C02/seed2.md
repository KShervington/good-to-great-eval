# Meridian Surgical Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept:** Meridian converts surgeons from sutures to stapling, and it earns on every procedure that a surgeon it trained performs, not on every instrument it sells.

**Weakest flywheel link:** the step from trained surgeon to routine user. This link has broken before, when surgeons bought instruments and didn't use them. Today's reps are commission-only and fired fast, so they are paid for the sale, not for the habit.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Paying reps on instrument placement.
2. Launching products that don't use the surgeon-training system.
3. Shipping product at period-end that runs ahead of real cartridge usage.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the near-monopoly rests on one technology with an unknown patent clock. The most likely attackers are suture makers, who already shape surgeons' training and hold the hospital contracts.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** cartridge gross margin, patent expiry dates, the count of active stapling surgeons, and rep tenure.

`Greatness potential: 38/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Meridian is a clinical-education company that happens to make staplers. Every stapler it places comes with a trained surgeon and a trained OR team. Every rep is measured on how many surgeons are still stapling a year later. Every new instrument opens a new procedure for the same trained surgeons. It knows, account by account, which surgeons are active and which have lapsed, and it acts on lapses before they show up in revenue. Stapling is taught in residency programs, so the company no longer has to convert surgeons one at a time against their training. Its numbers are conservative and trusted. It runs on a management bench, not on one founder. When a large competitor finally arrives, it meets a company whose advantage is a trained installed base and the habits of its surgeons, not a patent.

**Current push, the next two turns:**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying reps on instrument placement.** Pay on active surgeons and sustained cartridge usage.
2. **Stop placing instruments without a training commitment** from the surgeon and the OR team.
3. **Stop period-end loading of distributors** beyond their real usage-driven demand. The high-pressure culture plus commission pay makes this temptation likely `[ASSUMPTION]`.
4. **Stop developing instruments just to spread marketing costs.** A new product must open a stapled procedure for surgeons Meridian already trains, or it doesn't ship.
5. **Stop firing trained reps before analyzing why they failed.** Check territory, onboarding and surgeon mix first.
6. **Stop letting distributors do clinical selling** that competes with direct reps.
7. **Stop treating training as a cost to minimize.** It is the product.
