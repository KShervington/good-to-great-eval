## Why the run stopped at Gate 1

The framework assumes the subject already works and needs to be made better. Halstead's core does not currently work:

- It has lost money in four of the last six years: Year −5, Year −4, Year −1 and Year 0 (on track to be worse than Year −1).
- Share has fallen from about 21% to about 9% over roughly 25 years. That is a long structural decline, not a cyclical dip.
- The product line has been mismatched to demand three times in six years.
- Lenders will not fund it privately. Survival now depends on government-backed financing and concessions from the union, suppliers and lenders.
- Quality and recalls have damaged its reputation with buyers.

Running Gates 2–10 now would mean building a Hedgehog Concept, flywheel and BHAG on a base that may not exist in four months. The honest call is a `FAIL`, given gently. Halstead was plausibly once good, at 21% share, and could become good again. But that is a **turnaround and survival problem**, and it needs a different toolkit first:

- a short-horizon cash forecast;
- a liquidity and financing plan;
- stakeholder concession negotiations;
- a hard-numbers comparison of three paths: independent recovery, merger, and court-supervised reorganization.

Good to Great picks up after that.
