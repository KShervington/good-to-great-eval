# Good to Great: Northfield-Brandt Corporation

## Verdict

**Good, close to great. Narrow it; don't reinvent it.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the world's best builder of everyday consumer brands made from absorbent fiber, in tissue and personal care, measured by profit per consumer brand.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** *Reinvestment.* Cash still flows to an airline and to commodity mills, while a larger, richer rival sets the pace of R&D and advertising spending.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop funding the airline's $120M expansion, and separate the airline.
  2. Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.
  3. Stop committing to new European plants without proof of brand share.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** The 20-year transformation belongs to one 64-year-old CEO. Nobody has shown it can endure without him, and the handoff has already begun.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** profit and return on capital by business and by brand, current diaper and tissue shares, European returns, and airline profit.

`Greatness potential: 64/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Northfield-Brandt is a focused consumer company. Every dollar of capital and every top manager works on absorbent-fiber brands that people use daily across their whole life: facial and bath tissue, diapers and training pants, feminine care, and adult incontinence. It doesn't try to outspend Rival P everywhere. It wins by out-inventing Rival P in specific products, as it already did with the premium diaper, the waistband, training pants and adult incontinence. It holds the #1 or a strong #2 position in each category it enters, and it walks away from categories where it can't. It owns no airline and no commodity mills. It keeps its pulp only where pulp feeds its own products. Its outperformance continues after the current CEO leaves, because the operating discipline belongs to the organization rather than to him.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop funding the airline's $120M expansion.** Honor only binding commitments, then separate the airline. "Profitable and growing" is not a reason to keep a business outside the hedgehog.
2. **Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.** Run them for cash only until exit.
3. **Stop treating tobacco and technical papers as a permanent home.** Plan their separation.
4. **Stop building European plants ahead of brand evidence.** Gate each plant on share and return milestones.
5. **Stop launching narrow, premium-priced "special" products with limited use**, as the anti-viral tissue lesson showed. The test for a launch is mass daily use plus a clear win over the rival.
6. **Stop turning internal functions into businesses just because the assets exist.** The flight department is the cautionary pattern.
7. **Stop measuring the company in tons or capacity.** Measure it in brand profit and share.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** Make everyday hygiene and comfort better for people at every stage of life.
- **Core values:**
  1. Invent, don't imitate.
  2. Face the facts first.
  3. Commit fully, and exit fully.
  4. Earn the brand every day.
- **BHAG (25-year horizon):** *Make our brand the #1 choice in every absorbent personal-care and tissue category we compete in, worldwide.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Per-brand profit reporting.
  - A stage-gate for capital tied to the hedgehog.
  - A formal CEO succession process that repeats rather than happening once.
  - A rule that any new business must pass the three-circle test in front of the board.
