# Good to Great: Northfield-Brandt Corporation

**Verdict: Already great. Endurance is about to face its first real test.** (Stewardship mode)

**Hedgehog Concept:** *Northfield-Brandt creates and leads branded, absorbent personal-care categories (tissue, diapers, training pants, feminine and adult care) and wins them brand by brand against the strongest packaged-goods rival in the world.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** capital freed by the mill sales is leaking back out into an airline and commodity paper instead of going into brands.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. The $120M airline expansion.
2. Reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.
3. Premium-priced novelties outside core everyday use (the anti-viral tissue lesson).

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the CEO is 64 with 20 years in the job, his COO has held the role for two months, and greatness has never been tested without him.

**Numbers that would change this:** profit and return on capital by segment and by brand (especially tobacco and technical papers), current category shares, returns on Europe, and airline profit.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Northfield-Brandt becomes a pure branded consumer-products company. It is the category creator and leader in absorbent personal care, and it is run so that its leadership survives the man who built it. Every dollar of capital and every strong manager goes to brands where the company is #1 or can credibly become #1. The commodity mills, the tobacco and technical papers (unless the numbers prove they belong) and the airline are separated deliberately and from strength. That separation completes the move the CEO began 20 years ago when he sold the mills. Europe expands category by category on proven returns, not on a timetable. The handover to the next CEO is planned, visible and boring. The scorecard tracks profit per consumer brand, so success and drift both show up early.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  [1] Invent / redefine an absorbent category
              │
              ▼
  [2] Premium branded leadership (#1 share)
              │
              ▼
  [3] Profit per consumer brand  ◄── denominator
              │
              ▼
  [4] Capital freed from non-core assets + brand profits
              │
              ▼
  [5] Reinvest in R&D, materials, plants for leading brands
              │
              ▼
  [6] Extend proven categories (adjacent segments, then Europe)
              │
              └──────────────► back to [1]
```

**Weakest link: [4]→[5].** Capital is still flowing to a $120M airline expansion and to mills that need regular reinvestment.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Stop the leak: freeze the new airline commitments and decide the fate of the paper assets.
2. Gate Europe: fund it category by category against shown returns.

**Doom-loop check:**
- *Consistent pushes over 20 years:* clean. The company has executed one direction since the mill sales.
- *Warning sign:* the airline is a slowly accumulating second direction. It came from a finance-staff idea, survived a fatal crash and years of losses, and is now expanding. That pattern is "big program as diversification."
- *Warning sign:* European investment is heavy, but no returns have been disclosed.
- *Positive sign:* the anti-viral tissue was killed. That shows the company can stop a weak program.

**Verdict:** loop closes.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Stop funding the airline expansion.** Separate aviation through a sale or spin-off while it is profitable and has value.
2. **Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.** Separate them on a deliberate timetable, not in a fire sale.
3. **Stop launching premium-priced products for narrow uses.** The anti-viral tissue lesson: innovation must serve everyday absorbent needs at a price people will pay.
4. **Stop funding Europe on a calendar.** Fund it on category-level evidence instead.
5. **Stop treating the specialty papers as default keepers.** Decide on return on capital and Hedgehog fit within 12 months.
