# Good to Great: Larkspur Financial

**Verdict: Good, not yet great.** The franchise is real. The metric the company steers by has never been verified, and its incentive design matches what a regulator has already tied to misconduct inside Larkspur.

- **Hedgehog:** Be America's primary, trusted everyday bank for households and small businesses, and earn profit per *active* primary relationship, not per product sold.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from sales to real primary relationships. No one has shown that cross-sold products are actually used.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:** (1) paying store teams on product counts and daily quotas; (2) treating "eight products" as a target; (3) running risk and HR inside the business lines they are meant to police.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the central bank said product incentives and quotas fostered falsified loans at a subsidiary. Store sales today run on product goals, product-based pay and line-run risk and HR.
- **Numbers that would change this:** active/funded rate of cross-sold accounts, sales-integrity terminations and hotline reports, branch turnover, how goals are set.

Greatness potential: 42/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is the bank that most U.S. households and small businesses treat as *their* bank. Their paychecks land there and they pay bills from it. They borrow there because the banker knows them, not because someone needed to hit a daily number. Every account counted is an account the customer opened on purpose and uses. Store teams are paid for customer outcomes: retention, primary-relationship depth, problems solved. An independent risk and conduct function sees every business line, so a bad practice in one region surfaces in weeks rather than staying local for years. The low-cost deposits that come from being the primary bank fund prudent lending, which is how Larkspur came through the crisis. That advantage compounds because customers trust it and stay. The "one in three households" claim means the same thing to a regulator, a journalist and a customer.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Larkspur can be the best in America at being the primary, trusted everyday bank for households and small businesses in its communities, and its engine is profit per active primary relationship.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Chosen | Ruled out (cannot be best at) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Largest mortgage originator; cross-sell leader; national store network; crisis-tested lender | Primary everyday banking for households and small businesses through a dense local network, backed by low-cost deposits | Global investment banking and trading; lowest-price mortgage volume; growth by large acquisition; being the "most products per household" bank |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Products per household; profit per store; profit per household; low-cost deposits per household | **Profit per active primary relationship.** A household or small business counts as primary if it has its main checking/operating account with Larkspur, with regular deposits and active use. | Products per household. It counts what was sold, not what is valued, and it can be gamed. |
| **Passion** | "Stores" culture; community banking as the heart of the firm; the vision statement of meeting all of a customer's needs | Serving the customer's whole financial life, locally | Hitting the number for its own sake `[ASSUMPTION: the store culture's pride is in serving customers, not quotas; Gate 4 review must confirm]` |

**Why this and not "cross-sell to eight":** the vision statement says *meet all of a customer's financial needs*. Cross-sell is a proxy for that. Once the proxy is paid and enforced daily, it can drift away from what it was meant to measure. This Hedgehog keeps the vision and swaps the proxy for one that is harder to fake: active, funded, primary use.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Crisis resilience came from a deposit-funded, relationship-heavy balance sheet, not from trading.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: chasing mortgage share, chasing product counts, buying growth.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It demotes the company's signature metric and its pay system.

**Confirmation tests (12 months):**
1. Profit per primary relationship is measurably higher than profit per non-primary household. If it is not, the denominator is wrong.
2. Active/funded rate of cross-sold products is ≥ `[ASSUMPTION: 80%]`. If it is far lower, reported cross-sell overstates the franchise and the Hedgehog becomes urgent, not optional.
3. Stores piloting outcome-based pay keep or grow primary relationships without the conduct incidents seen under quotas.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying store team members on products sold and cross-sell counts.** Stop daily product quotas.
2. **Stop using "eight products per household" as a goal** that cascades into regional and store targets.
3. **Stop reporting cross-sell externally without an active/funded qualifier.**
4. **Stop counting unfunded or inactive accounts as relationships** in any internal scorecard.
5. **Stop running risk management, HR and compliance inside the lines they police.**
6. **Stop chasing mortgage origination share as a goal in itself.** Mortgage stays as a relationship product.
7. **Stop framing regulatory findings as "industry issues" or as belonging to "former" units.** Each one gets a Larkspur-specific root-cause review.
8. **Stop pursuing large acquisitions for at least three years** `[ASSUMPTION: horizon]`. Growth comes from the flywheel, not from buying it.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** every account counted is customer-initiated and verifiable. Every sales anomaly reaches central risk within one reporting cycle. Every regulatory finding gets a blameless autopsy within 60 days.

**Framework and freedom:** business lines keep wide operating autonomy (decentralization stays). The boundaries are central: incentive design, conduct monitoring and risk limits. That is freedom within a framework, rather than freedom without one.

**Bureaucracy check:** daily quota tracking is partly a substitute for trusting bankers to serve customers. With the right people and outcome-based pay, much of that tracking can go.

**BHAG (15 years):** be the bank that one in three U.S. households uses as its *primary* bank, measured by active use, and the most trusted large bank in America by independent survey.
