# Good to Great: Larkspur Financial

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. The number the whole bank steers by has never been checked against reality.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the primary, everyday financial home for American households and small businesses, and grow only by deepening the relationships customers actually use. *Denominator: profit per active primary household.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** "products customers actually use." Today that link runs on daily quotas and per-product pay, not on customer need.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Paying incentives on raw product counts.
  2. Setting daily product-sales goals in stores.
  3. Letting risk and HR sit inside the business lines they are meant to police.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** The retail bank runs on the same mechanism, sales quotas plus pay per product sold, that a regulator said last July fostered loan falsification in a sister unit.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - the share of cross-sold products that are funded or active
  - the trend in sales-misconduct terminations and hotline reports
  - branch turnover

Greatness potential: 42/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is still the bank in one of every three American households. The difference is that every relationship it counts is one the customer chose and uses. A store banker's job is to find the customer's real needs, and their pay reflects whether the customer is better off and stays. The cross-sell number is lower than today's but honest, and the bank reports it alongside the share of products that are active. An independent risk function with a direct line to the board sees sales practices in every unit, so problems surface while they are small and cheap. That makes earnings growth after the merger slower but durable. The bank wins on low-cost deposits, primary-bank status and trust, not on product counts. When the low-rate squeeze tempts it to push harder, it refuses.

```
   │          ▼
   │   [4] Better-fit products the customer actually USES   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   [5] Profit per active primary household  (denominator)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   [6] Reinvest: stores, digital, pay-for-outcomes, independent controls
   │          │
   │          ▼
   └── [7] Reputation & referrals → more households choose Larkspur
```

**Current push:**
- **Turn 1:** Make link 4 honest. Measure whether every product opened in a store is funded and used, and pay for that rather than for openings.
- **Turn 2:** Push link 2. Make primary-relationship growth the headline retail goal.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Consistency:** Strategic consistency is high. The relationship strategy has run for decades, and that is a strength.
- **The danger is the setting it runs in.** Integration savings are ending and low rates squeeze margins. The tempting reaction is to raise sales goals to hold earnings growth. That would be reaction without understanding, and it would speed up a flywheel whose link 4 may already be partly fictional.
- **Big programs:** The crisis acquisition was a large program, but it is now complete. A further acquisition to "jump-start" growth would be a warning sign.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying incentive compensation on raw product counts and cross-sell.** Pay instead on active, funded relationships, customer satisfaction and retention, with clawbacks.
2. **Stop setting daily and monthly product-sales goals for store staff.**
3. **Stop running risk management and HR investigations inside the business lines.**
4. **Stop using "eight products per household" as a goal, internally or externally.** Keep it at most as an outcome to observe.
5. **Stop reporting cross-sell without an active or funded rate beside it.**
6. **Stop treating the consumer-finance fine as a legacy matter in a former subsidiary.** It describes a mechanism, and that mechanism is still in use.
7. **Stop chasing mortgage and CRE market share for its own sake.** Volume outside a relationship is outside the Hedgehog Concept.
8. **Do not answer the post-integration earnings gap by raising sales intensity.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. The customer's real interest comes before the sale.
2. Integrity: do the right thing when no one is measuring it.
3. Team members own outcomes, not quotas.
4. Disciplined, conservative risk-taking.

**Core purpose:** Help American households and small businesses succeed financially.

**BHAG** (20-year horizon): *Be the bank the most American households choose as their primary bank, and the one they trust most.* Both halves are measured, and neither can be traded for the other.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- an independent CRO with a direct line to the board
- a board-level conduct dashboard
- clawback provisions on incentive pay
- evidence of customer consent at account opening
- a centralized ethics hotline with trend reporting to the board
- a deliberate succession bench for the CEO and for the head of every major line
