# Good to Great: Marbury Laboratories

## Verdict

**Marbury is already great, so this plan protects what works rather than changing it.** The case rests on 23 straight years of higher earnings, roughly 39% return on equity, almost no debt, and world leadership in diagnostics and U.S. infant formula.

**Hedgehog Concept (to confirm):** Marbury leads the world in science-based products that make health care more cost-effective, and it earns more profit per employee by winning on volume with cost-driven buyers.

**Weakest flywheel link:** manufacturing quality at scale. Two years of unresolved regulatory findings at the diagnostics plants threaten the company's strongest franchise.

**Top three temptations to refuse:**
1. Buying a pharmacy benefit manager.
2. Merging for scale with a large drugmaker.
3. An open-ended branded-drug R&D race against the largest firms.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the diagnostics quality concerns are unresolved. The intravenous-solution recall about 25 years ago shows what this kind of failure costs.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** shareholder returns versus the market, the regulators' actual findings, profit by product line, and the joint venture's economics.

`Greatness potential: 74/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marbury is what it already is, kept in shape. It is the dependable, science-led supplier that cost-pressured hospitals, payers, government programs and parents choose first. It wins because its diagnostics, nutrition, hospital products and focused drugs lower the total cost of care, not because it charges more. Growth comes from volume, with last year's 8.0% volume against 0.8% price as the template. Profits come from manufacturing excellence, and reinvestment goes into focused R&D where Marbury can lead. Its quality is beyond reproach, which matters most in diagnostics. Its leadership succession is orderly. It stays independent while competitors merge, because its advantage is focus rather than size.

## 5. Temptations to refuse (stewardship mode)

1. **Buying a pharmacy benefit manager.** Competitors' multibillion-dollar deals make this look necessary. It is not a business Marbury can be best at.
2. **Merging for scale.** Mergers destroy focus, and organic consistency has been the engine for 30 years.
3. **An open-ended branded-drug R&D race.** Fund drugs only in niches Marbury can lead, such as antibiotics, epilepsy and the JV's hormonal oncology `[ASSUMPTION on which niches]`.
4. **Raising prices to keep the earnings streak alive.** This contradicts the cost-effective positioning, and buyers now hold the power.
5. **Protecting the quarter by slowing quality spending.** The intravenous-solution recall about 25 years ago is the cautionary precedent.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION, inferred]`:
1. Quality without exception.
2. Science in service of practical care.
3. Consistency over spectacle.
4. Integrity with regulators and customers.

**Core purpose:** to make good health care more affordable through science-based products.

**BHAG** (15–20 years): *Make every cost-conscious health system in the world turn to Marbury first, and lead the world in diagnostics and nutrition by an unassailable margin.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A quality function that is independent of plant management and can halt shipments.
- A succession process owned by the board, with a named president.
- A capital-allocation rule: no acquisition outside the hedgehog, and organic growth by default.
