# Kessling Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**State:** Good, not yet great. The case for "good" rests on history, not on numbers.

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Kessling can be the best in the world at turning hormone and inflammation science into safe, affordable medicines, measured by gross profit per research dollar.

**Weakest flywheel link:** evidence strong enough to satisfy regulators. Two flagship products have been hit on safety or efficacy grounds.

**Stop doing:**
1. Trying to match the biggest firms' research budgets across every therapeutic area.
2. Buying non-drug businesses such as seeds and agriculture.
3. Fighting regulators in court instead of fixing the evidence.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Kessling's next profit engine is a compound whose rights belong to another firm. It arrives just as Kessling's own franchises have lost credibility.

**Numbers that would change this answer:** return on equity versus peers, research spending as a share of sales, the sales share and returns of the non-drug businesses, and the terms of the arthritis license.

Greatness potential: 12/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Kessling has stopped trying to be a small copy of the largest research-based drug companies. It is the company physicians trust first for hormone-related and inflammatory disease. It owns a deep, cost-advantaged base in steroid and hormone chemistry, and it uses that base to launch a steady line of products whose safety evidence is stronger than regulators require. In-licensed compounds feed franchises Kessling already leads; they never stand in for one. Non-drug businesses are gone or walled off. Leadership is chosen for ability, from inside or outside the family, and an independent board holds it to account. Success shows up as more profit per research dollar than larger rivals earn. It is not measured by research spending or company size.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):**
> *Kessling will be the world's best at turning hormone and inflammation science into safe, affordable medicines that doctors trust, earning more profit per research dollar than any larger rival.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Broad research-based prescription drugs; antibiotics; hormone and steroid chemistry, including low-cost fermentation; inflammation therapy; diabetes and metabolic drugs; seeds | Hormone and steroid chemistry, with inflammation therapy as its natural extension (adrenal-hormone first, low-cost steroid route, arthritis compound) | **Broad Rx:** the biggest firms outspend Kessling. **Antibiotics:** Kessling was sixth, not first, and its key combination product was withdrawn. **Diabetes:** the flagship is under a safety cloud. **Seeds:** no distinctive capability. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per prescription; profit per franchise; profit per kg of active ingredient; **gross profit per research dollar** | **Gross profit per research dollar**, measured over a rolling five-year window. A mid-sized firm can only win on research productivity, not research volume. | Pure sales growth or research spending as a share of sales, because both reward imitating the giants. |
| **Passion** | Founder's culture of medicine-making firsts; care for employees; pride in the home city; research identity | Making medicines that are firsts and hold up `[ASSUMPTION: research staff identify with the hormone and steroid heritage]` | "Diversified agribusiness" `[ASSUMPTION: no evidence of passion for it]` |

**Intersection tests**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Mostly. The adrenal-hormone first, the steroid fermentation route and the oral diabetes drug are all endocrine. Wartime antibiotics are the exception, and the Hedgehog deliberately leaves them out.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: seeds, broad antibiotic research, and trying to keep pace with the giants in every area.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It demotes a historic antibiotic franchise and reverses a six-year-old diversification.

**What would confirm it:**
1. Margins on hormone and inflammation products beat the company average.
2. Research hit rates in these areas beat hit rates elsewhere in the portfolio.
3. The steroid manufacturing cost advantage is verified against competitors.
4. The arthritis launch wins meaningful physician share within 24 months.

If items 1–3 fail, the Hedgehog is wrong. Loop back to Gate 5.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Launch the arthritis compound on overbuilt evidence** (link 3→4). At the same time, start an owned follow-on program in inflammation so the franchise does not depend on another firm's rights (link 2).
2. **Resolve the diabetes-drug safety question with a definitive study or an honest relabeling** (link 3). Trust lost in one franchise leaks into all of them.

**Doom-loop check: early signs are present.**
- *Acquisition to jump-start momentum:* the seed company, bought through a share exchange.
- *Reaction without understanding:* the court fight over the antibiotic withdrawal.
- *New direction after new direction:* the open question of whether to lean on licensing or on diversification.
- *Benchmarking against the wrong peer set:* comparing itself with the largest research firms.

None of these is fatal yet. Together they describe a company searching for momentum outside its core.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** benchmarking research spending against the largest firms across all therapeutic areas.
2. **Stop** acquiring non-drug businesses. Put the seed and agricultural units under divestiture review `[ASSUMPTION: their returns are below the drug business; verify]`.
3. **Stop** litigating regulatory decisions as the first response. Fix the evidence instead.
4. **Stop** starting new antibiotic research programs outside the Hedgehog. Run the existing antibiotics for cash `[ASSUMPTION: no hidden leadership position]`.
5. **Stop** signing licenses that give no path to owned follow-on compounds.
6. **Stop** treating family membership as a qualification for top roles.
7. **Stop** asking "licensing or diversification?" The answer is neither as a strategy. Licensing is a tool to feed the Hedgehog.

**Non-negotiable disciplines**
- Every research program, license and acquisition must fit the Hedgehog sentence, or it is declined.
- Internal safety evidence must exceed the regulatory minimum before launch.
- No single licensed product may exceed a set share of drug profit `[ASSUMPTION: 30%]` without an owned follow-on in development.
- Capital is allocated against a return hurdle tied to return on equity.

**Freedom within the framework:** research teams choose molecules, methods and partners freely inside hormone and inflammation science.

**Bureaucracy check:** if family-approval layers exist `[ASSUMPTION]`, they compensate for missing independent governance. Replace them with the board.

- A capital-allocation hurdle.
