# Kessling Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Kessling is good, not yet great, and it is drifting.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** *Kessling can be best in the world at discovering and making medicines for inflammation and hormone disorders, built on its steroid chemistry. It should judge itself by profit per research dollar, not by research spending.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning safety evidence into the trust of regulators and doctors. Two of its signature products have been hit in five years.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Growing seeds and other non-drug businesses.
  2. Spreading research across every disease area to keep pace with bigger rivals.
  3. Fighting regulators in court instead of out-evidencing them.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the next big profit source is a compound whose rights belong to another firm, while Kessling's own flagship products are compromised.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - Research spending as a share of sales, against peers.
  - Profit by product line, especially steroids and the arthritis compound.
  - Return on capital of the seed business.
  - The founding family's ownership stake.

Greatness potential: 17/100

---

**Evidence basis:** This run had no search, memory or past chats, so the evidence-gathering step and any market research were skipped. The dossier, as of early Year 0, is the only evidence. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`. No financial figures are invented. Current sales, earnings and return figures are missing, which weakens every core gate.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Kessling is narrow and deep. It is the company specialists turn to first for inflammation and hormone-disorder medicine.

- **Research:** It does not try to match the giants' research budgets. It out-converts them, so each research dollar turns into more approved, durable, profitable medicines in one area.
- **Manufacturing:** It makes those medicines at low cost, using the process chemistry it has led since its founding pill.
- **Licensing:** It treats licensed compounds as bridges into its own franchise, not as substitutes for discovery.
- **Safety:** It earns trust by being the first to surface bad news about its own products.
- **Governance:** It is run by the best person available, whether or not they are family. A majority-independent board makes sure the company outlasts any one leader, product or deal.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Kessling can be best in the world at discovering and making medicines for inflammation and hormone disorders, built on its steroid chemistry. It should judge itself by profit per research dollar, not by research spending.*

### Three circles

| Circle | Candidates considered | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Broad research-based prescription drugs | **No.** It cannot match the largest firms' research budgets. |
| | Antibiotics | **No.** It was sixth-largest 16 years ago and has since lost a combination product worth about 12% of domestic gross income. |
| | Seeds and agriculture | **No.** There is no evidence of any edge. |
| | Oral diabetes therapy | **Not now.** It was first to market, but safety studies cloud the product. It still belongs to the hormone franchise. |
| | **Steroid and hormone chemistry** (first adrenal-hormone product, low-cost fermentation route to steroids) | **Yes, potential.** This is a demonstrated, rare capability. |
| | **Inflammation** (corticosteroids plus the arthritis anti-inflammatory) | **Yes, potential.** It fits the steroid base. |
| | Low-cost formulation and process chemistry | **Supporting capability**, not the core on its own. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per employee; profit per product; profit per prescription in a specialty | Plausible alternatives. |
| | **Profit per research dollar** | **Chosen.** It answers the central strategic question, which is how to win while being outspent. |
| **Passion** | Research and science tradition (first research hire about 60 years ago) | Strong evidence. |
| | Employee welfare and the home community | Strong evidence. |
| | Steroid and hormone science specifically | `[ASSUMPTION]` Needs confirmation from the scientists themselves. |

### Tests

- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The adrenal-hormone first, the cheap steroid route and the first oral diabetes drug (an endocrine medicine) all fit. Three of the four historic wins sit inside it; antibiotics is the exception.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: seeds, new antibiotic programs, and research across all disease areas.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means shrinking a historic line, selling a recent acquisition, and admitting that Kessling cannot be the all-round peer of the giants.

### What would confirm it

1. Steroid, endocrine and inflammation products earn above the company's average margin and return on capital.
2. Research output in these areas (candidates reaching late-stage trials per dollar spent) beats the rest of the portfolio.
3. Rheumatologists and endocrinologists adopt the arthritis compound fast, and it pulls through other Kessling products.
4. The best scientists choose to work on these programs.

**Status:** working hypothesis. Collins's companies often needed years to settle theirs.

```
  │                    │

  │                    │
  │                    ▼
  │   6. Partners bring compounds to Kessling first;
  │        profits reinvested in focused discovery
  └────────────────────┘
```

**Current push** (the next two turns):

1. **Repair link 2.** Commission an independent re-evaluation of the diabetes drug and publish what is found. Build the arthritis launch on more safety evidence than regulators require.
2. **Make the arthritis compound a bridge, not a crutch.** Use its revenue and specialist relationships to fund in-house successors in inflammation. Negotiate with the licensor for rights to follow-on compounds.

**Doom-loop check: signs present.**

- **Reaction without understanding:** fighting the antibiotic withdrawal in court.
- **A deal to jump-start momentum:** the share-exchange purchase of the seed company.
- **Salvation thinking:** the open question of whether to lean more on licensing and diversification.

None of these is fatal yet, but together they form a pattern.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** growing, or buying more of, seeds and agriculture. Begin an orderly exit.
2. **Stop** running research programs across every disease area in order to look like the giants.
3. **Stop** treating regulators as adversaries to beat in court.
4. **Stop** promoting the diabetes drug ahead of an honest re-evaluation of the safety evidence.
5. **Stop** judging success by research spending compared with the largest firms. Judge by output per dollar.
6. **Stop** starting new antibiotic discovery programs `[ASSUMPTION]`, pending product-level profit data.
7. **Stop** filling top seats by kinship.
8. **Stop** signing licensing deals that give no path to follow-on rights or in-house successors.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**

- Every research program is reviewed against the Hedgehog each year.
- Every safety signal goes to the board within days.
- Every acquisition or licensing deal must strengthen the inflammation and hormone franchise.

**Framework and freedom:** scientists choose their methods and targets freely, but only inside the franchise boundary.

**Bureaucracy check:** a family-only board probably needed informal controls that independent directors would make unnecessary. `[ASSUMPTION]`

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Science that holds up.
2. Patient safety comes before product defense.
3. Care for the people who do the work.
4. Thrift and ingenuity in making medicine.

**Core purpose:** To give doctors medicines they can trust, made well and affordably.

**BHAG (20 years):** Kessling becomes the company specialists worldwide think of first for inflammation and hormone medicine, with most of its drug profit coming from compounds it discovered itself.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A majority-independent board.
- A written succession process that considers non-family candidates.
- A safety committee with authority to stop launches.
- An annual research portfolio review against the Hedgehog.
- A licensing policy that requires follow-on rights.
