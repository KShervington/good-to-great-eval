## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kessling is a mid-sized, independent prescription-drug company. It owns the world's leading franchise in hormone, steroid and anti-inflammatory medicine. Its edge comes from chemistry and manufacturing: it makes these molecules better and cheaper than anyone, and it uses that cost advantage to fund focused research instead of broad research. It no longer asks "can we match the giants' research budgets?" It asks "do we earn more per research dollar than anyone in our field?", and the answer is yes. It licenses in compounds only when they fit the franchise, and it always builds its own follow-on molecules so that no single license can sink it. Regulators and specialist physicians regard it as the most rigorous safety-evidence house in its field. Its leaders, its board and its succession are chosen for merit and are open beyond the founding family. The founder's ethos survives: a better pill, and care for employees and for the home city.

**Weakest link: the hand-off from step 2 to step 3.** Owned molecules are not reaching the market. The near-term leader is licensed, and the owned diabetes drug is under a safety cloud.

**Current push, the next two turns:**

1. Fund an owned follow-on program in anti-inflammatories that builds on Kessling's steroid chemistry. It should use the licensed launch to build specialist relationships, not to replace in-house discovery.
2. Lead the safety science on the diabetes drug through independent, transparent analysis, instead of defending the product.
