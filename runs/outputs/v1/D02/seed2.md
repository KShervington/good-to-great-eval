## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is the default answer to "who keeps my dish machine running?" for independent restaurants in every neighborhood it serves, and it serves only neighborhoods where it is dense. Every placed machine sits within a short drive of a technician who already services several others nearby. That density lets Corvane answer emergencies inside four hours better than 95% of the time in *every* territory, not just the first one. Density also produces positive contribution per technician after field labor, and it funds the next machine without new equity. Machines come from several low-cost channels: chain-remodel lots, closures, and redeployment from churned accounts. None of them is a single lucky pipeline. Chefs sell Corvane to other chefs, and Dev no longer has to. Health-inspection reliability is part of the promise. New metros open only after a written density gate has been passed, never because of a founder's spring timetable or a large contract outside the core.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Corvane keeps independent restaurants' dish lines running by owning both the machine and its care, and it sells only where route density lets it answer inside four hours.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | What Corvane can **not** be best at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best in the world at | Refurbishing dish machines; field service for all commercial dish machines; uptime for independents | **Uptime for undercounter and door-type machines at independent restaurants, inside a dense service radius** | Conveyor and hotel service (the manufacturer has certified depth); chemical supply at scale (the national bundler); being everywhere |
| Economic engine | Revenue per account; gross margin per machine; contribution per technician | **Contribution per field technician, after field labor** | n/a |
| Passion | Dev: chefs and independents. Teodor: service excellence. Marguerite: bringing machines back. Callum: routing. | **Making sure an operator never loses a service night to the dish line** | n/a |

**Why "per technician":** field labor is the largest cost, and it sits outside the reported 63% margin. Today there are about 20.6 machines per technician (351 ÷ 17). Each machine earns about $2,071 a year of gross profit before field labor ($274 × 12 × 63%). At an assumed $70k loaded cost per technician `[ASSUMPTION]`, it takes about 34 machines per technician just to cover field labor. Density is therefore the economics, not just the service.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The 4.8 rating and the word "fast" in reviews come from the dense home metro (94.1%), not the thin East territory (81%).
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: the third metro, Larchmont, conveyor units and scattered accounts.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It turns down $412k a year that the board likes, and it delays the CEO's goal.

**Confirmation evidence (within 12 months):**
1. Fully loaded contribution per technician is positive in the home metro and rises as clusters fill.
2. East territory response improves as East density rises.
3. Logo churn is low enough that redeployed units become a supply channel rather than a leak.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Low-cost machines in
 │      (secured remodel lots · closures · redeployment · refurb)
 │              ↓
 │   2. Bundled placement ($274→$289/mo), only inside existing clusters
 │              ↓
 │   3. Route density ↑  (machines per technician)
 │              ↓
 │   4. Fast response (≥95% inside 4 hrs) + preventive care + passed inspections
 │              ↓
 │   5. Chef word-of-mouth, "fast" reviews → more accounts nearby + pricing power
 │              ↓
 └── 6. Contribution per technician ↑  ← denominator; cash funds step 1
```

**Weakest link:** step 1. All-in cost per machine:

| Source | All-in cost per machine (landed + refurb + install) |
|---|---|
| Delmar | about $2,130 |
| Auction or closure | $3,690–$4,590 |
| New | about $8,030 |

Payback before field labor moves from about 12 months on Delmar units to 21–27 months on auction units. A rise in price to $289 trims that to about 20–25 months.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Secure every remaining Harrow's unit Delmar can offer, and stand up at least two replacement channels.
2. Fill the East territory's existing clusters until response recovers. Don't extend its radius.

**Doom-loop check: at risk, not yet in one.** Three new directions are on the table at once while the second territory is unproven: a new metro, a new customer type (hotels) and a new machine class (conveyor). Larchmont is also a "big deal to jump-start" revenue. None has been acted on yet.
