# Varden Communications: Good to Great (stewardship mode)

**Verdict: already great, and showing the first two signs of decline.** Varden has 70 years of self-renewal behind it and is world No. 1 in cell phones, paging and two-way radio. The job now is to protect what made it great, not to reinvent it.

Greatness potential: 34/100

**Hedgehog:** Varden is best in the world at making wireless communication smaller, more reliable and more widely available. That includes making its own products obsolete before anyone else does.

**Weakest flywheel link:** self-obsolescence. The proudest new launch is an analog phone, arriving just as carriers plan digital networks.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. The goal of doubling every five years.
2. An open-ended commitment to the satellite venture's full launch.
3. Using analog dominance to force carriers into premium distribution.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Varden's own cellular leadership is defending the standard that Varden would normally be first to obsolete.

**Numbers that would change this:** digital handset share by standard, profit by segment, satellite demand forecasts, and carrier reaction to the distribution terms.

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Varden does what it has always done, and does it on purpose: it obsoletes its own best-selling product before a rival can. Within three years it is the leading supplier of digital handsets and infrastructure in each major digital standard it chooses to back. It funds that shift from its analog profits instead of protecting them. Carriers treat it as their most trusted partner for building digital networks, not as a supplier that rations its hottest phone. It holds the satellite venture to the same demand-and-kill discipline as any internal project, with an exposure cap the board has signed off. Growth comes from profit per subscriber served, not from a size target. Semiconductors focus on the chips where Varden can be best in the world, not on a contest with the dominant PC chip supplier. Six Sigma quality and open technical debate keep working, and that debate actually changes decisions.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

Stewardship mode applies here. Varden already lives by this Hedgehog Concept: its history of leaving TV and car radio and pushing into cellular is the evidence. The risk is drifting away from it, not lacking one.

**Sentence:** *Varden is best in the world at making wireless communication smaller, more reliable and more widely available, and at being first to obsolete its own products.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | What it is *not* best at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world** | Radio-frequency engineering; Six Sigma manufacturing at scale; miniaturization; semiconductor design; global satellite telephony; consumer branding | RF engineering + Six Sigma quality + miniaturization, applied to wireless devices *and* networks | Winning PC microprocessors against the dominant supplier `[ASSUMPTION]`; running a satellite telephone *service*; using distribution leverage as a strategy |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per handset; profit per employee; profit per technology generation; profit per subscriber served | **Profit per wireless subscriber served** (handset + infrastructure + paging + radio) `[ASSUMPTION]`. It ties the whole wireless business together and rewards following subscribers into digital. | Pure revenue size, which is what the doubling goal measures |
| **Passion** | Engineering excellence; quality; self-renewal; global reach | Engineering, quality and renewal: the "make our own products obsolete" culture | Protecting an installed base |

**Tests**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes: the exits from TV and car radio, the move into cellular, and the quality award.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: analog defense, coercive distribution, the PC chip war, and size for its own sake.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It means cannibalizing roughly 45% world share in analog phones.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. Segment profitability shows wireless profit per subscriber holding up through the move to digital.
2. Embedded and communications chips earn more per dollar of R&D than PC microprocessors.
3. Carriers choose Varden as lead digital supplier in at least one major standard within 18 months.

```
   │              │
   │              ▼

   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    4. Profit per subscriber served  ◄── denominator
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    5. Reinvest in R&D for the NEXT standard
   │              │
   │              ▼
   └─── 6. Obsolete our own products before rivals do   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
```

**Weakest link: step 6.** It is currently running backwards. Analog success is being used to argue against the next standard.

**Strained link: step 3.** The premium distribution program asks carriers to trade freedom for access, at the exact moment they are choosing digital suppliers.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. Commit to dated digital handset and infrastructure launches in each major standard Varden chooses to back. Fund them from analog profits.
2. Turn carrier relationships from leverage into partnership around the digital roadmap.

**Doom-loop check:** several early signs are present.
- **Size target driving direction:** the doubling goal.
- **Big bets that could become jump-starts:** satellite, PC microprocessors, and large emerging-market investments, all at once.
- **Reaction without understanding:** wireless pricing pressure is described as a "difficult comparison" problem, not as a sign of analog commoditization `[ASSUMPTION]`.

This is not yet a doom loop. It is the setup for one.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship: temptations to refuse)

1. **Stop managing to "double every five years."** Doubling from $27.0B means about 15% compound growth, or about $54B by Year 4. A size target rewards whatever adds revenue, inside the Hedgehog or not. Replace it with the scorecard in section 8.
2. **Stop treating the analog installed base as a moat.** "Tens of millions of customers can't be wrong" describes the past. Carriers decide the future.
3. **Stop the premium distribution program.** Using a hot phone to force share and shelf space puts carrier goodwill at risk just as carriers pick digital partners.
4. **Stop underwriting the satellite venture's downside without limits.** Commit to no full launch phase without independent demand evidence, a board-approved exposure cap, and pre-agreed kill criteria.
5. **Stop funding a head-on PC microprocessor war** unless segment data shows Varden can lead there `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Uncompromising integrity.
2. Six Sigma quality as a way of working, not a program.
3. Constant self-renewal, including obsoleting ourselves.
4. Open, evidence-driven technical debate.
5. Respect for every person.

**Core purpose:** to connect people reliably, wherever they are.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** put a reliable wireless connection in the hand of every person on Earth who wants one, on whatever standard wins.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- **Annual obsolescence review.** Every business must present the product that would kill its current best-seller, with a funded plan to ship it first.
- **Bet-gating.** Every venture above a size threshold gets independent demand evidence, an exposure cap and kill criteria before each major phase.
- **Red-flag channel to the board.** Any engineer can escalate a standards or risk concern, and it must be answered.
- **Succession on record.** Future CEO candidates are assessed against the scorecard, not lineage.
