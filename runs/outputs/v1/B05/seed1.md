# Good to Great: Varden Communications

**Verdict: already great, and showing early signs of decline. Run in stewardship mode.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Lead every generation of wireless communication by making our own products obsolete first, built at world scale to Six Sigma quality, and earn more profit per wireless subscriber than anyone.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** self-obsolescence. The move from analog to digital has stalled while carriers plan digital networks.
- **Temptations to refuse:**
  1. Defending analog, with the new clamshell as the hero product.
  2. Pushing carriers into the premium distribution program.
  3. Committing to the satellite launch phase without demand data and a hard cap on exposure.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the world's No. 1 handset maker sells mostly analog phones just as carriers turn to digital, and a senior cellular leader is publicly defending analog.
- **Numbers that would change this:** digital handset share by standard, profitability by segment, the satellite venture's demand forecasts, and how carriers react to the distribution plan.

Greatness potential: 40/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Varden is the company that ends its own analog franchise before anyone else can. It treats the analog clamshell as a bridge to digital, not as the destination. It ships competitive digital handsets and infrastructure in every standard its carriers choose, and it earns carriers' loyalty instead of extracting it. Its Six Sigma discipline and patent engine are pointed at one question: what comes after the current generation of wireless? It funds long-range bets the way it always has, as small experiments with capped downside. It does not let any single bet put the balance sheet at risk. It measures itself by profit per wireless subscriber and by whether earnings grow at least as fast as sales, not by doubling in size. Its leadership is clearly accountable for the transition, and its culture of combative debate ends in dated decisions rather than open-ended argument.

**Hedgehog sentence (working hypothesis):** *Lead every generation of wireless communication by making our own products obsolete first, built at world scale to Six Sigma quality, and earn more profit per wireless subscriber than anyone.*

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. The exits from TV and car radio, and the rise to No. 1 in cellular, paging and two-way radio, all fit.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It rules out defending analog for its own sake, coercing the carriers who generate subscribers, and open-ended non-wireless bets.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It means cannibalizing the 45%-share analog franchise now.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
- Segment profitability showing that wireless drives most of the profit.
- Digital handset share showing the company can lead in at least the major standards.
- Whether semiconductors' value comes mainly from wireless and embedded uses rather than the PC microprocessor fight.

```
 │   2. Obsolete our own products first: ship the next generation   ◄── WEAKEST LINK (analog→digital stalled)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   3. Six Sigma manufacturing at world scale → quality + falling cost
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   4. Carriers choose and trust Varden as partner   ◄── fragile (premium distribution program)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   5. Subscriber growth → volume → profit per wireless subscriber
 │            │
 └────────────┘ 6. Cash funds the next round of bets
```

The loop closes. It explains roughly a decade of fivefold growth.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Turn step 2:** commit, with dates, to competitive digital phones and infrastructure in the standards carriers are choosing. Reposition the analog clamshell as a bridge product.
2. **Protect step 4:** pause the premium distribution program until carriers have been heard. The carriers are about to choose digital suppliers, so this is the worst moment to make them resent the company.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Defending analog as strategy.** Make the clamshell the last great analog phone, not the flagship of an analog future.
2. **Coercing carriers** with high-share and display requirements, at the exact moment they pick digital suppliers.
3. **Escalating satellite commitments** without independent demand forecasts and a hard cap that includes subcontract obligations.
4. **Managing to "double every five years."**
5. **Pursuing the PC microprocessor war for its own sake**, pending profitability data.
6. **Letting technical debate run without a decision date.** Combative debate is an asset only if it ends.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** (inferred, `[ASSUMPTION]` where noted):
1. Continual self-renewal: we obsolete our own products.
2. Quality to Six Sigma.
3. Open, rigorous technical debate that ends in decisions.
4. Long-range bets, seeded small.
5. Integrity with customers and partners `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Core purpose** `[ASSUMPTION]`: to let people communicate wherever they are.

**BHAG (25 years):** Define every generation of wireless communication. Each new network and device generation should be shaped first by Varden technology. Note that this is a goal about leadership in each generation, not about revenue size.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- **Self-obsolescence review:** a standing annual question for each business: "what kills this product line, and are we building it?"
- **Stage gates with capped exposure** for every long-range bet.
- **Mandatory carrier input** into roadmap and distribution decisions.
- **Decision dates** on every major technical debate.
- **Promotion to top seats on the same standard** for family and non-family executives.
