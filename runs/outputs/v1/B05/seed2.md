## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Stop defending analog as a strategy.** Keep selling analog, but stop treating the installed base as proof of the future.
2. **Stop using "double every five years" as a goal.** Replace it with profit per subscriber and next-generation leadership.
3. **Stop committing to the satellite launch phase without a demand gate.** No commitment before independent market research, explicit kill criteria and a cap on subcontract obligations.
4. **Stop the premium distribution program as designed.** Conditioning supply on carriers giving Varden a high share of their shelf spends goodwill right before carriers choose their digital suppliers. Test carrier reaction first.
5. **Stop funding a head-on PC microprocessor challenge.** Keep the semiconductor capability aimed at wireless and embedded markets where Varden can be best.
6. **Stop evaluating emerging-market investments by size.** Judge China and other markets by subscribers connected and profit per subscriber.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Six Sigma quality on every new generation, including the first digital units.
- Every product line funds a team building its own replacement.
- Dissent from engineers reaches the executive committee unfiltered.

**Framework:** business units have full freedom over how they win, inside the Hedgehog and the scorecard. Leaving the Hedgehog requires a gated, capped bet approved at the executive committee.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rule is evidently compensating for wrong people. The opposite risk applies: combative debate can become a ritual that loses to seniority.
