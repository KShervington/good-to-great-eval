# Calder Business Machines Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Calder will be the best in the world at automating how banks and other money-keeping institutions record, sort and post their transactions, earning more profit per financial-institution customer by supplying the whole chain, from the printed check to the computer that posts it.

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning bank relationships into profit that funds *focused* R&D. Margins are about 2%, and R&D is spread across every front.

**Stop doing:**
1. Fighting Rival I across the whole general-purpose computer market.
2. Buying businesses to grow sales.
3. Promising that "profits will recover" with no date attached.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** sales rose about 4× in 12 years while net profit *fell*, from roughly $12M to roughly $8M. The company is growing without earning.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit by line (computers, banking, accounting, defense, forms) and earnings for Years −3 to −1. If bank automation is not the most profitable line, the Hedgehog is wrong.

Greatness potential: 28/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Calder no longer tries to be a smaller Rival I. It is the company banks cannot run without, at home and abroad. Its checks, magnetic ink, sorters, bookkeeping machines and easy-to-program computers form one system that handles a bank's paper from the moment a check is printed to the moment it is posted. Most of its revenue comes from rentals and consumables that recur every month and grow with transaction volume. The company knows exactly how much it earns per institution and steers by that number. R&D money goes only where it widens that lead. Accounting machines for general business are harvested and migrated to electronic systems, not defended. Managers judge success by margin and profit per customer, not record sales. The company is run by a team in which the forceful leader asks questions before giving answers, so it does not depend on him. `[ASSUMPTION: the profit data, once produced, will support this focus.]`

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Long relationships with banks & bookkeepers           │
   │    + domestic and international sales channels           │
   └───────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                   ▼
   2. Systems designed for bankers' actual work
      (MICR ink, sorters, bookkeeping machines,
       business-English programmable computer)
                   ▼
   3. More of each bank's transaction chain installed
      (checks → ink → sorting → posting → computer)
                   ▼
   4. Recurring rental + consumable revenue
      → PROFIT PER FINANCIAL-INSTITUTION CUSTOMER ↑
                   ▼   ◄── WEAKEST LINK (2% margin; R&D diffused)
   5. Focused R&D on next-generation bank systems
                   ▼
   6. Better systems + reputation among bankers
      → more institutions, home and abroad ──► back to 1
```

**Current push, the next two turns:**
1. **Link 4:** measure profit per institution and by line, so the engine is visible at all.
2. **Link 4→5:** move R&D from broad computer competition to bank-system development, so profit feeds the loop instead of leaking out of it.

**Doom-loop check: signs present.**
- Acquisitions used to jump-start growth (forms printers, the computer maker).
- One new direction after another under the "diversify" banner.
- Heavy R&D across several fronts at once.
- Sales growth celebrated while profit shrank.
- A strategic partnership that lapsed because the board did not decide.

The loop closes, so the gate passes. The doom-loop pattern is the default path if nothing changes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop competing across the whole general-purpose computer market against Rival I.** Sell computers as the heart of bank and accounting systems only.
2. **Stop acquiring businesses to add sales.** No acquisition outside the bank and financial-institution chain.
3. **Stop explaining the margin collapse as temporary R&D** without a dated recovery plan.
4. **Stop measuring success by total sales.** The $389M record is the wrong headline.
5. **Stop growth investment in mechanical adding machines.** Harvest them for cash and migrate the customers.
6. **Stop funding R&D projects that cannot name their link to bank or accounting systems.**
7. **Stop non-bank business-forms expansion.** Keep the forms and checks that sit inside the bank chain.
8. **Stop letting the military division draw top engineers** unless the technology transfers back.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Monthly profit by line and per institution, circulated to the top team.
- Every R&D project must pass an "in or out of the Hedgehog" review before it is funded.
- Every major customer win or loss gets a blameless review.

**Framework boundaries:**
- *Fixed:* line managers have freedom on how to win banks and accounting customers.
- *Their freedom ends at:* new markets, acquisitions and broad computer programs. Those need top-team and board approval against the Hedgehog.
- *Bureaucracy check:* the rules that exist only to compensate for wrong-seat people are not documented. Review them after the bench assessment.
