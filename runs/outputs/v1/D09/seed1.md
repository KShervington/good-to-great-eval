# Larder Line: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Larder Line is good, not yet great, and its most valuable supply is quietly draining away.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Corbridge's same-day cold chain for surplus protein, dairy and fresh food, moving the perishables other groups can't carry to agencies that will use them.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** donor trust turning into perishable volume. Freshway supplies 44% of all pounds, and "family" no longer guarantees the food arrives.
- **Top three things to stop:** applying for the café grant as a Larder Line program; buying the $71k third van now; running the composting route and the gleaning crew.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Freshway's Eastside pickups fell 36% per pickup after the markdown app arrived, and the app goes chainwide next year. Road construction is the comfortable explanation, but nobody has tested it.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the cold-chain share of pounds; Freshway weights by store, week and category; what agencies actually use; whether anyone else in Corbridge runs refrigerated rescue.

Greatness potential: 34/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Larder Line is the city's cold chain for perishable surplus. Every grocer, distributor and processor in Corbridge with short-dated dairy, meat or fresh food knows a trained driver in a refrigerated van will collect it the same day. Each of the 44 agencies gets what it asks for (more dairy, less bread) and uses nearly all of it. Larder Line counts what gets eaten rather than what gets picked up, and it reports households reached without being asked. No single donor supplies more than about a third of its food. It turns down good ideas that aren't cold-chain rescue, including ones its own leaders love. It runs the same way whether or not its founder is in the room.

**The retreat's three questions, answered:**
1. **Third van:** No, not now. Fix van #2 ($6,800). Take the refrigerated van off the Monday compost route, which frees roughly one van-day a week. Revisit in Q2, once the Freshway diagnosis and new perishable sources show whether the extra supply exists. At $71,000, the van equals about a third of reserves (≈$206k, four months) and 11.5% of the annual budget, and it would be capacity bought before the supply is known.
2. **Café grant:** Not as a Larder Line program. If Renata wants to build the Larder Kitchen, the board should support it as a separate venture. Larder Line becomes its food supplier under the same agreement every agency gets.
3. **Newer programs:** End the composting route and the gleaning crew. Hand the backpack pilot to the two schools or an existing backpack program, and keep the schools as recipient agencies. Finish all three transitions humanely by the end of Q1.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):**
> *Larder Line is Corbridge's same-day cold chain for surplus protein, dairy and fresh food. It gets the perishable food other groups can't carry, from any source, to the agencies that will actually use it.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world (in Corbridge) at** | pounds rescued overall; cold-chain rescue; driver reliability and agency relationships; job training; school nutrition | **Same-day refrigerated rescue of perishables, run by long-tenure trained drivers.** It is reportedly the only cold-chain rescue in the city `[ASSUMPTION: Teddy's claim, unverified]`. |
| **Not best at** | | Workforce training and food service (the café), school feeding (backpacks), composting, gleaning, and moving large volumes of bread and dry goods, which any group with a car can do |
| **Resource engine** (time, money, brand) | cost per pound; pounds per volunteer; households per dollar; usable perishable pounds per route-hour | Route-hours consume both the money (vans, staff) and the time (volunteer drivers). Funders and the county are moving toward outcome measures (households). The brand rests on "they bring the food we can't get elsewhere." |
| **Passion** | "Pounds are the heartbeat"; "people eat"; drivers' 3.4-year tenure; agencies' "like family" | **Getting good food to people while it is still good.** The passion is for people eating, not for pounds. |

**Denominator:** **usable perishable pounds delivered per route-hour.** "Usable" means not composted at the agency. "Perishable" means protein, dairy and fresh produce.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The cold chain is why Freshway gives, why agencies value Larder Line, and what Teddy is proudest of.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: the café, composting, gleaning, backpack operations, and chasing bread volume.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It tells the founder that pounds are not the heartbeat and tells the chair no.

**Confirmation tests (90 days):**
1. **Uniqueness:** confirm no other Corbridge rescue group runs refrigerated meat and dairy pickup.
2. **Mix:** cold-chain items are a meaningful share of pounds `[ASSUMPTION: unknown today]`. If they are under about 15%, rethink the concept.
3. **Demand:** at least half of a 10-agency sample says what Agnes said: more dairy and protein, less bread.
4. **Supply:** perishable surplus remains available after the Freshway app, from Freshway's end-of-day leftovers and from new sources. If it doesn't, the concept moves to "perishable logistics for whatever surplus exists" and the denominator stays the same.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  1. Reliable, same-day, temperature-safe pickups
            ↓
  2. Donors hand over perishables; new sources open
            ↓      ◄── WEAKEST LINK (Freshway 44%, markdown app)
  3. More usable perishable lbs per route-hour   ← denominator
            ↓
  4. Agencies get what they need, use it, report households
            ↓      ◄── second-weakest (nothing measured past pickup)
  5. Proof of impact → funders renew, volunteers stay
            ↓
  6. Money + long-tenure drivers sustain the cold chain
            ↺ back to 1
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Link 2 → 3:** diagnose the Freshway drop and negotiate a post-app handoff: a pickup timed after the markdown window that collects whatever the app didn't sell. Open two or three new perishable sources.
2. **Link 4 → 5:** weigh at drop-off and run a monthly check-in with agencies. Start with St. Brennan's.

**Doom-loop check:** early signs are present but there is no full loop yet. Since Year −2, Larder Line has added three programs, and it now faces a café, a third van and an "always yes" stance, all without anyone understanding the supply shift. That pattern is a change of direction driven by reaction, which is how a doom loop starts. The flywheel itself still closes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't apply for the $150k café grant as a Larder Line program.** The comparable café broke even only in Year 3, which is exactly when the rent-free kitchen ends. The café needs predictable ingredients, while rescue supply is variable and currently shrinking. It would also take the ED's and chair's attention away during the Freshway negotiation. Offer to supply it instead.
2. **Don't buy the third van this year.** Fix van #2 for $6,800 and recover the compost van-day first.
3. **Close the Monday composting route.** Hand it to the compost partner.
4. **End the gleaning crew.** Produce is not the gap.
5. **Hand off the backpack pilot.** That frees two volunteer drivers every Friday.
6. **Stop leading with pounds at pickup.** Report usable pounds and households, and stop leaving the households field blank.
7. **Stop chasing volume agencies can't use.** That means bread beyond demand and produce beyond what an agency can move.
8. **Stop saying yes on the spot.** Every new request gets one question: does it raise usable perishable pounds per route-hour?

**Faith statement:** *Corbridge will keep producing perishable surplus, and Larder Line is the only group that can carry it cold. If it follows the food instead of the pounds, it prevails.*

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** good food reaches neighbors while it is still good, not the landfill.

**Core values:**
1. Food safety and freshness come first. The cold chain is never broken.
2. We show up when we said we would, and we text first.
3. Volunteers are the organization, not its labor pool.
4. Honest numbers, including the unflattering ones.
5. Donors and funders trust us, and we earn that trust every pickup.

**BHAG (10-year):** *By Year 10, no edible protein or dairy surplus in Corbridge goes to waste, because every grocer, distributor and processor has a same-day route to an agency that will use it.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A hedgehog test for every new request
- A donor-concentration rule: no donor supplies more than 35% of pounds
- Monthly agency feedback and drop-off weighing
- A written post-app protocol with every grocery donor
- A named deputy ED, with donor relationships documented outside Teddy's head
- An annual stop-doing review at the board retreat
