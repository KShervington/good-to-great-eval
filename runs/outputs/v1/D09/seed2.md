# Larder Line: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Larder Line is Corbridge's cold chain for donated food. It gets perishable protein, dairy and produce to the agencies that can use them, the same day, and it counts success in food eaten rather than pounds lifted.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Freshway Eastside pounds per pickup fell 36% (412 → 263) after the markdown app arrived there. The app goes chainwide next year, and Freshway supplies 44% of all pounds. Waiting for the Route 9 road to reopen in spring would mean learning the answer just as the chainwide rollout hits.

**Weakest flywheel link:** proof of use. Larder Line has no data on food eaten or households reached, and agencies compost surplus without anyone tracking it.

**Stop:** the café grant, the third van (fix van #2 instead), and the composting van route.

**Numbers that would change this:** Eastside pickup weights compared against arrival time and product mix; Freshway's rollout date; the dairy and meat share of pounds; how much food agencies throw away.

Greatness potential: 30/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larder Line is not bigger. It is the organization that makes sure no rescuable perishable food in Corbridge goes to waste while a nearby household needs it. It runs a narrow, reliable cold chain: trained long-tenure drivers, refrigerated vans and same-day routes. That cold chain is matched to what each agency can actually move that week. Its donor base no longer depends on one chain's goodwill. It works with grocers' markdown tools, collecting what the app doesn't sell, instead of competing with them. It reports pounds used and households reached as well as pounds lifted, so funders, donors and the county see the same truth the board sees. Programs that don't feed the cold chain have been handed to better-suited owners. It would leave a real hole if it vanished, and it would keep running if its founder stepped back.

## 3. The flywheel

```
  (1) Trained, long-tenure drivers + reefer vans (cold-chain capacity)
        │
        ▼
  (2) Donors trust LL with perishables (dairy, protein, produce),
      including post-markdown leftovers
        │
        ▼
  (3) Same-day matching to what each agency can actually move
        │
        ▼
  (4) Food eaten: used perishable pounds per route-hour; households reached
        │
        ▼
  (5) Proof: impact data for funders + diversion data for donors'
      sustainability reports
        │
        ▼
  (6) Money, volunteers and donor loyalty ──► back to (1)
```

**Weakest link:** the step from (3) to (5). Larder Line counts pounds at pickup and stops there. St. Brennan's composts surplus produce, and "nobody asks." The county field for unduplicated households was left blank. As a result, the funder half of the loop runs on a number that funders are starting to discount.

**Second-weakest link:** step (2). One chain supplies 44% of pounds, and that chain now has a tool that sells off the food Larder Line used to collect.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Secure step (2):** negotiate a post-markdown pickup slot with Freshway, and start signing up non-Freshway cold-chain donors.
2. **Build step (4):** measure use and households at 5 sentinel agencies, and shift the mix toward what agencies ask for (more dairy, less bread).

**Doom-loop check:** several signs are present.
- Four new directions in two years (gleaning, backpacks, composting, café).
- A big capital purchase (the third van) proposed as the answer to a problem that may be shrinking supply.
- The Eastside dip explained by construction without testing the other explanation.
- New programs justified by "funders love that story" rather than by the loop.

The loop itself closes, so the gate passes. The doom-loop behavior goes on the risk register.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** good food reaches people instead of the waste stream.

**Core values:**
1. Food is respected: same day, cold when it needs to be.
2. Agencies say what they need, and Larder Line listens.
3. Drivers are the organization.
4. The numbers tell the truth, including the bad ones.
5. Reliability comes before range.

**BHAG (20-year):** By Year 20, no rescuable perishable food from any Corbridge grocer, wholesaler or distributor is thrown away while a household nearby needs it.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Written donor agreements, so supply doesn't depend on Teddy's relationships.
- A route playbook and driver curriculum owned by operations.
- A use-and-households dashboard the board reviews independently of the ED.
- A deputy ED.
- An annual board review of the stop-doing list.
