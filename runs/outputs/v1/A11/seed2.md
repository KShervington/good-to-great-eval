# Good to Great: Lindqvist Company

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Lindqvist wins by being the #1 or #2 combination food-and-drug store in each region it serves, and earns its money as profit per household in those regions.

Greatness potential: 34/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Lindqvist does not try to be everywhere or to own everything. It runs dense networks in a limited set of regions where it is the #1 or #2 grocer. In each region its stores are large and put food and pharmacy under one roof, so a household can do its weekly shop and its health needs in one trip. It treats every scanned item as a customer's "vote" and uses that data faster and better than any rival to set assortment, prices and store design. Its plants exist only where they give the store brand a real cost or quality edge, and its quality standard is beyond reproach. It does not try to beat the largest chain on price in every market. It beats it on convenience and relevance in the markets it has chosen. Its main measure is profit per household in each region it serves.

**Hedgehog sentence:** *Be the #1 or #2 food-and-drug store in every region we serve, and grow profit per household there.*

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Partly. Lindqvist grew by replacing small stores with bigger formats, and its exits came where competitors were stronger locally.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: family centers, unneeded plants, weak markets, small-store remodels and acquisitions.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It implies more market exits and a smaller manufacturing arm.

**What would confirm it:**
- Test combination stores beat conventional stores on profit per square foot and on trips per household.
- Markets where Lindqvist is #1 or #2 earn clearly more than its weaker markets.
- Store-brand plants beat outside suppliers on landed cost.

**Current push:**
1. Prove the test-store economics.
2. Move capital out of weak markets and surplus plants and into converting stores in the strongest regions.

**Weakest link:** the step from "large combo stores" to "more trips per household" has not been demonstrated with numbers.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop holding markets where Lindqvist cannot plausibly reach #1 or #2. Sell or swap those stores.
2. Stop building or remodeling small conventional stores. Convert or close them.
3. Stop matching Rival A's discount prices across every market. Price to defend the core regions only.
4. Stop expanding the family-center general-merchandise format.
5. Stop running plants that do not beat outside supply on cost or quality. No new mega-plants until the review is complete.
6. Stop pursuing growth through acquisition. The antitrust consent limits it anyway, and organic density is the better path.
7. Stop any processed-product formulation that would not survive a public inspection.
