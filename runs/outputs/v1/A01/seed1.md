# Halvorsen-Pike Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Halvorsen-Pike will be the best in the world at paper-based consumer products, turning cellulose fiber into household brands people call by name, and it will judge every business by **profit per consumer brand**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** product innovation in absorbent products. The leaking diaper and the missing nonwovens capability show it.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop funding commodity mills by tradition. Prepare to sell them.
2. Stop the aircraft charter "profit center". Sell most of the fleet.
3. Stop spreading capital evenly across all businesses.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the company cannot both fight Rival P head-on and keep modernizing commodity mills. Funding both means losing both.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** return on capital by segment, brand share trends, and category growth rates. If consumer returns don't clearly beat mill returns, the Hedgehog Concept fails.

`Greatness potential: 35/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halvorsen-Pike is a focused consumer company that still makes things from paper. The paper ends up in the household, not on a printing press. It owns a few category-defining brands in tissue, towels, feminine care and (if the product can be fixed) diapers. It ranks first or a strong second in each category it chooses, and it competes on product innovation that combines its deep cellulose expertise with newly acquired nonwovens capability. Capital goes only to businesses that raise profit per consumer brand. The mills and timberlands that once defined it have been sold, and the proceeds were invested in the brands. Its returns beat the market for years, not just for one cycle. It outlasts the CEO who started the turn because the choices are built into its capital rules, its people and its identity.

**Hedgehog sentence:** Be the best in the world at paper-based consumer products, and run the business on profit per consumer brand.

**What it rules out:** coated and newsprint papers, specialty paper roll-ups, timber as an operating business, aviation services, and consumer categories that are not fiber-based.

**Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The company's most enduring win, facial tissue, is exactly this intersection.

**Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means selling the namesake mill and fighting the world's best consumer marketer.

**Status:** working hypothesis. It is confirmed if:
1. Segment return on capital shows consumer businesses clearly beat the mills across the cycle.
2. Facial tissue and toilet tissue hold share against Rival P's push.
3. An improved diaper stops losing share within roughly 18–24 months. `[ASSUMPTION on timing]`

## 3. The flywheel

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                              │
 [1] Fiber + nonwoven innovation ──►  [2] Better household products
                                       (tissue, towel, fem care, diaper)
                                              │
                                              ▼
 [5] Profit reinvested in R&D ◄── [4] Profit per consumer brand ◄── [3] Brand advertising +
     and brand advertising            rises (share + price premium)     distribution win shelf & share
```

Each turn builds on the last. Better products earn share. Share earns profit per brand. That profit funds the next round of innovation and advertising.

Mill sale proceeds are a **one-time capital reallocation, not a flywheel push**. They should be spent through the loop, not used as a substitute for it.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Fix the diaper. Find the root cause of the leakage, then close the gap with a better product, not with more advertising.
2. Defend toilet tissue and facial tissue against Rival P's national push. Spend on advertising and distribution there before chasing new categories.

| Reaction without understanding | Possible. Matching Rival P's spending dollar for dollar without first fixing the product would be exactly this. |
| Inconsistency | Possible. Wavering on the mill sale after announcing it. |

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Commodity mill modernization capital beyond safety and maintenance.** Prepare to sell the coated, newsprint and pulp mills, including the namesake mill.
2. **Allocating capital by tradition.** Every dollar must answer one question: does it raise profit per consumer brand?
3. **The aircraft charter and maintenance "profit center".** Sell most of the fleet.
4. **Specialty, envelope and cigarette paper acquisitions.** Stop adding to them and divest the ones already held.
5. **Holding timberland as an operating identity.** Monetize the prime western acreage and put the proceeds into brands. `[ASSUMPTION: the land carries large unrealized value]`
6. **Fighting Rival P everywhere at once.** Pick the categories where the product can win. Don't launch new consumer categories until tissue and the diaper are fixed.
7. **Using advertising to cover product weakness.** Don't spend heavily behind a diaper that leaks.
