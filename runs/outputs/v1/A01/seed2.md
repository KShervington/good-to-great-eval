# Halvorsen-Pike Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halvorsen-Pike will be the best in the world at branded, fiber-based personal-care paper products, and will judge every decision by profit per consumer brand.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** product innovation. Diapers lost on product improvement, and the company has no expertise in synthetic nonwovens.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop funding commodity printing-paper and newsprint mills. Sell them, the namesake mill included if it is one of them.
2. Stop allocating capital by tradition across every business.
3. Kill the air-fleet charter plan.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the company cannot fund mill modernization and a fight with Rival P at the same time. Meanwhile capital still flows by default to mills that cannot be best in the world.

**Missing numbers that could change the answer:** segment profit and return on capital (especially specialty papers and the Canadian joint venture), brand-level shares, and category growth rates.

Greatness potential: 35/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halvorsen-Pike is a consumer products company with paper in its DNA, not a mill company that happens to sell tissue. It has sold its commodity printing-paper and newsprint mills, its Canadian joint-venture stake and its surplus timberland. It has put that capital into brands, product research and distribution. It wins against Rival P and Rival S in fiber-based personal care by improving products faster than they do, and its category-defining facial-tissue brand is the beachhead and the proof. Every capital request answers one question: does it raise profit per consumer brand? Leadership comes from a bench of consumer operators, so the company does not depend on a single person, which matters given the CEO's health. Its returns beat the market for a decade or more, and the strategy keeps working after the people who made the original decision are gone.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Low-cost commodity papermaking. (b) Consumer paper brands. (c) Cellulose-fiber science. (d) Running a diversified paper conglomerate. | **(b) + (c): fiber-based personal-care products sold under strong brands.** It invented facial tissue and still leads it. **It cannot be best at:** commodity printing paper or newsprint (Rival S is bigger and more profitable, and pricing is cyclical), aviation services, specialty envelope or cigarette paper as a strategic core, or synthetic nonwovens (yet). |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per ton. Profit per mill. Profit per consumer brand. Profit per household. | **Profit per consumer brand.** Consumer paper rewards brand, distribution and innovation. Commodity tons reward only cost parity, and parity takes endless capital. |
| **Passion** | Mill craft and town identity. Fiber science. Products people use every day. | **[ASSUMPTION] Making everyday products that people trust, built on fiber know-how.** This is the weakest circle. Today's passion is tied to the mills, so the bridge has to be built deliberately. |

**Sentence:** *Best in the world at branded, fiber-based personal-care paper products, measured by profit per consumer brand.*

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The company's most distinctive success is inventing and still leading facial tissue.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: the mills, the air fleet, specialty paper acquisitions and the land bank.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means giving up the company's identity and fighting Rival P head-on.

**Confirmation tests (evidence that would turn this into PASS):**
1. Segment return on capital shows consumer businesses earning well above the commodity mills across a full cycle.
2. Facial-tissue share holds while Rival P pushes into toilet tissue.
3. The relaunched diaper reaches leakage parity with Rival P's product in blind consumer tests within 12–18 months.
4. Profit per consumer brand rises for two consecutive years after reinvestment.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Commodity printing paper and newsprint:** stop the modernization capital and sell the mills, including the namesake mill if it makes those grades `[ASSUMPTION: the dossier does not say what the namesake mill produces]`. Identity is not a reason to keep a business.
2. **Canadian newsprint joint venture:** exit the stake.
3. **Pulp and sawmills beyond internal fiber need:** sell them `[ASSUMPTION: some pulp capacity or supply contracts are needed for tissue]`.
4. **Surplus timberland, including the 300,000+ western acres:** sell to fund the consumer push. Land is not the business.
5. **Air-fleet charter and maintenance "profit center":** kill the proposal and shrink the fleet.
6. **Acquiring specialty, envelope and cigarette paper businesses:** stop buying. Divest or harvest once segment returns are visible.
7. **Allocating capital by tradition or seniority:** replace it with a hedgehog test on every request.
8. **Half-funded consumer products:** diapers get full commitment with a parity deadline, or they are exited. No middle path.
