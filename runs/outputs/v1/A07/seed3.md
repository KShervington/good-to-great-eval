# Keystone Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Keystone has a good franchise but a broken engine.** It becomes great only if it stops betting on interest rates and earns its living by pricing mortgage credit risk.

- **Hedgehog:** Be the best in the world at understanding and pricing the credit risk in home mortgages, pass interest-rate risk to capital markets, and use that skill to safely widen homeownership.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** credit-risk measurement. There is no loss data by loan type, and guarantee volume is rising.
- **Stop:** (1) funding long fixed-rate loans with short-term debt; (2) chasing volume or portfolio size; (3) expanding apartment lending before loss data exists.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the interest margin has been negative since Year −3, and solvency at market value is unknown.
- **Numbers that would change this:** net worth at market value, the duration gap, and credit losses by loan type.

Greatness potential: 33/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone no longer makes money by borrowing short and lending long. It moves interest-rate risk off its own balance sheet through mortgage-backed securities, adjustable-rate purchases and matched funding. It keeps the one risk it can understand better than anyone: whether American households will repay their mortgages. Its loan-level data across lenders, regions and loan types is the deepest in the country. It charges each risk tier a fee that covers expected losses and pays a fair return on the capital set aside for that tier.

That skill lets it serve moderate- and lower-income borrowers through product design and accurate pricing rather than hidden subsidy. The mission and the shareholder return then point the same way. It holds real capital against its risks and treats the Treasury backstop as a privilege to protect, not a cushion to lean on. It opens its books to overseers before they ask.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Keystone earns its living by being the best in the world at understanding and pricing the credit risk in home mortgages, not by betting on interest rates, so that it can safely widen access to homeownership.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|

| **Economic engine** | Profit per mortgage (rewards volume). Profit per dollar of assets (rewards leverage and carry). Profit per unit of risk taken (kept). | **Profit per unit of mortgage risk**: fee or spread, minus expected loss, divided by the capital held for that risk |
| **Passion** | CEO's long concern with the gap between households that can and cannot afford a home. The charter's housing mission. Staff identification with the mission `[ASSUMPTION]`. | **Widening homeownership** |

**Confirmation tests:**

- **Does it explain the past?** Yes. The current losses came from interest-rate risk, not credit risk. Keystone's historic wins came from being the dependable buyer when credit tightened.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out carry trades on the portfolio, rate bets and volume targets.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. When rates fall, it means giving up easy portfolio income. It also means telling political sponsors that some lending will cost more.

**What would confirm it:** two or more years, including at least one regional downturn, in which credit losses for each risk tier stay within what was priced, and profit per unit of risk stays positive.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Loan-level data & credit models (deepest in the market)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    2. Accurate price per risk tier (guarantee fees, purchase spreads)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    3. Lenders route more loans; investors trust Keystone securities
   │       (liquidity ↑, funding cost ↓)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    4. Profit per unit of risk ↑  →  retained capital ↑
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    5. Capacity to serve harder-to-serve borrowers responsibly
   │       → public trust & political license
   │            │
   └────────────┘  (more loans across more risk tiers = more data)
```

**Current push:**

- **Link 1 to link 2, the weakest link.** Build loss data by loan type and start pricing guarantees by risk tier.
- **Defense.** Close the duration gap so that interest-rate losses stop draining capital out of link 4.

**Doom-loop check:** Keystone has launched four initiatives in about three years: the $10B sale, adjustable-rate purchases, mortgage-backed securities and the swap program, and apartment lending.

- The first three follow one logic: shed interest-rate risk. That is coherent.
- Apartment lending follows a different logic. It adds a new credit risk the company cannot yet measure. This is the doom-loop warning sign.
- There is a second danger. A return of falling rates would revive the old carry model. Switching back would be the classic new-direction-after-new-direction trap.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt. Every new purchase gets matched funding or is securitized.
2. **Stop** measuring success by portfolio size or purchase volume.
3. **Stop** expanding apartment lending until there are two or more years of loss data. Cap it at a fixed share of capital `[ASSUMPTION: e.g., 5%]`.
4. **Stop** charging one guarantee fee regardless of risk. Price by tier.
5. **Stop** counting the Treasury line as capital in planning or in investor messaging.
6. **Stop** giving overseers selective data. Disclose by default.
7. **Stop** pursuing the mission through underpriced loans. Serve it through product design, underwriting skill and transparent, explicitly funded programs.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**

- A monthly duration-gap report to the board.
- A risk price on every new product before launch.
- A capital requirement based on stress tests, not on the regulatory minimum.

**Freedom within a framework:**

- **The framework:** credit risk only, priced and capitalized.
- **Where business units have autonomy:** product design, lender relationships and securities structure, all inside that frame.

**Bureaucracy check:** any approval layer that exists because no one owns interest-rate risk should be removed once the capital-markets seat is filled.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** to connect American households to the world's capital at the lowest *sustainable* cost, so that more of them can own homes.

**Core values:**

1. Price every risk and hide none.
2. The household's home outlasts our quarter.
3. Tell overseers the whole truth.
4. Our privileges are a public trust, not our skill.

**BHAG (20 years):** make the mortgage market safe enough that every household able to sustain a home can get financed, without Keystone ever drawing on the Treasury line.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**

- An independent risk function that reports to the board.
- Capital set by stress tests at market value.
- Guarantee fees priced by risk tier.
- Pay tied to profit per unit of risk, not to earnings-per-share growth or volume.
- Overseers' access to data by default.
