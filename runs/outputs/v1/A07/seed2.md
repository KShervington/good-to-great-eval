# Keystone Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## Verdict

**A good franchise with a broken balance sheet. Fix the rate mismatch first, then narrow to one thing.**

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Be the best in the world at measuring, pricing and managing residential mortgage risk. Earn a profit on every unit of risk taken, and use that to widen who can own a home.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** spread → capital. The old fixed-rate book still bleeds, so no capital is compounding yet.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Holding long fixed-rate loans on short-term funding.
  2. Buying volume the price doesn't cover.
  3. Growing guarantees faster than capital and loss data.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** borrowing costs exceed portfolio yield, and have since Year −3. Until that gap closes, losses fund every other move.
- **Numbers that would change this answer:** market-value net worth, portfolio size, yield and cost of funds, recent net income, and credit-loss rates by loan type.

Greatness potential: 40/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone is the country's most trusted manager of mortgage risk, not its biggest mortgage holder.

- **Rate risk is out of the holding book.** It holds only mortgages that are match-funded or hedged, so a rate spike no longer threatens the company.
- **Credit risk is priced loan by loan.** It guarantees securities that pass that risk on to investors, using loss data no one else has.
- **Lenders come first to Keystone in every cycle,** because it is the buyer that stays in the market during credit squeezes.
- **Its mission is delivered through designed products.** It reaches lower-income and higher-risk borrowers through products built and priced for that risk, not through loosened standards.
- **Its regulator and the audit office see everything.** Its profits do not depend on the Treasury backstop being used.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Working hypothesis:** *Keystone will be the best in the world at managing residential mortgage risk. It will earn a profit on every unit of risk it carries, in service of opening home ownership to more households.*

| **Economic engine** | Profit per mortgage held. Profit per dollar of volume. Profit per lender relationship. **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. Keystone has been the largest buyer during credit squeezes, which is risk-bearing at the moment others pull back. It also explains the current losses: Keystone bore rate risk without pricing or managing it.

**Does it rule things out?** Yes:
- Unhedged portfolio growth.
- Volume for its own sake.
- Products whose losses it cannot measure.
- Lobbying to widen its scope.

**Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means shrinking or hedging the identity Keystone has had for decades as a buyer-and-holder.

**Evidence that would confirm it within 12–24 months:**
- New business shows a positive risk-adjusted spread every quarter, across rate moves.
- Guarantee-fee income grows while credit losses stay within the levels priced in.
- Interest-rate sensitivity of net worth falls toward a defined limit.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Measure risk loan by loan (rate + credit, with Keystone's own loss data)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    2. Price and structure each purchase or guarantee to earn
   │       profit per unit of risk (match-fund, hedge, or sell the rate risk via MBS)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    3. Positive spread + guarantee fees          ◄── WEAKEST LINK TODAY
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    4. Capital and credibility (net worth, ratings, regulator trust)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    5. Lenders rely on Keystone in every cycle → more volume, more product types
   │       (including designed products for lower-income borrowers)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   └─── 6. More loan-performance data across more cycles and regions
```

**The current push:**
1. Close the rate mismatch on the legacy book through sales, hedges and longer-dated funding, and set a hard sensitivity limit.
2. Make every new purchase and guarantee clear a priced risk hurdle. The higher-rate new purchases already earn a positive margin, so this rule protects what already works.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Warning signs already visible.** Four big moves have arrived in roughly three years: the $10B sale, adjustable-rate mortgages, mortgage-backed securities (MBS) with swaps, and apartment loans. Each is defensible. Together they could read as new direction after new direction unless one idea ties them together. The Hedgehog Concept is that idea.
- **Signs to watch.** Waiting for rates to fall as the rescue plan. A big acquisition or launch staged to "restore earnings."

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop holding long fixed-rate mortgages on shorter-term funding without a hedge or match.
2. Stop buying volume, or chasing the "largest buyer" title, when the price doesn't cover the risk.
3. Stop growing guaranteed securities faster than capital and credit-loss data can support.
4. Stop treating the Treasury backstop and top credit rating as a substitute for capital.
5. Stop pursuing the housing mission by loosening underwriting. Pursue it through products designed and priced for higher-risk borrowers.
6. Stop selective disclosure. Give the audit office and regulator the data they say they lack.
7. Stop expanding into new loan types, apartment loans included, before their losses can be measured. Apartment loans stay **on probation**.
8. Stop planning around a hoped-for fall in rates.
