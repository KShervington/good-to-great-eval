# Good to Great: Keystone Mortgage Association

## Verdict

**The franchise is good but the engine is broken. It is fixable, but only if Keystone refuses what its government backstop makes easy.**

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Keystone will be the best in the world at moving residential mortgage risk from lenders into the capital markets at the lowest sound cost. It will measure itself by profit per unit of mortgage risk carried, and it will do this to keep home-buying money flowing to every household that can carry a loan.

**Weakest flywheel link:** risk discipline. The old fixed-rate book is still funded short, and the new credit guarantees are not priced from loss data.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Funding long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter debt.
2. Judging success by portfolio size.
3. Treating the Treasury backstop as if it were capital.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Keystone is still losing money, and nobody has published its net worth at market value. Marked to market, it may be insolvent `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Numbers that would change this answer:** market-value net worth, the duration gap, portfolio yield against cost of funds, and credit losses by loan type.

Greatness potential: 40/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone makes money on mortgage risk it understands and has priced. It does not make money by guessing where interest rates will go. Every mortgage it holds is funded with debt of matching life, or it is passed through to investors as a guaranteed security. Every guarantee carries a fee set from real loss data by loan type. Lenders choose it because its cash and securities are the cheapest and most dependable route to new lending in every cycle, including credit squeezes. It serves lower-income and higher-risk borrowers through an explicit, budgeted program, not by hiding unpriced risk in the main book. It holds capital as if the Treasury backstop did not exist. Its books are open to its regulator and to the audit office. The three tests of great hold:
- **Superior performance:** sustained, risk-adjusted returns.
- **Distinctive impact:** mortgage money stays available where and when private buyers pull back.
- **Lasting endurance:** the risk and capital mechanisms work no matter who is CEO or which party appoints the five directors.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Holding mortgages (no: this is what is losing money). Originating loans (no: the charter forbids it and lenders do it). Being the biggest buyer (no: size is not skill). Turning mortgages into priced, funded, tradable capital-markets instruments (yes). | **Converting residential mortgages into capital-markets instruments at the lowest sound funding cost, and pricing their rate and credit risk better than anyone.** Its scale, near-government funding rate, nationwide lender network and MBS swap program are all hard to copy. |
| **Cannot be best at** | Retail lending, non-residential real estate, out-guessing rate cycles, apartment lending (no loss history yet) | These are ruled out. Apartment loans stay at pilot size. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per mortgage purchased. Profit per dollar of portfolio. Profit per unit of mortgage risk (interest-rate plus credit). | **Profit per unit of mortgage risk carried.** Profit per dollar or per loan rewards growth. Profit per unit of risk rewards discipline and makes the cheap-funding temptation visible. |
| **Passion** | Shareholder returns. Market leadership. Closing the gap between households that can and cannot afford a home (the CEO's stated reason for doing this work). | **Expanding sound homeownership.** `[ASSUMPTION]` We assume staff share the CEO's mission. This is untested. |

**Confirmation tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Partly. Keystone was most valuable to the market when it supplied liquidity in credit squeezes. It was hurt worst when it carried unpriced rate risk.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out unmatched holding, growth for its own sake, unpriced mission loans and pushing beyond the charter.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It caps cheap-funded portfolio growth, which is the easiest profit available. It also forces the CEO's mission into a priced, budgeted box.

**Evidence that would confirm it, within 12–24 months:**
- New business shows a positive risk-adjusted spread.
- The guarantee fee covers actual credit losses by loan type.
- Lender volume grows without the portfolio's share of risk growing.

**Core tension to manage:** the passion circle (serving higher-risk borrowers) and the denominator (profit per unit of risk) pull against each other. They meet only when mission lending is priced and budgeted explicitly.

```
       and matched funding          ◄── WEAKEST LINK     │

                   ▼                                     │
   (5) Profit per unit of mortgage risk                  │
       + households served (incl. priced mission program)│
                   │                                     │
                   ▼                                     │
   (6) Capital, credibility with investors & overseers ──┘

```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Stop the bleed at link 4.** Match-fund or securitize all new purchases. Run down or hedge the old fixed-rate book against a published market-value target.
2. **Price credit at link 4.** Build loss data by loan type and set guarantee fees from it before the guarantee book grows further.

**Doom-loop check:** Four new initiatives have started in about three years: the mortgage sale, adjustable-rate purchases, MBS and swaps, and apartment loans. Three of them fit the Hedgehog Concept. Apartment lending is a new direction with no loss data, so treat it as a pilot, not a growth line. The loop's other danger is the backstop. It makes portfolio growth look like flywheel momentum when it is really leverage. The loop closes, so the verdict is PASS, with the risk step as the weakest link.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter-term debt. Every new holding must be duration-matched or passed through.
2. Stop measuring success by portfolio size, purchase volume or market share. Measure profit per unit of risk.
3. Stop treating the Treasury backstop as capital. Hold capital as if the backstop did not exist.
4. Stop taking credit risk without a fee priced to it. Mission loans go only through an explicit, budgeted program.
5. Stop giving overseers only the data Keystone chooses to provide.
6. Stop growing apartment lending until it has its own loss history.
7. Stop acting as buyer of last resort in credit squeezes at any price. Provide liquidity, but priced liquidity.
8. Don't lobby to widen the charter beyond residential mortgages. The limit is useful discipline.

**Charisma check:** there is no evidence either way. The CEO's political and legal background makes persuasion a likely strength, so the board should test whether bad news reaches it unfiltered.

**Faith statement:** No private rival can copy Keystone's charter, funding position and place in housing finance. If it stops betting on rates and prices every risk it takes, it will prevail.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make sure money for buying a home is there in every market and every cycle, including for households the market overlooks.

**Core values:**
1. Earn it. Profit comes from priced risk, never from a free ride.
2. Open books, so there are no surprises for the board, the regulator or the auditors.
3. Match what we owe to what we own.
4. Widen homeownership responsibly.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** Become the institution that keeps mortgage money flowing through every rate shock and housing downturn, earning top-quartile risk-adjusted returns without ever drawing on the Treasury backstop.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A capital standard set as if there were no backstop.
- Mandatory rate-shock and national-housing-downturn stress tests reported to the board.
- Guarantee fees set by a model from loss data.
- A duration-gap limit written into board policy.
- Voluntary full access for the regulator and the audit office.
- Executive pay tied to risk-adjusted profit, not to earnings growth or portfolio size.
- A named succession plan for the CEO and the heads of risk.

Six of the eleven gates are CONDITIONAL. That is more than half, and it traces to two root problems:
- **Missing numbers.** Without market-value net worth, the duration gap and loss rates, the core verdicts can't be confirmed.
- **The structure itself.** A borrowed government backstop combined with a dual mandate makes risky growth cheap and politically rewarded.

**Decline risks (Gate 10):**

| Stage | How Keystone could slide | Early warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hubris | After the turnaround, the funding edge from the backstop gets mistaken for management skill | Returns credited to strategy alone; critics and overseers dismissed |
| 2. Undisciplined pursuit of more | Cheap agency debt makes it tempting to grow the retained portfolio and push into riskier loans to hit earnings or mission targets | Portfolio or guarantees growing faster than capital; pay tied to EPS growth |
| 3. Denial of risk | All risk sits in one asset class, capital is thin, and oversight is resisted; a national housing downturn is assumed away | Falling capital ratios; lobbying against capital rules; stress tests that exclude a nationwide price decline |
| 4. Grasping for salvation | Accounting maneuvers, earnings smoothing or political rescue instead of fixing the risk | Restatements; aggressive accounting choices; leaning on political allies |
| 5. Capitulation | Drawing on the Treasury, or government takeover | Any need to draw the Treasury line |

Stages 2 and 3 are the most likely path, because the structure rewards them. This is the main reason greatness potential is held at 40 even though the turnaround logic is sound.
