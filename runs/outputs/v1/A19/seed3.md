# Good to Great: Marlowe Drug Co.

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Marlowe will be the most convenient drugstore in America for everyday health and beauty needs, and it will measure itself by **profit per customer visit**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** getting from more visits to more profit per visit. Sales have more than doubled, but earnings per share have not moved.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop opening restaurants. Pause the 22 planned units.
2. Stop investing in the discount department stores and prepare an exit.
3. Drop "growth across all divisions" as the strategy. Make no more acquisitions outside drugstores.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Sales grew from $432.5M to $996.6M in eight years, while EPS went from $1.30 to $1.15 reported (about $2.2 before the LIFO change). The company cannot show which division earns its keep.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by division
- Profit per customer visit by store
- Regional market share against Rival E

Greatness potential: 45/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Marlowe is a drugstore company and nothing else. It runs a dense network of well-located, self-service drugstores, anchored by the pharmacist's counter and a strong line of house-brand products. Each store sits where customers already pass, so they come often, buy a little each time, and leave satisfied. Every capital decision is tested against one question: does it raise profit per customer visit? Restaurants, discount department stores, the travel agency and the commodity plants have been sold or wound down. The management attention and capital they consumed now go into store density, store quality and pharmacy service. Earnings grow faster than sales year after year. The company's edge comes from disciplined repetition of the same loop, not from any single family leader or acquisition.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Drugstore operations at scale (about twice the volume of the nearest drugstore competitor); house-brand health and beauty products (500+ SKUs, 1,800 agency outlets); pharmacist-led service; store pruning and opening discipline (51 closed, 94 opened in two years) | **Convenient, high-frequency drugstore retail with strong house brands.** |
| **Cannot be best at** | Family restaurants, discount general merchandise, travel, ice cream, coffee roasting, plastic containers, Mexican retail | Every non-drug business. In none of them does Marlowe have scale or a distinctive advantage. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | Profit per store; profit per square foot; **profit per customer visit**; profit per prescription | **Profit per customer visit** `[ASSUMPTION]`. Drugstore economics depend on frequent, small, convenience-driven trips, and this measure rewards location, house-brand mix, pharmacy and low shrink all at once. Profit per square foot is the fallback. |
| **Passion** | Pharmacist founder's heritage; management's own words: "community centers for health and beauty service," and "holds fast to its basic identity as drugstores" | **Being the neighborhood health-and-beauty store.** |

**Sentence:** *Marlowe is the best in America at being the closest, most convenient drugstore for everyday health and beauty needs, and it grows by raising profit per customer visit.*

**Tests**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Self-service drugstores became the core of the chain, house brands extended its reach, and the drug identity has held for 75 years.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: restaurants, discount stores, travel, commodity manufacturing, and probably the in-store lunch counters over time.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. Soda fountains and lunch counters are part of the founding story, and management has just called the restaurant outlook "positive."

**What would confirm it (working hypothesis):**
1. A divisional profit and loss statement shows drugstores earning a higher return on capital than restaurants and discount stores. `[ASSUMPTION, untested]`
2. Store-level data shows that profit per visit varies widely, and that location and convenience explain much of that variation.
3. Stores in dense clusters outperform isolated stores.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Convenient, dense store locations
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    2. More frequent customer visits (pharmacy + everyday needs)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    3. Higher PROFIT PER CUSTOMER VISIT
   │       (house brands, pharmacy mix, low shrink, buying scale)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    4. Cash freed from non-core businesses + higher store profits
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    5. Reinvest only in sites/stores that pass the convenience test;
   │       prune marginal stores every year
   │            │
   └────────────┘   (density → purchasing leverage → brand → more visits)
```

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Measure link 3. Build profit per visit by store and rank all 616 stores. Right now the company cannot tell whether a new store or a new restaurant adds profit or only adds sales.
2. Feed link 4. Redirect capital and management time from restaurants and discount stores into the 32 planned drugstores, and put each of those through a convenience and site test.

**Weakest link:** the step from visits (link 2) to profit per visit (link 3). Net margin fell from 2.2% seven years ago to 1.5% two years ago, and it was 0.76% reported last year. Management names shrinkage, cost control and productivity as the fixes, and they belong exactly on this link.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs are present.**
- Stated growth "across all divisions."
- Acquisitions as momentum: the discount stores, the travel agency, 29 stores from another chain, the Mexico stake.
- A new family-restaurant chain launched while margins are falling.

**Counter-signal:** the steady closure of marginal drugstores is real flywheel discipline, and it should be kept.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Open no new restaurants.** Pause the 22 planned units pending divisional profit data. Start with the free-standing family restaurants.
2. **Stop capital spending on discount department stores** beyond safety and shrink control, and prepare a sale or wind-down.
3. **Stop using "growth across all divisions" as the strategy.** Growth is allowed only where profit per visit rises.
4. **Make no acquisitions outside drugstores**, including travel, food, or general merchandise.
5. **Stop expanding vertical integration** into commodities (ice cream, coffee, containers) that don't differentiate the drugstore.
6. **Make no new investment in the Mexico stake.** Hold it passively or sell it.
7. **Stop judging the year by sales.** The $1B milestone is not a success metric.
8. **Phase down in-store lunch counters** where their profit per square foot trails drugstore merchandise. `[contingent on data]` This is the uncomfortable item.
