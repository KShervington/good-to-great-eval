### Draft definition of great (for the next run)

1. **Superior performance:** families using Tessel miss fewer refills and doses than they would otherwise, and they keep using it through the long middle of caregiving, not just the crisis weeks.
2. **Distinctive impact:** if Tessel vanished, discharge teams and primary caregivers would lose the one thing that tells the whole family what is due and what is ready, without phone calls.
3. **Lasting endurance:** it works without your hands, beyond one pharmacy, and beyond you.

### What "good" looks like: three milestones

1. **Strangers stay.** At least 25 weekly active circles with no personal tie to you, and at least 35% of each month's new circles still active in week 8. This is the one that matters most.
2. **Refills without your hands.** Refill status reaches families automatically from at least two pharmacy sources. Those sources can be independent pharmacies sending exports that are parsed automatically, a chain connected through the aggregator, or both.
3. **Strangers pay.** At least 15 paying circles from outside your network, *or* an employer signs a paid pilot with written success criteria.

When all three hold, re-run the full framework. You'll then have the evidence for a real Hedgehog Concept.

## The employer question (held, not decided)

Graham's case has substance. At his quoted $3–5 per employee per month, an 1,800-person employer would pay roughly $5,400–9,000 a month, about 250–400 times your current monthly revenue. That assumes pricing across all employees, which is how such benefits are usually quoted per his framing. Selling to employers deserves a real test. Three facts keep it from being a decision now:

- **It changes who pays, not whether the product works.** Anonymized usage reporting would show the HR head the same 17% weekly activity you see today. An employer pilot needs milestone 1 just as much as consumers do.
- **The requested build is a different company.** A staffed concierge chat is a services business with headcount. Single sign-on, admin dashboards and reporting are months of solo work, aimed at a pilot that is conditional and "next spring."
- **Changing direction before proving the core** is the early shape of what Collins calls the doom loop: new direction after new direction, with no momentum behind any of them.

What to do instead: hold one discovery conversation (see Next 90 days). The questions to answer for the next run's Gate 5 are below.

- Is the economic engine *per circle* (consumer) or *per covered employee* (employer)?
- Is the strongest asset the app, the audience (*The Caregiver's Ledger*), or the discharge-moment channel?

## Next 90 days

All five actions aim at the three milestones. The owner of each is you unless noted.

1. **Find out why 34 circles went quiet.** Split the 41 circles by source (personal network, newsletter, forum, discharge card) and by whether any member is on Android. Call 10 quiet circles. If mixed-platform families are dropping off, give Android relatives a lightweight way in, such as SMS or a web link for reminders, before considering a native build.
2. **Take your hands off refill sync.** Automate the Monday export, so the email is parsed and loaded with no manual step. Ask your pharmacist whether two neighboring independent pharmacies would send the same export. Separately, escalate the aggregator application with a direct contact and a specific question about the timeline. This way refill sync has more than one path.
3. **Instrument the discharge channel.** Give the social worker's cards a unique sign-up code so you can measure that channel's activation and week-8 retention. If those numbers are strong, ask her to introduce you to one or two other discharge teams. *Co-owner: the social worker.*
4. **Run one employer discovery call, and build nothing for it.** Ask the HR head three things: what a pilot would have to show to be renewed; whether the company would pay for the pilot; and whether it could start without single sign-on and the concierge. *Intro: Graham.*
5. **Rewrite the accelerator application this week.** State the retention numbers plainly. Make the three milestones the use of funds, in place of "Android + chain integration + part-time developer." Lead with this point: the biggest risk is that families don't come back weekly, and here is how you'll prove they do. Accelerators tend to trust founders who name the real risk.
