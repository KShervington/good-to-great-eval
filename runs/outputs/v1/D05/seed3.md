# Good to Great: Tessel

## Verdict

**Tessel is not yet good. The run stops at Gate 1.** It isn't a bad product. The core just still runs on the founder's hands and the founder's network. Only 7 of 41 care circles are active weekly. Refill sync, the feature people say they'd pay for, depends on one pharmacist emailing a file that you load by hand every Monday.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** not drafted yet. It needs proof of "good" first.
- **Weakest link:** retention among families who don't know you. There is no flywheel yet.
- **Hold for now:** (1) building SSO, an admin dashboard and a staffed concierge for the HR pilot; (2) building Android; (3) waiting on the aggregator as the only route to refill value.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** paying users would cover about $21 a month, and most users can't reach the refill feature they value.
- **Numbers that would change this:** how many of the 7 active circles have no tie to you; retention by source (newsletter, forum, discharge card); refill-sync usage per circle.

Greatness potential: 8/100

## Gate 1: Baseline (FAIL: not yet good)

### Evidence of good: what actually works

These are real assets, and they shouldn't get lost in the verdict:

- **The newsletter is the strongest thing Tessel has.** *The Caregiver's Ledger* has 2,140 subscribers and a 52% average open rate, written weekly. That is an audience that trusts you. It is also already the main source of sign-ups.
- **There is a referral signal at the moment of need.** A stroke-unit social worker hands out Tessel cards at discharge, and families ask for it by name. Discharge is exactly when a care circle forms.
- **The founder's insight is sharp and earned.** "Caregivers don't want another app. They want fewer phone calls." Pairing that with "a supply-chain problem with love in it" is a good basis for a future Hedgehog Concept.
- **Interview pull for refill sync is clear.** It came up across 26 interviews, including "I'd pay anything."
- **External validation exists:** a $10K competition win, a 4.9★ rating (from only 11 ratings), and a basic security review and privacy policy already done.
- **Founder–problem fit is real:** you use Tessel daily for your own father, with your aunt (a retired nurse) checking the clinical content.

### The honest read

The skill's "not yet good" test asks whether the core works without the steward's personal effort, network or manual workarounds. All three markers are present:

| Marker | Evidence from the dossier |
|---|---|
| Users come from the founder's network | The first users came from your Wednesday support group and former colleagues. One of the 7 weekly active circles is your own family. `[ASSUMPTION]` Several more of the 7 are probably network-adjacent; the dossier doesn't say. |
| Retention unproven outside that circle | 41 circles created, 7 weekly active (17%). 318 accounts, but only 41 circles, so most accounts never formed a circle. No cohort data split by source. |
| Key feature is hand-operated | Refill sync works for one family-owned pharmacy, through a weekly email export that you load by hand. Chain access has been stuck "under review" since August. |
| Willingness to pay unproven | 3 subscribers at $6.99, about $21/month, six weeks in. The "I'd pay anything" quote hasn't turned into payment yet. |

That last row has an important nuance. The paid tier is probably being tested **without** the feature people say they'd pay for, because refill sync only works near one pharmacy. So weak conversion doesn't yet prove people won't pay. It proves the valued thing isn't reachable. That points to a specific fix (milestone 2), not a general despair.

Under the skill's rules this is a `FAIL`, not a `CONDITIONAL`. Building a Hedgehog Concept, a flywheel and an enterprise strategy on 7 active circles of unknown origin would be building on sand.

### What "good" would look like

Families who have never met you set up a circle, keep using it week after week, and get refill value without you touching any data. Some of them pay.

### Three milestones that would prove it

Targets are `[ASSUMPTION]`, sized for a solo founder over about 90 days.

1. **Stranger retention.** Reach at least 20 weekly active circles with no personal tie to you, each active for at least 8 consecutive weeks. Tag every circle's source (newsletter, forum, discharge card with a unique code, network) so this can be counted honestly. The discharge-card cohort is the cleanest test you have, because those families arrive at the exact moment of need and don't know you.
2. **Refill value without your hands.** Deliver the refill-due part of refill sync for any pharmacy, without an aggregator. Compute due dates from fill date, quantity and dosing, and let families capture those from a photo of the pill bottle label. They already use photo notes for pill bottles. Automate the family pharmacy's Monday export so you stop loading it yourself. `[ASSUMPTION]` Knowing when a refill is due delivers most of the value of "never discover on Sunday night it ran out," even without knowing whether the pharmacy has it ready. This is a hypothesis to test, not a certainty.
3. **Strangers pay.** Reach at least 15 paying circles from outside your network, with the refill-due feature available to them.

Hit all three and Tessel is good. Then re-run the full gates.

### What this means for the decisions in front of you

The run ends at Gate 1, but the finding directly shapes the three choices in your application:

- **Graham's employer channel.** It may well become the economic engine. As a rough illustration, one 1,800-person employer at $3–5 per employee per month would be about $5,400–9,000 a month, versus about $21 today, if they paid on total headcount. Neither that pricing nor the one-in-five survey could be checked in this run. But the pilot's conditions are an SSO build, an admin dashboard, anonymized reporting and a **human-staffed concierge**. That last one is a services business, not a feature. For one person, that is roughly a quarter of building, aimed at a buyer who will judge Tessel on whether employees keep using it. That is exactly the thing that isn't proven yet. Keep the HR relationship warm, ask that head of HR what usage evidence would make them pilot *without* the concierge, and come back with milestone 1 in hand.
- **Android.** A third of your waitlist uses Android, but a second platform doubles the surface area before the first one retains. Hold it until milestone 1.
- **Part-time developer.** Hire for milestone 2 if anything. Don't hire to widen scope.
- **The accelerator.** Your stated biggest risk is pharmacy access. The evidence suggests the bigger risk is retention outside your network, and that pharmacy access is one route to it, not the only one. Framing the application that way is more honest and probably more convincing.

### Limits of this run

There was no memory, prior run or web search available here. So there's no earlier baseline, and none of these were verified: Graham's per-employee pricing, the caregiver survey, or how long pharmacy-data aggregators usually take to grant access. Everything above comes from the application alone.
