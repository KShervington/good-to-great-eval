## One-screen verdict

**Halvard Strings is already great, so this run is in stewardship mode.** It has grown every year for 14 years at roughly 4× its market's rate. Operating margin has held at 24–29% for a decade, ROIC averages ~34%, and professional-player share rose from 9% to 31%.

**Hedgehog (confirmed):** *Halvard makes the violin-family strings professional players choose in blind tests, and sells them at a protected premium through specialist shops.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** the specialist-shop channel, now shrinking about 3% a year in North America.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. Halvard Six as proposed.
2. The PE group's student brand.
3. Marketplace listings or sub-MAP pricing as shops consolidate.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** in a single session, the board is being asked to re-enter both businesses it left in order to become great.

**Numbers that would change this:** what a second shift does to core capacity, how much revenue sits with the top shops, and a blind test of the guitar prototype by *professional guitarists*.

`Greatness potential: 78/100`

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept (confirmed, not invented)

> **Halvard makes the violin-family strings professional players choose in blind tests, and sells them at a protected premium through specialist shops.**

| Circle | What it is | What it is *not* |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Strings that professional violin-family players prefer blind, built on in-house winding plus a player-led veto. | Guitar strings, student strings, mass-retail merchandising, low price. Halvard has never shown best-in-world potential in any of these. |
| **Economic engine** | Premium price that is never discounted, 61% gross margin, 78% three-year retention, and shops that are glad to stock it. | Volume at 31% gross margin. |
| **Deep passion** | The players: the Listening Room, the annual orchestra survey, and a CEO who is an acoustics engineer and sends credit to the panel. | Category size for its own sake. |

**Denominator:** **operating profit per professional/advanced violin-family player retained.** [ASSUMPTION: the company tracks player share and retention, so this fits its data. Gross profit per set through specialist shops is a reasonable alternative.]

**Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The Year −14 exit from student and guitar strings, followed by heavy investment in player testing and winding, is the turning point the whole financial record follows from.

**Does it rule things out?** Yes. It excludes both agenda items as proposed.

**Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means saying no to a market 6× larger and to an offer of 900 end-caps.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
- Professional share keeps rising.
- Three-year retention stays ≥78%.
- Margin stays in the 24–29% band without discounting.
- Sales through specialist shops keep growing even as shop numbers fall.

## 5. Temptations to refuse (stewardship-mode stop-doing list)

1. **Halvard Six as proposed.**
   - **Not best in world:** the 2.3× corrosion result is a lab durability metric. Halvard wins on blind preference by professionals, which has never been tested with guitarists.
   - **Wrong engine:** 31% gross margin against 61%. Assuming 8% core growth, blended gross margin would fall from ~61% to roughly 54% by Year 3 [ASSUMPTION].
   - **Wrong channel:** national-chain end-caps are the mass-retail route Halvard exited in Year −14.
   - **Wrong use of capacity:** a second shift on winding machines in a plant where cello and bass lead times just hit five weeks.
   - **Wrong panel:** the CEO's idea of having the Listening Room test guitar prototypes respects "players decide." But 40 violin-family professionals judging guitar strings would be weak evidence either way.
2. **Buying the PE group's student brand at ~1.1× revenue.** It reverses the defining decision, mixes a student brand into the shops that sell Halvard as premium, and buys growth instead of earning it.
3. **Listing on general online marketplaces or promoting below MAP**, even as shop numbers fall. Price integrity is part of the engine, and shop margins drive link 4.
4. **Letting any product bypass the Listening Room**, or letting the panel judge categories outside its expertise.
5. **Setting revenue-growth targets the core cannot meet at quality.** Growth of 7–9% in a 2% market is excellent. "Only 2% of string players" is the moat, not the problem.

**What would change the call on #1:** a version of the guitar line that passes *every* condition below.
- Blind preference by a panel of *professional guitarists*.
- Specialist channels only.
- Gross margin near core levels.
- No impact on core capacity or lead times.

No such version exists in the dossier.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- The Listening Room's veto.
- Never below MAP.
- The annual 61-orchestra survey.
- Metals hedged forward.
- No debt and a deep cash reserve.

**Framework boundaries:** freedom to innovate on anything within violin-family strings for professional and advanced players, through specialist channels. Anything outside that scope requires the written new-category rule and the full board.

**Bureaucracy check:** nothing in the dossier suggests rules that exist to compensate for wrong people.
