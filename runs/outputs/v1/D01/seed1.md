# Tallypaw: verdict

**Good, not yet great. The biggest threat is sitting in the board's "housekeeping" notes.**

- **Hedgehog:** Tallypaw is the distributor-neutral ordering brain for independent vet clinics, built by people who have run one.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** winning new clinics through channels Tallypaw controls. Today that depends on Maya's demos and on VetSource.
- **Stop doing:** (1) the dental pilot; (2) corporate-chain pilots; (3) treating VetSource as housekeeping.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** VetSource carries 38% of ARR and renews in five months. It has ignored two emails, and it just announced it is building your best module.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** the VetSource contract terms (who owns the clinic relationship, the data and the billing); logo churn and CAC by channel; the share of clinics that buy from more than one distributor.

Greatness potential: 22/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tallypaw is the system an independent vet clinic runs its drug and supply ordering on, whichever distributors it buys from. Clinics stay for years because Tallypaw cuts stockouts, waste and ordering time better than any supplier's own portal can. It wins new clinics through practice-manager referrals and a repeatable sales team made up of people who have worked in clinics, so it no longer depends on Maya's demos. No single distributor controls more than a quarter of its revenue. Reminders stay as a cheap on-ramp, not a product line. It turns down chains and dental because its edge is the small clinic. Its economics improve on one measure: lifetime gross profit per clinic.

**Denominator:** lifetime gross profit per clinic, which is ACV × gross margin ÷ annual logo churn.

Derived estimate [ASSUMPTION: computed from the shared numbers, assuming new clinics land at the average ACV and NRR includes churned revenue]:
- Gross profit per clinic-year is about $6,000 ($7,700 × 78%).
- Implied gross new ARR last year was about $0.84M ($2.4M − $1.5M × 104%). That is roughly 109 new clinics, which implies about 19 clinics lost, or about 9% blended logo churn.
- The half-churn ratio then implies about 7% churn for inventory clinics and about 14% for reminder-only clinics.
- That gives lifetime gross profit of roughly **$86K per inventory clinic** versus **$43K or less per reminder-only clinic**.

**Does the concept pass the three tests?**
- **Explains past wins:** yes. Inventory drove attach, retention and reviews.
- **Rules things out:** yes. It rules out dental, chains and reminder feature work.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to:** yes. It kills the CEO's idea and the Head of Sales's plan, and it forces independence from the largest channel partner.

**Confirmation tests over 12 months:**
1. Inventory-clinic churn stays at or below half of reminder-only churn, including for VetSource-referred clinics.
2. Tallypaw keeps ≥90% of VetSource-referred clinics after VetSource's native tool ships [proposed threshold].

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **The dental pilot.** Decline the LOI and keep both engineers on the core. Revisit only after the Hedgehog Concept is confirmed, and only with someone from inside dental on the bus.
2. **Corporate-chain pilots.** No third pilot, and no features built for chains such as multi-site rollups or enterprise single sign-on.
3. **Treating VetSource as housekeeping.** It becomes the first item at every board meeting until it is resolved.
4. **Building new reminder features.** Keep parity only; reminders are the on-ramp.
5. **Maya as the default demo.** Cut her share to no more than one demo in four within 90 days [proposed target].
6. **Spending sales and marketing money that can't be traced to a channel.** That includes trade shows, until their CAC is known.
7. **Any roadmap item that doesn't strengthen ordering and inventory for independent clinics.**
8. **Signing a long office lease in March,** before the VetSource outcome is known. Take a short or flexible term.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **Clinic-first:** built by people who have done the job.
2. **Neutral by design:** the clinic's interests come before any supplier's.
3. **Admit it first:** blameless post-mortems, with leaders speaking up first.
4. **Simple beats slick.**

**Core purpose:** help independent vet clinics stay independent and run as well as any chain.

**BHAG (14-year horizon):** By 2040, half of the independent vet clinics in every market Tallypaw serves run their ordering on it.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A sales playbook that non-founders can run.
- A hiring pipeline from clinics (practice managers and vet techs).
- Formal blameless post-mortems across the company.
- A roadmap filter: "which circle does this serve, and what does it displace?"
- A channel-attributed scorecard reviewed at every board meeting.
- A rule that no single partner holds more than 25% of ARR.
