# Tallypaw: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Tallypaw is good, not yet great.** Its great version is narrower than either plan the board is debating.

- **Hedgehog:** Tallypaw runs drug and supply ordering for independent vet clinics from the clinic's side of the counter, and grows by raising gross profit per inventory-active clinic-year.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** new-clinic acquisition. New clinics arrive through VetSource (38% of ARR) and Maya's personal demos, not through a repeatable direct engine.
- **Stop doing:** (1) the dental pilot; (2) corporate-chain pilots; (3) treating VetSource as a housekeeping item.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the partner behind 38% of ARR is building its own inventory tool. Renewal is five months out, and VetSource hasn't answered two emails.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** who contractually owns the VetSource-referred clinics; CAC and churn by channel and by module; how many independent clinics Tallypaw can actually reach.

Greatness potential: 25/100

*Evidence note: no memory, past runs or web search were available, so this run uses the dossier alone. Market facts (independent-clinic counts, consolidation trends, VetSource's actual product) are unverified and marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tallypaw is the inventory brain that independent vet clinics own. A practice with one to four vets runs its drug and supply ordering on Tallypaw no matter which distributor it buys from, because Tallypaw works for the clinic and not for the seller. Every new clinic starts on inventory. Reminders are included as a commodity feature, not sold on their own. Clinics stay about twice as long as reminder-only clinics do today, and practice managers recommend Tallypaw to each other. Growth comes from a direct engine that needs neither Maya in every demo nor VetSource in every deal. The company turns down dental and corporate chains. If the independent-vet market proves too small for venture-scale returns, the board chooses to be durable and great over big and diluted, and makes that choice explicitly.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Tallypaw runs drug and supply ordering for independent vet clinics from the clinic's side of the counter, and grows by raising gross profit per inventory-active clinic-year.**

*Status: working hypothesis. See the confirmation tests below.*

| Circle | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Inventory and ordering for independent clinics with 1–4 vets. This is the module most mentioned in reviews, attached by 71% of clinics, and associated with half the churn. | **Reminders:** a commodity running on a rented SMS vendor. **Corporate chains:** 0 of 2 pilots converted, and the "we were one of them" edge is specifically a small-clinic edge. **Dental:** no domain insight, and the founders' edge doesn't transfer. **Full practice management:** `[ASSUMPTION]` established incumbents already hold it. |
| **Economic engine** | **Gross profit per inventory-active clinic-year.** | Per vet (no data). Per dollar of drug spend managed: promising, worth testing later, but there is no data yet. Per enterprise contract: contradicted by the pilot results. |
| **Passion** | The daily operations of small clinics. The CEO is a former practice manager, the CS team are former vet techs, and the CTO built the inventory engine. `[ASSUMPTION]` The team cares about keeping independent clinics viable. | "Big markets." Passion for scale is not the same as passion for the work. |

**Denominator math (derived):** ~$7,700 ACV × 78% gross margin ≈ **$6.0k gross profit per clinic-year**, blended. Inventory clinics churn at half the rate, so they produce roughly double the lifetime gross profit. This assumes inventory and reminder-only clinics have similar ACV; split ACV by module to check.

**Tests**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Inventory is what reviewers praise, what reduces churn, and what VetSource chose to bundle.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: dental, chains, reminder feature races, and general practice management.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It kills both board proposals and challenges the belief that "we need a second market."

**The strategic bet inside the sentence.** "The clinic's side of the counter" is the line VetSource can't cross. A distributor's inventory tool will naturally steer orders toward that distributor. A tool that compares and orders across several distributors is something no single distributor can credibly offer. `[ASSUMPTION]` This depends on clinics in Tallypaw's markets buying from more than one distributor, and on other distributors being willing to integrate.

**Confirmation tests**
1. After CAC, lifetime gross profit for inventory-active clinics is at least 2× that of reminder-only clinics, measured by channel.
2. Directly sold inventory clinics retain as well as VetSource-bundled ones. That would show the stickiness comes from the product, not the bundle.
3. Market size: every 1,000 clinics ≈ $7.7M ARR at today's price. A $50M ARR outcome needs about 6,500 clinics, or a higher ACV per clinic. Count the independent clinics in markets Tallypaw can reach.
4. In 20 clinic interviews, a majority say they would connect a second distributor if Tallypaw supported it.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 (1) Clinic-insider knowledge of how small clinics actually order
          │
          ▼
 (2) Reorder suggestions clinics trust (inventory module)
          │
          ▼
 (3) Clinics save money and time → stay ~2× longer
          │
          ▼
 (4) Practice managers recommend Tallypaw to peers   ◀── WEAKEST LINK
          │   (today this is outsourced to the VetSource bundle and Maya's demos)
          ▼
 (5) More inventory-active clinics → more gross profit per clinic-year
          │
          ▼
 (6) More ordering data + reinvestment in the engine and vet-tech CS
          │
          └──────────────► feeds back into (1) and (2)
```

**Current push (the next two turns)**
1. **Make acquisition independent of VetSource and of Maya.** Set up direct contractual relationships where the agreement allows, build a demo playbook, run a referral program among practice managers, and attribute every dollar of spend to a channel.
2. **Convert the ~90 reminder-only clinics to inventory.** This is the fastest lever on component 3 and on the denominator.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the dental pilot.** Decline the LOI and return the two engineers to the core.
2. **Stop corporate-chain pursuit.** No third pilot.
3. **Stop treating VetSource as housekeeping.** It becomes the first board agenda item until it is resolved.
4. **Stop selling reminder-only as a standalone new plan.** New clinics start on inventory. `[ASSUMPTION]` Test this on one quarter of new deals before making it policy.
5. **Stop investing beyond parity in reminders.** Fix the SMS cost, then leave the module alone.
6. **Stop founder-only selling.** Maya's demo becomes a recorded, teachable playbook.
7. **Stop unattributed sales and marketing spend.** No new spend that can't be traced to new clinics.
8. **Stop the long roadmap.** Cut to three items or fewer, now and not "after the dental pilot."
9. **Stop making multi-year fixed commitments until VetSource is resolved.** This includes the March lease: take the shortest sensible term.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values**
1. Built by clinic people, for clinic people.
2. On the clinic's side of the counter.
3. Admit mistakes first.
4. Earn the reorder: suggestions must be trustworthy enough to act on without checking.

**Core purpose:** help independent vet clinics stay independent and well run.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** *By 2040, most independent vet clinics in every market we serve run their drug and supply ordering on Tallypaw, across whichever distributors they choose.*

**Clock-building mechanisms**
- A recorded demo playbook.
- A clinic-background hiring profile for every customer-facing seat.
- Blameless post-mortems company-wide.
- A written Hedgehog test, reviewed by the board, for any new market or product.
- A channel-attribution dashboard.
- A partner-concentration cap.

- Stewards: Maya (CEO) and Jonah (CTO).

**Baseline sheet (G1).**

*Evidence of good:*
- ARR up 60% ($1.5M → $2.4M).
- Clinics up 41% (220 → 310).
- 78% gross margin.
- NPS 61 (n = 138).

*Why it isn't "not yet good":* the product works and retains customers across 310 clinics without manual workarounds. Founder-led sales is a risk to endurance, not a sign that the core doesn't work.

*Three tests of great:*
- **Superior performance:** partly met. Growth is strong; NRR is modest.
- **Distinctive impact:** plausible for inventory, but unproven, since VetSource may try to fill that hole.
- **Lasting endurance:** not met. The company depends on one partner, one seller, and a 20-month runway.
