# Tallypaw: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Tallypaw is good, not yet great. The next five months decide which way it goes.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Tallypaw is the distributor-neutral inventory brain for independent vet clinics, and it grows by raising lifetime gross profit per clinic.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** winning new clinics. About 38% of ARR came through VetSource, and most of the rest came through Maya's own demos.
- **Stop doing:** (1) the dental pilot, (2) corporate-chain pilots, (3) investing in reminders beyond upkeep.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** VetSource accounts for 38% of ARR and is up for renewal in five months. It is building its own inventory tool and hasn't answered two emails. The board filed this under "housekeeping."
- **Numbers that would change this answer:**
  - Who holds the contract and billing for VetSource-referred clinics.
  - CAC and new clinics by channel.
  - The share of clinics that buy from more than one distributor.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tallypaw is the system independent vet clinics trust to run their pharmacy and supply room, whichever distributors they buy from. It was built by people who have stood behind the counter, and its support comes from former vet techs whom customers know by name. Clinics stay because the software saves them money and prevents stockouts. They tell other practice managers about it, so new clinics arrive through word of mouth and a sales motion that doesn't depend on Maya's calendar. No partner can switch off a third of revenue. Every clinic added improves reorder suggestions and benchmarks for every other clinic. Growth comes from going deeper into inventory and wider into new independent-vet regions. It does not come from new industries or corporate chains. Tallypaw would be great even if it never became the biggest vet software company, because it would be the one independents can't replace.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Tallypaw is the distributor-neutral inventory brain for independent vet clinics. It knows how a small clinic actually buys, stocks and runs out of drugs, and it grows by raising lifetime gross profit per clinic.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out (and why) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Inventory for independents; reminders; full practice software; enterprise vet ops; small-practice healthcare software (dental) | **Inventory and ordering for 1–4 vet independent clinics, regardless of distributor** | Reminders: a commodity running on a rented SMS pipe. Chains: 0 of 2 pilots converted. Dental: "we were one of them" doesn't transfer. Full practice software: no evidence of an edge `[ASSUMPTION]`. |
| **Economic engine (denominator)** | ARR per clinic; gross profit per clinic; retention per CS person; **lifetime gross profit per clinic** | **Lifetime gross profit per independent clinic** | ARR per clinic alone ignores the churn halving that inventory produces, and that halving is the engine. |
| **Passion** | Helping independents compete; craft of inventory; customer care | **Keeping independent clinics independent and well-run** | Being "venture-scale" is a funding goal, not a passion. |

**Why "distributor-neutral":** if VetSource builds inventory into its own portal, that tool will steer clinics toward VetSource's catalog. Tallypaw's lasting reason to exist is that it is on the clinic's side of the counter. This is the uncomfortable part of the hedgehog, because it turns the biggest partner into a likely competitor.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The inventory module is the one customers mention most, and inventory clinics churn about half as often.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: dental, chains, and investment in reminders.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes, on two counts: neutrality toward VetSource, and giving up the "second market" story.

**Confirmation tests (90–180 days):**
1. The share of clinics that buy from two or more distributors. If most buy from one distributor, neutrality matters less, and the hedgehog narrows to "inventory for independents."
2. The churn gap between inventory and reminder-only clinics holds when split by cohort and by channel, including VetSource-referred clinics.
3. Direct-channel CAC payback is under about 18 months `[ASSUMPTION threshold]`.
4. Head-to-head retention against VetSource's native tool once it ships.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **The dental pilot.** Free the two engineers now. Re-entry criteria:
   - VetSource dependency below 20% of ARR.
   - Direct channel producing at least 70% of new ARR.
   - Evidence that dental clinics share the same inventory problem shape. `[ASSUMPTION thresholds]`
2. **Corporate-chain pursuit.** No new pilots. Chains are also the force that consolidates Tallypaw's customers.
3. **Investing in reminders beyond maintenance.** Treat reminders as parity. Renegotiate or replace the SMS vendor, or pass through costs.
4. **Maya as default demo-giver.** She should run under 50% of demos within 90 days.
5. **Unattributed sales and marketing spend.** No renewal of trade shows or campaigns without pipeline-by-channel data.
6. **Roadmap items that don't serve inventory.** Deliver the short roadmap the board asked for this month, not "after the dental pilot."
7. **Waiting on VetSource emails.** Passive renewal is not a strategy (see section 9).

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **Practice-native.** We design from inside the clinic.
2. **On the clinic's side of the counter.** We serve the clinic, not the supplier.
3. **Own the mistake first.** This is Jonah's post-mortem habit, made company-wide.
4. **Plain talk.** No slide decks needed, but always bring the numbers.

**Core purpose:** Keep independent veterinary practices independent by giving them operations as good as the chains'.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** A majority of independent vet clinics in every market Tallypaw serves run their pharmacy and supply room on Tallypaw.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Hire CS from vet techs and practice managers.
- A written sales playbook distilled from Maya's demos.
- Blameless post-mortems in every function.
- A monthly cohort-churn review split by module and channel.
- A partner concentration cap: no single channel above 25% of ARR.
- A hedgehog test applied to every roadmap item.
- Board-level approval required for any new market or segment.
