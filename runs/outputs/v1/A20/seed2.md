# Good to Great: Brightwater Drug Corporation

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Brightwater becomes the most convenient discount drugstore in the Southeast and Texas. It builds dense neighborhood clusters and measures itself by profit per customer visit.

**Weakest flywheel link:** cluster density. Cash and attention are going to unrelated businesses and scattered acquisitions instead of filling in the core metros.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Buying businesses unrelated to the drugstore.
2. Owning the department stores, security company and food-service distributor.
3. Running the company around political campaigns.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the largest U.S. drug chain has just entered the home state. Meanwhile there is no visible evidence of which Brightwater business actually earns its capital.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit by business line, profit per store and per visit, market share in the top metros, and the successor CEO's track record.

Greatness potential: 20/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Brightwater is a drugstore company and nothing else. In every Southeast and Texas metro it serves, it is the drugstore people reach first, usually next to the supermarket they already visit. It is self-service, priced at a discount, and has the pharmacy as its anchor. It grows by filling in its own map, store by store, rather than buying distant chains or unrelated businesses. Every store knows its profit per customer visit. The company runs on a professional CEO and a deep management bench, so it no longer depends on its founder's presence. The founder's original advantages were seeing a format early, fighting for the right to discount, and picking sites where traffic already flows. In the great version, those become a system that renews itself.

```
 │                       │

 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 │    6. Lower prices + strong local brand
 │        → landlords and supermarket partners offer the best sites
 └──────────────────────┘
```

**Current push, the next two turns:**
1. Measure profit per visit for every store and every cluster. Right now the flywheel turns blind.
2. Deepen density in the home-state metros where Rival W just bought 29 stores. Defend the core before expanding the perimeter.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs are present.**
- Five unrelated businesses have been entered in about nine years, and the candy company was bought and then sold. That is new direction after new direction.
- Chains have been bought in five regions within four years, including the non-contiguous Mid-Atlantic. That is acquisition used to jump-start momentum.
- The leader's attention has been episodic.

The loop closes on paper, but the company's recent behavior pushes against it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all acquisitions outside drugstores.** No new fields.
2. **Divest the junior department stores** `[ASSUMPTION: no profit data; divest unless line-level numbers show exceptional returns]`.
3. **Divest the security and alarm business.**
4. **Divest the food-service supply business.**
5. **Stop buying chains outside contiguous core territory.** Review whether the Mid-Atlantic chain fits. Sell it if it doesn't densify.
6. **Stop measuring success by store count and total sales.** Use profit per visit and return on capital.
7. **Stop leading through the founder's personality.** Replace it with mechanisms such as store-level P&Ls, blameless reviews and written acquisition criteria.
8. **Stop running political campaigns from the chairman's seat.** If the founder runs again, he should hand formal authority to the board and CEO first.

- *Strengths:* employee-first convictions, fierce resolve shown in the court fight and the borrowed $1M, and a successor named last year.

- *Behavior change:* step back from the spotlight. Credit the CEO and the store teams publicly, and put the founder's own role decision in writing.
