# Meridian Radio Corporation: Good to Great

**Verdict: great on the record, and already showing early signs of decline.** The run uses stewardship mode, with the decline stress test at the center.

**Hedgehog (working):** Meridian is best at making TV sets that dealers and repairmen trust to work and to be fixable. Its engine is profit per dealer.

**Weakest flywheel link:** reinvestment. Money is going into hand-wiring, inspection, a color-capacity race and pay TV, not into reliability engineering for the solid-state sets that are coming.

**Temptations to refuse:**
1. Treating hand-wiring as the brand.
2. A debt-funded race for No. 1 in color at the top of the boom.
3. Open-ended pay-TV spending.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** leadership is waving off both low-cost imports and integrated circuits without data.

**Numbers that would change this:**
- Cost and field-failure rates for hand-wired versus printed-circuit sets
- Import volumes and prices
- Color market share
- Current debt and capital spending

`Greatness potential: 25/100`

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Meridian keeps the one thing that made it No. 1: sets that dealers and repairmen trust. It stops confusing that trust with the method that currently produces it. It builds solid-state, printed-circuit color sets designed from the start to be reliable and to be fixed in minutes, for example with modular, swappable sub-assemblies. It measures quality by how often sets fail in homes, not by how many inspectors stand on the line. It adds color capacity in step with what its dealers actually sell, not to beat Rival R on a share chart. It treats imports as a competitor to study, not one to scorn. It is run by a leadership team that includes a top-level engineer and has a named successor ready for the elderly chairman.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Sets that work and are easy to fix
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │   2. Repairmen & dealers recommend Meridian
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │   3. Strong sell-through, tight dealer inventory
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │   4. PROFIT PER DEALER (denominator) & price premium
   │            │
   │            ▼
   └── 5. Reinvest in reliability & serviceability engineering
          (in the technology the industry is moving to)
```

**Weakest link: step 5 back to step 1.** Reinvestment currently flows to three places:
- Hand-wiring and inspection-based quality, which is costly and tied to a technology on its way out.
- Color capacity sized to an ambition rather than to dealer demand.
- Years of pay-TV development.

None of these strengthens step 1 in a solid-state world. If transistorized, printed-circuit sets become *more* reliable than hand-wired tube sets `[ASSUMPTION: fewer hand-made joints, less heat]`, step 1 erodes and the whole loop slows.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Measure field reliability honestly, for Meridian's sets and for rivals' printed-circuit and solid-state sets.
2. Start a reliability- and serviceability-first solid-state color chassis.

**Doom-loop check:** none of the classic signs yet, but the seeds are there.
- **Big program to jump-start momentum:** the capacity race against Rival R.
- **Possible debt to fund it:** a plausible next step.
- **Long-running side bet that depends on regulators:** pay TV.

2. Field-failure tracking through the dealer and repairman network, so quality is designed in rather than inspected in.

## 4. The bus

**Leadership read (Gate 2): CONDITIONAL.**

The founder was a flamboyant, visionary promoter, the profile of a strong individual leader rather than a builder of successors. His lieutenants now steward the company. Neither is an engineer.

- **Strengths:** financial prudence (the company had no debt in Year −6) and operational discipline (best-in-class inventory control).
- **Gaps:**
  - The mirror is missing. Imports are dismissed and integrated circuits are publicly written off, and in both cases the problem is located outside the company.
  - The founder's doctrines, such as "hand-crafted" and "not until it's ready," appear to be running unexamined.
  - Succession is unknown, and the chairman is elderly.
- **One behavior change:** stop making public statements about competitors or technologies until there is internal data behind them. Ask "what would have to be true?" before "here's why it won't matter."

| Seat / commitment | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chairman (former finance chief, elderly) | **Keep for now; build the successor** | Financial discipline is an asset. Succession is a missing clock. |
| President (former lawyer, runs operations) | **Keep, and pair with a technical peer** | Operational record is strong. Technology judgment is the gap. |
| **Chief technology / engineering executive with real authority** | **Missing** | No one at the top owns the tube-to-solid-state transition. |
| **Competitive intelligence on imports** | **Missing** | Nobody appears to be tracking import volumes or prices. That data is absent from the dossier. |
| **Named successor for the chairman** | **Missing** | Required for endurance. |
| Quality inspectors (about one worker in six) | **Move seat** | Shift from end-of-line inspection to process and design reliability, field-failure analysis, and supplier quality. Retrain, not remove. |
| Tube subsidiary | **Keep** | Picture tubes remain core in color. Pace the new plant to demand. |
| Dealers and repairmen | **Keep (core partners)** | They are the engine. Bring them into reliability feedback. |
| Pay-TV program | **Off the main bus (ring-fence)** | Outside the Hedgehog, and its timeline is set by regulators. |

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** No one in a key seat is misaligned. The critical seat, a technology leader, is simply empty.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Stop treating hand-wiring as the brand.** Keep it only where field data shows it wins. The promise is "it works and it's fixable," not "it's hand-wired."
2. **Stop chasing No. 1 in color as a goal in itself.** Size tube and set capacity to dealer-backed demand plus a downturn scenario. Don't fund a share race with debt at the top of a boom.
3. **Stop open-ended pay-TV spending.** Set a spending cap and a regulatory deadline, then license, partner or shelve.
4. **Stop dismissing imports without data.** "Cheap" is a description, not an analysis.
5. **Stop making public pronouncements against emerging technologies.** They lock the company into positions it may need to reverse.
6. **Refuse the counter-temptation:** don't answer imports with a stripped-down, low-price Meridian badge. That would trade away the dealer trust the flywheel runs on.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Inventory and dealer-stock control (already world-class)
- Field-reliability measurement on every model
- Serviceability as a design requirement

**Framework boundaries:** engineers have freedom over *how* sets are built, provided measured field reliability and service time meet the standard.

**Bureaucracy check:** inspecting one worker in six may be a rule that compensates for a process that doesn't build quality in. Test this with the cost study.
