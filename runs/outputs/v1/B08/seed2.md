# Good to Great: Meridian Radio Corporation

**Verdict: already great, and showing early signs of decline.** Run in stewardship mode: the main risk is falling, not staying merely good.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** Meridian makes the home TV that servicemen and dealers trust most, reliable and easy to fix in whatever circuit technology delivers that, and earns a premium per set for that trust.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** reliability currently comes from hand-wiring and inspection rather than design, so this link breaks when circuit technology changes.
- **Temptations to refuse:** (1) racing Rival R to No. 1 in color by adding capacity, especially with borrowed money; (2) treating hand-wiring as the brand; (3) open-ended pay-TV spending.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** leadership is about to commit capital to the old production model while publicly dismissing integrated circuits and low-cost imports. It has measured neither.
- **Numbers that would change this:** hand-wired vs. printed-circuit cost per set; import prices and volumes; full-year margins for Years −5 to −1; color share; debt and capital spending.

Greatness potential: 30/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Meridian is still the set the repairman recommends. It earns that trust through engineered reliability and serviceability, not through a production method. It moves to printed circuits, transistors and eventually integrated circuits on its own schedule, and it designs each new chassis so it fails less and repairs faster than anything else on the market. A good example would be plug-in modules a serviceman can swap in minutes. It grows color capacity in step with dealer demand and profit per set, not to win a rank contest with Rival R. It treats low-cost imports as an early warning: it studies them, takes them apart and prices against them on purpose. An engineering voice sits at the top table, and a named succession plan exists. The company stays great because its trust-to-premium loop keeps working when the technology underneath it changes.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Meridian makes the home television that servicemen and dealers trust most, reliable and easy to service in any circuit technology, and earns a premium per set for that trust.*

In stewardship mode this gate confirms the concept Meridian already lives by. It adds one refinement: the concept is **reliability and serviceability**. It is not **hand-wiring**. Hand-wiring is a practice that has served the concept, and practices can change while the core stays the same.

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Hand-wired construction (a practice, not an outcome); color picture tubes (Rival R pioneered and leads); pay TV (unproven, depends on regulators); dealer and inventory discipline (best in industry); **reliable, serviceable sets that repairmen recommend** | Serviceman-trusted reliability, delivered through a best-run dealer network |
| **Cannot be best at** | Lowest-cost mass production (imports and automated rivals); broadcasting and content (Rival R is a broadcaster); color technology pioneering (Rival R got there first); regulated subscription services | Rule these out explicitly |
| **Economic denominator** | Profit per dealer; profit per household lifetime; **profit per set** | **Profit per set** `[ASSUMPTION]`: trust earns a premium on each unit. Test profit per dealer as the alternative. |
| **Passion** | Quality craftsmanship; American manufacturing; *being No. 1* | Quality the customer can count on. "Being No. 1" is a passion for rank and does not belong in this circle. |

**Confirmation tests:**
1. Field failure rates on a printed-circuit or transistor pilot line match or beat the hand-wired line. This tests whether reliability can live outside hand-wiring.
2. The dealer price premium over comparable sets holds when the circuit type changes.
3. Servicemen's recommendation rates don't drop on the new chassis. `[ASSUMPTION]` There is no current survey; one needs to be created.

The concept explains past wins: it accounts for the black-and-white lead built on repairman word of mouth and dealer discipline. It also rules things out: rank racing, pay TV, and a low-end price war. Committing to it is uncomfortable because it says the company's signature practice is negotiable.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Engineer reliability & serviceability into every chassis
   │           (any circuit technology)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   2. Fewer failures, faster repairs → servicemen trust & recommend
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   3. Dealers stock and push Meridian (best-in-class inventory control
   │           keeps their turns healthy)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   │   4. Premium holds → PROFIT PER SET  (denominator)
   │                       │
   │                       ▼
   └── 5. Reinvest in reliability engineering, picture-tube quality and
           serviceman feedback (field-failure data)
```

**Weakest link: 1 → 2.** Today reliability comes from hand-wiring plus heavy inspection (about one production worker in six is an inspector). When the industry's circuit technology changes, the source of trust changes with it, unless reliability is built into the design.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Build one solid-state or printed-circuit chassis to a serviceability standard, and measure its field failures against the hand-wired line.
2. Tie the color expansion to dealer orders and profit per set, not to overtaking Rival R.

**Doom-loop check.** There are early signs, though no full doom loop yet:
- The No. 1-in-color ambition is a big-program push driven by rank.
- Pay TV is a multi-year bet waiting on an outside breakthrough (regulatory approval).
- The import response is denial. That is not a reaction yet, but denial now often produces a panicked reaction later.
- No sign so far of new direction after new direction.

## 5. Stop-doing list (stewardship mode: real temptations only)

1. **Stop chasing No. 1 in color as a goal in itself.** Add capacity when dealer orders and profit per set justify it. Don't borrow to win a share ranking.
2. **Stop treating hand-wiring as the brand.** The brand is "the set that doesn't let you down." Retire hand-wiring line by line as soon as engineered alternatives match its field reliability.
3. **Stop open-ended pay-TV spending.** Cap it, set a dated regulatory milestone, and end it if the milestone is missed.
4. **Stop public dismissals of imports and integrated circuits.** Public positions make it hard to reverse course later. Say "we're studying it," and then actually study it.
5. **Refuse the opposite panic:** a low-end price war with imports. Meridian cannot be the cheapest. Compete on value for the premium, and know the exact price gap that trust can justify.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values (preserve):**
1. Quality the customer can count on, defined by outcomes, not methods.
2. Respect for the servicemen and dealers who stand behind the product.
3. Integrity in what the brand promises.
4. Inventory and operating discipline.

**Core purpose:** to put a set in American homes that families can count on.

**BHAG (about a 25-year horizon):** remain the brand servicemen recommend first, through every circuit generation, with the lowest field failure rate in the industry.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A field-failure data system fed by servicemen.
- An engineering-led product review with authority to retire old practices.
- A capital rule: capacity follows dealer orders and profit per set.
- Stage gates for side bets such as pay TV.
- A written succession plan.
