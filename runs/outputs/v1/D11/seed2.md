# Heart Failure Transitions Program (HFTP), St. Aldric Regional Medical Center: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. The 30-day success story may hide a 90-day problem.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Keep St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients safely home for 90 days, not 30, by fixing their medicines and showing up at their door.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** proof. There are no risk-adjusted outcomes and no value priced by Finance.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) taking the lead on Hospital at Home this year; (2) graduating high-risk patients at day 30; (3) chasing 75% universal enrollment.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** at 90 days, 32.7% of enrolled patients were readmitted versus 33.9% of those not enrolled. The 5.9-point gap almost disappears, and this number appears in a pharmacist's accreditation checklist, not in the memo.
- **Numbers that would change this:** 90-day readmissions and mortality by risk tier, risk-adjusted; a breakdown of the 556 non-enrolled patients; and Finance's value per avoided bed-day.

`Greatness potential: 30/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great HFTP is not the biggest transitions team in the state. It is the team that measurably keeps the region's sickest, hardest-to-reach heart-failure patients out of the hospital for a full 90 days. It sorts every heart-failure discharge by risk. It gives the top tier a pharmacist medication reconciliation within days, along with home visits and contact through day 90. Low-risk patients get a lighter, cheaper touch. Patients who can't be reached by phone become a priority, found by community health workers at their door, rather than an exclusion. Every report to leadership opens with risk-adjusted 90-day results and a value figure that Finance has signed. HFTP partners with Hospital at Home but does not run it. Its budget is defended with numbers, not stories.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **HFTP keeps St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients safely home for 90 days, not 30, by fixing their medicines and showing up at their door.**

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** (narrow world: this hospital's region) | Transitions for all HF patients; home-based acute care; multi-prescriber medication cleanup; in-person access for hard-to-reach patients | Untangling multi-prescriber medication regimens (Mr. D.) plus fast in-person access (82% seen within 7 days vs 46%) **for high-risk patients** | Acute inpatient-level home care (no acute-care track record); remote-monitoring technology; low-risk patients, who do reasonably well without the program anyway `[ASSUMPTION]` |
| **Resource engine** | Cost per enrolled patient; 30-day readmissions averted; penalty points; bed-days avoided | **90-day inpatient days avoided per high-risk patient enrolled** | "Per enrolled patient" rewards volume. "Per 30-day readmission" rewards pushing readmissions past day 30, which the 90-day data suggests may already be happening. |
| **Passion** | Hospital at Home; home visits; patient stories; staff growth | Keeping vulnerable people safely at home, hands-on: low turnover, scales delivered door to door, the daughter's letter to the CEO | Leading a new department. That passion may be partly about title and scope (see Gate 2). |

**Denominator:** *90-day inpatient days avoided, per high-risk patient enrolled.* This links to the hospital's resources through three channels: bed capacity in a 418-bed regional hospital `[ASSUMPTION: occupancy is high enough that freed beds have value]`, the readmissions penalty, and any value-based contracts `[ASSUMPTION: unknown whether St. Aldric holds any]`.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
- Within 6 months, a risk-tiered analysis shows a meaningfully larger 90-day effect in the top tier than in the program overall. Failing that, a pilot extending contact to day 90 for the top tier shows that effect.
- Finance values a high-risk bed-day avoided above the marginal cost of a high-risk enrollment.
- If both fail, the hedgehog is wrong. The honest conclusion would then be that HFTP is a good penalty-management program, which is worth funding on those terms but not great.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop pursuing the Hospital at Home lead role this year.** Offer to be the transitions partner for its discharges, with a decision gate at month 12.
2. **Stop graduating high-risk patients at day 30.** About 16.8% of enrolled patients are first readmitted between days 31 and 90, compared with about 12.1% of those not enrolled.
3. **Stop chasing a universal 75% enrollment target.** Measure reach into the top tier instead.
4. **Stop giving low-risk patients the full six-touch protocol.** Move them to a lighter, cheaper pathway.
5. **Stop presenting enrolled-versus-not-enrolled comparisons as proof of effect.** The groups differ by design.
6. **Stop treating "unable to reach by phone" as an exclusion.** That group is probably where the highest-risk patients are. `[ASSUMPTION]`
7. **Stop using stories (Mr. D., the scales) as the value case to leadership.** Keep them for patient voice and team morale.
