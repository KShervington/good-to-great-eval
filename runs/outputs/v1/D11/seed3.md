# Good to Great: Heart Failure Transitions Program (HFTP), St. Aldric Regional Medical Center

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. The evidence for "good" is also weaker than the memo suggests.**

**Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** HFTP keeps St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients home for 90 days, not 30. It does this by finding and fixing what sends them back, medications first, and handing each patient to a clinician who keeps them.

**Weakest flywheel link:** the day-30 "graduation" hands patients to no one.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Leading the Hospital at Home launch.
2. Presenting the enrolled-vs-not-enrolled 30-day gap as the program's effect.
3. Chasing a blanket 75% enrollment rate instead of targeting by risk.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** at 90 days, readmission rates for enrolled and non-enrolled patients are nearly identical (32.7% vs 33.9%). HFTP may be delaying readmissions rather than preventing them.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** a risk-adjusted 30- and 90-day comparison, 90-day mortality by group, and finance's price per readmission and per bed-day.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great HFTP is a narrower, sharper team. It no longer tries to touch every heart-failure discharge equally. Its intensity goes to the patients most likely to come back: those on many medications, those with several prescribers, those with prior readmissions. Its pharmacists reconcile every prescriber's list before day 10. Nobody "graduates" without a booked visit with a named clinician who owns them afterward. Every quarter it reports 30-day readmissions, 90-day readmissions, mortality and days at home, all against a fair, risk-adjusted comparison, priced in dollars that finance has signed. That report lets it answer the VP's question in one line. If HFTP disappeared, the hospital would lose measurable days at home for its sickest heart-failure patients, and no other team could replace them quickly. Its reputation would rest on numbers, not on letters to the CEO.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *HFTP keeps St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients home for 90 days, not 30, by finding and fixing what sends them back, medications first, and handing each one to a clinician who keeps them.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** (a narrow world: this region's HF patients) | Post-discharge follow-up speed; medication reconciliation across fragmented prescribers; home-based knowledge of patients' lives; acute care at home | **Finding and fixing medication and home causes of readmission in the first 14 days for complex patients.** The Mr. D. case (two diuretics from two specialists) is the clearest proof. The 82% vs 46% seven-day follow-up gap shows the reach. |
| **Cannot be best at** | Acute inpatient-level care at home (Hospital at Home); long-term chronic HF management; general care coordination for all conditions | These belong to physicians, the HF clinic and primary care. HFTP's job is the handoff to them, not replacing them. |
| **Engine** (budget and headcount leverage) | Penalty dollars avoided; enrollment rate; 30-day readmissions per enrollee; cost per enrollee | **Denominator: hospital days avoided per enrolled patient at 90 days**, converted to dollars once finance prices a bed-day and a readmission. |
| **Passion** | Patients' lives at home; catching what others missed; CHW home visits | **Catching the thing no one else caught.** Every story in the memo is this. `[ASSUMPTION: staff passion is inferred from the memo, not surveyed]` |

**Confirmation tests:**
1. A risk-adjusted comparison shows enrolled high-risk patients have fewer hospital days at 90 days than a fair comparator, after accounting for deaths.
2. Patients who had a pharmacist-found medication discrepancy fixed have lower 90-day readmission than similar patients without one.
3. Patients graduated with a completed clinician visit by day 45 do not show the readmission catch-up seen in days 31–90.

If test 1 fails after a clean analysis, the Hedgehog is wrong. In that case HFTP's honest value is penalty-window management only, and it should be funded and sized for that.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Leading Hospital at Home.** It is acute inpatient-level care, a different competence from transitions. It would add about 52% headcount (6 on 11.6 FTE) to a team that cannot yet price its own value. Offer HFTP as a design adviser on the discharge interface instead.
2. **Presenting enrolled-vs-not-enrolled 30-day rates as the program's effect.** The comparator includes nursing-facility discharges, unreachable patients and decliners, who are sicker or less stable.
3. **Claiming the hospital-wide rate and penalty as HFTP's result.** The hospital's 17.8% is just the blend of the two groups: (1,146 × 15.9% + 556 × 21.8%) ÷ 1,702 = 17.8%. The federal penalty is Medicare-only, risk-adjusted, covers several conditions and uses lagged data `[ASSUMPTION: general knowledge, unverified here]`.
4. **Chasing a blanket 75% enrollment rate and running the same protocol intensity for every patient.** Target by risk instead. Check whether weekend discharges are high-risk before funding the weekend shift. Note that 1,146 ÷ 1,702 = 67.3%, which suggests the enrollment rate is calculated over *all* HF discharges, even though the memo says enrollment is offered only to patients discharged home. Confirm the denominator before setting any target.
5. **Graduating patients at day 30 into nothing.**
6. **Spending pharmacist hours on hospital-wide accreditation tasks** unrelated to HF transitions.
7. **Using the 94% "would recommend" score as outcome evidence.** It measures satisfaction, from a 34% response rate.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

1. Home is the goal.
2. Tell the truth about results, including the unflattering ones.
3. Fix the cause, not the symptom (medications first).
4. Own the patient until someone else does.

**Core purpose:** help people with heart failure live longer, and well, at home.

**BHAG (one line, about 10 years):** No St. Aldric heart-failure patient leaves without a reconciled medication list and a clinician who owns them past day 30, and the region's HF patients spend more days at home than those of any peer system.
