# Bellmoor 250: Good to Great Run

## Verdict

**The run stops at Gate 0. Bellmoor 250 is a one-time ten-day event run by a commission that is chartered to dissolve.** Collins's framework measures greatness by whether it lasts and compounds over years, and the charter rules that out. No Hedgehog Concept or flywheel can honestly be built here.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** none. The subject isn't eligible.
- **Weakest link:** there is no flywheel. Instead there is a single point of failure: Calder Mills. That one sponsor accounts for 63% of pledges and still owes $160,000.
- **Top three things to stop:**
  1. Planning against 180,000 visits and 25 million impressions.
  2. Deferring the app decision. Decline it unless it is fully funded and has an owner after the commission dissolves.
  3. Spending commission time on "Bellmoor Days."
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Calder's $160,000 April installment depends on a company whose Bellmoor plant is "under review." The person who secured the pledge has retired.
- **Numbers that would change the answer:** written reconfirmation of Calder's installment, a cash-flow plan for the period after May, and an attendance method that someone can defend.

Greatness potential: 4/100

The book is about sustained greatness. *Social Sectors* includes **lasting endurance** as one of its three tests. A body that is legally required to end soon after its event cannot pass that test. This is how the commission was designed, and it was designed correctly. It is not a failing. But it means the Hedgehog Concept, the flywheel, Built to Last and the decline gates would all be decoration. Producing them anyway would be the kind of flattery the method exists to prevent.

### Reframes considered

If the council decides to pursue Bellmoor Days, or the library wants to build on the archive, either one is a strong candidate for a full run with its own steward.

## What to use instead: pre-mortem starting register

Two of Collins's ideas carry over to any project, including a finite one. The first is **confronting the brutal facts while keeping faith**. The second is **"first who"**: noticing when a critical relationship sits with the wrong person, or with nobody. The register below is a starting point for that review. It is not a Gate 4 artifact.

### Brutal facts

| # | Fact | Evidence (dossier) | Severity | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The lead sponsor is a single point of failure | Calder's $270,000 is 63% of the $431,000 in pledges and 20% of the total budget. $160,000 is due in April. The parent company is consolidating, and Bellmoor operations are "under review." | **Critical** | Chair + Sponsorship chair |
| 2 | The Calder relationship had one person behind it, and he has left | Ed Farrow brought in the pledge and has now retired. No successor has been named. | **Critical** | Sponsorship chair |
| 3 | Cash-flow cover runs out around the time money is due | The town loan covers the tall-ship and fireworks deposits only until May. The tall-ship final deposit is due in March, before Calder's April installment. | High | Treasurer |
| 4 | The attendance target has no basis | 180,000 visits equals 5.7 times the town's 31,400 population, or 18,000 a day. It came from a workshop. The only comparable is a larger city's unverified "over 100,000." | High | Program chair + ED |
| 5 | Revenue projections may rest on fact 4 | $121,500 in tickets and $70,000 in merchandise. `[ASSUMPTION]` These were sized against the same attendance figure. | Medium–High | Treasurer |
| 6 | The impressions target is far beyond the evidence | One headline announcement (the tall ship) reached 1.4 million impressions. Hitting 25 million would take about 18 more of comparable reach. | Medium | Communications |
| 7 | The app adds unfunded scope with no owner after the festival | $96,000 quote less a $20,000 offer leaves $76,000 unbudgeted. The commission dissolves, so a "legacy" app would have no one to maintain it. The 72% figure measures stated intent, not use. | Medium | Youth chair + Treasurer |
| 8 | Volunteer gap | 410 of 600 signed up (68%). The pageant cast alone is 300. `[ASSUMPTION]` Some of the cast overlaps with the volunteers. | Medium | Volunteers chair |
| 9 | "A lasting legacy" has no definition | No measure or owner is attached to it. | Medium | Legacy chair |

**Faith statement:** the town will turn out for its own 250th, and the parts built to last (the archive, the symphony and the time capsule) will outlive the commission that made them.

### Next 90 days

1. **Reconfirm Calder above the vacancy.** Seek a meeting with Calder or parent-company leadership now, rather than waiting for Farrow's successor. Ask for the $160,000 to be confirmed in writing or paid early. *Owners: Chair + Sponsorship chair.*
2. **Build a "no-Calder" budget.** List the cuts in priority order if the $160,000 does not arrive. Ask the town whether the cash-flow loan can be extended past May. Make sure the March tall-ship deposit is funded independently of Calder. *Owners: Treasurer + ED.*
3. **Decide the app in January, and the recommended answer is no.** Decline it as commission spending. Revisit only if it is 100% externally funded and a body that survives the commission (for example, the library) commits in writing to own and maintain it. The walking-tour content could feed the oral-history archive instead. *Owners: Youth chair + Treasurer.*
4. **Re-base the targets.** Replace 180,000 with a figure built from venue capacity, a counting method and per-event estimates. Re-check the ticket and merchandise projections against it. Replace the 25 million impressions with something the commission can verify, such as coverage in named outlets or ticket sell-through. *Owners: Program chair + ED.*
5. **Define the legacy and hand it over properly.** Finish the remaining 99 oral-history interviews. Sign a handover agreement with the library. Name a permanent owner for the symphony score, the time capsule and its opening date, and a "Bellmoor Days" handover pack for the council: staging inventory, sponsor contacts, lessons learned, and the volunteer list with each volunteer's consent. Then stop working on Bellmoor Days itself. *Owner: Legacy chair.*

### A note for the chair

The book still has something to offer. The commission can practice its habits: facing facts, having the right person in each critical seat, and saying no to good-but-off-mission ideas like the app. What it can't do is become a Good to Great company, because it was built to finish. Under Collins's definition, the greatness available to Bellmoor 250 lives in what it **hands off**. That makes the handover in action 5 the most "Collins" thing the commission can do. It is the nearest equivalent to building a clock instead of telling the time.
