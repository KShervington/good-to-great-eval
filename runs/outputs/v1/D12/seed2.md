# Bellmoor 250: Good to Great run

## Verdict

**Gate 0 FAIL: the framework doesn't fit Bellmoor 250 itself.** Bellmoor 250 is a ten-day event, and the commission running it dissolves after its audit. Good to Great depends on compounding over years, and this subject has no years ahead of it. There is no honest Hedgehog Concept or flywheel to build for it, and inventing one would mislead the chair.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Calder Mills owes **$160,000** (12% of the budget), due in April. The person who secured that pledge has retired, and no successor has been named. The regional paper reports that Calder's Bellmoor operations are "under review." The bridge loan stops covering deposits in May.

**Do instead:** run a production risk review. Build a no-Calder budget. Say no in January to the unbudgeted $96k app unless another body will own and fund it. Replace the 180,000-visit target with one that has a method behind it.

**What would change the answer:** the council chartering an ongoing body (such as Bellmoor Days), or the library's oral-history archive becoming the subject.

Greatness potential: 5/100

### Reframing attempts

| Candidate ongoing subject | Fits the framework? | Who is the steward? | Why it isn't *this* run |

| **Bellmoor Days** (annual festival) | Yes, if chartered | Town council, as a new body | The charter forbids the commission from continuing; the council would have to create and fund it separately |

Each real ongoing endeavor has a steward who outlives the commission. The commission cannot steward any of them, so no reframing keeps the subject and the requester together. Result: **FAIL; run ends here.**

### A note for the chair

This is not a judgment on the festival's quality. Collins defines "great" as performance that lasts beyond one person, one product, or one lucky break, and a commemoration is built to happen once. Asking it to be "great" in that sense pushes the commission toward the very things that threaten its delivery: making itself permanent, adding legacy projects with no owner, and adopting targets that sound big. The test that suits Bellmoor 250 is different: deliver ten excellent days on budget, and leave behind legacy pieces that each have a permanent owner.

## What to use instead

**Method:** an event-production risk review. That means a pre-mortem ("it's the day after the festival and it went badly; why?"), a dated critical path, a cash-flow plan with downside scenarios, and a scope freeze.

**Three Collins ideas still work for a one-off, used as tools rather than as the framework:**
- **Confronting the brutal facts:** keep faith that the festival will be excellent, and look squarely at the cash risk.
- **A stop-doing list:** scope discipline for a fixed budget and date.
- **"Clock building":** every legacy item needs an owner who outlives the commission.

## Findings from the dossier that a risk review should start with

These are not gate verdicts. They are the facts the dossier already shows, quantified.

**1. Calder Mills concentration and timing (severity: critical)**
- Calder's pledge of $270k is **63%** of all sponsorship ($431k). The other 10 sponsors total $161k.
- The outstanding $160k is **11.7%** of the $1,362,500 budget.
- The money arrives in the wrong order. The tall-ship final deposit is due in March, Calder pays in April, and loan coverage for deposits ends in May.
- Three signals point the same way. The pledge's champion (Ed Farrow) has retired with no named successor. The parent company is consolidating production at its southern plant over 18 months. Its Bellmoor operations are "under review."
- It is not known whether the pledge is a signed agreement approved at parent-company level. `[ASSUMPTION: it is a local-level commitment]`

**2. The attendance target has no method behind it (severity: high)**
- 180,000 visits ÷ 31,400 residents = **5.7 visits per resident**, or about 18,000 a day for ten days.
- The only comparison is a neighboring city's unverified "over 100,000."
- The ticket ($121.5k) and merchandise ($70k) projections may rest on this number. `[ASSUMPTION]`

**3. The media target is a vanity metric (severity: medium)**
- 25M impressions would take about **18 tall-ship-sized announcements** at 1.4M each.
- It doesn't drive any decision, so drop it or replace it with something that does.

**4. The app is unbudgeted scope with no owner after dissolution (severity: high)**
- Net cost is $96k − $20k = **$76k**, about 5.6% of the budget, with no budget line.
- The evidence for it is stated intent (72% of 640 students). That is not usage.
- An AR app needs hosting, OS updates and maintenance after the festival. Nobody exists to do that, so the "most visible legacy piece" would likely stop working within a year or two. `[ASSUMPTION]`
- The decision falls in January, before the Calder answer arrives in April.

**5. Bellmoor Days is scope creep for the commission, but a good idea for the council (severity: medium)**
- The commission shouldn't pursue it.
- Its most useful contribution would be a **handover pack** for the council:
  - real attendance figures with the method stated
  - a volunteer list with consent to recontact
  - sponsor results and contacts
  - actual costs per event

**6. Legacy needs a steward check (severity: medium)**

| Legacy item | Permanent owner | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Oral-history archive | Library ✓ | 141 of 240 interviews (59%) |
| Symphonic work (score, performance rights) | ? `[ASSUMPTION: unassigned]` | Movements 1–2 delivered |
| Time capsule | ? (presumably town) | Not specified |
| AR app | None | Proposed |

"A lasting legacy for Bellmoor" becomes measurable as: **every legacy item has a named owner that outlives the commission, and the archive is complete and accessioned.**

**7. Volunteers (severity: low to medium)**
- 410 of 600 signed up (68%).
- It is unclear whether the 300-person pageant cast counts toward the target. That is a missing number.
