# Good to Great: Pacific Crest Financial Corporation

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Be the safest, best-matched home lender in the home state. Gather household savings through the densest branch network in the state and lend them back as small, conservative adjustable-rate mortgages, earning a steady spread without carrying the rate or credit risk competitors are taking on.

**Weakest flywheel link:** Stable, low-cost household deposits. Deregulation is turning savers into rate-shoppers, and riskier rivals can pay more for their money.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop investing in the brokerage and real-estate services business.
2. Stop buying other thrifts for size or rank.
3. Stop holding the legacy fixed-rate book unhedged.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** An old fixed-rate mortgage book of unknown size is still funded by deposits whose cost moves with market rates.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Return on equity and net income trend
- Size and yield of the fixed-rate book
- Home-state deposit and mortgage share
- Profitability of the new non-lending units

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is a deliberately narrow institution. It is the home-state household's default place to save and to borrow for a home. It is known for never having to be rescued, whatever rates do. Almost every asset reprices in step with its deposits. Credit losses stay at the bottom of the industry because it makes small, well-underwritten residential loans in markets it knows branch by branch. It grows when its existing metros generate more households, not when deregulation makes a new product legal. It judges itself on returns per unit of risk carried, not on asset size or national rank. When riskier competitors stumble, it has the capital and the reputation to collect their depositors and the best of their offices at sensible prices. It acts as a disciplined buyer, not a builder of an empire. `[ASSUMPTION: this is the version the evidence supports; the company has not stated it.]`

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *The lowest-risk, rate-matched home lender in its home state, turning a dense branch network's household savings into small adjustable-rate mortgages.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | Ruled out (cannot be best) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Conservative ARM-based home lending in one large state; (b) full-service "financial supermarket"; (c) national thrift roll-up; (d) branch-based household savings gathering | **(a) + (d):** pioneered variable-rate lending in the 1970s; about 60% of its book was variable by decade's end; all new loans have been adjustable since regulators allowed it; branches in nearly every major metro in the state | Brokerage, real-estate services, commercial or speculative real estate, national secondary-market scale (Rival K is structurally bigger there), high-yield investing |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per office; profit per household relationship; profit per dollar of assets; **profit per unit of interest-rate and credit risk carried** | **Profit per unit of rate-and-credit risk carried.** Profit per office is the operating sub-metric. | Profit per dollar of assets rewards exactly the asset-gathering that is sinking riskier peers |
| **Passion** | Home ownership for ordinary households; prudence and long tenure; "being big" | Helping ordinary households save and own homes, run prudently `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from 60-year mortgage heritage and the conservative reputation; no staff or culture evidence in the dossier]` | Passion for scale. The "second-largest" framing is a warning sign, not a passion. |

**Denominator:** profit per unit of interest-rate and credit risk carried. Operationally this means after-tax profit divided by a blend of the repricing gap and nonperforming assets. A simpler stand-in is ROE alongside the one-year repricing gap and foreclosure ratio, read together.

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Early ARMs kept the company standing through the rate squeeze. Conservative lending has avoided repeating the early-1960s foreclosure spike, which peaked at $23.2M and cost shareholders their dividend.
- **Rules things out?** Yes. It rules out last year's brokerage and real-estate purchase, most out-of-state expansion, and the high-risk assets deregulation now allows.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It means growing slower than peers, possibly losing the size rank, and unwinding a fresh acquisition.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
- Three-year ROE at or above the large-thrift peer median.
- A ROE gap between the ARM book and the fixed book showing that ARMs are what drive profit.
- Branch-level data showing that density lowers deposit cost.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   [1] Densest branch network in home-state metros
              │
              ▼
   [2] Stable, lower-cost household savings deposits      ◄── WEAKEST LINK
              │
              ▼
   [3] Small, conservatively underwritten ADJUSTABLE-rate home loans
              │
              ▼
   [4] Rate-matched spread + low credit losses
       (high profit per unit of risk carried = denominator)
              │
              ▼
   [5] Capital strength & reputation for safety through every rate cycle
              │
              ▼
   [6] Deepen existing metros: in-fill offices, absorb distressed
       competitors' depositors/offices at disciplined prices ──► back to [1]
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **De-risk turn [3→4].** Size the legacy fixed-rate book. Use the swap program Rival K is offering, or other tools, to make it liquid and then sell or hedge it. Converting loans into mortgage-backed securities does not remove rate risk by itself. It makes the risk tradable, so the company can shed it.
2. **Defend turn [1→2].** Measure deposit cost and profit by office. Keep core household savers through convenience and trust rather than by matching the top rates deregulated rivals pay.

**Doom-loop check:** There are early signs. In three years the company made one large merger (Year −2) and then bought a brokerage and real-estate firm (last year), and it is openly debating more thrift acquisitions and out-of-state expansion. That pattern of a new direction each year, driven by what deregulation newly permits, is the beginning of a doom loop. It is not one yet. The ARM strategy has been held consistently for over a decade, and that consistency is the asset to protect.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop investing in brokerage and real-estate services.** Freeze capital to the unit now, and decide within 90 days whether to sell or wind it down.
2. **Stop acquiring thrifts for size or rank.** Buy only offices and deposits in existing metros, at distressed prices, with books that pass the risk denominator.
3. **Stop holding the legacy fixed-rate book unhedged.**
4. **Stop originating any fixed-rate loan for the portfolio.** Already effectively the case; write it into policy so it survives a rate dip.
5. **Stop considering the high-risk assets deregulation now permits.** No speculative real estate, development lending or high-yield securities, whatever peers do. `[ASSUMPTION: specific asset classes are illustrative; the dossier says only "riskier assets"]`
6. **Stop expanding beyond the home state** until the hedgehog is confirmed in-state.
7. **Stop measuring success by national rank or asset size** in board reports and executive pay.
8. **Stop competing for deposits on headline rate alone.** Let rate-chasing money go to the rivals paying for it.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** To help ordinary households save safely and own their homes.

**Core values:**
1. **Prudence first.** Never risk the institution for a quarter's growth.
2. **Stewardship of savers' money.** Deposits are a trust, not cheap funding.
3. **Match what we owe to what we own.**
4. **Long view.** Judge decisions on rate cycles, not years.

**BHAG (about 20 years):** *Become the home lender most households in the state trust with their savings, and go two full rate cycles without a loss year or a dividend cut.* This was kept to a business-scale BHAG because the subject is an institution with a decades-long horizon.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Executive pay tied to ROE and the risk-adjusted denominator, not asset size.
- A written acquisition and new-business test, requiring a pass on all three circles.
- An ALM head with veto power over unmatched growth.
- A board-level rate-shock and credit dashboard.
- A succession pipeline drawn from lending and risk, not deal-making.
