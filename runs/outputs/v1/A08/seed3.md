## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is a narrow, almost boring machine. It gathers household savings across its home state and lends them back out as plain, small adjustable-rate home mortgages. Its operating costs are the lowest in the industry, its loan losses stay near zero, and its asset yields reprice about as fast as its deposit costs. Through the late-1970s rate shock it kept earning, and it will keep earning while deregulated competitors chase commercial real estate, development deals and brokered deposits. When those competitors blow up, it is still standing with the capital to buy their branches cheaply, but only where the purchase deepens its in-state franchise. It does not sell stocks, run real-estate brokerages or chase size. It measures itself on one number, risk-adjusted profit per dollar of mortgage assets across a full rate cycle, and it turns down anything that doesn't move that number.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop expanding the brokerage and real-estate services business.** Freeze its capital now, and decide within 90 days whether to sell or wind it down.
2. **Stop moving into the asset classes deregulation now allows**: commercial real estate, construction and development lending, direct equity real-estate investment, and high-yield securities.
3. **Stop originating unhedged fixed-rate loans for the portfolio.** Already the case for new home loans; keep it absolute, with no exceptions for "relationship" business.
4. **Stop acquiring other thrifts for size.** Any deal must cut cost per dollar of assets within the home state and come with a clean, independently marked loan book.
5. **Stop expanding out of state** until the in-state franchise is clearly the lowest-cost operator.
6. **Stop paying up for brokered or hot-money deposits** to fund growth.
7. **Stop measuring success by total assets or national rank** ("second-largest in the nation"). Report risk-adjusted profit per mortgage dollar instead.
8. **Stop advertising spend that can't show a lower cost of funds.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Lend only what we'd be glad to own through a recession.
2. Every dollar of cost is a dollar taken from a saver or a borrower.
3. Match what we owe to what we own.
4. Tell the board the bad news first.

**Core purpose:** to help households in our state save safely and own their homes, at the lowest sustainable cost.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** the most profitable and safest home lender in the country per dollar of assets, through every rate cycle, never needing a rescue and never cutting the dividend.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A published single-number scorecard.
- An independent credit veto.
- A rate-risk limit set by the board.
- Rules for acquisitions: in-state only, cost per dollar of assets must fall, and loans are independently marked.
- An annual stop-doing review.
- CEO succession drawn from inside the lending and operations culture.
