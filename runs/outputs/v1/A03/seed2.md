# Brennard Laboratories: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Brennard can be the best in the world at reliable, standardized products that make health care measurably more cost-effective for hospitals and clinicians. It should compete only where it can be #1 or #2, and judge itself by **profit per employee**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** hospital trust in Brennard's quality. The IV recall linked to 49 deaths broke it, and every other link depends on it.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Sell or exit golf balls, household gloves and the non-dairy creamer.
2. Stop the blockbuster drug-discovery race against research-led rivals.
3. Stop spreading capital evenly across five directions.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Brennard's own product killed patients. It still hasn't regained the IV lead. Until quality is beyond question, it has no right to a hospital-centered strategy.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:** profit and return on capital by division, market share in IV, infant formula and diagnostics, and total sales and earnings.

Greatness potential: 32/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard is a focused health-care company that hospitals and clinicians trust because its products work the same way every time. That standardization is the founding physician's original idea, applied at scale. It sells into the hospital: IV solutions, hospital drugs such as its anesthetic, nutritionals including infant formula, and diagnostics. In every category it enters, it holds the #1 or #2 position, and it sells on lower total cost of analysis and treatment, not on novelty. It has stopped trying to out-discover research-led drug makers and stopped trying to out-market consumer-goods companies. Its quality and regulatory record sets the standard for its industry, so the scar from the recall becomes a reason customers trust it. Capital goes only to lines that pass the #1/#2 test and lift profit per employee. The company runs on mechanisms (portfolio reviews, an independent quality function, management succession) rather than on any single chief executive.

**Hedgehog sentence:** *Brennard will be the best in the world at reliable, standardized products that make health care more cost-effective for hospitals and clinicians, competing only where it can be #1 or #2, measured by profit per employee.*

**Does it pass the three tests?**
- **Explains past wins?** Partly. The founding business (standardized pills) and the former IV leadership fit it. The sweetener does not. It was a profitable consumer product that one regulatory decision erased.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: consumer goods, the blockbuster discovery race, and any category where Brennard would be #3 or lower.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means walking away from the chief executive's preferred consumer path, from the scientists hired under the previous chief executive, and from the prestige of "big pharma."

**What would confirm it:**
- Division economics show that hospital, nutritional and hospital-drug lines earn above-average profit per employee.
- Brennard holds or can reach #1/#2 in at least three hospital categories.
- Hospital buyers say, when surveyed, that total cost of care drives their purchasing.
- IV share recovers once quality is proven.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Link 1, quality.** Make the IV line demonstrably the safest on the market, with independent audits and published results.
2. **Link 6, exits.** Exit the consumer lines that fail the hedgehog test and move that capital and management attention to links 1 and 3.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are present.
- A new consumer push followed the sweetener ban, which looks like reaching for replacement earnings.
- Five directions are funded at once.
- Strategy has shifted with each change of chief executive: research expansion under the previous one, diversification under the current one.
- The response to crises has been defensive: protesting the ban and pleading no contest.

None of this is fatal yet, but the pattern is a doom loop in the making. The cure is to commit to one loop and push it for years.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Exit golf balls, household rubber gloves and the non-dairy creamer.** None of them serves hospitals or clinicians, and none can be #1/#2 without heavy marketing spend.
2. **Review the tabletop sweetener** for exit. It is a consumer product that also concentrates regulatory risk. Brennard has already lost one sweetener to a ban.
3. **Stop competing for blockbuster new drugs** with research-led companies. Keep hospital drugs such as the anesthetic, and harvest the off-patent antibiotic for cash rather than defending it.
4. **Stop spreading capital across five directions.** Fund only lines that pass the #1/#2 test and the profit-per-employee test.
5. **Stop acquiring to replace lost earnings.** No acquisitions until the hedgehog is confirmed, and after that only acquisitions that accelerate the flywheel.
6. **Stop treating regulators as adversaries.** Exceeding regulatory standards becomes a selling point. Protest is not the default response.
7. **Stop launching new consumer brands,** however attractive the opportunity looks.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION; validate with employees]`
1. Quality is our license to exist. We don't ship anything we wouldn't use on our own family.
2. Clinicians and patients first. Cost-effectiveness is a form of care.
3. Candor: bad news travels up fast and is welcomed.
4. Discipline: we compete only where we can lead.

**Core purpose:** make health care dependable and affordable for the people who deliver it and the patients who receive it.

**BHAG (20-year):** be #1 or #2 in every hospital category we serve, and be the supplier hospitals name first when asked who lowers their cost of care.

**Mechanisms (clock building):**
- Annual portfolio review against the #1/#2 test and profit per employee.
- An independent quality function with authority to stop shipments and a direct line to the board.
- Explicit succession planning for the CEO and division heads, so the company no longer depends on sudden deaths and personal loyalty.
- A capital-allocation rule: no funding outside the hedgehog without board-level justification.
