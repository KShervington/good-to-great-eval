## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Brennard is the company hospitals and clinicians cannot do without and never have to doubt. It is no longer a drug company or a consumer company. It is the most trusted maker of the everyday clinical products that decide what care costs and how well it works: IV solutions and IV drugs such as its anesthetic, clinical and infant nutrition, and diagnostics that cut the cost of analysis and treatment. It wins on narrow, defensible positions rather than on blockbuster discovery, and it measures itself by how much profit it earns from each hospital relationship. Its quality record is good enough to sell on. It has stopped chasing whatever replaces the last lost profit, whether a consumer brand, an acquisition or a moonshot drug. That return to its founder's original idea, standardized and reliable products for doctors, is what makes it great.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Golf balls.** They have no connection to health care.
2. **Household rubber gloves.** These are consumer goods, not clinical products.
3. **Non-dairy creamer and tabletop sweetener.** These are consumer food, with the same exposure that already destroyed the sweetener business once.
4. **Racing Rival U for blockbuster prescription drugs.** Brennard has no breakthrough in sight, and Rival U is the research leader. Harvest the off-patent antibiotic, sedatives and vitamins. Keep hospital drugs such as the IV anesthetic.
5. **Splitting capital evenly across five divisions.** New capital goes only to Hedgehog businesses.
6. **Acquiring to replace lost profit.** The urge to "replace the sweetener" is how the consumer group was built.
7. **Treating regulators as the enemy.** The lesson of the ban and the recall is to build quality and regulatory strength, not to protest harder.
8. **Copying Rival U's step into unrelated fields.** Its move into seeds is not a model.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- No sterile product ships without an independent quality release.
- Every new product must show a hospital cost or outcome benefit, and a reason it is hard to copy.
- Every capital request states its effect on profit per hospital account.

**Framework and freedom:** Divisions decide freely *how* to win inside the Hedgehog Concept. Nobody decides alone to go outside it.

**Bureaucracy check:** after the recall, rules have probably piled up. Replace checklist layers with one accountable quality owner who has real authority. `[ASSUMPTION]`

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:**
  1. Patient safety comes before shipment.
  2. Usefulness to clinicians, not novelty.
  3. Candor about bad news.
  4. Cost-effectiveness in our products and in how we run the company.
- **Core purpose:** to help clinicians give better care at lower cost with products they never have to doubt. `[ASSUMPTION: derived from the founding story]`
- **BHAG (20-year):** *Every hospital we serve counts on Brennard for at least one product it would not want to run without, and in all that time no hospital has had reason to question its safety.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A quality chief with authority to stop shipments and a direct line to the board.
  - A capital rule: Hedgehog businesses only.
  - Divisional reporting by profit per account.
  - A formal succession plan with at least two internal candidates.
  - Blameless reviews after every serious incident.
