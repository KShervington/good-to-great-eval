# Good to Great: Brennard Laboratories (as of early Year 0)

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Brennard makes the products that make hospital care cheaper and more reliable (clinical nutrition, IV therapy, diagnostics, hospital drugs), and it measures itself by profit per employee.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** hospital trust in Brennard's quality. The IV recall broke it, and every other link depends on it.

**Stop doing:**
1. Consumer brands unrelated to health: golf balls, household gloves, creamer, tabletop sweetener.
2. Competing for blockbuster drugs against research-led rivals.
3. Spreading capital across five divisions with no single test.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** contamination in Brennard's own IV bottles was linked to 49 deaths. The company pleaded no contest and has not regained its lead. The best strategy available runs straight through the customers it failed.

**Missing numbers that could change this:** profit and return on capital by division, total-company earnings trend, infant-formula and diagnostics market share, and research spending as a share of sales.

`Greatness potential: 38/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Brennard is the company hospitals turn to first when they need to treat patients reliably at lower cost. It feeds patients through clinical and infant nutrition. It gives them IV therapy and hospital-use drugs that never fail. It runs the diagnostics that tell clinicians what is wrong faster and cheaper. It does not sell golf balls, and it does not bet the company on finding the next wonder drug. Its manufacturing quality is the best in its industry and it can prove it. That proof wins hospital accounts. Those accounts carry volume across several product lines. Volume and focus raise profit per employee, and that profit funds the next product that lowers the cost of care. This is the founder's original idea of standardized, dependable medicine for doctors, aimed at the place where health-care cost pressure is heading. `[ASSUMPTION: hospital cost pressure will rise, per the hospital-products head's expectation]`

**Hedgehog sentence:** *Products that make hospital care cheaper and more reliable, measured by profit per employee.*

**Does it explain past wins?** Partly. The long-running hospital IV and nutrition lines and the antibiotic sold to doctors fit it. The sweetener did not fit, and its loss shows the danger of profit sources outside the core.

**Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out the whole consumer group, the blockbuster race, and most non-hospital diversification.

**Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It reverses the current CEO's favored strategy and points the company at the very market where it just failed.

**Confirmation tests (needed to move from hypothesis to PASS):**
1. Division data shows the hospital, nutrition and diagnostics lines earn returns at or above the company average.
2. Hospital purchasing managers rank cost-of-care and reliability as top buying criteria, with roughly 20 or more interviews done.
3. The IV share decline stops within 12–18 months of a visible quality overhaul.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Provably reliable, cost-saving hospital products
   │          (nutrition, IV, hospital drugs, diagnostics)
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │   2. Hospital trust → preferred-supplier status & contracts
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │   3. More product lines carried per hospital (volume)
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │   4. Manufacturing scale + narrow portfolio → lower unit cost
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │   5. Rising PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   └── 6. Reinvest in quality systems, nutrition science,
          diagnostics instruments → next cost-saving product
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Rebuild link 1 → 2. Make quality visible and verifiable to hospitals through independent audits, published standards, and a quality chief with stop-ship authority.
2. Free capital for link 6 by exiting the consumer brands and moving it to diagnostics and nutrition.

**Doom-loop check:** the warning signs are already present.
- **Reaction without understanding:** each crisis (the ban, the recall) has been followed by more diversification.
- **New direction after new direction:** the previous CEO pushed research, while the current one pushes consumer brands.
- **Acquisitions as jump-starts:** the consumer group was assembled rather than grown.

Verdict: the company is at risk of a doom loop now. The loop above closes, but only if Brennard stops changing direction.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Golf balls, household rubber gloves and non-dairy creamer: divest.
2. Tabletop sweetener: divest it with the consumer group. It also concentrates regulatory risk of the kind the earlier ban made painful.
3. The blockbuster drug-discovery race against research-led rivals like Rival U.
4. Diversifying into unrelated businesses (Rival U's move into seeds is a temptation, not a template).
5. Funding all five divisions in proportion to their history instead of their fit with the Hedgehog Concept.
6. Lobbying regulators to reverse decisions instead of designing products that pass them.
7. Hiring senior leaders for adjacent industries the company is about to leave (for example, more consumer marketers).

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:** `[ASSUMPTION: drafted from the founding story; to be confirmed with long-tenured staff]`
1. Products clinicians can rely on, every unit.
2. Lower cost of care is a service to patients, not a sales line.
3. Tell the truth about failures quickly.
4. Science in service of practice.

**Core purpose:** make medicine dependable and affordable where patients are treated.

**BHAG (25-year):** by Year 25, become the supplier hospitals worldwide rank first for reliability and cost-of-care value, across nutrition, therapy and diagnostics, with no consequential product-safety recall in that period.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A quality function independent of the business units.
- Profit per employee by unit, reported every quarter.
- A capital-allocation rule: no investment outside the Hedgehog Concept without board-level review.
- Formal CEO succession with at least two internal candidates. The prior CEO's sudden death shows the cost of not having one.
