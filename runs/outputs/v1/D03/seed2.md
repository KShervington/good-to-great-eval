# Ranvik: Good to Great

**Verdict: good, not yet great. The January board vote decides which way it goes.**

**Hedgehog:** Ranvik is the fastest, safest way for a farmer to use a neighbor's machine, the first time and every time after, in counties dense enough that one is always close.

**Weakest flywheel link:** repeat rentals. 71% of surveyed renters swapped phone numbers with their owner. Only 11% rebooked that owner on Ranvik. Some of the "community" is the product walking off-platform.

**Top three stops:**
1. Input financing. The year-one model assumes $28M of loans; the facility is capped at $6M until those loans perform after harvest.
2. The two-state spring launch.
3. Charging the tenth rental between neighbors the same fee as the first.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** financing would take both engineers through planting season, with about 19 months of runway.

**Missing numbers that would change this:**
- How often matched pairs transact again off-platform.
- Same-owner rebooking before and after May's unmasking.
- Year-over-year renter retention.
- GMV per county outside the core.

Greatness potential: 17/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is where it is boring and obvious to share a neighbor's machine. In every county it serves, a farmer facing a wet week or a broken planter finds a machine within about 15 miles and books it in an afternoon. When the same two farmers deal again next season, they still book on Ranvik. They do it because protection, payment, hour-meter records and a lower repeat fee make the app easier and safer than a handshake, not because they are forced to. Ranvik earns more per listed machine every season. It grows county by county, adding a new one only when the last is dense. A rep playbook builds that density, not Ines personally. Ranvik does not lend money, sell inputs, or chase whatever fintech investors are funding this year.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 [1] Dense local supply: more machines listed per county
          │
          ▼
 [2] Fast nearby match in a critical window (fewer date-conflict declines)
          │
          ▼
 [3] Safe first rental: protection, card/ACH, hour-meter record
          │
          ▼
 [4] Repeat rentals stay on Ranvik  ◄── WEAKEST LINK
     (lower repeat-pair fee, protection only on Ranvik bookings, automatic usage records)
          │
          ▼
 [5] Higher net revenue per listed machine; owners earn more and list more machines
          │
          ▼
 [6] Neighbors see it work; owners and renters recruit others in the same county
          │
          └──────────────► back to [1]
```

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Close link 4.** Give repeat pairs an economic and safety reason to stay. One option is a reduced fee after the first booking between the same pair. Another is to reframe "Ranvik Pro" as a flat owner subscription that covers repeat bookings. Either way, market protection and hour-meter records as available only on Ranvik bookings.
2. **Strengthen link 2.** There were 1,140 declined requests, mostly date conflicts, giving a fill rate of about 82%. Fix calendar accuracy with reminders, quick "still available?" confirmations and peak-week prompts. Each decline is a lost first match. Some declines may also be stale calendars from owners renting off-app `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Doom-loop check:** the warning signs are present, though the loop has not started. Three new directions are proposed for one season (financing, two states, Pro). The biggest one comes from outside the flywheel. It is justified partly by competitors' fundraising and is meant to jump-start revenue rather than compound what works. The January decision is the fork.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't launch input financing this January.** Decline it as scoped (math in the appendix). Revisit only if all four revisit conditions are met:
   - Same-pair rebooking is at least 25% and net revenue per machine is up two seasons running.
   - Runway is at least 24 months after the next raise.
   - The product reinforces the flywheel. A pay-after-harvest option for *Ranvik rentals* is the only credit idea that clearly does.
   - Facility terms match realistic year-one volume, and any first-loss exposure is explicit.
2. **Don't launch two new states next spring.** The home 12 counties produce about $218k of GMV each. The other 29 average about $43k, roughly five times thinner. Densify what you already have.
3. **Stop charging a repeat pair the same 14% as a first match.** On a combine or planter booking (average about $2,500) the fee is around $330. That is a strong reason to make a phone call instead.
4. **Stop reporting relationship stories as proof of health without the matching leakage number.** The Ostrander and Kemp shared baler is a success for the community and zero revenue for Ranvik. Report both facts.
5. **Stop treating CEO-run barbecues as the main growth motion.** Hand them to field reps with a purpose: listings added, protection explained, repeat-pair offer shown.
6. **Stop seeking input-retailer and new-state dealer introductions** (the current investor ask). Ask instead for marketplace-pricing and insurance/protection advisors.
7. **Don't reintroduce phone masking.** Removing it was right for operations, since support tickets fell 38%. Win repeat bookings by being better than the phone call, not by hiding the phone number.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every new initiative names the flywheel link it pushes and its expected effect on net revenue per listed machine.
- Every investor update reports same-pair rebooking, year-over-year cohort retention and GMV per county.
- Enter no new county or state until existing ones clear a density threshold `[ASSUMPTION: e.g., ≥$100k GMV and ≥85% fill rate]`.
- Respond to booking requests within a set time during peak weeks.

**Framework (freedom within limits):**
- Field reps choose their own county tactics inside the density playbook.
- Engineers choose how to solve links 2 and 4.
- No new product line reaches a board vote without Paul's written pre-read and kill criteria.

**Bureaucracy check:** no heavy rules found. Phone masking was a rule that blocked real coordination, and it was rightly dropped.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION, inferred from the update]`:
