# Ranvik: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working):** Ranvik gets a farmer a protected, nearby high-value machine in the week the weather opens, one dense county at a time.

**Weakest flywheel link:** keeping repeat pairs on Ranvik. 71% of surveyed renters swapped numbers with their owner, but only 11% rebooked that owner in the app. Much of the "community" is revenue leaving the platform.

**Stop doing:**
1. Input financing. Decline it at the January board.
2. The two-state spring launch.
3. Reporting off-platform neighbor deals as a win.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the financing model assumes $28M of year-one loans. The facility is $6M until loans perform, and nobody will know whether they perform until after next harvest. That works out to roughly $150k in fees plus an interest share, and it costs both engineers through planting season.

**Numbers that would change this:** the off-platform repeat rate, net revenue per county outside the core, and who carries damage-protection losses.

`Greatness potential: 10/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the default answer in every county it serves to one question: *"The window just opened and I need a planter (or combine, or second baler) by Thursday. Where do I get one?"* It is very dense in each county before it opens the next one. Peak-week requests rarely bounce for date conflicts. Its damage protection, hour-meter records and payments make even a repeat rental between neighbors easier on Ranvik than off it. It does not lend money, it does not chase states it can't fill, and it does not mistake a friendly survey for a moat. Its economics show up as rising net revenue per county. That figure funds more field reps in the next adjacent county, and the loop repeats. It does not depend on Ines being at every barbecue.

**Denominator:** net revenue per active county per season. Today that is about $12.5k across 41 counties. Inside the 12 core counties it is about $29k. In the other 29 it is about $5.7k.

**Tests**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Combines and planters were 9% of bookings but 31% of GMV, with an average booking of about $2,500 against about $550 for everything else. Most were first-time pairings. The core counties, where density exists, produce 68% of GMV.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: lending, new states before density, an owner subscription aimed at revenue, community as the product, and subsidizing low-value repeat rentals that will leak anyway.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It says no to the lead investor's proposal and to the CEO's enthusiasm. It also accepts that some low-value repeat rentals between neighbors will leave the platform and are not worth fighting for.

**Status:** working hypothesis. **Confirmed if:**
1. Repeat high-value pairs rebook on Ranvik at a materially higher rate than low-value pairs once protection and records are tied to rebooking.
2. Net revenue per county in focused thin counties rises toward core levels.
3. The peak-window decline rate for high-value machines falls.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Pay-after-harvest input financing.** Decline at the January board. The only financing that could ever fit is deferring payment on Ranvik rentals themselves, and only after the marketplace is dense.
2. **The two-state spring launch.** The 29 non-core counties average about $43k GMV each, against about $219k in the core. Adding more thin counties now makes the problem worse.
3. **Counting phone-number swaps and direct sharing (Ostrander–Kemp) as success.** Report them as what they are: proof of matching value and evidence of leakage.
4. **"Ranvik Pro" as a subscription revenue test this season.** Fold any fleet-owner tools into the rebook and calendar work instead.
5. **Ines personally running every barbecue, fair and co-op meeting.** Field reps run the "Neighbors" events, reframed as high-value supply recruitment in thin counties.
6. **Spending promotions on low-value categories where repeat pairs leak anyway.** Point subsidy at peak-window high-value supply. [ASSUMPTION: promotion mix is not in the dossier]

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** no farmer loses a field window for lack of a machine.

**Core values:**
1. The farmer's window comes first.
2. Trust is earned one transaction at a time.
3. Tell the truth about the numbers, especially to ourselves.
4. Depth before breadth.

**BHAG (15–20 years):** in every major row-crop county in North America, any farmer can get a protected high-value machine within 20 miles in any planting or harvest week.

**Mechanisms that don't depend on Ines:**
- A county density threshold rule.
- The leakage dashboard.
- A field-rep event playbook.
- The CTO's counter-case slot.
- A carrier-backed protection program.
