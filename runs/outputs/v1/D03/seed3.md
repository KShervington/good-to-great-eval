# Ranvik: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. Say no to input financing in January.**

**Hedgehog Concept:** *Ranvik is the fastest, safest way for a farmer to put a stranger's high-value machine to work in a critical window, built one dense county at a time.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** keeping repeat pairs on the platform. 71% of renters swap phone numbers, but only 11% rebook the same owner on Ranvik.

**Stop doing:**
1. Input financing. Decline it at the January board.
2. Two new states next spring. Densify the counties next to the core 12 instead.
3. Counting phone swaps and off-app sharing as wins.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** financing would tie up both engineers for about 5 of your roughly 19 months of runway. Its model also assumes $28M in year one against a $6M facility that can only grow after loans perform.

**Numbers that would change this answer:**
- How many pairs who met on Ranvik keep renting to each other off the app.
- Repeat rates broken out by county.
- Recourse and first-loss terms on the facility.
- Who carries damage-protection losses.

Greatness potential: 22/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik owns the critical window. When a wet week compresses planting or a combine breaks mid-harvest, a farmer opens Ranvik and gets a protected, metered machine from within about 15 miles the same afternoon. This works in every county Ranvik operates in, not just the original 12. Owners of expensive machines list on Ranvik because it is the only way they will lend a $300k+ machine to someone they don't know `[ASSUMPTION on machine values]`. Repeat pairs stay on the platform because the protection, hour-meter records and guaranteed payment are worth more than the 14% fee. Low-value neighborly swaps that move off-app are accepted as free word of mouth. Ranvik expands ring by ring from proven density, never by planting flags. Its field playbook runs without Ines in the room, and its board judges every new product against one sentence.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Turn 4: make the on-platform repeat worth more than the phone call.**
   - One-tap rebooking for known pairs.
   - Standing seasonal arrangements.
   - Protection that applies only to Ranvik bookings, stated clearly.
   - Automatic invoices tied to hour-meter usage.
   - Hour-meter support for combine and planter brands first.
   - Don't fight leakage on rakes and hay equipment. Win it on machines owners can't afford to lend unprotected.
2. **Turn 2: peak-window fill.** 1,140 requests were declined, mostly for date conflicts. That is about 18% of requests and up to about $829k of GMV that didn't happen. Some of those renters probably booked elsewhere, so treat it as an upper bound. Fixes:
   - Auto-suggest the nearest available alternative on every decline.
   - Run a waitlist.
   - Have field reps pre-recruit planters and combines before spring.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs present.**
- One winter proposes three new directions at once: financing, two states and Pro.
- Financing is a big program meant to jump-start revenue rather than build on the existing loop.
- It reacts to fintech fundraising headlines.
- The update celebrates metrics, like phone swaps, that work against the engine.

None of these is committed yet. The January board is where the doom loop either starts or doesn't.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Input financing.** Decline at the January board. Revisit only if all of the following hold:
   - Net revenue per county is rising in at least one new ring.
   - Runway is above 24 months.
   - The product is tied to rentals, for example pay-after-harvest on rental fees, which sits closer to the Hedgehog Concept.
2. **Launching two new states next spring.** Densify the 6–8 counties adjacent to the core 12 instead.
3. **Counting phone-number swaps, "a neighbor I can call" and off-app equipment sharing as success metrics** in board and investor updates.
4. **Starting a paid Pro tier before leakage is measured.**
5. **Spreading field reps evenly across 41 counties.**
6. **Running the go-to-market through Ines personally.**
7. **Re-imposing phone masking to stop leakage.** It fought how farmers actually work at the field edge, and turning it off cut support tickets by 38%. Earn the repeat booking instead of trapping it.
8. **Letting fintech funding rounds set the strategy story.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every booking offers protection.
- Every decline gets a suggested alternative.
- Liquidity and leakage are reviewed weekly by county.
- No new county ring until the previous ring reaches its density threshold.
- Every new product proposal gets a one-page Hedgehog test before engineering time is spent.

**Framework boundaries:**
- Field reps choose tactics freely inside their assigned ring.
- Engineers choose how to improve turns 2–4, but not whether to work outside them.
- The team is small and the rules are few. Keep it that way.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** keep every farm's critical window covered by making a neighbor's idle machine safely available.

**Core values:**
1. A farmer's critical window comes first.
2. Safe for both sides, every time.
3. Earn the repeat booking; don't trap it.
4. Density before reach.
5. Honest numbers, especially the bad ones.

**BHAG (15–20 years):** across North American row-crop country, any farmer can get the machine they need, within 15 miles, protected and booked within the hour, during planting and harvest.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A written county-density playbook run by reps.
- A weekly liquidity and leakage dashboard.
- Damage protection underwritten by a carrier partner.
- A Hedgehog test as a standing board agenda item for any new line of business.
