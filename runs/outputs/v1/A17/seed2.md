# Stanwick Mailing Systems: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Stanwick can be the best in the world at moving a business's outgoing mail accurately and cheaply, through a leased, serviced relationship at every customer site, and it grows by raising profit per customer relationship.

**Weakest flywheel link:** the product engine. Stanwick has shown no ability in office electronics, and its patents are licensed free to all comers. A licensee with a better electronic meter could break the loop.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. No more retail-systems or point-of-sale ventures.
2. Stop fighting the copier giant head-on on price.
3. No acquisitions that buy growth outside the mailroom.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the $42M write-off, about 11% of a year's revenue, came from chasing a market others lead. Management cannot yet show which of its lines earn their capital.

**Missing numbers that would change this:** profit and return on capital by line (copiers above all), customer count and profit per customer, installed-base trend versus licensees, and the new CEO's record.

`Greatness potential: 58/100`

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Stanwick is the company every American business trusts with its mail. Its field force visits nearly every serious business mailer. It turns those visits into knowledge of each customer's mail flow. It then upgrades that customer from a basic meter to a complete mailing system: scales, folding and inserting, high-volume and electronic meters. The credit arm finances these upgrades. Its main moat is not patents, which it must license free. The moat is the installed base, the service relationship and its standing with the Postal Service. It measures itself by profit per customer, not revenue growth. It says no to retail systems, general copiers and anything else where a bigger, better-funded firm already leads. Meters stop being treated as a mature cash cow to fund diversification. They become the base of a narrower, deeper business that gets better every year.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** entering retail point-of-sale or electronic retail systems, in any form.
2. **Stop** competing head-on with the copier giant on price for the general copier market. Narrow copiers to small units sold through existing mailing relationships, or exit.
3. **Stop** acquiring companies to buy growth outside the mailroom.
4. **Stop** describing meters as a "mature" cash cow whose job is to fund diversification.
5. **Stop** setting goals in total revenue. Set them in profit per customer and return on capital.
6. **Stop** letting the credit subsidiary finance anything other than Stanwick equipment.
7. **Stop** putting new core capital into price-marking. It can grow on its own cash flow, or be evaluated for sale if its returns fall below mailing's.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** help businesses send their communications accurately, cheaply and reliably.

**Core values:**
1. **Integrity with the Postal Service.** Meters print postage, which is effectively government revenue, so trust is existential.
2. **Service that shows up.** The field relationship is the product.
3. **The customer's total mailing cost comes before our product mix.**
4. **Disciplined stewardship of capital.**

**BHAG (20 years):** *Make the licked stamp extinct in American business, so that every serious business mailer runs its mail through a system Stanwick installed and services.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- profit-per-customer reporting by territory;
- a formal stop-doing review in annual planning;
- a postal-relations function that outlasts any single executive;
- succession planning for every division head;
- a capital rule that no new line gets core capital unless it raises profit per mailing customer.
