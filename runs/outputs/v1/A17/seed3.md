# Stanwick Mailing Systems: Good to Great

**Verdict: Good, not yet great. Stanwick's one best-in-world position is the business mailroom, and the rest of the portfolio is diluting it.**

- **Hedgehog:** *Stanwick gets a business's mail out the door. It owns the meter-centered mailroom and earns a rising profit per customer site through leases, service and mail-handling equipment.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning routine meter service visits into placements of other mailroom equipment. This is unproven.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:** (1) all retail point-of-sale work; (2) price-fighting the copier giant for general office share; (3) buying growth in markets where Stanwick doesn't own the customer relationship.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** a new CEO takes over while the "diversify into office electronics" argument is still live, just after a $42M write-off showed Stanwick can't win there.
- **Numbers that would change this answer:** profit and return on capital by business line, profit per customer site, and copier market share.

Greatness potential: 64/100

---

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick is not an office-equipment conglomerate. It is the indispensable operator of America's business mail stream. Each leased meter is the anchor of a site relationship, and a field force with unmatched reach visits those sites on a regular schedule. Stanwick uses that access to place scales, folders, inserters, sorters and financing, all of them things that move mail. It earns more per customer site every year. It works with the U.S. Postal Service as a trusted partner, so that metered business mail is the cheapest and easiest way for the Postal Service to handle volume. It funds the electronics that make its meters and mailrooms better rather than chasing electronics markets that belong to larger companies. Its moat is the service network plus the Postal Service's trust. Patents are not the moat: the consent decree already gives them away for free.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *Be the best in the world at getting a business's mail out the door, and earn a rising profit per customer site from the meter-centered mailroom.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Postage meters; office equipment; retail systems; copiers; field service; mailroom systems | **Meter-centered business mailroom systems plus the service network behind them.** 85–90% meter share, a license-free decree that still hasn't produced a credible rival, and Postal Service approval history all point here. **Cannot be best at:** copiers (a larger company invented the category), retail POS (proven by the $42M loss), or general office electronics (Rival A and larger firms are in the same scramble). |
| **Economic engine denominator** | Profit per meter; profit per product sold; profit per customer site; profit per field-service call | **Profit per customer site** `[ASSUMPTION]`. Leasing is mandatory and the field force visits each site repeatedly, so the site relationship is the unit that compounds. This denominator rewards adding mailroom equipment, financing and service to sites Stanwick already serves. |
| **Passion** | The meter heritage; serving business customers; "being a tech company" | **The mailroom and the meter.** The company's identity is built on the meter `[ASSUMPTION: the passion is broad and not confined to the retiring chairman]`. |

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Growth over five decades came from meters and the Post Office relationship.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: point-of-sale, general copiers, and retail expansion of the price-marking business.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It means telling a new CEO and Wall Street that the "mature, regulated" business is the growth engine.

**Confirmation evidence (12–24 months):**
1. Meters and mailroom equipment earn the highest return on capital of any line.
2. Revenue per customer site rises when the field force carries adjacent mailroom products.
3. The Postal Service's new rate-setting treats metered mail neutrally or favorably.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Turn 3→4:** equip and pay the field force to identify and place mail-handling equipment at existing meter sites, with the credit subsidiary used only for Stanwick mailroom leases.
2. **Turn 6→1:** fund a postal-relations and engineering effort to shape how the new Postal Service prices and processes metered mail.

**Doom-loop check:** warning signs are present. These include a lurch into retail POS through a joint venture rather than built capability, a copier entry against a giant on price, an acquisition (price-marking) outside the mail stream, and now a leadership change at the same time. That is the pattern of seeking big moves to jump-start growth. The loop closes on mailroom logic, so this gate passes, but the company has been drifting toward a doom loop.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all retail point-of-sale activity.** No follow-on investment, no "phase two" of the venture.
2. **Stop price-fighting the copier giant for general office share.** Either reposition copiers as a back-office product sold through existing relationships with a return target, or divest.
3. **Stop acquiring growth in markets where Stanwick does not own the customer relationship** (retail systems, standalone office electronics, computers).
4. **Stop expanding price-marking into adjacent retail systems.** Harvest it and evaluate a sale.
5. **Stop the credit subsidiary from financing anything that isn't Stanwick mail-handling equipment.**
6. **Stop letting the field force carry products unrelated to the mail stream.** Its time is the scarcest resource on the flywheel.
7. **Stop describing meters internally and to investors as "mature."** That framing has been pushing capital toward weaker businesses.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** to help businesses communicate reliably and efficiently through the mail.
- **Core values:**
  1. **Integrity with the public's money.** A meter prints currency, so trust with the Postal Service is non-negotiable.
  2. **Service at the customer's site.** Show up, fix it, keep the mail moving.
  3. **Engineering rigor on the mail stream.**
  4. **Discipline over size.** Grow where the company can be the best, nowhere else.
- **BHAG (20 years):** *Make metered and Stanwick-processed mail the way the majority of American business mail enters the postal system, with Stanwick the partner the Postal Service relies on to run it.* (Metered mail was 43% of U.S. postage thirteen years ago; current figure unknown.)
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Line-level return-on-capital reporting that is mandatory before any new market entry.
  - A Hedgehog test applied to every acquisition and venture proposal.
  - A field-force compensation plan tied to profit per site.
  - A standing postal-relations function.
  - Succession planning below the CEO, so the next transition is not tied to one legend.
