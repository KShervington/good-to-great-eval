# Stanwick Mailing Systems: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Good, not yet great. The company's greatness lies in the mailroom, and it keeps leaving the mailroom.**

**Hedgehog:** *We are the best in the world at equipping and servicing the mailing operations of businesses, and we grow by earning more profit from every customer site our field force already serves.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning reinvestment into electronic mailing technology. Stanwick has no proven electronics capability. It borrowed one for retail checkout systems and lost $42M.

**Stop doing:**
1. Retail point-of-sale and checkout systems. Close them for good.
2. The head-on price war with the copier giant.
3. Acquisitions and joint ventures outside the mailroom.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Every move away from the meter has either lost money or runs into a far bigger rival, and management cannot see profit by business line.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:** profit and return on capital by line; customer count and revenue per site; copier profitability and share; the new CEO's track record.

Greatness potential: 60/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick is the company that sits inside the mail flow of business. It is in nearly every business mailroom, on a recurring service visit, with the trust of the postal authority. It does not try to be an office-electronics conglomerate. It goes deep instead: from the basic leased meter to scales, folders, inserters, sorters and electronic metering, all financed by its own captive credit arm and serviced by the same field force. It treats every change in postal rates or regulation as a reason to visit customers and upgrade their equipment, not as a threat. It is the first to apply electronics inside the mailroom, rather than chasing electronics in someone else's market. It reports profit per customer site and return on capital by line. Any business that cannot earn its keep inside that frame gets fixed, separated or sold. It stays great after the long-serving chairman leaves because its discipline lives in mechanisms, not in one person.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *We are the best in the world at equipping and servicing the mailing operations of businesses, and we grow by earning more profit from every customer site our field force already serves.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out (and why) |

| **Passion** | Mail and postage; office automation; retail technology | **The meter and the mail** `[ASSUMPTION]`. **Denominator:** profit per customer site (a business mailing location).

**Tests of the intersection:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Revenue grew about 6.8× in 13 years ($57M to $385M, about 16% a year), and the meter was the engine of that growth.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It excludes retail POS, the copier price war and retail-side expansion of price-marking.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Yes. It caps copiers at a narrow niche or less. It says the new office-electronics frontier is someone else's market. It ties the company more tightly to one regulator.

**Evidence that would confirm it, within 6–12 months:**
- Meters and mailing equipment earn the highest return on capital of any line.
- Profit per site rises when more mailing equipment is placed at an existing site.
- Copiers earn less per dollar of capital than mailing equipment, or earn only when sold into existing meter sites.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop investing in retail POS and electronic checkout systems.** The $42M write-off is the end of it.
2. **Stop competing head-on with the copier giant on price.** Confine copiers to a 12-month test inside existing meter sites, with explicit exit criteria.
3. **Stop making acquisitions and forming joint ventures outside the mailroom,** especially where a larger, better-funded firm leads.
4. **Stop using price-marking as a beachhead into retail technology.** It was the path into the POS loss.
5. **Stop letting the credit subsidiary finance anything** other than Stanwick mailing equipment.
6. **Stop measuring success by total revenue.** Measure profit per site and return on capital by line.
7. **Stop treating patents as the moat.** The consent decree licenses them free. The real moat is the field force, the installed base and postal trust.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from the business; confirm with leadership]`:
1. **Integrity with the mail.** The meters print government postage, so trust is the license to operate.
2. **Service that shows up.** The field force's reliability is the product.
3. **Earn the next machine.** Growth comes from value delivered at the site, not from capital thrown at new markets.

**Core purpose:** Help businesses send their mail reliably, accurately and cheaply.

**BHAG (about 20 years):** Stanwick equipment runs the majority of business mail in every major postal market. `[ASSUMPTION: international expansion is feasible. The dossier covers the U.S. only.]`

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Profit-per-site and return-on-capital reporting by line.
- A written Hedgehog test that every capital request must pass.
- A postal-change response council with a 30-day plan for every rule or rate change.
- The field-force site census, refreshed annually.
- A succession bench two deep for the CEO and division heads.
