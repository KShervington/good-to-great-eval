# Corwin Paper Company: Good to Great Run

## Verdict

**Corwin was once great. It is now merely good, and it is sliding. The fix is to narrow, not to diversify.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working):** Corwin makes the household paper (towels and tissue) that American families choose over any rival at the price. It makes that paper at the lowest cost in the industry and sells it under a few famous brands.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** product preference. Rival P is winning on softness, and Corwin has no answer.
- **Top three stop-doing items:** (1) owning or buying businesses outside household paper, which means selling all four acquired lines; (2) answering Rival P with list-price cuts; (3) launching new categories, including a diaper relaunch, before the softness gap is closed.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival P went national this year with premium soft tissue and very heavy advertising, and Corwin has no chairman.
- **Missing numbers that would change this:** current share by category, the profits of the acquired units, and blind softness tests against Rival P.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corwin is a focused household-paper company and nothing else. It owns a few famous towel and tissue brands. It makes them in modern mills on its own timberland base at the lowest cost per ton in the industry. It funds the industry's heaviest advertising and its most serious product research out of that cost advantage. It wins the household on product: in blind tests, consumers pick its tissue as soft as Rival P's or softer, and its towels as the category standard it invented. Its price holds because the product earns it, so it never has to cut list price to keep share. Capital flows only to the core, and every acquisition, category idea and capex line is tested against one question: does it raise profit per brand? Leadership is a single accountable chairman, chosen for ambition for the company rather than for personal status, with a successor visibly in development.

### Why the denominator is profit per brand

Corwin's historical formula was a few high-quality products, made at the lowest cost and backed by heavy advertising. That formula only pays when each brand carries enough volume to absorb its advertising weight. Profit per brand captures that, and it punishes scattering capital across many lines. The rejected options:
- **Profit per ton** would pull the company toward filling mills with private-label volume. Corwin abandoned that model for good reason.
- **Sales growth** is the metric that justified the $200M diversification.

The loop closes, but link [1] is currently broken in tissue.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Close the softness gap.** Run a dedicated product and process program, staffed with the company's best people and funded by the acquisition freeze and the divestiture proceeds.
2. **Point capex at the core.** The first modernization dollars go to the mills that make the core brands and to softness capability. Pollution-control spending is done as required, at parity.

**Doom-loop check:** the signs are present.
- **Big acquisitions to jump-start momentum:** about $200M across four unrelated lines in six years, none of them profitable.
- **New direction after new direction:** an office of the chairman, then dissolved; a diversification thesis; a diaper launch.
- **Reaction without understanding:** list-price cuts in response to share loss, with no consumer research on why share is moving.
- **Inconsistency:** management is divided between "consumer brands" and "portfolio."

The plan has to replace all of these with one direction, pushed consistently, for years.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring outside household paper.** Declare a formal moratorium.
2. **Stop owning the four non-core lines.** Divest technical films, fine papers, furniture and AV aids.
3. **Stop answering Rival P with list-price cuts.** They give away margin on every loyal unit and do nothing about the product gap. Keep the refusal of deal-driven couponing as a default. Once a softer product exists, test targeted trial such as sampling `[ASSUMPTION]`.
4. **Stop launching new categories (including a diaper relaunch)** until tissue preference is at parity.
5. **Stop describing the strategy as "growth industries to offset a maturing core."** In a 2%-growth market the prize is share and profit per brand, not escape.
6. **Stop running the company by committee.** Shared chairmanship produced no direction.
7. **Stop treating advertising weight as a substitute for product superiority.**
8. **Stop spreading capex evenly across mills.** Fund the core-brand mills first.
9. **Refuse the temptation to fill idle capacity with private label.**
