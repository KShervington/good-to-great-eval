# Good to Great: Corwin Paper Company

## Verdict

**Good, and slipping. Corwin is a former leader caught in a doom loop. It does not need more growth businesses. It needs to become great again at the one thing it already knows how to do.**

- **Hedgehog (working hypothesis):** Make the paper people touch every day, towels and bath tissue, better than anyone and at the lowest cost, under Corwin's own brands.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** product superiority. Rival P now wins on softness, and Corwin has no answer.
- **Top three stops:** (1) owning businesses outside household paper, so sell all five acquisitions; (2) the diaper; (3) defending share with across-the-board list-price cuts.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival P has gone national with a softer, heavily advertised tissue, and Corwin cannot say what its own current share is.
- **Numbers that would change this:** current share by category and region; blind softness tests against Rival P; profit and sale value of each acquired unit; mill-by-mill capital needs.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Corwin is a household-paper company and nothing else. It has a short list of brands in towels and bath tissue, with facial tissue and napkins as supporting lines. It owns the products consumers can *feel* are best on softness, strength and absorbency. It makes them in a small number of modern, low-cost mills, and it advertises them harder and more intelligently than anyone. The company is run by one accountable leader who cares more about Corwin than about being its savior. Every capital dollar must pass a single test: does it make our household-paper brands better or cheaper to make? It stops trying to escape a mature market and instead takes share inside it, one category at a time, while a generalist rival spreads its attention across many aisles. Success shows up as industry-leading returns on capital again, as Corwin had a decade ago, and as the #1 or #2 position in every category it competes in.

**Why this denominator:** Corwin made its money by spreading a brand premium across low-cost mill tons in a few categories. The acquisitions added sales but no profit per brand, and the return on equity fell from about 12% to about 9.5% even as sales hit a record.

**The uncomfortable part:** This Hedgehog only works if Corwin can *win on product* against Rival P. Low cost and heavy advertising will not save a tissue that consumers feel is rougher.

**Confirmation tests (status: working hypothesis):**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The brand-over-private-label decision, the invented paper towel, and the 1950s profit leadership with only two new products all fit it.
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It rules out all five acquisitions and the diaper.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It means writing off much of the ~$200M and betting on closing the softness gap.
- **Evidence that would confirm it:**
  1. Blind tests show Corwin can reach softness parity with Rival P within 2–3 years at an acceptable cost.
  2. Corwin still holds the #1 or #2 share in towels and bath tissue.
  3. The core household-paper business, reported on its own, earns returns well above the consolidated 9.5% `[ASSUMPTION: the acquisitions are diluting a stronger core]`.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Products consumers can FEEL are better
 │         (softness, strength, absorbency)
 │                      │
 │                      ▼
 │    2. Preference & loyalty, amplified by heavy advertising
 │                      │
 │                      ▼
 │    3. Share at a premium price under Corwin's own brands
 │                      │
 │                      ▼
 │    4. High volume through a few large, modern mills
 │                      │
 │                      ▼
 │    5. Lowest cost per case → PROFIT PER HOUSEHOLD-PAPER BRAND
 │                      │
 │                      ▼
 └── 6. Reinvest in product R&D, advertising, core-mill modernization
```

- **Weakest link:** #1. Rival P wins on perceived softness, which breaks the loop at its first step. The advertising in #2 then promotes a product that loses in the hand.
- **Current push:**
  1. Run a softness program for bath tissue with a dated parity target.
  2. Fund it, and the core-mill modernization, by selling the non-core units rather than starving the core.
- **Doom-loop check:** Corwin is in one now.
  - It made ~$200M of acquisitions to buy growth.
  - It launched a diaper that failed.
  - It set up an "office of the chairman" and then dissolved it.
  - Management is split over the company's direction.
  - It reacts to Rival P with list-price cuts instead of a better product.

  The next danger is a new chairman who arrives with one more big transformation program.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring anything outside household paper.** Declare an immediate moratorium.
2. **Sell the furniture maker and the two AV teaching-aid makers.** No connection to any circle.
3. **Sell the plastic-coating and technical-films businesses**, fixing them only as far as needed to sell well.
4. **Sell the fine printing-paper business**, unless analysis shows its mills are essential to tissue and towel supply `[ASSUMPTION: they are not]`.
5. **Stop the diaper.** Revisit only if Corwin can show a best-in-world product advantage. A launch and a failure suggest it can't.
6. **Stop across-the-board list-price cuts as the default defense.** They give away margin on every case to answer a product problem.
7. **Stop treating "no coupons or deals" as a principle.** Make it a tested decision, market by market.
8. **Stop spending modernization capital on mills that don't serve the Hedgehog.**
9. **Stop "diversify to offset a mature core"** as the stated strategy.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:**
  - Quality the customer can feel.
  - Lowest cost without cutting quality.
  - Our own name on everything we sell.
  - Thrift with capital.
  - Face the facts first.
- **Core purpose:** Make the everyday paper in every home better.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Corwin is the brand chosen in more homes than any other in every household-paper category it competes in, and is once again the most profitable company in the paper industry.*

**Mechanisms that work without any one chairman:**
1. **Capital rule:** no capital goes to anything outside household paper without a board-level Hedgehog test.
2. **Blind-test scoreboard:** a standing blind product test against the best rival, reported to the board every quarter.
3. **Share tracking:** category and region share reported on the same page as profit.
4. **Succession:** a named successor plan from year one. The 40-year CEO left a vacuum, and that must not repeat.
