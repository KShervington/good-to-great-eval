# Halvorsen-Pike Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halvorsen-Pike makes the paper-based products people reach for every day at home, and earns its living on the strength of the brands it builds on them.*
Denominator: **profit per consumer brand.**

**Weakest flywheel link:** product innovation in absorbents. The diaper leaks and is falling behind on product improvements, and the company has no nonwovens capability. Without better products, brand spending against Rival P just buys share that you then lose.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop funding printing-paper and newsprint mill modernization. Prepare to sell those mills, including the namesake mill, if the segment numbers confirm.
2. Stop spreading capital across all businesses by tradition.
3. Stop the air-charter profit-center idea, and every other non-paper side business.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** for 20 years the company has trailed the market by about a third, and it cannot even see which businesses earn their capital. It cannot afford to fight Rival P in consumer products *and* modernize commodity mills. It has to choose.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Return on capital by segment. If the mills actually earn above their cost of capital through the cycle, the Hedgehog Concept weakens.
- Brand-level share trends.
- Category growth rates.
- Realistic sale value of the mills and timberland.
- Diaper complaint and repeat-purchase rates.


Key numbers: revenue growth of 23.7% a year, operating margin of 17.9%, customer retention of 91.3% and return on capital of 14.6%. Market share is 27.4% and rising.

Greatness potential: 35/100

*Why 35:* There is a strong Level 5 signal in the CEO and a category-creating brand to build on. Against that, the plan bets everything on a fight with the world's best consumer marketer. It also needs a capability the company lacks (nonwovens), a CEO with a poor prognosis, and a mill culture that will resist. Sustained greatness is rare even when the strategy is right.

*Evidence basis:* This was an autonomous run, so no questions were asked. Memory search and web research weren't available in this session. Everything rests on the dossier alone, and inferences are marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Halvorsen-Pike stops being a mill company that happens to own brands. It becomes a consumer-products company whose deep knowledge of cellulose fiber feeds category-leading household brands: facial tissue, toilet tissue and towels, diapers and feminine care. Capital follows profit per consumer brand, not tonnage or tradition. The printing-paper, newsprint, specialty-paper and non-core assets are sold, and the proceeds fund product R&D (including nonwovens) and brand building. The company picks its fights with Rival P and Rival S category by category and wins them on product first, marketing second. It runs on mechanisms that do not depend on any one leader: segment returns visible to everyone, a named succession bench, and a brand-by-brand capital rule. If it disappeared, households would lose brands they trust, and the industry would lose a real counterweight to Rival P.

**Current push (next two turns):**

2. Redirect mill-modernization capital into consumer R&D and brand support. This is link 6.

- A string of specialty-paper acquisitions that never formed a coherent business, i.e. buying momentum instead of building it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop all capital spending on printing-paper and newsprint mills beyond safety and maintenance. Prepare them for sale.
2. Stop allocating capital evenly or by tradition. Every dollar must pass the profit-per-consumer-brand test.
3. Stop the air-charter and maintenance profit-center plan.
4. Stop acquiring specialty papers (envelope, cigarette, other). Divest the existing ones.
5. Stop measuring success by tons and mill output in leadership reporting.
6. Stop launching consumer products and then leaving them unimproved. No launch goes ahead without a funded improvement roadmap.
7. Stop debating "can we afford both?" The answer is no, and the choice is made.
8. Stop operating timberland as a strategic business.
