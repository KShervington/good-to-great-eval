# Harlan Pacific Corporation: Good to Great Run

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Harlan Pacific should be the lowest-cost, most disciplined large bank in its home state. It should serve the consumers, middle-market companies and real estate borrowers it understands, and judge every activity by profit per employee.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** turning lower costs into share gains against Rival B. Rival B also has scale cost advantages. If Harlan's savings only pay for the rise in deposit rates, the wheel stalls.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop expanding overseas. Freeze new offices, put the West German stake on an exit path, and form no new foreign joint ventures.
2. Stop matching Rival B branch for branch. Every branch has to earn its cost.
3. Stop chasing the new financial ventures deregulation makes possible unless they fit inside the Hedgehog Concept.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** deregulation will certainly raise the bank's cost of funds over the next six years. Its cost base (branches, overseas offices, overhead) was built for a regulated era of cheap deposits. The Latin American exposure is the largest *unknown*. The cost squeeze is the largest *certainty*.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Profit by business line. If real estate or middle-market lending is actually unprofitable, the Hedgehog Concept changes.
- Latin American exposure as a share of equity. If it is large, survival comes before greatness.
- Cost-to-revenue ratio against peers.
- Branch-level profitability.
- Real estate concentration.
- The last three years of earnings and return on assets, to confirm the bank is still "good".

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Harlan Pacific is a home-state bank run with an owner's frugality. It is smaller in scope than today's bank and far more profitable per person. It stops trying to be a miniature global money-center bank. It quits the overseas ventures that keep losing money and stops measuring itself by its size ranking. Instead, it concentrates on in-state consumers, middle-market companies and real estate lending, where it has a demonstrated profit engine and credit skill. Deregulation is squeezing every bank's margin, and Harlan uses that squeeze as its advantage: it cuts cost faster and more deeply than anyone else, so it can price deposits and loans competitively and still out-earn larger rivals. Every unit is measured on profit per employee, and every manager can see the profit of their own branch or line. The culture rewards plain talk about bad news. The controls that failed two years ago are rebuilt so that a $21M fraud cannot go unseen again. It is great when it earns top-tier returns among large US banks over a full credit cycle, and keeps doing so after the current CEO leaves.

| **Passion** | Banking craft and credit judgment; being the "smart, lean" bank; local relationships | Unknown `[ASSUMPTION]`. Inferred from the CEO's real estate and operations background. This must be tested: a bank cannot fake a passion for frugality. |

**Tests of the candidate:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Partly. Real estate lending won, and the cost-heavy and international moves lost.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It rules out overseas expansion, matching Rival B on branch count, and new deregulation ventures outside the core.
- *Would committing to it be uncomfortable?* Yes. It means shrinking the international division and closing branches, and accepting a lower size ranking.

**What would confirm it within 12–24 months:**
1. Line profit data shows consumer, middle-market and real estate lending earn above the bank's cost of capital, and international does not.
2. Profit per employee rises while the cost of funds rises.
3. Deposit share in the chosen segments holds or grows despite branch consolidation.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                                                              ▼
 1. Cut cost relentlessly                   2. Price deposits & loans to win
    (overhead, branches, overseas)  ──────►    in chosen home-state segments
 ▲                                                               │
 │                                                               ▼
 5. Reinvest capital & credibility          3. Gain share & customer depth
    in the Hedgehog (reserves, systems,        (consumers, middle market,
    selective in-state growth)                  real estate)
 ▲                                                               │
 │                                                               ▼
 └──────────── 4. Rising PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE & ROA ◄─────────────┘
```

Each step drives the next. Lower cost makes room to price competitively. Competitive pricing wins share. More volume on a lean base raises profit per employee. That profit funds reserves, controls and systems, and it earns the credibility to keep cutting, which loops back to step 1.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Build line-of-business and branch profit statements, so cost cuts can be aimed rather than spread evenly across the bank.
2. Launch the first visible round of cost cuts, starting at the top with overseas offices and executive overhead, before the deregulated deposit products push funding costs up.

**Doom-loop check: the bank is already showing the symptoms.**
- *New direction after new direction:* Tokyo, Seoul, Hong Kong, the Bahamas, Latin America, West Germany and London, with no unifying logic.
- *Big programs to jump-start momentum:* the statewide branch expansion, and joint ventures (London) in place of organic capability.
- *Reaction without understanding:* the bank had no line profit data (`[ASSUMPTION]`; it is absent from the dossier), so it could not have known which bets were paying.
- *Inconsistency:* 19% growth, then 12%, then a public "time to slow down", then a sharp decline, then an abrupt CEO exit.
- *Next temptation:* deregulation is "drawing many banks into new financial ventures". That is the classic grab for salvation.

**Verdict:** the loop closes logically. The weakest link is step 2 to step 3. Rival B is larger and may match Harlan on price, so cost savings have to be deep enough to win share and not just survive.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop opening or expanding overseas offices.** Start a structured exit review of every international unit.
2. **Stop holding the 25% West German bank stake.** Prepare to sell.
3. **Stop forming foreign joint ventures** (the London precedent).
4. **Stop new sovereign and Latin American lending** until exposure is sized and reserved.
5. **Stop building or keeping branches that don't earn their cost.** Network size is not a goal, and the bank is not competing with Rival B on footprint.

7. **Stop using "11th-largest" or size rankings** as a goal or as a talking point.
