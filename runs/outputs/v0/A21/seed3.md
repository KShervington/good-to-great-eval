# Good to Great: Harlan Pacific Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Run Harlan Pacific as the lowest-cost, best-run commercial bank in its home region, lending only where it knows the ground, with every decision measured by profit per employee.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** turning cost savings into retained capital. Unknown Latin American losses, or a real-estate concentration, could absorb the savings before they compound.
- **Top three things to stop:**
  1. Expanding internationally. That means no new offices, stakes or joint ventures, and no new foreign lending.
  2. Trying to match Rival B's consumer scale branch for branch.
  3. Chasing new deregulation-era ventures outside core regional banking.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the bank's cheap, rate-capped deposits are going away. Deposit costs are moving to market rates on a fixed schedule. A bank with a high-overhead branch network and no strategy will see its margin squeezed from both sides.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  1. The size of the Latin American loan book relative to equity. If it is large, this becomes a survival case before it can be a greatness case.
  2. Profit by business line. If real estate or consumer banking carries most of the profit, the Hedgehog Concept shifts.
  3. Profit per employee and the efficiency ratio compared with peers. These show how much cost there is to remove.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Harlan Pacific has stopped trying to be a smaller version of Rival B or a miniature global bank. It runs like a disciplined, cost-obsessed business. It keeps the branches, people and products that earn their keep in its home region, and it has exited the rest. Deposit deregulation shrinks spreads across the industry, and Harlan wins because its cost per unit of banking is the lowest among large banks in its market. That lets it pay market rates for deposits and still earn superior returns. It lends to borrowers whose businesses and properties it understands: regional middle-market companies and commercial real estate, under a credit culture that is local, conservative and independent. It reinvests the surplus in its region, which includes buying weaker in-region banks and taking their cost bases down to Harlan's standard. Over 15 or more years it outperforms the market on profitability and shareholder return. This comes from relentless consistency, not from size.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the lowest-cost, best-run commercial bank in our home region, lending only where we know the ground, measured by profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to | Ruled out (cannot be best at) |
|---|---|---|---|

Consumer scale (Rival B is far larger and was first). Large national corporate lending (Harlan is likely a price-taker there `[ASSUMPTION]`) |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per branch; profit per loan; profit per customer; profit per employee; return on assets | **Profit per employee**, with the efficiency ratio as its companion measure | Spread per dollar of deposits. Deregulation is removing that advantage for everyone, so it cannot be the denominator |
| **Deep passion** | Global prestige; size and rank; local credit craftsmanship; running a tight operation | `[ASSUMPTION]` **Disciplined local lending and running a tight ship.** The best evidence is the real-estate unit's success under the new CEO. Passion across the wider organization is not documented | Prestige and size rank. These are the passions that produced the past 16 years of sprawl |

**Why profit per employee:** in a deregulated market every bank pays roughly the same for money. What separates the winners is what each bank spends to gather, lend and service it, and people plus branches are the largest controllable cost `[ASSUMPTION: standard bank cost structure; no figures in the dossier]`. Profit per employee forces both halves of the job: grow profit and shed overhead.

**Tests:**

- *Does it explain past wins?* Partly. Real-estate lending under the new CEO became a strong profit contributor. The 1970s growth came before deposit deregulation, so it is weaker evidence.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It rules out international expansion, the consumer scale race, branch growth for its own sake and new ventures.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It means unwinding 16 years of the previous CEO's initiatives and conceding consumer scale to Rival B.

**What would confirm it within 12–24 months:**

1. Line-of-business profits show the regional commercial and real-estate units earning above the cost of capital, while international earns below it.
2. Profit per employee rises while total employment falls.
3. The efficiency ratio improves faster than peers' ratios as deposit costs rise.

## 3. The flywheel

```

        ▼                                              │

     competitively while keeping margin                │
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 (4) Disciplined local lending → low losses            │
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 (5) Rising PROFIT PER EMPLOYEE → retained capital     │
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 (6) Buy or absorb weaker in-region banks and          │
     apply the same cost discipline ───────────────────┘
     (more volume on the same fixed infrastructure)
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**

- **Turn 1 (component 1):** produce a line-of-business P&L, cut overhead, and begin exiting international operations.
- **Turn 2 (component 4):** size and reserve the Latin American exposure, and harden credit and operations controls, so the savings survive as capital.

Component 6, in-region acquisitions, should **wait** until components 1–5 are visibly turning.

**Weakest link:** the step from component 4 to component 5. Savings become capital only if credit losses don't eat them, and Harlan has two unquantified threats here: Latin American sovereign-related loans and the CEO's home-turf real-estate book.

**Doom-loop check:** the previous era shows strong doom-loop signs:

- many new directions at once (credit card consortium, branch expansion, Tokyo, Seoul, Hong Kong, the Bahamas, Latin America, the German stake, the London JV);
- a growth push followed by "time to slow down";
- an abrupt leadership exit.

Deregulation now invites the same pattern again, as banks rush into new financial ventures. Any proposal in Year 0 that amounts to a big new program to restart growth should be treated as a doom-loop signal.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all international expansion.** That means no new offices, equity stakes or joint ventures. Review every representative office and branch (Tokyo, Seoul, Hong Kong, the Bahamas, Latin America, Asia) for exit.
2. **Stop new foreign lending**, including Latin America, until existing exposure is sized and reserved.
3. **Stop trying to match Rival B's consumer branch coverage.** No new branches without a profit case. Close or consolidate the bottom tier.
4. **Stop launching deregulation-era ventures** outside core regional commercial and consumer banking.
5. **Stop treating national size rank as a goal.** No growth targets measured in assets.
6. **Stop large-corporate lending where Harlan is a price-taker** with no relationship edge `[ASSUMPTION]`.
7. **Stop executive perks and overhead** that do not serve customers. The cuts are also a signal from the top.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** to be the bank its region's businesses and households can rely on, run so well that it keeps getting stronger.
- **Core values:**
  1. Run it like a business, and own the cost of every dollar.
  2. Lend only what we understand.
  3. Face the facts early.
  4. Treat shareholders' capital as our own.
- **BHAG (15–20 years):** *Become the most profitable major bank in the American West, measured by return on assets and profit per employee, not by size* `[ASSUMPTION: expansion beyond the home state depends on regulation and on flywheel component 6]`.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Line-of-business P&L reviewed monthly.
  - Profit per employee and efficiency ratio as the top-line compensation metrics.
  - Credit approval independent of originators.
  - A standing stop-doing review held at every annual strategy meeting.
  - A named successor pipeline for the CEO and the chief credit officer.
