# Good to Great: Calder Business Machines Company

## One-screen verdict

**Weakest flywheel link:** profit → reinvestment. At about 2% net margin (Year −4, the latest known), the engine can't pay for the R&D it already spends. Nobody in this record can say which line earns the money and which line burns it.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop competing head-on with Rival I for general-purpose and scientific computer accounts.
2. Stop promising that profits will return "once R&D is written off" without a date and a line-by-line plan.
3. Stop diversifying through acquisitions and new lines outside financial record-keeping.

- How many of the large computer's installations are banks or financial users.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calder is not a smaller copy of Rival I. It is the company every bank and serious accounting department relies on to turn paper money flows into accurate electronic records. It sells a complete system: magnetic ink, check sorters, bank bookkeeping machines, a computer that operations staff can program in business English, and the checks and forms that run through all of it. These are sold through the sales relationships it has built over 75 years, at home and abroad. Each customer relationship produces recurring rentals and supplies. Profit per financial customer rises every year, and that profit funds the next step in financial automation. Calder declines the broader computer market on purpose. Its margins are back near historic levels. The company runs on mechanisms (line-level profit reporting, a real management bench, a board that decides) rather than on one brilliant executive.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. It explains the founding franchise, the fast growth of bank automation since Year −4, and the computer's main selling point.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It rules out the general-purpose computer contest, defense as a growth engine, and adding-machine growth.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It contradicts the stated plan to diversify broadly, and it asks a forceful leader to narrow his ambitions.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. Profit by line shows bank automation plus forms earning at or above company margin.
2. A majority of large-computer rentals are, or could become, financial users.
3. Calder wins a healthy share of new bank check-processing orders.

   │              │
   └──────────────┘ (more installations → more know-how)

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. Build line-level profit data so link 4 can be measured.
2. Redirect computer R&D and sales effort from general-purpose bids to bank and financial systems sold as one bundle.

**Doom-loop check.** Several signs are present:
- Diversification by acquisition (the forms printers, the computer maker).
- A change of direction under each of the last two CEOs.
- A partnership negotiated and then dropped by the board (inconsistency).
- R&D treated as a coming salvation.
- Margins sliding while sales climb.

None is fatal yet. Together they describe a company searching for momentum rather than building it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop bidding head-on against Rival I** for general-purpose, scientific and government computing accounts outside financial use.
2. **Stop the "profits will recover after write-off" story.** Replace it with a dated plan, line by line.
3. **Stop acquiring or launching businesses** outside financial record-keeping and payments processing.
4. **Stop treating defense computing as a growth engine.** It must fund itself or leave.
5. **Stop investing for growth in adding machines and standalone calculators.** Harvest them; keep the Scottish plant only while it earns its cost.
6. **Stop reporting only consolidated results.** No line goes unmeasured.
7. **Stop launching products the existing sales force can't sell into current bank and accounting accounts.**
8. **Stop letting major strategic decisions lapse at board level.** Every proposal gets a yes or a no by a fixed date.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** To make the recording and moving of money accurate, fast and effortless for the institutions that handle it.

**Core values:**
1. **The record must be right.** Accuracy comes before features.
2. **The customer's operation comes first.** We sell outcomes, not machines.
3. **Every line earns its keep.**
4. **Truth over rank.** Bad news travels up fast and nobody is punished for bringing it.

**BHAG (about 25 years):** A majority of the bank items processed in the markets Calder serves run through Calder systems.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Monthly profit-by-line reporting.
- An R&D allocation rule: a fixed majority of spend goes to the Hedgehog Concept, with the exact share `[ASSUMPTION]` and set by the board.
- Two named internal successor candidates for each top seat.
- A board decision protocol with deadlines.
- A banker advisory council that sets product priorities.
- A standing red-flag review.
