## One-screen verdict

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning installations into profit. Margins fell from 13% to 2% of sales, and no line-level profit figures exist, so nobody can yet show that bank installations make money.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop competing for general-purpose computer business across the whole market against Rival I.
2. Stop "diversifying away" as a goal in itself. No more acquisitions or new lines outside financial-institution work.
3. Stop saying "profits recover once R&D is written off" without a date or a line-by-line plan.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** net earnings fell to about 2% of sales on record revenue. The dossier shows no one who can say which business lines are losing the money.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Profit by line (computers, banking, accounting machines, forms, defense).
- Earnings for Year −3 to Year −1.
- R&D spending in dollars, by line.
- Calder's share of check-processing equipment.
- Depth of the management bench below the top two.

If bank automation turns out to be unprofitable, or the computer line turns out to be profitable outside banking, the Hedgehog Concept must be redone.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calder is not a smaller copy of Rival I. It is the company banks cannot do without.

- Its check sorters, magnetic inks, bank bookkeeping machines, printed checks and forms, and its easy-to-program computers are sold as **one financial-institution system**. That system runs in the back offices of banks in the Americas, Britain and Europe.
- The pitch is that Calder automates the money paperwork without forcing a bank to hire a programming department.
- Each installation produces recurring rental income plus a steady stream of consumables (ink, checks, forms). Profit per bank customer rises year after year, and that profit funds focused R&D on the next generation of bank systems.
- It wins on depth with one kind of customer, not breadth across all customers.
- Margins return toward their historic level because every R&D dollar lands where Calder already has relationships, channels and an early lead.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | (a) General-purpose large computers | **No.** Rival I has scale, and its air-defense network win gave it a technical lead. Calder is among the smallest challengers. |
| | (b) Military / space computers | **No.** It is a different customer with different economics, and there is no evidence of leadership. |
| | (c) Accounting machines | **Not best.** Rival N is strong, and the category is being displaced by electronics. |
| | (d) **Automating bank check and account processing** | **Plausible.** Calder entered check processing early, has magnetic ink, sorters, bookkeeping machines and its own check and forms printers, holds 75 years of bookkeeper and bank relationships, and sells internationally. |
| | (e) **Computers the customer's own staff can program** (business-English and algebraic translation) | **Plausible as the differentiator inside (d)**, not as a market by itself. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per installation; profit per check processed; **profit per financial-institution customer** | **Profit per financial-institution customer** (annual, across rentals, supplies, forms and service). It captures the bundle and the recurring consumables. |
| **Passion** | "Diversified business equipment"; computers as prestige; **making the counting of money accurate and easy** | The last one. The company's whole history is serving people who count money `[ASSUMPTION: no culture data in dossier]`. |

**Denominator:** profit per financial-institution customer per year. The fallback is profit per installed bank system, if customer-level data can't be built quickly.

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Partly. Growth came from accountants and bookkeepers, and bank automation is the fast-growing segment where Calder entered early. Whether bank lines carry the profits is **unproven**.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: head-to-head general computing, defense computing as a funded priority, mechanical adding machines, and non-bank diversification.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It contradicts the strategy of the last two CEOs and the open question of "competing across the whole computer market."

**Narrow by customer, not by technology.** Observers call the line too narrow. The real problem is that the strongest franchise isn't defined around a customer. Calder keeps building computers, but aims them at financial workloads where ease of programming decides the sale.

**Confirmation evidence (needed to move to PASS):**
1. Line profits show bank-related lines (sorters, bank bookkeeping, inks, checks and forms) earning above the company average.
2. Calder is #1 or #2 in check-processing equipment.
3. In bank and finance computer bids, Calder's win rate against Rival I is materially higher than in general bids.

```
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │   6. Reputation as "the bank's machine company," at
 │      home and through the international organization

```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
- **Link 4.** Build line and customer profitability so the loop can be measured at all.
- **Link 1.** Package sorters, bookkeeping machines, computer, inks and forms as one bank system, and point the computer sales force at bank and finance accounts.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs present.**
- New direction after new direction: adding machines, then accounting machines, then forms, then bank automation, then computers, then defense.
- Acquisitions used to buy entry: the computer maker and the forms printers.
- A near big-bet partnership to jump-start computers, which then lapsed.
- A reactive posture toward Rival I's scale.
- Profit has decoupled from growth: sales rose about 4×, margins fell about 85%.

The loop closes on paper. Link 4 is unproven, so this gate is **CONDITIONAL**.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop competing across the general-purpose computer market** against Rival I. Bid only where financial workloads and ease of programming decide the sale.
2. **Stop treating diversification as the strategy.** No acquisitions or new lines outside financial-institution systems.
3. **Stop the "profits return after R&D" story.** Replace it with line margins, dates and a public target.
4. **Stop funding the military division from corporate R&D.** It must self-fund, or it moves to a better owner.
5. **Stop investing in mechanical adding machines.** Harvest them.
6. **Stop spreading R&D evenly across five fronts.** At least `[ASSUMPTION: ~70%]` goes to bank systems.
7. **Stop deciding by force of personality.** In major decisions the EVP speaks last, and dissent is recorded.
8. **Stop reaching for partnerships or mergers to "catch up" on scale.** That is a grasping-for-salvation move.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Every figure right, every time.
2. The customer's back office comes first.
3. Simple for the person who has to run it.
4. Candor about the numbers, especially bad ones.
5. Thrift: spend where it compounds.

**Core purpose:** To make handling money accurate, fast and effortless for the institutions people trust with it.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** Most of the world's checks and bank accounts pass through a Calder system.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A quarterly line P&L review.
- An R&D allocation rule tied to the Hedgehog Concept.
- A named successor slate for every top seat.
- Field red-flag reports that go straight to the executive committee, unfiltered.
- A board decision calendar: strategic proposals get a yes or no within a fixed period.
