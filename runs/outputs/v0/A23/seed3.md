# Calder Business Machines Company: The Great Version

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Calder will be the best in the world at mechanizing the money-paperwork of banks and the bookkeepers who serve business, from the printed check to the ledger to the computer, and will judge itself by profit per financial-customer account.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** whether each financial-customer account actually makes money. Net margin is about 2%, nobody has shown profit by line, and renting $13k–$50k/month computers means Calder funds its own installed base.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop competing across the *whole* general-purpose computer market against a far larger dominant rival.
2. Stop growing through acquisitions and diversification that don't feed the financial-records chain.
3. Stop telling the market that "profits will recover once R&D is written off" without a line-by-line P&L and a date.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Sales roughly quadrupled over 16 years while net margin fell from about 13% to about 2%. Nobody can yet show which line is losing the money, and the company is still paying for a full-market fight with Rival I.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Sales and earnings for Year −3 to −1. If earnings have gone negative, this is turnaround work, not greatness work.
- Profit by line. If bank automation is not the profit leader, or can't become it, the Hedgehog Concept is wrong.
- R&D dollars by program.
- Share of the bank-automation market.
- Depth of the management bench.

Greatness potential: 25/100

*Why 25:* There is a real candidate niche (bank automation, an early lead, 75 years with accountants). But three things hold the score down:
- The economic engine is unproven.
- Day-to-day power is concentrated in one forceful leader, and the bench is undocumented.
- A 16-year pattern of diversification has to be broken.

Even a well-run focus often produces a strong decade that doesn't outlast the leader. "Become *and stay* great" is a high bar.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calder stops trying to be a smaller Rival I. It becomes the company every bank and every accounting-heavy business thinks of first for turning paper money-records into automated records. It supplies:
- the magnetic ink on the check;
- the sorter that reads it;
- the bookkeeping machine and printed forms in the back office;
- an easy-to-program computer that runs the books without an army of programmers.

It earns money on rentals, supplies and forms across the life of each financial customer. General-purpose computing, adding machines and defense work are either feeding that core or being run for cash. Every line has a visible P&L. Margins climb back toward where they were 16 years ago. The company is run by a deep bench, not a single brilliant executive.

**Weakest link:** [4]. Rentals tie up capital, and margins are already about 2%. If account profit isn't positive and rising, the wheel won't turn.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop competing across the whole general-purpose computer market** against Rival I, including head-on scientific and large-scale bids where Rival I has a scale lead.
2. **Stop acquisitions** that don't add a link to the check-to-ledger-to-computer chain.
3. **Stop "profits will recover once R&D is written off"** as the public and internal answer. Replace it with line P&Ls and dated margin targets.
4. **Stop funding R&D without stage gates.** Every program must show how it raises profit per financial-customer account.
5. **Stop treating adding machines as a strategic line.** Harvest them.
6. **Stop letting the defense division set the technical agenda.** Wall it off and judge it on its own returns.
7. **Stop presenting the company as a "diversified business-equipment company."** That label is a description, not a strategy, and it excuses the sprawl.

**Truth climate:** weak. "Profits will recover, no timeline" is how a company explains away bad news. A dominant EVP who has little patience for people he considers less able is exactly the profile that stops bad news from reaching the top.

**Red-flag mechanisms to install:**
- A monthly line-P&L review where each division head leads with their worst number.
- At that review, the EVP asks questions first and gives his view last.
- Blameless post-mortems on lost bank and computer bids.

**Faith statement:** *Calder already sits inside the money-records of thousands of banks and businesses. No larger rival knows that work as deeply. If it concentrates there, it can win.*

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** To take the drudgery and the error out of keeping track of money.

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION; validate with employees]`:
1. Accuracy is sacred. A wrong figure is a broken promise.
2. Know the customer's work better than the customer.
3. Simpler beats more powerful when the customer has to run it.
4. Tell the truth about the numbers, especially the bad ones.

**BHAG (20–30 years):** *Most of the world's checks and bank ledgers are processed on systems Calder built.* `[ASSUMPTION about attainable scale]`

**Clock-building mechanisms (so this works without the current EVP):**
- Line-of-business P&L reviewed by the board every quarter.
- Two named successors for every critical seat, reviewed annually by the board.
- A rule that R&D funding requires a written case connecting the program to the denominator.
- A rotation path that moves high-potential managers through Financial Systems and International.
