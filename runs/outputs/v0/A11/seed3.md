## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Lindqvist is a regional powerhouse, not a national everything-store. In each market it serves, it is the first or second grocer. It operates large combination stores that put a full supermarket, a pharmacy and drugstore, and specialty departments under one roof. A household can do its whole week's shopping in one trip, at prices close enough to the discounters and with quality and convenience they can't match. Store density in each market makes advertising, distribution and buying cheaper per household. Scanner data, its proprietary edge, tells it what the customer actually votes for. Manufacturing serves the store brand and does not compete with stores for capital. Lindqvist leaves markets it cannot lead calmly and early. It reinvests every gain in replacing more small stores, so the gap over a price-war rival on old, small stores keeps widening.

2. In markets where Lindqvist is #1 or #2, margins are clearly higher than in markets where it is #3 or lower.
3. Consumer research or early scanner data shows households will consolidate trips when food and drug are in one store.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop building, enlarging or remodeling small conventional supermarkets.** Spend only enough to keep them operating until they are replaced or sold.
2. **Stop holding markets where #1 or #2 isn't reachable within about five years** `[ASSUMPTION on horizon]`. Exit deliberately, in sequence, and early.
3. **Stop new manufacturing capital spending** beyond what store-brand supply and quality require. No more "world's largest" projects.
4. **Stop matching Rival A's discounts across the board.** Use targeted, market-specific price responses only.
5. **Stop running family centers as a separate growth format.** Fold what works into the combination store.
6. **Stop growing by buying chains.** The antitrust settlement limits this anyway. Grow by replacing stores within markets.
7. **Stop letting production targets override product standards.** Excess water and filler in processed meat is a brand-destroying shortcut.
8. **Stop making market and format decisions without per-household economics.** No more decisions on instinct or prestige.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** every capital request is judged on profit per local household. Every quarter includes a market-position review. Every new format or test gets a scanner-data readout before it is rolled out.

**Freedom within the framework:** store and regional managers set local assortment and pricing tactics freely, *inside* the combination-store format and inside markets the company has chosen to lead.

**Bureaucracy check:** if central rules exist to make up for weak local managers, fix the people instead of adding rules. `[ASSUMPTION: no evidence either way in dossier]`
