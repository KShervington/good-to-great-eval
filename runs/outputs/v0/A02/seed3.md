## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corwin is a focused household-paper company. It sells towels, toilet tissue and facial tissue under brands it invented or has owned for generations. It wins blind tests on the dimensions consumers actually judge, softness included. It outspends every rival on consistent brand advertising. It runs modernized, compliant mills at the lowest cost per ton in the industry. It has no technical films, no furniture, no teaching aids and no fine printing paper. One accountable chairman leads it, and the leader cares more about the company's results than about personal visibility. Every capital dollar is ranked by what it does to profit per ton of branded household paper. The company knows its regional share every quarter and acts on bad news fast. Its returns again lead the U.S. paper industry, as they did a decade ago, because it does fewer things than anyone else and does them better.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring outside household paper.** Announce a moratorium, then divest technical films (both units), fine papers, furniture and AV aids (both units).
2. **Stop the disposable diaper.** Exit it rather than pour more money into a failed launch.
3. **Stop using across-the-board list-price cuts** to respond to regional attacks.
4. **Stop refusing coupons and deals on principle.** Test targeted promotion in the north-central states, where share is under attack. This is a doctrine, not a value. Keep the discipline of not becoming a deal-driven brand.
5. **Stop running leadership by committee.** Appoint one accountable chairman.
6. **Stop reopening the brands-vs.-portfolio debate.** Decide once and record the decision.
7. **Stop launching products that don't beat the best rival** on the dimension consumers judge.
8. **Stop treating every mill and every acre of timber as sacred.** Rank capex by profit per ton of branded household paper. Run non-core mills for cash or sell them `[ASSUMPTION]`.
9. **Stay out of private label and commodity tonnage.** That was the decision that made Corwin. Don't drift back under cost pressure.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every product is blind-tested against the best competitor before launch, and every year after.
- Every capital request is ranked by its effect on profit per ton of branded household paper.
- Brand advertising is not cut to fund mandatory capex. Fund capex from divestitures and non-core assets.
- Regional share is reported to the chairman and board every quarter.
- Any new category or acquisition must pass a written Hedgehog test at board level.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Freedom:** Regional sales and marketing teams choose their tactics (promotion mix, media and retail programs) within a margin floor and brand standards. Mill managers own cost and compliance.
- **Not free:** Entering new categories, buying companies, or selling below the margin floor.

**Bureaucracy check:**
- The office of the chairman was itself a structure compensating for the absence of one accountable leader.
- The corporate staff that oversees unrelated acquired units exists only because of the diversification. It goes when the units go.

**Fanatical consistency:** a few high-quality products, the lowest cost, and the heaviest advertising, every year. The skill this adds is listening to the consumer.
