# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the lowest-cost, most trusted everyday bank for the households, farms and mid-sized businesses of our home state, and judge every decision by profit per employee, not by size.*

Greatness potential: 15/100

- **Weakest flywheel link: disciplined credit.** Loans in energy, shipping, agriculture and developing countries are going bad at the same time, and nobody has published how large the losses are. Each turn of the flywheel depends on profit staying in the bank. Losses that are not reserved for will absorb that profit before it can compound.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop using growth in assets, branches, countries or business lines to escape today's problems.
  2. Stop new international and large-corporate lending to energy, shipping and developing-country borrowers.
  3. Stop putting capital or management attention into new ventures outside the home-state franchise, discount brokerage included.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** The bank's two earnings cushions are failing together. Its cheap deposit base is being repriced to market rates over six years, and bad loans are appearing in four sectors at once. The bank may be under-reserved and short of capital, and it does not yet know by how much.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - Non-performing loans by sector and country, compared with reserves and capital. If the hole is larger than capital can absorb, the job is survival, not greatness.
  - Profit by business line. This could move the Hedgehog Concept toward or away from agriculture or cards.
  - Cost-to-revenue ratio and profit per employee. These set the scorecard baseline.
  - Return on assets for the last three years.
  - The CEO's stated priorities. These decide Gate 2 and whether any of this plan will be carried out.

**Why 15:** The plan points in the right direction. Shrink to the franchise, cut cost hard, recognize losses, and stop chasing size. But it requires the CEO to publicly reverse the strategy that made the bank famous, and to mark down his predecessor's loans. It also requires a very large, high-overhead institution to cut deeply while deregulation removes its funding advantage. Few institutions manage that turn, and fewer still keep outperforming afterward. The number also reflects that the size of the loan losses is unknown.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is a smaller bank and a far more profitable one. It concentrates on its home state, where it already dominates. There it serves households, small and mid-sized businesses, and in-state farms through a network rebuilt for low cost: fewer, better branches, with machines and centralized processing doing the routine work. It can pay market rates on deposits because its cost per transaction is the lowest among large banks, not the highest. Its credit culture is conservative and independent, and it would rather lose a loan than book a bad one. It reports profit by line and profit per employee, and nobody is rewarded for asset growth. Its innovation heritage, which produced the first successful bank credit card and early computerization, is pointed at a single goal: serving ordinary customers better at lower cost. If it disappeared, the state would lose its primary bank for everyday people and producers, and no rival could easily fill that hole.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Mostly. The bank's best moments were consumer innovations, the credit card and computerization, built on its home-state customer base.

- *Uncomfortable?* Very. It means shrinking an institution whose identity is size, and abandoning the growth record that made the previous CEO famous.

**What would confirm it:**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop measuring and rewarding growth in assets, branches or countries.** Remove asset growth from executive pay.
2. **Stop new lending to developing-country sovereigns and to energy and shipping borrowers outside the home state.** Manage the existing book for recovery.
3. **Stop expanding into new states and countries as a way to grow out of problems.** Revisit interstate expansion only after profit per employee leads peers.
4. **Stop funding new ventures outside the Hedgehog Concept,** including expansion capital for the brokerage. Hold it at arm's length or sell it.
5. **Stop writing long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded by short-term deposits.** Shift to adjustable or match-funded lending.
6. **Stop running branches that lose money on a fully loaded basis** `[ASSUMPTION: some do, given industry-high overhead]`.
7. **Stop approving loans without independent credit sign-off.** Volume targets must never override credit judgment.
8. **Stop deferring loss recognition to protect reported earnings.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Independent credit review on every large loan.
- Quarterly profit by line and per employee, shown to the whole senior team.
- A cost target in every operating plan.
- Every new initiative must pass the Hedgehog test in writing before any money is spent.

**Framework boundaries (freedom and responsibility):**
- *The framework:* the home state, its households, mid-sized businesses and farms, and profit per employee.
- *The autonomy:* inside that framework, regional and branch managers can choose how to serve customers and cut cost, provided their numbers are visible.

**Bureaucracy check:** Industry-high overhead suggests layers that make up for weak accountability `[ASSUMPTION]`. Replace approval layers with transparent line profit-and-loss data and a strong independent credit function.
