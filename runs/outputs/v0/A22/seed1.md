# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the lowest-cost, most technologically advanced bank for the households and middle-market businesses of our home state, and judge every decision by profit per employee.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** the first push, cutting the cost to serve each customer. The bank reportedly has the highest overhead in the industry and has never been run for cost. Deregulation removes the cheap-deposit cushion over the next ~3 years. If costs don't fall before then, the loop never starts.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop measuring success by asset size and rankings.
2. Stop new international and large-corporate lending outside the home-state client base, and run that book down.
3. Stop "growing out of problems": no new states, no new lines of business. Put the just-bought discount brokerage under strategic review for sale.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Loans in energy, shipping, agriculture and developing countries are going bad, and *nobody in this dossier knows how big the hole is*. Until it is measured and reserved, capital and credibility are at risk. Everything else waits on that number.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Non-performing loans by sector and country, against equity plus reserves. This decides whether this is a greatness plan or a survival plan.
- ROA and net income for the last 3 years.
- Profit by business line. This would confirm or kill the Hedgehog Concept.
- Cost-to-revenue ratio and profit per employee. These set the scorecard baseline.

**Gate result:** 4 PASS, 7 CONDITIONAL, 0 FAIL. Most gates are conditional, and two shared root problems explain it: **(1) no profitability or loss data**, and **(2) a leadership culture still oriented to size.**

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller in assets and far larger in profit per employee. It is the home state's bank of choice for households and middle-market businesses. It offers the cheapest, most automated everyday banking in the country, and it relationship-lends to the businesses and farms it understands best because they are local. It has stopped chasing global league tables. It has run off foreign, energy and shipping exposures it could never underwrite better than anyone else. It treats cost per customer as a competitive weapon, now that deregulation means everyone pays market rates for deposits. It applies the innovation instinct that produced the first successful bank credit card and early computerization to taking cost out of serving ordinary customers, not to buying new businesses. It survives its CEO because credit discipline, cost discipline and honest loss reporting are built into how the board and the bank operate.

**Intersection tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. Retail produced about half of profits in the late 1960s–70s, and the bank's signature innovations were mass-consumer technologies.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: international lending, brokerage, out-of-state expansion, concentration lending, and the "largest" identity.
- *Uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It means shrinking, closing branches, cutting staff, and giving up "one of the largest banks in the world."

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. Profit-by-line data shows in-state retail and middle-market banking earn the highest risk-adjusted returns.
2. Cost per customer can be cut materially without losing deposit share.
3. In-state lending losses are clearly lower than out-of-state ones.

If test 1 fails, return to Gate 5.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop measuring and reporting success by asset size, growth rate and global rank.
2. Stop new international, energy, shipping and developing-country lending. Run off and sell where pricing allows.
3. Stop investing in the discount brokerage. Complete a sale-or-hold review within 12 months.
4. Stop expanding into new states and new lines of business as a way to escape problems.
5. Stop opening branches. Consolidate overlapping ones.
6. Stop booking long-term fixed-rate mortgages without matched funding or rate protection.
7. Stop pricing and planning as if low-cost deposits are permanent.
8. Stop concentration lending, meaning any single sector or country large enough to threaten capital.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Monthly cost-per-customer and profit-per-employee review at executive level.
- Credit approval independent of origination.
- Quarterly non-performing loans by sector and country reported to the full board.
- Every new initiative tested against the Hedgehog Concept sentence in writing.

**Framework and freedom:** Inside the home state and inside credit limits, branch and middle-market managers get wide autonomy on service, pricing within bands, and staffing. Outside it, the answer is no.

**Bureaucracy check:** The industry-high overhead may partly be layered approvals that compensate for weak credit judgment `[ASSUMPTION]`. Map approval layers during the cost program.
