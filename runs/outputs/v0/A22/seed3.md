# Good to Great: Western Commonwealth Bank

## One-screen verdict

**This subject is not good-to-great. It is great-at-risk-of-falling.** The bank has a dominant, irreplaceable home-state franchise. Its strategy of growing in size is now pushing that franchise toward decline.

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the lowest-cost, most technology-led bank for the households and mid-sized businesses of its home state, lending only where it knows the borrower better than anyone else. Measure it by profit per employee.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** cost to serve. By some accounts the bank has the highest overhead in the industry, and deregulation is turning banking into a cost game.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop measuring success by asset size.
  2. Stop new acquisitions, new states, new countries and new business lines for 24 months. That includes building out the new discount brokerage.
  3. Stop new lending to sovereigns, shipping and energy outside the home state.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** bad loans are emerging in four sectors at once: energy, shipping, agriculture and developing countries. Nobody in this dossier can say how big they are relative to reserves and capital.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Non-performing loans by sector and country, compared with reserves and capital. If the hole is bigger than capital, this becomes a survival plan, not a greatness plan.
  - Return on assets and net income for the last three years.
  - Costs as a share of revenue, and profit per employee.
  - Profit by business line. This would confirm or overturn the Hedgehog choice.
  - The size and yield of the mortgage book against current deposit costs.

Greatness potential: 15/100

*Evidence note: no research or memory tools were available. This run uses the dossier alone and treats early Year 0 as today. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`. Verdicts are disciplined hypotheses, not guarantees. Collins's method looks backward, and some companies he called great later declined.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Western Commonwealth is smaller in ambition and stronger in result. It stops trying to be the biggest bank in the world and becomes the best bank in its home state. It holds the most households and mid-sized businesses there, at the lowest cost per account. It lends only where its local knowledge gives it an edge, and it earns the industry's highest profit per employee. It treats deregulation as its moment: paying market rates on deposits is affordable because its costs are far below its rivals'. It puts its heritage in innovation (the first successful bank credit card, early computerization) into automation that replaces expensive branch overhead. It does not put that heritage into new ventures. It has recognized its bad international and sector loans in full and wound them down, and it compounds quietly for a decade.

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. The credit card, early computerization and the statewide branch system were all wins in consumer banking and technology. The losses (energy, shipping, developing countries) all sit outside it.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: most international lending, the brokerage, expansion into new states, and a large part of the branch network.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Very. It means giving up the "one of the largest banks in the world" identity.

**What would confirm it within 12 to 24 months:**
1. Profit by business line shows that home-state consumer and middle-market banking earns well above the bank's average return.
2. Profit per employee rises while assets shrink.
3. Deposit retention holds as rates are deregulated.
4. Home-state credit losses run well below international and sector losses.

```
 │                         │
 │                         ▼

 │                         │
 │                         ▼

 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 │   5. More primary relationships per household and business
 │        (deposits + card + loans + fees)
 │                         │
 │                         ▼
 └── 6. Stickier funding and better credit information on each customer
```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Recognize credit losses in full.** Run an independent review of the energy, shipping, agricultural and developing-country loans, then reserve and disclose. The flywheel cannot turn on top of a hidden hole.
2. **Start the cost turn.** Build a profitability map branch by branch, consolidate the weakest branches, and freeze hiring with reductions through attrition. Every dollar saved funds market-rate deposits.

**Doom-loop check: currently in a doom loop.**
- **Big moves to jump-start momentum:** the brokerage purchase, and "expanding into new businesses and states to grow out of the problems" sitting on the options menu.
- **Growth without understanding:** the 15% average annual growth, and the jump of about 50% in two years, came without profit-by-line data to justify them.
- **Reaction without understanding:** losses in four sectors at once point to a portfolio built for size, not for knowledge.

The loop closes on paper. The bank is not yet pushing it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop using asset growth as the measure of success.** That covers internal targets, investor messaging and executive pay.
2. **Stop acquisitions and new business lines for 24 months.** No further investment in building out the brokerage.
3. **Stop expanding into new states and countries.** Close or shrink overseas offices that exist only to book corporate or sovereign loans.
4. **Stop new lending to developing-country governments, and to shipping and energy borrowers outside the home state.** Existing exposure goes to the wind-down unit.
5. **Stop writing long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded by deposits that are moving to market rates.** Price for rate risk, or originate to sell.
6. **Stop keeping branches whose job was to collect cheap deposits that will no longer be cheap.** Every branch must earn its place on profit, not on presence.
7. **Stop counting "largest agricultural lender in the world" as a strength.** Agricultural lending is judged on loss-adjusted return, like everything else.
8. **Stop hiring outside the Hedgehog Concept.** Any vacancy outside it stays empty.

## Appendix: gate workings

**Gate 0: subject card.**
- **Name:** Western Commonwealth Bank.
- **Type:** business (listed bank holding company).
- **Steward:** CEO and board.
- **Time horizon:** decades.
- **Stage:** mature. It is a former growth champion entering a deregulated, credit-stressed phase.
- **Fit:** strong. It is an ongoing endeavor with rearrangeable resources and measurable outcomes.

**Gate 1: baseline sheet.**
- **Evidence of good:**
  - The dominant bank in its home state.
  - Pioneered the first successful bank credit card and early computerized record-keeping.
  - Assets reached $112.9B, and it was the only one of the 20 largest banks to average 15% growth (11 to 5 years ago).
  - Retail produced about half of profits in the late 1960s and 1970s.
- **Gaps:** none of this evidence covers current profitability.
- **Definition of great:**
  1. Superior performance: top-quartile return on assets and profit per employee among large U.S. banks.
  2. Distinctive impact: the home state's indispensable household and business bank.
  3. Endurance: the performance survives deregulation and changes of CEO.

**Gate 2: leadership read.**
- **Strengths:** analytical training and experience in monetary policy, which suit the macroeconomic and funding problems.
- **Gaps:**
  - His career inside the bank and his stated priorities are unknown.
  - He inherited a growth culture and a loan book that is now deteriorating.
  - The predecessor left for an outside post, and there is no evidence of deliberate succession grooming.
- **Window and mirror risk:** he may defend the inherited strategy to look continuous, or blame it to look clean. Both block a blameless autopsy.
- **One behavior change:** personally announce the independent loan review and the expansion freeze, and own the result.

**Gate 6: framework boundaries.**
- **Framework (fixed):**
  - Lending only in the home state, apart from wind-down.
  - Every unit reports profit per employee and its cost ratio.
  - Pay is tied to loss-adjusted profit, not asset growth.
- **Freedom inside the framework:** branch and regional managers decide how to serve customers, how to staff, and which local products to emphasize.
- **Bureaucracy check:** approval layers built up during rapid growth to control far-flung lending will become unnecessary once that lending stops. Remove them.
- **Fanatical consistency:** a monthly profit-per-employee review at every level.

**Gate 7: tech triage.**

| Technology | Classification |
|---|---|
| Automated teller machines and electronic branch automation | **Accelerates.** Directly lowers cost to serve. Pioneer opportunity. |
| Customer- and branch-level profitability systems | **Accelerates.** Makes the denominator visible. Pioneer opportunity. |

| Core record-keeping systems | **Neutral / parity.** Keep up. |

**Gate 9: clock-building mechanisms.**
- A Chief Credit Officer independent of the CEO, reporting to the board risk committee.
- Profit-by-line reporting as a permanent board agenda item.
- Executive pay tied to the scorecard.
- A written succession plan, with two internal candidates developed in the core business.
- An annual brutal-facts review, chaired by someone other than the CEO.
