## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ashcombe is a shaving company that happens to sell a few other things, not a diversified consumer-products company that happens to sell razors.

- **Product:** it releases a new premium cartridge system on a steady cadence, and each one is clearly better than the last.
- **Global reach:** it sells that system in every major market, so one R&D investment earns returns across the world.
- **Measure of success:** profit per repeat shaver, not razors shipped.
- **Disposables:** it keeps a disposable on the shelf as a flank defense and as an entry point that leads people up to systems. It never makes the disposable the strategy.
- **Price fights:** it refuses fights it can't win on price, so the lighters go. It does not diversify out of anxiety, because the watch, calculator and smoke-alarm years already taught that lesson.
- **Continuity:** it develops leaders from inside, so the next chief executive continues the flywheel instead of resetting it.

**Is this commitment uncomfortable? Yes.**
- It implies letting the lighter go.
- It implies not chasing disposable unit share even if total share dips.
- It implies treating a successful deodorant ($118M investment, about a quarter of its market) as outside the core.

**Confirmation tests over 12–24 months:**
1. Line data shows shaving's return on capital beats toiletries, pens and appliances.
2. A disposable-to-system trade-up rate can be measured and improved.
3. The next premium system holds a price premium against disposables.
4. Shaving profit per customer rises even if unit share slips slightly.

If tests 1 or 3 fail, reopen this gate.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop fighting the lighter price war.** Exit disposable lighters.
2. **Stop pushing the disposable to win total razor unit share.** Defend the low end, don't lead with it.
3. **Stop competing with Rival B on price in any category:** lighters, low-priced pens or disposables.
4. **Stop entering new categories outside shaving.** No new watch, calculator, perfume or alarm-style ventures, and no "diversified consumer products" acquisitions.
5. **Stop launching new toiletry categories.** Hair coloring and shampoo have already failed once. Run the existing toiletries for cash.
6. **Stop running non-shaving small appliances** at the German subsidiary. Sell or harvest them.
7. **Stop using total revenue and razor unit share as headline success metrics.** Replace them with the scorecard below.
