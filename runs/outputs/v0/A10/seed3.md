# Good to Great: Harwell-Brandt Company

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Harwell-Brandt wins by owning the #1, clinically proven brand in each everyday self-care need of the mouth, throat and stomach, and judges itself by profit per #1 brand.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** *proof*. The flagship brand's main health claim was ruled unsupported, and the order stands. A self-care franchise that can't prove what it says has nothing to compound.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop changing the company's identity. There have been two declared directions in about a year.
  2. Sell the bakery and optical businesses.
  3. Stop the open-ended "revive pharmaceuticals" push until a pipeline test passes. Larger drug companies set the pace there, and regulators have already forced a carve-up of the drug unit.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** return on equity is about 9% and margins fell about 40% in one year. The company is funding many businesses, few of them leaders, and has not chosen between them.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Segment profits and returns by business.
  - Market shares in gum, cough drops, antacids and shaving.
  - Research and development spending, plus late-stage pipeline quality.

  A strong proprietary drug pipeline would flip the Hedgehog Concept toward pharmaceuticals. Weak profits in the consumer brands would undercut it.

Greatness potential: 12/100

*Why so low even with a plan:* Collins found very few companies that made a sustained good-to-great shift. This company starts with weak returns, a sprawling portfolio, unresolved legal and ethics exposure, a new leadership team with no track record, and a habit of changing direction. Acting on this plan improves the odds but does not make greatness likely.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Harwell-Brandt is a focused consumer-health company, not a holding company. It owns the leading, scientifically supported brand in a small set of everyday self-care needs: oral care (mouthwash, breath-freshening gum), throat (cough drops) and stomach (antacid and effervescent remedies). It sells them across the countries where it can operate cleanly. Its pharmaceutical heritage is used for one purpose: clinical rigor that makes every claim hold up, plus a stream of prescription-to-over-the-counter candidates. The bakery and optical units are gone. Shaving is sold or harvested for cash. Capital goes only to brands that are #1, or that can credibly become #1. It is known for claims regulators never have to correct and plants with no fatal accidents. It earns returns well above its industry for fifteen or more years, under leaders who stay out of the headlines.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Clinically proven efficacy                │
   │    (pharma-grade testing on self-care)       │
   └───────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                   ▼
   2. Claims that survive regulators → credible advertising
                   ▼
   3. Consumer trust → #1 shelf position in pharmacy/grocery
                   ▼
   4. Pricing power → PROFIT PER #1 BRAND
                   ▼
   5. Reinvest in: clinical science, line extensions,
      Rx-to-OTC switch candidates, small brands that can become #1
                   │
                   └────────────► back to 1
   (Fuel injected once: proceeds from selling bakery, optical, shaving)
```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Fix link 1.** Build a substantiation function so no claim goes out without controlled evidence behind it. This turns the corrective-advertising failure into the company's signature discipline.
2. **Concentrate capital.** Divestiture proceeds go only to #1 brands and to switch candidates.

**Doom-loop check, with signs present today:**
- **New direction after new direction:** consumer-products leader, then pharmaceutical revival, within about a year.
- **Acquisitions used to jump-start momentum:** the $243M cash bakery purchase two years ago, plus a decade-long acquisition string.
- **Reaction without understanding:** a margin collapse followed by a new strategic story, before the portfolio review is finished.
- **Inconsistency:** a new chairman and CEO after only a few years of the previous chief.

The company is currently in a doom loop. The plan works only if it stops announcing and starts pushing.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** (proposed; must be ratified with employees):
1. Every claim is true and proven.
2. No one gets hurt making our products.
3. We earn business cleanly or not at all.
4. We lead or we leave: be #1 in a category, or exit it.

**Core purpose:** *Help people take care of their everyday health with remedies they can trust.*

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Hold the #1 brand in every self-care need we serve, in every major market we operate in, with returns in the top quartile of consumer and health companies.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- **Claims board:** an independent clinical review that can veto advertising.
- **Capital rule:** the "#1 or path to #1" test applied to every investment and acquisition.
- **Market-exit rule:** country exit triggered by any compliance breach.
- **Safety audits:** plant safety audits reported directly to the board.
- **Succession:** CEO succession planned from inside the self-care business.
