# Good to Great: Harwell-Brandt Company

## Verdict (one screen)

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Harwell-Brandt makes the world's most trusted everyday remedies for the mouth, throat and stomach, and stays only in categories it can lead with claims it can prove.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** proof. The flagship mouthwash's cold-prevention claims were ruled unsupported, the company was ordered to run about $10M of corrective advertising, and the Supreme Court let that order stand. Every turn of the flywheel depends on proven efficacy that earns trust, and that link is broken today.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop acquiring, and stop announcing new strategic directions. Two opposite futures in one year is a doom loop.
2. Sell the bakery chain and the optical business. They sit outside every version of the company's logic.
3. Stop making any health claim that is not substantiated before launch.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Return on equity is about 9% after margins fell about 40%. In that position, leadership has announced two opposite futures in twelve months: first "leading consumer-products company", now "revive pharmaceuticals". Nothing in the record shows it yet knows which units actually earn their capital.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Segment and brand profits, and return on capital by unit.
- Prescription R&D spend and the drug pipeline. A real lead in a narrow therapeutic area would reopen the pharma Hedgehog.
- Market shares in cough drops, antacids, gum and shaving.
- Cost of capital, to judge whether 9% ROE destroys value.

**Bottom line:** The company has good brands inside a portfolio that is not yet good, and it shows active doom-loop behavior. Greatness is possible only through hard narrowing, and the odds are long.

Greatness potential: 15/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Harwell-Brandt is a focused self-care health company, not a conglomerate. It holds the #1 or a strong #2 position in a small number of everyday remedy categories: mouthwash, cough drops, antacid/effervescent remedies, and possibly sugarless gum if gum earns its place as oral care. It sells these in many of its roughly 140 countries. It uses the scientific capacity of its old drug-house heritage to prove what its products do, so pharmacists, doctors and regulators become allies rather than adversaries. It does not own a bakery, an optical company or a second-place razor business. It does not chase the large research-based drug companies on their own ground unless an audit shows a narrow therapeutic area where it can genuinely lead. It grows by pushing the same loop consistently: proof, trust, credible claims, repeat purchase, profit per leading brand, reinvestment in proof. It does not grow through deals.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Proven efficacy (clinical substantiation before claims)
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    2. Trust from pharmacists, doctors, regulators
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    3. Credible advertising that survives scrutiny
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    4. Category-leading share and habitual repeat purchase
   │              │
   │              ▼
   │    5. Operating profit per category-leading brand  ◄── denominator
   │              │
   │              ▼
   └─── 6. Reinvest in substantiation science, new forms,
           and rollout of leading brands to more countries
```

**Weakest link:** #1, proven efficacy. Regulators found the mouthwash's cold claims unsupported, and the order was upheld.

**Current push (next two turns):**
- Rebuild #1: create a claims-substantiation function with veto power, and run the corrective advertising cleanly rather than grudgingly.
- Strengthen #5: get brand-level profit visibility so capital goes to category leaders, not to defending also-rans.

**Doom-loop check (failing today):**
- Big moves to jump-start momentum: decades of mergers, plus a $243M cash bakery purchase two years ago.
- Direction after direction: consumer-products leader, then pharma revival, inside one year.
- Leadership churn: a multi-year CEO replaced by a new chairman and CEO.
- Reaction without understanding: a new direction announced while the portfolio review is still underway.

The loop closes logically, but the company's behavior is a doom loop. That makes this gate `CONDITIONAL`.
