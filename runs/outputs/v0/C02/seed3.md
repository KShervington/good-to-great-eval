## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Meridian is not "the stapler company." It is the company that changed how surgeons close tissue, one procedure at a time:

- **Training:** every procedure it enters comes with a validated training curriculum, published outcome data and a cadre of surgeon advocates.
- **Revenue:** it comes from recurring cartridges used by surgeons who staple routinely, not from instruments sitting in OR cabinets.
- **Teaching:** stapling is taught in surgical residencies, so new surgeons arrive already converted.
- **Sales force:** reps are clinical educators, paid when surgeons keep using the product.
- **Leadership:** the founder's resolve has been turned into mechanisms. These are the training system, the usage scorecard, a quality-and-complaint loop and a successor bench, so greatness doesn't depend on him.
- **Competition:** when large competitors arrive, they find surgeons trained on Meridian, outcomes data built on Meridian, and a lead in each procedure that takes years to copy.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying and celebrating on instrument placements.** Commission on cartridge pull-through at 90 and 180 days.
2. **Stop developing instruments whose purpose is spreading marketing cost.** Build only instruments that open new stapled procedures.
3. **Stop letting distributors own surgeon adoption.** They handle fulfilment only.
4. **Stop entering procedures without a training curriculum and an outcomes plan.**
5. **Stop founder-sponsored gadget projects** outside stapling and the closure mechanics that serve it.
6. **Do not enter sutures** or a general wound-closure fight with the suture makers.
7. **Stop running the company without monthly usage and margin numbers.**
8. **Stop treating rep churn as cost control.** Measure payback on the $8K training per rep.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make surgery faster, safer and less bloody through better mechanics.

**Core values:**
1. Surgeon and patient outcome first.
2. Train before we sell.
3. Tell the truth about the product, especially when it fails.
4. Earn usage, not placements.
5. Ingenuity in service of the operating room.

**BHAG (20-year):** stapling becomes the taught standard of care in every U.S. surgical residency, for every procedure where it improves outcomes.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A training curriculum run as a product, with versioning and outcome tracking.
- A usage-based compensation system.
- A quality and complaint loop with board visibility.
- A clinical evidence program run with teaching hospitals.
- Independent directors.
- A named successor bench reviewed yearly.
