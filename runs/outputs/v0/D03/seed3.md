# Ranvik: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Ranvik is the fastest, safest way for a farmer to get a big machine from a farmer they don't know yet, when the weather window won't wait, built one dense county at a time.*

**January decision: say no to input financing.** It sits outside the Hedgehog. Its year-one model does not match its own facility terms. It would take both engineers away through planting season.

**Weakest flywheel link:** the second rental. A pair that met on Ranvik has little reason to book the next rental through the app. 71% of surveyed renters swapped phone numbers with their owner, but only 11% of renters re-booked the same owner on Ranvik.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Input financing, the lending-compliance hire, and the request for lending intros.
2. The two-state launch next spring. Make the 29 thin counties dense first.
3. Treating "a neighbor I can call" as the success metric. It is how revenue leaks off the platform.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the "community" in the update is probably disintermediation. Phone masking has been off since May. The Ostrander–Kemp story is two customers who left the marketplace to co-own a baler. A $2,500 combine booking carries about $350 in combined fees, which is a strong reason to call directly next time. Nobody is measuring how much of this happens.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Off-platform repeat rate for pairs who met on Ranvik.
- Actual net revenue by county.
- Whether net revenue includes protection premiums, and who carries claim risk.
- Recourse and first-loss terms of the forward-flow facility.
- Gross monthly spend by season.

Greatness potential: 14/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the default answer in every county it serves to one specific question: *"The window just opened, and I need a planter or combine I don't own, by tomorrow."* Ranvik is dense enough that the answer is almost always yes. It is trusted enough that renting a $300k machine from a stranger feels routine, because protection, payment and hour-meter records come with every booking. It is useful enough that the second, fifth and tenth rental between the same neighbors still goes through the app, because the app is easier and safer than cash and a phone call. Ranvik grows by deepening counties, not by adding states or lending products. Its economics are measured per county, and a county is a self-sustaining unit that a field rep owns. It works without Ines at every barbecue.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Dense supply of high-value machines in a county
 │        (accurate calendars, planters/combines listed)
 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 │   2. Urgent requests get a fast "yes" in the weather window
 │        (peak fill rate ↑, date-conflict declines ↓)
 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 │   3. First-time high-value pairing, booked, paid and protected
 │        on Ranvik   ──►  NET REVENUE PER COUNTY ↑
 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 │   4. Safe outcome: hour-meter record, claims paid (the baler fire)
 │        → renting from a stranger feels normal
 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 │   5. ⚠ The repeat pair stays on Ranvik because it's easier and safer
 │        than cash-by-phone  ← WEAKEST LINK
 │                       │
 │                       ▼
 └── 6. Owners earn more and list more machines; renters tell neighbors
```

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Measure, then reinforce link 5.** Ship "only on Ranvik" repeat value before planting: one-tap rebook, a test of a lower repeat-pair rate, protection and hour-meter records that exist only for on-app bookings, and ACH convenience. Do **not** turn phone masking back on. It fought users, and support tickets fell 38% after it was removed.
2. **Push link 1 in the 29 thin counties.** Field reps recruit planter and combine supply specifically, and keep owner calendars accurate. This attacks the 1,140 declines for date conflicts.

**Doom-loop check (several warning signs present):**
- **Three new directions at once:** financing, two new states, and Ranvik Pro.
- **A big program to jump-start growth:** $28M of lending to "more than double" revenue.
- **Strategy taken from peers' fundraising:** "two ag-fintech companies raised $40M+."
- **Reaction without understanding:** expanding before the leakage problem has been measured.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Input financing.** Decline at the January board, drop the lending-compliance hire, and withdraw the investor ask for lending intros.
2. **Two-state expansion next spring.** Also stop asking for dealer and retailer intros in new states. Ask for intros in the 29 existing thin counties.
3. **Measuring success by phone-number exchange and "a neighbor I can call."** Replace it with on-platform repeat capture.
4. **Ines personally running the barbecue, fair and co-op circuit.** Hand it to the field reps. Redesign Ranvik Neighbors so the *next* rental gets booked in the app, for example with the baler-fire claim story and a rebook demo.
5. **Building Ranvik Pro as a separate product line this season.**
6. **Charging the full 14% on every repeat rental between the same pair, untested.** Test a lower repeat-pair rate; capturing some of that revenue beats losing all of it. `[ASSUMPTION — needs the pricing test]`
7. **Using other companies' fundraising rounds as evidence for strategy.**
8. **Investor updates with no "what isn't working" section.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every booking is offered protection.
- Every request gets a response within a set number of hours during peak windows. The number is to be set.
- Every new proposal must state how it raises net revenue per county.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Field reps** choose tactics inside their counties.
- **Engineers** choose how to make on-app booking beat the phone.
- **Nobody** launches a new geography or product line without board sign-off against the Hedgehog filter.

**Bureaucracy check:** at 9 people, there is little bureaucracy to strip out. The real risk is *importing* it. A regulated lending line would bring compliance overhead into a 9-person company.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** keep farms running when timing matters most, by putting the machines that already exist to work.
- **Core values:**
  1. **Respect the window.** The field doesn't wait, and neither do we.
  2. **Stand behind every rental.** When something breaks, we pay and we show up.
  3. **Tell farmers the truth.** That includes the board and ourselves.
  4. **Earn the next booking.** Never trap people; be worth coming back to.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** No farmer in the Midwest loses a planting or harvest window for lack of a machine that exists within 25 miles.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A per-county scorecard reviewed monthly.
- Field reps who each own named counties.
- A claims process that runs without the founders.
- A written Hedgehog filter for every new product or geography.
- Community events run by reps, not by the CEO.
