# Ranvik: Good to Great

## Verdict (one screen)

**Bottom line:** Ranvik is genuinely *good*. GMV grew 2.26x, net revenue grew 2.34x, and both sides of the marketplace grew. It is not yet on a path to *great*, for two reasons. Its best moment, the first match between strangers, turns into relationships that then leave the platform. And the January proposal would point every engineer at a different business. **Recommendation: decline input financing at the January board meeting, defer the two new states, and spend next season making Ranvik win the second booking as well as the first.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Ranvik is the fastest trusted way for a farmer to get a big machine from a nearby stranger in the week it matters, built one dense county at a time.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** "Window saved → next need comes back to Ranvik." 71% of renters swapped phone numbers with their owner, and phone masking is now off. Yet only 11% rebooked the same owner on Ranvik. Ranvik's "community" is, in part, its leakage.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:**
  1. Input financing.
  2. Two-state launch next spring.
  3. Counting off-platform relationships as a success metric.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** The financing case doesn't hold up on its own numbers. The model assumes **$28M** of year-one loans on a **$6M** facility. Pay-after-harvest loans can't show repayment until after harvest, so expansion can't come in year one. At $6M, revenue is about **$236k**, not $1.1M. The cost would be both engineers for about five months, which covers the entire pre-season build window.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  1. The leakage rate: what share of first pairings transact off-platform afterward.
  2. Count and peak-week utilization of listed high-value machines.
  3. Who underwrites damage protection, and the maximum exposure per claim.
  4. Launch dates of the 29 non-home counties: young, or stalled?
  5. The forward-flow terms: first-loss or repurchase obligations.

`Greatness potential: 20/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ranvik is the place a farmer opens the moment a wet week, a breakdown or a short harvest window threatens a field. It wins because its network is wider than any farmer's address book, not because it is the only way to find a neighbor. In every county it serves, enough high-value machines are listed that a peak-window request almost never goes unfilled. A declined request is rerouted to another machine a few miles away within minutes. Each handoff is paid, protected and hour-metered, so owners trust strangers with a $300k machine. Repeat neighbors keep booking through Ranvik because the protection, payment record and calendar are worth more than the fee. It grows outward one adjacent dense county at a time, and it runs on a county playbook rather than on Ines's personal presence at every barbecue. It does not lend money, sell inputs or chase county counts.

| **Deep passion** | Neighbors helping neighbors (Ines, users); careful logistics (Paul); keeping fields on schedule | **No field waits for a machine that's sitting idle nearby** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from the "wet week planter" story and Ines's farm background]` | "Community" as an end in itself. It is a real passion, but it pulls against the engine. |

**Evidence it explains past wins:**
- Combines and planters were 9% of bookings (about 478) but 31% of GMV (about $1.2M), averaging about $2,500 per booking against about $551 for everything else.
- "Most of those were first-time pairings": the planter found 11 miles away and booked in an afternoon.
- 23 points of the 34% repeat rate came from renters booking a *different* owner. The network beats the address book.

**What it rules out:** input financing, new states before density, owner SaaS as a separate revenue line, community events with no supply goal, and chasing small-implement volume.

**Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It says Ranvik's proudest story, "a neighbor I can call," is not the business. It also says no to a pitch from the lead investor.

**Confirmation tests (next season):**
1. In counties where high-value listings rise, peak fill rate and net revenue per high-value machine rise too.
2. A leakage study shows that new first-time pairings keep growing faster than relationships leave.
3. Renters who already know an owner still book on Ranvik when that owner's machine is busy. Peak demand is correlated, so the neighbor's planter is often busy in the same wet week.
4. High-value categories rise above 31% of GMV.

## 3. The flywheel

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │ 1. Dense high-value supply in a county        │
        │    (combines, planters, sprayers listed)      │
        └───────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                        ▼
        2. Peak-window requests filled fast
           (date-conflict declines rerouted to a machine a few miles away)
                        ▼
        3. Window saved, deal trusted
           (paid, protected, hour-metered)
                        ▼
        4. Next need comes back to Ranvik   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
           (the network beats the address book when every neighbor's machine is busy)
                        ▼
        5. Owners earn more per machine → list more big machines;
           renters tell neighbors
                        ▼
        6. Net revenue per high-value machine rises
           → funds field reps opening the next ADJACENT county
                        │
                        └──────────────► back to 1
```

**Current push (next 1–2 turns):**
- **Turn link 2.** 1,140 requests were declined, mostly over date conflicts. That is about 18% of requests and up to about $0.83M of GMV at the average booking value (an upper bound, since some likely rebooked). Build "declined → here are three nearby alternatives" before spring.
- **Turn link 1.** Run a high-value supply drive in the 12 home counties and the strongest adjacent non-home counties before planting.

**Doom-loop check (early warning signs are present):**
- **Big program to jump-start momentum:** input financing, pitched as "more than double this season's net revenue" in one move.
- **New directions stacked at once:** financing, two new states and Ranvik Pro, all for one 9-person team, in one spring.
- **Hype-driven:** "Two ag-fintech companies raised $40M+" is a reason to do something only if it fits the Hedgehog.
- **Reaction without full understanding:** turning off phone masking fixed a real problem at the field edge (support tickets fell 38%). It also removed the main barrier to leakage, and the effect was never measured.

Ranvik is not yet in a doom loop, but January decides which way it goes.
