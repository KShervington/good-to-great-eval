# Ranvik: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> *Ranvik is how a farmer gets the high-value machine they need, from an owner they don't yet know, before the weather window closes, built one dense county at a time.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** what happens after the first match. 71% of surveyed renters swapped phone numbers with their owner, but only 11% rebooked that owner through Ranvik. The platform makes the introduction, and the repeat business probably goes direct. Density is the second weak link: 29 of the 41 counties average about one-fifth the GMV of the core 12.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. **Don't launch input financing in January.** Defer it against explicit criteria, and don't make the lending-compliance hire.
2. **Don't launch two new states next spring.** Bring the 29 thin counties up to core density first.
3. **Stop measuring community by phone numbers swapped.** Measure on-platform repeats. Stop having the CEO personally run the barbecues; turn them into supply-listing drives run by the field reps.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the community story you're proudest of may be the leak. A repeat booking between neighbors who know each other is worth about $96 of Ranvik revenue ($512k net revenue across 5,310 bookings), and a phone call skips it. The January proposal would put both of your engineers on lending for five months, right before the season that has to fund your next raise. Runway is about 18.7 months.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- How often number-swapped pairs transact off-platform.
- GMV and contribution margin by county.
- Who bears the loss on a total-loss combine claim.
- The booking-request funnel: expired and cancelled requests, not only declines.
- The real terms of the bank facility: first-loss, repurchase, servicing.

Greatness potential: 16/100

*Method note: this run had no memory, past chats or web search, so no prior baseline or market research was available. Everything below comes from the founders' season update. Inferences are marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In the great version, Ranvik is the first thing a farmer in its region opens when the weather turns and they're one machine short. They book a planter, combine, baler or grain cart from an owner they've never met, within the hour, protected, with engine hours recorded.

It wins county by county. Each county gets deep enough supply of high-value machines that most requests are filled even in peak weeks. Pairs that already know each other keep booking in the app because rebooking, protection and payment there are easier than a handshake and an invoice.

It does not lend, sell inputs, or run a social network. Its community is the trust layer and the supply-recruiting engine; it is not the product. Each new county opens with a repeatable playbook, not Ines's personal presence. The loss ratio on damage protection is boring and the tail risk is covered.

**Denominator:** net revenue per county-season. The operating proxy is the **number of counties above a density threshold**. A working threshold is ≥ $150k GMV per season `[ASSUMPTION]`, to be calibrated against fill-rate data.

**Tests of the concept:**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes: high-value first-time pairings and the 12-county core.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: input financing, new states, low-value categories, and community as the product.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It says no to the board member's plan and to the CEO's enthusiasm, and it reframes the community story.

**Evidence that would confirm or kill it (by next harvest):**
1. High-value categories grow their share of net revenue.
2. Counties brought above the threshold show rising fill rates and revenue per county.
3. Measured off-platform leakage among known pairs is below about 40%, or repeat-pair pricing and tooling recovers it `[ASSUMPTION threshold]`.
4. The protection loss ratio stays below about 60%, with a total combine loss covered by a third party.

If test 3 fails badly, the first match alone has to carry the economics. That would need a higher first-booking take rate or a per-introduction fee, and the Hedgehog Concept should be revisited.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   │                         │
   │                         ▼

   │       (fill rate; declined requests rerouted)

   │                         ▼
   │    3. Trusted, protected transaction
   │       (damage protection, hour meters, payment)
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │    4. Owner earns safely; the pair rebooks IN-APP   ◄── WEAKEST LINK (11%)
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │    5. More owners list high-value machines and keep calendars accurate
   │                         │
   │                         ▼
   │    6. County density rises → net revenue per county-season ↑
   │                         │
   └───────── more farmers in that county try Ranvik first
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
- **Turn A (links 2→3):** the **wet-week rescue**. When an owner declines a request or a calendar conflicts, automatically offer the next-nearest matching machine, and nudge owners to refresh calendars before planting and harvest. Last season had 1,140 declines, against 5,310 completed bookings (about 18% of requests that got an answer). At $727 each, that is up to about $829k of unmet GMV, if even part of it could be filled `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Turn B (link 4):** make the second booking easier in the app than on the phone. Add one-tap rebook, a lower take rate for repeat pairs (test it), protection valid only on-platform, and hour-meter records plus tax-ready statements for owners.

**Doom-loop check: early signs are present.**
- **Three new directions in one off-season:** financing, two states and Ranvik Pro.
- **A big program pitched to jump-start growth:** "more than double this season's net revenue."
- **Partly hype-driven:** two ag-fintech companies raised $40M+.
- **Inconsistent:** the success metric (community) points away from the engine (on-platform transactions).

None of these has happened yet. January decides it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Input financing launch:** defer at the January board meeting, against written revisit criteria.
2. **Two-state spring expansion:** stop it. 68% of GMV in 12 counties means the model hasn't yet reproduced itself even inside current territory.
3. **Community measured as phone numbers swapped:** replace it with on-platform same-pair repeats.
4. **CEO-run barbecues:** hand them to field reps as listing drives. The goal is a listed combine, not an introduction.
5. **Ranvik Pro as a paid subscription:** stop until leakage and power-owner economics are known.
6. **GMV growth as the headline investor metric:** lead with fill rate, dense-county count and same-pair repeat.
7. **Spend on low-value categories that are easy to repeat off-platform:** stop marketing them. Allow listings, but don't promote them.
8. **Unbounded protection exposure:** no combine-class protection without confirmed third-party tail cover `[ASSUMPTION: current structure unknown]`.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** no farmer loses a crop for want of a machine sitting idle down the road.

**Core values:**
1. **Speed when the weather turns.** Every hour of a window matters.
2. **Machines come back as good as they left.** Protect the owner first.
3. **A farmer's word is good, and we make it safe to trust a stranger's.**
4. **Honest numbers, especially the bad ones.**

**BHAG (15-year):** within a day's tow, any farmer across the Corn Belt can get any field machine they need, inside the weather window, through Ranvik.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A county density playbook that a field rep can run without Ines.
- A weekly in-season fill-rate dashboard.
- A standing red-flag agenda item at every board meeting, owned by Paul.
- A protection reserve-and-reinsurance policy.
- A one-page Hedgehog filter that every new initiative must pass in writing.
