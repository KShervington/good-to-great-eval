# Brightwater Drug Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the most convenient, fairly priced neighborhood drugstore in the Sunbelt markets we choose to dominate, measured by profit per customer visit.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** reinvestment. Cash from the drugstores is going into department stores, security guards, food-service supply, optical and out-of-region chains instead of densifying drugstore clusters. The wheel turns, but capital leaks out on every turn.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop buying businesses that aren't drugstores. Put the junior department stores, the security company and the food-service distributor up for sale.
2. Stop expanding optical centers until the numbers show they raise drugstore profit per visit.
3. Stop adding chains outside contiguous Sunbelt clusters, and review the Mid-Atlantic chain.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Rival W, the largest U.S. drug chain (about 600 stores, sales near $1B), is now operating in Brightwater's home state. It arrived while Brightwater's founder has spent five years running for office and the company has been spreading itself across five unrelated industries.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by business line.
- Profit per store and per customer visit, compared with Rival W in shared markets.
- Store count and market share by metro.
- Returns on the acquisitions made over the last four years.
- The new drugstore CEO's track record.

If the non-drug lines prove highly profitable and capital-light, the stop-doing list would soften from "sell" to "ring-fence." The Hedgehog Concept would not change.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brightwater is a pure drugstore company. It owns the convenience-and-pharmacy trip in a set of fast-growing Sunbelt metros, where it has more well-sited stores per neighborhood than anyone, Rival W included. Every store is judged by one number, profit per customer visit. Every dollar of capital goes into stores that raise that number: infill locations in existing clusters, pharmacy capacity, and regional distribution. The department stores, security business and food-service distributor have been sold to owners who value them more. The founder's best legacy is institutional, not personal. He leaves self-service, fair pricing and fair treatment of employees embedded in a management team and a board that run the company the same way whether or not he is in the room. When Rival W expands in the region, it finds markets where Brightwater is already the default stop.

     -> fairer prices, better in-stock
          |

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Close the leak at link 4. Freeze non-drug acquisitions and optical expansion, and start the sales process for the non-core businesses.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** buying businesses that aren't drugstores. No exceptions for "great deals."
2. **Stop** owning the junior department stores, security company and food-service distributor. Run an orderly sale over 12–24 months.
3. **Stop** opening optical centers. Freeze the count, then keep them only if a test shows they raise profit per drugstore visit.
4. **Stop** acquiring drug chains outside contiguous Sunbelt clusters. Formally review the Mid-Atlantic chain: densify it into a cluster or exit.
5. **Stop** reporting store count and total sales as headline success metrics. Lead with profit per customer visit.
6. **Stop** letting a partner's real-estate pipeline decide where stores go.
7. **Stop** having a founder who is part-time in politics make major decisions or acquisitions.
8. **Stop** (pre-emptively) any blanket price war against Rival W. Compete market by market, from density.
