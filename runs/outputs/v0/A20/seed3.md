## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brightwater is not a conglomerate. It is a drugstore company, and in practice the Sunbelt drugstore company. It holds the densest store network in each market it chooses. Stores sit on the highest-traffic sites, many next to its supermarket partner. It is self-service, priced as a discounter, and anchored by a pharmacy people trust. Every store, manager and capital decision is judged by one number: profit per customer visit. It grows by filling in markets it already leads before entering new ones. It turns acquisitions into Brightwater stores quickly or does not make them. It does not need its founder's charisma to run, because it is managed by measurement and by people who own their results. When Rival W enters one of its markets, Brightwater does not panic or diversify. It is already the more convenient, cheaper and denser option.

**Current push (next two turns):**
1. **Measure link 5.** Build a profit-per-visit figure for every store this quarter.
2. **Seal link 6.** Commit all new capital to drugstores and use the home-state clusters to defend against Rival W.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are present.
- **New direction after new direction:** candy was bought and then sold; department stores, security, food service and optical were all added.
- **Acquisitions used to jump-start growth:** five chain acquisitions in four years, some outside the core region (Mid-Atlantic).
- **Inconsistent leadership attention:** two political campaigns in five years.

No single sign is fatal. Together they form the classic pattern Collins describes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop owning junior department stores. Divest.
2. Stop owning security and guard services. Divest.
3. Stop owning food-service supply. Divest.
4. Stop opening new optical centers until the in-store test proves they lift drugstore profit per visit.
5. Stop acquiring drug chains until the last five are converted and earning. After that, buy only where it builds density in Sunbelt clusters.
6. Stop entering regions where Brightwater cannot become the densest operator. Review the Mid-Atlantic chain against this rule.
7. Stop running decisions through a part-time chairman.
8. Stop reporting store count and total sales as the headline. Lead with profit per visit and return on capital.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every store has a monthly profit-per-visit figure.
- Every new site passes a density and traffic test.
- Capital goes only into the core drugstore business.
- Every acquisition has a 12-month conversion plan with a named owner.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Fixed centrally:** pricing posture (discount), self-service format, pharmacy standards, site criteria.
- **Store managers decide:** local assortment, staffing and community ties, within their profit-per-visit targets.

**Bureaucracy check:** `[ASSUMPTION]` Any approval layers built to watch the acquired chains' managers are a symptom of unresolved bus decisions. Resolve the people questions and remove the layers.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. A fair deal for the customer, on price, honesty and convenience.
2. Fairness to employees, who are the company's greatest asset.
3. Pharmacist-grade trust and integrity.
4. Practical innovation in serving customers, in the self-service tradition.

**Core purpose:** make everyday health and household needs easy and affordable for Sunbelt families.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** be the number one drugstore, by density and by profit per visit, in every Sunbelt market Brightwater serves, and outperform the national leader inside its home territory.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- profit-per-visit reporting for every store;
- a capital allocation rule that funds the core only;
- an acquisition conversion playbook;
- a store-manager and pharmacist development pipeline;
- a board majority of independent directors;
- a written succession plan that does not depend on the founder.
