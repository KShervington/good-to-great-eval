## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Marlow is not the chain with the most cities. It owns its home three-state region, and perhaps one or two others where it already has a real base. In those regions it is the default place to buy a TV, a stereo or an appliance. Density keeps advertising and distribution costs per store the lowest in the market. Concentrated volume brings better terms from suppliers, and Marlow passes those savings back to customers as real low prices, not promotional noise. It still uses its installment-credit heritage to help customers afford big purchases `[ASSUMPTION: credit capability still exists]`. It grows one region at a time. A new region is entered only when the current one is saturated and profitable. Its success is judged on a single number, profit per region. That number keeps rising whoever the president or owner happens to be.

## 3. The flywheel

```

     │            │

     │            │
     │            ▼
     │   3. Genuinely lower prices (+ credit to afford)
     │            │
     │            ▼
     │   4. Traffic and volume per store
     │            │
     │            ▼
     │   5. Buying power → lower cost of goods   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
     │            │
     │            ▼
     │   6. Rising PROFIT PER REGION
     │            │
     └────────────┘  (fund infill stores in the same region first)
```

**Current push (next 1–2 turns):**
1. Measure profit per region honestly, including allocated advertising.
2. Redirect the next store openings into infill in the strongest region instead of a new-market blitz.

**Doom-loop check:** warning signs are present.
- *Big programs to jump-start momentum:* the multi-store advertising blitzes are exactly this.
- *A new direction with each new leader:* the founder grew by acquisition, the successor switched to organic blitz entry, and the owner changed two years ago.
- *Momentum bought with outside capital:* the parent's money substitutes for proven unit economics.
- *Earlier abrupt narrowing:* dropping small appliances may have been disciplined or reactive. The dossier doesn't say.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop multi-store advertising-blitz entries into regions where Marlow has no presence.
2. Stop opening stores where three-year density can't be reached.
3. Stop reporting growth as revenue and store count. Report profit per region.
4. Stop matching rivals' prices in markets where Marlow has no cost advantage. That just burns margin.
5. Stop treating parent capital as free. Charge every new store a hurdle rate for the capital it uses.
6. Stop adding categories or formats, including copying Rival T's superstores, until home-region economics are proven.
7. Stop keeping isolated outpost stores out of sentiment or sunk cost.
