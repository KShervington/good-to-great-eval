## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In its great version, Marlow is no longer a scattered chain that buys attention market by market. It is the dominant value retailer of TVs, audio and major appliances in a deliberately small set of metros, starting with its three-state home region, where it has 30 years of name and presence. Every new store fills in an existing cluster. That makes each advertising dollar work harder, lifts volume per store and earns buying leverage, so low prices are sustained by cost rather than subsidized by the parent. It knows its profit per metro to the dollar. It exits markets where it cannot be #1 or #2. It treats Rival T's superstore format as a fact to answer inside its own markets, not as a race to run nationally. Its results justify the parent's capital on retail terms. If the parent ever leaves, a business like that is worth more as a stand-alone or to a buyer, rather than being a cash drain.

**Why it is uncomfortable to commit to:** it rules out most of the current strategy. That means no simultaneous new-market entries, no growth judged by store count, and a likely exit from isolated Western and Southwestern outposts.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. Markets where Marlow has the most stores per metro earn more profit per store and per ad dollar than thin markets.
2. The home region out-earns the acquired and organically entered distant markets.
3. Fill-in stores in a core metro reach breakeven faster than new-metro stores.

If these tests come out the other way, the denominator is wrong and Gate 5 must be redone.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌──► 1. Add stores INSIDE a core metro (fill-in, not new metros)
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    2. Ad + distribution cost spread over more stores; local name strengthens
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    3. More traffic and volume per store
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    4. Buying leverage + faster inventory turns
   │            │
   │            ▼
   │    5. Sustainably low prices / better selection without margin collapse
   │            │
   │            ▼
   └─── 6. Higher PROFIT PER METRO → funds next fill-in store
```

**Loop closes:** yes. Each step causes the next, and step 6 funds step 1.

**Weakest links:**
- **1 → 2:** the current footprint is scattered, so the density effect is weak or missing.
- **4:** buying leverage at roughly 40+ stores against a growing rival is unproven `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Current push:**
- (a) Build profit-per-metro reporting so the loop can be measured.
- (b) Put all new-store capital into fill-in stores in the two or three best metros.

**Doom-loop check: several warning signs are present.**
- **Big programs to jump-start momentum:** advertising blitzes and several stores opened at once in each new market.
- **New direction after new direction:** the shift from the founder's buy-a-chain entry to organic entry, plus an ownership change, all within six years.
- **Reaction without understanding:** expansion continues while unit economics are not visible.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop entering new metros** until every existing metro reports profit per metro and the core metros clear the agreed hurdle.
2. **Stop measuring success by store count and total revenue.** Measure profit per metro and same-store sales instead.
3. **Stop using ad blitzes as the market-entry engine.** The president's premise of entering "without paying for local name recognition" doesn't hold up. The blitz is that payment, just recorded as an expense.
4. **Stop competing on price where there is no cost advantage behind it.** Set margin floors per category.
5. **Stop holding isolated stores in markets where #1 or #2 is out of reach.** Consolidate or exit after review.
6. **Stop accepting parent capital without a retail-specific return hurdle and a written horizon.**
7. **Stop treating "acquire vs. build" as a belief.** Decide each market by profit-per-metro math.
8. **Stay out of small appliances.** That exit was right, and the rule should carry over to any new category outside TV, audio and major appliances unless it passes the per-metro test.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A weekly store-level P&L.
- A monthly per-metro review.
- Every new store must join an existing cluster.
- A quarterly report to the parent built on the scorecard below.

**Freedom within the framework:** store managers own local merchandising, staffing and promotions within category margin floors and the metro ad budget.

**Bureaucracy check:** central approvals that exist only because store-level data is missing should go once the data exists.
