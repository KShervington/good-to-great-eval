# Stanwick Mailing Systems: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> *Stanwick makes business mail effortless better than anyone in the world, and it grows by earning more profit from each business customer its field force already serves.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** turning the field force's recurring visits into sales of adjacent mail equipment and leases. Every other link has evidence behind it. This one has none in the dossier.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop all retail point-of-sale and checkout electronics work. Finish the exit and start nothing like it.
2. Stop fighting the copier giant head-on for general market share.
3. Stop entering markets led by larger, better-funded firms in the name of becoming "broad office equipment."

**Single most urgent brutal fact:** the core meter business is a mature, regulated, decree-constrained near-monopoly, so growth cannot come from gaining share. The first big attempt to grow outside it just cost $42M, about 11% of this year's revenue.

- Profitability of the price-marking business.
- The new CEO's background.
- How the new U.S. Postal Service will price metered mail.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick is not an office equipment conglomerate. It is the indispensable mail partner to American business. A postage meter is the anchor. Around it sit the scales, folders, inserters, sorters, financing and service that a business's mailroom runs on, all installed, maintained and upgraded by a field force that already visits hundreds of thousands of offices. It grows by deepening each relationship, measured as profit per customer. It does not grow by chasing the copier leader or retail electronics. It treats the Postal Service as its most important partner and shapes how metered mail is priced and handled. It turns every postal rate change or regulatory shift into an upgrade cycle, not a threat. It does this with leadership and mechanisms that no longer depend on one 54-year chairman.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all retail point-of-sale and checkout electronics work.** Complete the exit and start no successor venture.
2. **Stop chasing general copier share against the market leader.** That means no price war for large accounts. Copiers stay only if they raise profit per meter customer.
3. **Stop entering markets led by larger, better-funded firms** under the "broad office equipment" thesis.
4. **Stop approving projects or acquisitions without return on capital by line.** Stop presenting total revenue growth as success.
5. **Stop extending price-marking into adjacent retail systems.** Keep it fenced until the keep-or-divest decision.
6. **Stop measuring the field force only on cost per inspection or visits per day.** Measure profit per account served.
7. **Stop letting the credit subsidiary finance equipment outside the Hedgehog.**
