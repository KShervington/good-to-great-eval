## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick does not sell office equipment. It is the system every U.S. business uses to get its mail out. Its field force is the asset no competitor can copy, even with free patent licenses. That force is in tens of thousands of offices on a regular schedule `[ASSUMPTION: scale inferred from 281,000 meters in service 13 years ago]`, and it knows each customer's mail flow better than the customer does. Each visit deepens the relationship: a better meter, a scale, a folder or inserter, a lease from the credit arm. Profit per customer climbs year after year. Stanwick reinvests in electronic metering and in being the Postal Service's most trusted partner, so that metered mail stays the cheapest and easiest way for a business to mail. It has stopped trying to beat giants in copiers and retail systems. It grows deeper rather than wider, and the next CEO inherits a system rather than a personality.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **No new markets where a larger, better-funded firm leads.** Point-of-sale stays closed, and there is no re-entry into retail electronics.
2. **No acquisitions or joint ventures as growth shortcuts** while the Hedgehog Concept is unconfirmed.
3. **Freeze copier expansion:** no new models, no price war and no added sales capacity. Copiers keep their current base only if a 120-day review shows they raise profit per mailing customer. Otherwise harvest or exit.
4. **Stop investing in price-marking beyond maintenance**, and do not extend it into retail systems. Decide its ownership once return-on-capital data exists.
5. **Stop calling Stanwick an "office equipment company"** in strategy, investor messaging and hiring.
6. **Stop using the credit subsidiary to finance anything other than Stanwick mailing systems.**
7. **Stop measuring management by total revenue growth.** Measure profit per customer and return on capital by line.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make sending mail effortless, accurate and economical for every business.

**Core values `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from history]`:**
1. Service in the customer's office, done right every time.
2. Integrity with the Postal Service. Stanwick handles public money in the form of postage.
3. Engineering leadership in the mailing system.
4. Long-term customer relationships over one-time sales.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** within 20 years, mailing systems beyond the meter earn more profit than the meter itself, and the Postal Service treats Stanwick as the standard for how businesses mail.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Profit-per-customer reporting.
- A capital-allocation rule: every investment must state which flywheel link it pushes.
- A formal Postal Service partnership office.
- A board-owned succession bench, so the next CEO transition is not a 54-year cliff.
