# Tillery Products Company: the great version

## One-screen verdict

**Weakest flywheel link:** whether the brand still commands a real price premium over resin cost. Twice already, resin swings and price controls have pushed earnings down. ## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tillery is a narrower company than today's. It is the household and food-service plastics leader whose name shoppers trust and retailers want on their shelves. It earns more profit per pound of resin than any molder of comparable products, because it designs better, launches steadily, and passes commodity costs through promptly instead of absorbing them. Party plan has been sold or wound down. Automotive and the German subsidiary each kept their place only if the numbers proved they could be best in their niche; otherwise they are gone. Growth comes from a steady cadence of new items in core categories, sold through the direct retail distribution the company already owns, not from a promised multiple of sales. It has a deep internal bench, a labor relationship that no longer produces 1,100-person strikes, and a measurement system that would sound the alarm on a bad year before the chief executive does. Its greatness does not depend on the person who started the turn.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop running party-plan direct sales.** Sell it or wind it down.
2. **Stop announcing sales targets.** Retire "quadruple sales" and set earnings-growth and return targets instead.
3. **Stop entering categories without owned distribution.** The recreational-goods lesson becomes a written rule.
4. **Stop acquiring outside the hedgehog.** Acquisitions are allowed only in household or food-service products, where the brand and channel transfer. This keeps the prior management's sound restraint.
5. **Stop absorbing resin spikes silently.** Use a standing pass-through and purchasing policy with a set response time.
6. **Stop adding SKUs without a kill rule.** Every new item must clear a profit-per-pound hurdle, and weak items get retired. The cadence speeds up, but discipline comes with it.
7. **Stop tolerating any informal retail-price "understandings."** The cease-and-desist order makes this a legal discipline as well as a strategic one.
8. **Stop managing by consolidated earnings only.** Every division reports profit per pound and return on assets.
9. **Stop treating automotive and international as growth platforms** until the data earns them that role.

**Truth climate and charisma check:** this is the risk to watch. An outsider arrives with a public verdict ("stodgy, complacent") and a public number. Subordinates who disagree with either may stay quiet. Four red-flag mechanisms would counter this:
- monthly segment reviews where bad news is presented first;
- blameless post-mortems on every failed product;
- a board-level resin and pricing dashboard;
- the chief executive leading strategy sessions with questions, not answers.

**Faith statement:** *A 60-year-old brand already on national shelves, with its own path to the retailer and two decades of compounding, does not need reinventing. It needs narrowing, measuring and pushing harder in the same direction.*
