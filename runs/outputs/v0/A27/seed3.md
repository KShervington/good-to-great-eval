# Good to Great: Tillery Products Company

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Tillery makes the everyday rubber and plastic products that homes and commercial kitchens trust most, sold where buyers already shop, and earns more per pound of resin than anyone because brand and design set the price, not the material.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** the pricing leg. Tillery has not shown that it can hold profit per pound once the minimum retail-price agreements are gone (dropped Year −5) and resin prices swing.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop funding the party-plan business as a growth bet. Put it into a sell-or-wind-down review.
  2. Stop launching products into channels the company doesn't already command. The recreational line is the lesson.
  3. Stop treating "quadruple sales" as the target. A sales target pulls toward acquisitions. Make the target earnings and profit per pound.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** management cannot currently say which divisions make money. Segment profit isn't on the table, yet the chairman is about to decide which divisions to keep.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - profit by division, especially party plan, automotive and the German business;
  - profit per pound of resin by product line;
  - the return-on-equity trend;
  - share against named housewares competitors;
  - the depth of the management bench.

Greatness potential: 35/100

The core franchise is strong: a national brand, direct supermarket distribution and about 20 years of compounding. What drags the score down is the leadership pattern. A charismatic outsider has publicly committed to a big number, and there is no documented succession. That pattern is better at producing a great *decade* than lasting greatness.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Tillery is a narrower, sharper company. It is the dominant maker of durable, well-designed rubber and plastic products for the home kitchen, household storage and cleaning, plus the commercial kitchens of restaurants and hotels. It sells through the mass-retail and food-service channels it already commands, and it releases a steady, relentless stream of useful new items that retailers want on their shelves because they sell.

It manages one number above all: profit per pound of resin. The brand and design earn a premium over a commodity input. Disciplined resin buying and pass-through pricing protect that premium in bad years.

It does not chase direct selling, recreational products or acquisitions outside its channel. Its earnings keep compounding at or above the historic rate of about 12% a year. It does this through a management bench deep enough that the chairman's departure would be a non-event.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop growing the party plan.** No new capital goes in. Run a sell, license or wind-down review within six months. It competes head-on with an entrenched specialist and took about seven years to make a profit.
2. **Stop launching products into channels Tillery doesn't already serve.** No repeat of the recreational line.
3. **Stop treating sales volume as the headline goal.** Restate the ten-year ambition in earnings and profit per pound. Sales growth should be a result, not the aim.
4. **Stop acquisitions outside the core channel.** Any deal must pass this screen: same shelf or same food-service buyer, resin-based, and accretive to profit per pound.
5. **Stop new capital spending in automotive and international** until the segment review shows they earn their place.
6. **Stop carrying products that dilute profit per pound.** Run an annual pruning of the bottom tier of the roughly 240+ items by profit per pound.
7. **Stop relying on the retail price.** The minimum-price agreements are gone and the cease-and-desist order stands. Pricing power must come from brand and design, and anything resembling price control is off-limits.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A monthly profit-per-pound review by line.
- A written resin-buying policy.
- A fixed new-product cadence.
- The acquisition screen.

**Framework boundaries:** division heads have full autonomy over products, merchandising and operations, as long as they stay inside the core channels, inside the resin-based product family, and above the profit-per-pound floor.
