# Good to Great: Larkspur Financial

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Larkspur wins by being the trusted primary bank for American households and small businesses, measured by profit per actively used primary relationship, not by products sold.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** the step from a sale to a customer actually using what was sold. Today the company measures how many products a household *holds* and pays store staff partly on products *sold*. Nothing in the dossier shows those products are funded or used. Every other part of the flywheel depends on this link being real.
- **Top 3 stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop paying store team members on product counts, and stop setting daily product-sales goals.
  2. Stop presenting "eight products per household" to investors and staff as the headline target.
  3. Stop letting business lines police their own sales conduct. Risk management and HR need an independent reporting line.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Larkspur has already been fined because incentive pay and sales quotas drove misconduct. In July, the central bank's $85M action found that the consumer-finance subsidiary steered borrowers and falsified income, and it tied that conduct to incentive compensation and sales quotas. The same tools run the largest business today: daily goals and pay tied to products sold, across 264,000 people, with oversight sitting inside the business being overseen. Assume the risk is present until data shows otherwise.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - The share of cross-sold products that are funded or active.
  - Ethics-hotline reports, customer complaints and terminations related to sales integrity.
  - Branch team-member turnover.
  - How branch sales goals are set and escalated.

  If these come back clean, the verdict moves toward PASS and greatness potential rises. If they show gaming, cross-sell has to be rebuilt from scratch before anything else.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is still the bank that came through the crisis stronger than its rivals. It has a cheap, loyal deposit base and conservative credit. The difference is that every relationship it counts is a relationship the customer would recognize and confirm. Store team members are paid to solve customers' needs, and success shows up as households making Larkspur their *primary* bank: paychecks deposited, bills paid, savings held. A product count is not success. Risk management and HR report independently of the sales lines, with a direct line to the board. Bad news about sales practices reaches the top fast and is acted on. The company earns more per primary household than any large rival, through low rate cycles and high ones. It would stay that way under a different CEO, because the integrity is built into the mechanisms rather than resting on one leader's judgment.

**Current push (next 1–2 turns):**
1. Make link 2→3 real and measurable. Count only active or funded products, and verify that customers consented to every new account.
2. Make link 6 visible to staff by redirecting incentive pay from product counts to customer usage, retention and experience.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Present, latent:** a quota doom loop. Earnings pressure (integration savings ending, low rates) leads to higher sales goals. Higher goals lead to low-quality or unwanted sales. Those lead to complaints, regulatory action and reputational damage. That damage creates more earnings pressure, and the loop repeats. The subsidiary fine shows this loop has already run once inside the group.
- **Watch:** "reaction without understanding" if regulators force fixes that leadership treats as one-off compliance items rather than system design.
- **Watch:** using a large acquisition to restart growth now that the last integration is done.
- **Not present:** strategic whiplash. The strategy has been consistent, but the danger is that the *metric* it has been consistent about is the wrong one.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** paying store team members on the number of products sold.
2. **Stop** setting daily and monthly product-sales goals in stores.
3. **Stop** presenting "eight" and product-count cross-sell as the headline metric to investors and staff.
4. **Stop** counting unfunded or inactive accounts as relationships in any internal or external report.
5. **Stop** letting business lines run their own risk and HR oversight of sales conduct.
6. **Stop** chasing mortgage origination share ahead of servicing capacity. The consent orders show the gap.
7. **Stop** offering any product or channel whose economics depend on steering customers to a worse option than they qualify for.
8. **Stop** treating fines and lawsuits as isolated legacy events, such as describing the subsidiary as "former". Treat them as signals about the system.
9. **Stop** considering large acquisitions for at least three years `[ASSUMPTION: no acquisition is planned]`. Push the flywheel instead.
10. **Stop** pursuing capital-markets growth for prestige when it isn't attached to existing commercial relationships.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** Help households and small businesses succeed financially.

**Core values:**
1. The customer's interest comes first, in every conversation and every product.
2. We sell only what the customer needs and will use.
3. Team members act as owners of outcomes, not quotas.
4. Risk discipline through every cycle.
5. Bad news travels up fast, and the people who carry it are protected.

**BHAG (about 15 years):** By Year 15, half of U.S. households name Larkspur as their primary bank, and it holds the highest customer-trust rating of any large U.S. bank.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent CRO and conduct function with board access.
- Incentive plans reviewed annually by central HR and the board compensation committee, with clawbacks for sales-integrity breaches.
- Customer confirmation (email or SMS) for every new account.
- Automated anomaly detection: accounts never funded, accounts opened and closed quickly, clusters by branch or team member.
- An ethics hotline with anti-retaliation tracking reported to the board.
- Split chair or independent lead director.
- A CEO succession plan that includes candidates who don't own the current metric.
