# Larkspur Financial: Good to Great

## Verdict (one screen)

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Larkspur wins by being the bank that American households and small businesses actually choose as their main financial home, and it measures success by profit per primary relationship, never by products sold.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** the first step, *right product for a real need*. Today branch pay and daily quotas reward the **number of products opened**, not whether the customer needed or uses them. Every later link in the loop depends on that step being honest.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop paying team members on product count or cross-sell, and end daily product-sales quotas in the stores.
2. Stop using "eight products per household" as a target and stop making cross-sell the headline metric.
3. Stop letting risk, compliance and HR report only into the business lines they oversee.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** In July of Year −1 the central bank said that misconduct in Larkspur's own former consumer-finance subsidiary (steering borrowers into costlier loans, falsified income) was allegedly fostered by **incentive pay and sales quotas**. The core retail bank runs on the same kind of mechanism: daily product goals plus pay tied to products and cross-sell. Its controls are decentralized, and no one has published evidence on whether it is producing the same behavior.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- The share of cross-sold accounts that are funded or active.
- Ethics-hotline reports, complaints and terminations related to sales practices.
- Branch employee turnover.
- How branch sales goals are set, and whether they rise every year.

If those numbers are clean, Larkspur is close to great and mostly needs to harden its controls. If they are bad, the cross-sell record is inflated and the company's reputation is at serious risk.

`Greatness potential: 42/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larkspur is the default primary bank for a third of American households. Customers keep their paycheck, payments, savings and borrowing there because the advice in the store and in the app is trustworthy. The company is still the best-capitalized and most crisis-resilient large U.S. bank. It earns peer-leading returns from cheap, sticky deposits and low credit losses rather than from product volume. Team members are paid for customer outcomes and long tenure, not for daily sales counts. Decentralization still gives local leaders freedom to serve customers. It no longer gives them freedom to grade their own risk. Larkspur reports active primary relationships to investors instead of products per household, and it keeps outperforming through rate cycles and leadership changes.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
- **Turn 1:** rebuild link [1]. Change the incentive design and measure active use, so that "a product sold" means "a need met".
- **Turn 2:** instrument link [3]. Define primary household, set its baseline, and report it internally next to cross-sell before replacing cross-sell externally.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **The current operating loop looks like this:** quotas → products opened → cross-sell rises → record EPS and investor praise → higher goals → more quota pressure.
  - It reinforces itself and contains **no customer-value check**.
  - That is the shape of a doom loop disguised as a flywheel.
  - Cross-sell rose **every quarter** of Year −1 in a weak economy. That steadiness deserves scrutiny, not celebration, until activity data confirms it.
- **Other warning signs:** integration savings are ending, rates are low, and the market expects growth like last year's 28% EPS gain. That combination creates pressure to "find" growth through either harder selling or a big acquisition.
  - No grasping is visible yet.
  - The conditions for it are.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop paying incentive compensation on products sold or on cross-sell ratios** for store team members and their managers.
2. **Stop daily and monthly product-sales quotas** in the stores. Replace them with service-quality and active-use measures.
3. **Stop the "eight products" goal** as a target and **stop leading investor communications with cross-sell**.
4. **Stop counting unfunded or inactive accounts as products** in any internal or external metric.
5. **Stop running risk, compliance and HR solely inside the business lines.**
6. **Stop chasing mortgage origination market share** for its own sake while servicing and fair-lending problems remain open.
7. **Stop describing regulatory findings as "industry-wide"** or "legacy subsidiary" issues as a reason not to look inward.
8. **Do not pursue a large acquisition** to replace fading integration savings.
9. **Do not expand capital markets** to compete with global investment banks `[ASSUMPTION: that this temptation exists]`.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every product opened must have customer consent and a documented need.
- Capital stays at or above current strength (Tier 1 common at least 9.46%, or regulatory requirement plus a buffer).
- Credit standards hold through the cycle.

**Framework boundaries:**
- *Freedom:* business units own their customer approach, local pricing within guardrails, and service design.
- *Not free:* units cannot set their own risk appetite, incentive structure or conduct standards. Those are central.

**Bureaucracy check:** if conduct depends on ever-tighter sales tracking and daily reporting, that is bureaucracy compensating for a bad incentive design. Fix the incentive and remove the tracking.
