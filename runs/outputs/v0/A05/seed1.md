## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Tolliver is a single-format company. It runs consumer-electronics superstores, rolled out one region at a time. Each new region gets a cluster of stores opened together, supported by one advertising budget and one distribution point. The company does not enter a region it cannot dominate. Its edge is three things at once: superstore selection, sharp prices from regional buying volume, and salespeople who know the products. The rest of its history gets sold, converted or closed: leased departments, the tax-credit acquisition, and eventually the mid-sized format. It manages to one number, profit per region, and it funds each new cluster from the profits of the last one. Its leader asks hard questions, and its systems make the answers impossible to ignore. Because of that, the company keeps working when the founder and his son are gone.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Buying chains for tax credits, store count or cheapness.
2. Entering a market with one or two stores. Enter only with a cluster.
3. Running leased departments in someone else's discount chain.
4. Treating the big-city chain as an expansion beachhead. Give it a fix-or-exit deadline and do not reinvest beyond that test.
5. Building any new mid-sized stores. Existing ones stay until per-format data says otherwise.
6. Expanding appliances, hardware or other non-electronics lines beyond what customers expect from a superstore.
7. Answering Rival S market by market with price wars in regions where Tolliver lacks density.
8. Announcing growth without stating route, pace and region.
