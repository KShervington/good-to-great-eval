## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> *Be the lowest-cost, lowest-loss adjustable-rate home lender in the West, funded by local household savings, and judge every move by profit per dollar of assets rather than by size.*

**Recommended direction:** take **Option one**, the focused path. Diversification (Option two) falls outside the Hedgehog Concept. Deregulation is an invitation to stop being what you are best at. Decline it.

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** low cost. The plan depends on the company being cheaper to run per dollar of assets than its peers, and nothing in the dossier proves that. The second weak link is deposit cost: reckless deregulated rivals will bid up savings rates.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop diversifying. Freeze the brokerage and real-estate services business pending a keep-or-exit test, and make no new non-lending purchases.
2. Stop buying thrifts for size. "Second-largest in the nation" is not a goal.
3. Stop carrying the legacy fixed-rate book passively. Shrink the rate gap, starting with an evaluation of Rival K's loan-for-security swap.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the company still funds part of its book with long fixed-rate loans on short deposits, and its current net worth is unknown. In this rate environment, that alone could decide whether it survives.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- **Net worth and tangible capital.** If either is near zero or negative, Gate 1 fails and this becomes a survival plan.
- **Size and average yield of the fixed-rate book.** This sets how urgent and how large the swap or sale needs to be.
- **Operating expense per dollar of assets versus peers.** If the company is not low-cost, the Hedgehog Concept shifts.
- **Brokerage and real-estate services P&L.**
- **Home-state deposit and mortgage share.**

`Greatness potential: 28/100`

*Why only 28 even with the plan:* the whole thrift model is under structural stress, the business is concentrated in one state, and the company is already drifting toward size and diversification. Great here means sustained outperformance of the market, and few institutions in this industry and era will achieve it. The upside is real, because conservatism is exactly what this period will reward. Reaching it, though, requires a cultural reversal that has not started.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is the thrift still standing, and still earning, after its industry's reckless era ends. It does one thing: it makes small, conservatively underwritten, adjustable-rate home loans to families in its home state, funded by those families' savings. It runs that business at lower cost and with lower credit losses than anyone else. Its interest-rate exposure is matched by policy, not by luck. It does not sell stocks, develop real estate or chase commercial loans. When competitors who borrowed their growth fail, it buys their deposits and branches cheaply, and only on its own terms. Its executives are paid on profit per dollar of assets and on credit quality, never on asset size. Its brand rests on being the safest place in the West to keep savings and the most dependable place to get a home loan, not on a celebrity.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **No new non-lending businesses.** Freeze the brokerage and real-estate services firm. Exit it unless it passes the keep test.
2. **No acquisitions for size.** Any thrift purchase must, at fair market value of its fixed-rate book, raise profit per dollar of assets within 24 months and adopt Pacific Crest's credit standards.
3. **No risky deregulated assets.** That means no commercial real-estate development, high-yield securities, direct equity investments or speculative construction lending, even where the law now allows them.
4. **No fixed-rate loans held in portfolio without matched funding.** Also stop treating the legacy fixed book as something to wait out.
5. **No matching reckless competitors' deposit rates** to defend market share. Lose rate-sensitive money instead of buying it at a loss. Also no brokered "hot money" deposits `[ASSUMPTION: available to thrifts post-deregulation]`.
6. **Stop using size rankings** ("second-largest in the nation") in goals, compensation or communications.
7. **Pause out-of-state expansion** until home-state profit per dollar of assets is best in class. Revisit it later only as a way to diversify geographic credit risk, never for growth's sake.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):** the same credit standards in every cycle; an interest-rate gap limit set and enforced by the board; monthly reporting on profit per dollar of assets for every office.

**Framework boundaries (freedom within a framework):** branch managers have freedom over service, staffing and local deposit gathering, inside fixed credit, pricing-floor and cost-per-asset limits.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rule-layering problem is visible in the dossier. Watch for approval processes added only to supervise acquired-thrift staff who don't share the credit culture.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG
