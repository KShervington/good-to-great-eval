## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is the lender still standing, and still beating the market, after the deregulation boom ends. It is deliberately *not* a financial supermarket. It gathers household savings through a dense in-state branch network and lends them back as small, well-underwritten adjustable-rate home loans. It holds only the risk it can price. Fixed-rate loans that customers want are originated and then sold or swapped into securities, not kept. Its edge is unglamorous: lower credit losses, a tightly matched rate book, and steady spread per loan dollar, year after year. While competitors grow fast into riskier assets, Pacific Crest gives up ranking on size in exchange for staying power. When those competitors fail, it buys their deposits and branches cheaply and only on its own terms.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop expanding brokerage and real-estate services.** Freeze new investment. Set a 12-month profit-and-synergy test, and divest if it fails.
2. **Stop chasing deregulated high-yield assets:** commercial real-estate development, land loans, equity stakes, speculative securities.
3. **Stop acquiring thrifts for size.** Each deal must pass a filter: in-state only, assets marked to market, deposits cheaper than Pacific Crest's own, and accretive on the denominator.
4. **Stop expanding out of state** until in-state returns are proven and the filter is in place.
5. **Stop holding new fixed-rate loans in portfolio.** Originate them only for sale or swap.
6. **Stop benchmarking on size** ("second-largest in the nation"). Report the denominator first.
7. **Stop treating the celebrity spokesman as the differentiator.** Review the spend against measured deposit and loan acquisition `[ASSUMPTION: no effectiveness data in the dossier]`.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every new loan has a known rate-reset path.
- Credit committee approval cannot be overridden for growth reasons.
- A monthly report shows the market-value rate gap.

**Freedom within the framework:**
- Branches and regions have autonomy on service, deposit products and local marketing.
- Credit standards and rate-risk limits are set centrally and fixed.
