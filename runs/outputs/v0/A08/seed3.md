# Pacific Crest Financial Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the lowest-loss, rate-matched home lender and household savings bank in our home state, and measure ourselves on risk-adjusted profit per mortgage dollar.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from stable spread to capital to competitive pricing. Its health depends on the size of the leftover fixed-rate book and on deposit costs, and neither number is known. If the old book is large and unhedged, the flywheel can't turn no matter how good the underwriting is.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop expanding brokerage and real-estate services. Freeze last year's purchase and put it on a prove-it-or-divest clock.
  2. Don't use any deregulation-era permission to buy high-risk assets, including commercial development, speculative land and high-yield securities.
  3. Don't expand out of state or buy thrifts to manufacture growth until the core earns above peers.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** management is choosing between diversification and focus without a disclosed number for net worth, return on equity or the fixed-rate exposure. Diversifying is a bet made with capital the company may not have to spare.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  1. Net worth and return on equity for the last three years.
  2. The size and average yield of the fixed-rate book.
  3. Cost of funds compared with peers.
  4. Home-state share of deposits and originations.
  5. Profit or loss of the brokerage and real-estate unit.

  If capital is thin, the plan shifts from "focus" to "focus plus urgent de-risking," such as swapping fixed-rate loans into Rival K's securities. If the new unit is clearly profitable and fits the deposit relationship, the stop-doing list loosens.

Greatness potential: 25/100

*Why 25:* the franchise is real. It has more than 60 years of history, is the second-largest thrift in the nation and pioneered adjustable-rate mortgages. The plan points it in the right direction. But the whole thrift model is under structural pressure from rate squeeze, deposit deregulation and securitization by Rival K. Its credit risk is concentrated in one state's real estate, and the most important numbers are unknown. "Great" here means beating the market for 15 years or more, which is a high bar for a single-state spread lender.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Pacific Crest is a boring bank on purpose. It is the most efficient and lowest-loss home lender in its state. Nearly all of its book reprices with rates or is laid off to the capital markets, so the interest-rate cycle no longer decides its fate. Its 183-office deposit network is the trusted savings home for local households, and that funding is cheaper and steadier than what competitors buy with high rates. It earns a modest but reliable spread on every mortgage dollar, keeps credit losses well below peers through the next real-estate downturn, and builds capital while rivals spend theirs on deregulation-era adventures. When those rivals stumble, it buys their good branches cheaply. It doesn't diversify into businesses it can't be best at. Its edge is a culture of disciplined underwriting and rate-matching that outlasts any single CEO or celebrity spokesman.

**Current push (the next two turns):**

**Doom-loop check:** there are three warning signs. A large merger in Year −2, a move into a new line of business last year, and two open debates about more acquisitions and out-of-state expansion together make three potential new directions in about three years. None of these is fatal yet, but this is the pattern Collins describes: big moves meant to jump-start momentum instead of steady pushes on one loop. **Verdict: the loop closes. The risk is distraction, not design.**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop expanding brokerage and real-estate services.** Freeze capital and headcount there, and decide in 12 months to prove fit or divest.
2. **Stop considering high-risk assets** that deregulation now allows, such as commercial and land development, speculative construction, high-yield bonds and equity stakes in developments.
3. **Stop originating fixed-rate loans for the portfolio** unless they are pre-sold or swapped into securities.
4. **Stop growing assets faster than capital.** Any growth target must have a capital-ratio floor attached.
5. **Stop pursuing out-of-state expansion** until return on equity beats peers for four consecutive quarters.
6. **Don't use thrift acquisitions to fix earnings.** Acquire only branches or deposits in the home state, at distressed prices, after integration of the Year −2 merger is complete.
7. **Stop paying top-of-market deposit rates to win volume** against aggressive competitors.
8. **Stop measuring success by "second-largest in the nation."** Size is not the denominator.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** the same underwriting standards every time; a measured rate-gap limit; a quarterly blameless review of every credit loss; a capital-ratio floor.

**Framework boundaries:** branch and lending managers have autonomy on service, local pricing within bands, and community presence. They have none on credit standards, rate exposure or new product lines.

**Bureaucracy check:** if the merger left layers of approval designed to police inherited teams, replace the layers with the right people `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

- **Core values:** prudence with other people's money; lend only what we would want repaid to ourselves; integrity with savers; long-term thinking over quarterly size.
- **Core purpose:** help the families of our state save safely and own their homes.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Be the safest and most profitable home lender in the West, and come through every rate cycle and real-estate cycle with our dividend intact.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:** a written underwriting code; a board-level rate-gap limit; a capital floor; succession depth in credit and ALM leadership; a stop-doing list approved by the board as policy.
