# Good to Great: Marlowe Drug Co.

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Marlowe will be the best, most convenient drugstore in every market where it can build density, and it will judge itself by one number: profit per customer visit.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** turning visits into profit. Net margin is about 1.5% on a comparable basis and 0.76% as reported. Shrinkage is rising. The cash the business does produce goes to restaurants, discount stores and side businesses instead of back into drugstore density.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop opening restaurants. Freeze the 22 units planned for this year.
2. Stop putting capital into the 25 discount department stores, and prepare an exit.
3. Stop treating "growth in all divisions" and "passed $1 billion" as the goal. Divest the side businesses: travel agency, coffee roasting, ice cream, and the container plant.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Sales doubled in nine years and per-share earnings did not follow. EPS was $1.95 seven years ago and $2.11 two years ago, about 1.6% a year in nominal terms. The switch to LIFO implies rising prices, so that is probably a decline in real terms. Marlowe is getting bigger without getting better.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by division.
- Profit per store, per square foot and per customer visit.
- Market share by region against Rival E.
- Pharmacy's share of drugstore sales and visits.

If restaurants or discount stores turn out to earn well above the drugstores on capital, the stop-doing list needs to be re-argued. The Hedgehog Concept would probably still hold.

**Gate tally:** 7 PASS, 4 CONDITIONAL, 0 FAIL. Most of the CONDITIONALs come from one shared root problem: the dossier shows no unit economics.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Marlowe is a drugstore company, and only that. It owns dense clusters of convenient, well-run drugstores in the markets where it chooses to compete. The pharmacist and the health-and-beauty aisle bring customers back often. Every visit earns more than it does at any rival, because house brands sit at the counter, shrinkage is tightly controlled, and store costs are held down. Each year's cash goes into the next convenient site and into pruning weak ones. Restaurants, department stores, travel and coffee are gone, sold to owners who can be great at them. Earnings per share grow faster than sales and faster than inflation. It keeps the founder's pharmacist identity, and it no longer runs a holding company whose businesses share a parking lot.

**Sentence:** *Marlowe will be the best, most convenient drugstore in every market where it can build density, and it will judge itself by one number: profit per customer visit.*

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Partly. The chain's scale came from drugstores and the self-service format. EPS hit a record two years ago after a period in which 51 marginal stores were closed, which fits a model where pruning helps.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: restaurants, discount stores, travel, coffee, ice cream, containers, and scattered openings.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It contradicts the stated strategy of growth in every division, and management's "positive" view of restaurants.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. Compute profit per visit for every store. Check whether the leaders are convenient sites in dense clusters.
2. Compare how much store-level profit variation is explained by per-visit profit, per-store profit and per-square-foot profit. Switch denominators if another one explains more.
3. Measure in-store lunch counters by per-visit profit with and without food. Freestanding restaurants fail the test by construction.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Make link 3 visible.** Build store-level profit-per-visit reporting and attack shrinkage store by store.

**Doom-loop check:** several warning signs are present.
- The stated aim is growth in every division.

One counter-sign helps: the disciplined pruning of 51 marginal stores. The flywheel closes logically. Whether the company will stop feeding the other loops is still open, so this gate is `CONDITIONAL`.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop opening restaurants.** Freeze the 22 planned units. Exit the 27 freestanding family restaurants. Keep in-store counters only where they raise drugstore profit per visit.
2. **Stop investing in discount department stores.** Run the 25 stores for cash, then sell them.
3. **Divest the travel agency.** There is no link to the Hedgehog Concept.
4. **Divest or close the coffee-roasting and ice cream plants**, unless they serve drugstores at better cost than buying from outside. The ice cream case falls with the restaurants.
5. **Question the plastic-container plant and the second laboratory.** Keep manufacturing only where house brands raise per-visit profit. Do not pursue outside sales.
6. **Plan an exit from the Mexico minority stake.** It is non-controlling, off-hedgehog, and takes management attention.
7. **Stop measuring success by total sales** ("passed $1 billion") and by store count.
8. **Stop opening or acquiring stores that do not build density.** No more scattered single-store markets.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every capital request states its effect on drugstore profit per visit.
- Every store is reviewed yearly, and the bottom tail is pruned. This continues the existing practice.
- Shrinkage is reported weekly, by store.

**Framework boundaries:** headquarters sets the format, house-brand assortment, pricing bands and site rules. Store managers and pharmacists own service, staffing and local merchandising inside those rules.

**Bureaucracy check:** heavy centralized controls on store managers should be reviewed once the right managers are in place `[ASSUMPTION: current control structure unknown]`.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values (proposed `[ASSUMPTION]`, to be confirmed with leadership):**
1. The pharmacist's trust comes first.
2. Convenience and fair value for the everyday customer.
3. Thrift: every dollar and every square foot must earn its place.
4. Candor about the numbers.

**Core purpose:** to keep communities healthy through trustworthy, convenient pharmacy and health-and-beauty service.

**BHAG (20–25 year horizon):** *a Marlowe drugstore within a short, easy trip of most households in every market we choose to serve, each one earning the industry's highest profit per visit.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Profit-per-visit reporting by store.
- An annual store-pruning review.
- A capital-allocation rule tied to the denominator.
- Outside directors who receive the full division economics.
- A succession bench developed from store and pharmacy operations, not from family alone.
