## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> *Dunmore-Kessler turns communications technology proven on the hardest government missions into the transmission and data-communications equipment that commercial networks cannot afford to have fail, and it judges every business by profit per engineer.*

Greatness potential: 30/100

**Weakest flywheel link:** the step from government technology to commercial leadership. The company is rewarded for *creating* commercial products from defense work, not for *winning profitably* with them. Products get made, but markets aren't won.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop acquiring. Make no new acquisitions until the last six are integrated and each earns its capital.
2. Stop investing in hot-metal typesetting and in purely mechanical printing equipment.
3. Stop promoting managers for "a commercial product from defense tech." Promote them for profitable share in a chosen niche instead.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** sales roughly doubled while earnings stalled for about five years, and last year the company spent another $25.5M in cash on acquisitions. **It is buying revenue, not profit.** Nobody in the dossier can show which segments earn their cost of capital.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Segment profit and return on capital. If printing turns out to be the high-return business, the Hedgehog Concept may flip.
- Hot-metal typesetting's share of printing sales, and its trend.
- Government backlog, and the government share of electronics sales.
- The margin on commercial products that came from technology transfer.
- Returns on each acquisition since the aerospace merger.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Dunmore-Kessler is a focused communications-electronics company. It is not a printing-press maker with an electronics hobby, and it is not a holding company of loosely related acquisitions. Government programs pay for and sharpen its engineering on the hardest problems: tracking, telemetry, satellite links and secure data. A disciplined, *selective* transfer process then turns a few of those capabilities into commercial products where reliability and performance command a premium: broadcast transmitters, microwave and radio links, satellite ground equipment and data-communications systems. In each niche it chooses, it is first or second. Every unit is measured on profit per engineer. Printing has been settled one way or the other: it has either been rebuilt as an electronic information-handling business or separated cleanly, so it no longer splits capital and attention. Earnings growth, not sales growth, is the proof.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. Segment data shows the communications and data electronics units earn higher returns on capital than printing, or can within two years. If printing earns more, revisit the concept.
2. At least two commercial niches reach #1 or #2 share in their segment.
3. Profit per engineer rises while headcount in non-hedgehog units falls.
4. The concept explains the past: the aerospace business grew fast because it worked on hard, well-funded problems, and earnings stalled when the company spread itself across too many fronts. *This is consistent with the dossier but unproven.*

**Discomfort test: passed.** The concept implies the 80-year printing heritage may not be the future. That is the hard part.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Acquisitions:** none for 24 months, and after that only bolt-ons that strengthen a chosen niche.
2. **Hot-metal typesetting:** no new development. Run it for service and cash.
3. **Purely mechanical press development:** no major new platforms until the printing decision (keep, transform or separate) is made.
4. **Head-on fights with conglomerates in broad studio equipment** (for example, full camera lines) where the company can't reach #1–#2 `[ASSUMPTION: share unknown]`. Keep transmitters and antennas if the data supports them.
5. **Technology transfer as a goal in itself:** stop starting commercial products without a named market, a target share and a margin hurdle.
6. **Government bids with no strategic value:** stop chasing programs that neither build core communications and data skills nor carry acceptable margins.
7. **Reporting sales growth as the headline number:** lead with earnings, return on capital and profit per engineer.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION; to be tested with the people]`:
1. Engineering rigor: it has to work when failure isn't an option.
2. Earn the capital you use.
3. Tell the truth early.
4. Customers' mission comes first.

**Core purpose:** *to make the world's critical communications work.*

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Become the independent company that the world's broadcasters, carriers and satellite networks trust first for the equipment that moves their signals and data.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Segment-return and profit-per-engineer reviews built into the annual plan.
- A niche entry and exit charter: #1–#2 within a set time, or exit.
- A gated technology-transfer process with a commercial market owner on every project.
- A formal succession bench for the CEO and the EVP Electronics.
