## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Dunmore-Kessler is a focused communications-engineering company, not a printing-and-electronics conglomerate. It takes the hardest signal and data problems in space and defense and turns that capability into a small number of commercial niches where reliability wins the sale: broadcast transmission, microwave and point-to-point radio links, and data-communications subsystems. In those niches it does not fight the big electrical conglomerates on price or breadth. It measures itself by profit per engineer and reports return on capital by unit. It grows by turning the flywheel and integrating patiently, not by buying the next company. The printing business has been given one honest test of whether electronics makes it distinctive. If it passes, it is modernized inside the framework. If not, it is separated from the company and set up to thrive on its own. Leadership can survive the current chairman, because the operating disciplines are written down and the bench is developed.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring** for at least 12 months. No further deals until integrations are complete and unit return on capital is published internally.
2. **Stop paying stock premiums for "talent."** Grow talent inside the flywheel instead.
3. **Stop putting growth capital into purely mechanical presses, hot-metal typesetting and corrugated machinery.** Run them for cash pending the segment data.
4. **Stop competing head-on with conglomerates in commodity-like lines**, such as general-purpose computer terminals. Keep only the data-communications pieces that serve the communications Hedgehog.
5. **Stop promoting managers for the count of defense-to-commercial launches.** Promote on commercial profit and return on capital.
6. **Stop presenting printing plus electronics as synergy without proof.** The "printing needs electronics" thesis gets one funded, measured product test, not corporate identity status.
7. **Stop treating headquarters location as settled.** HQ sits several states away from where the Hedgehog lives. Make the location question an explicit decision.
