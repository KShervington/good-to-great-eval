**Top three stop-doing items:**

**Most urgent brutal fact:** revenue grew roughly fifteen-fold through mergers, but earnings have stalled for five years. The growth was bought, and nobody has shown that the bought pieces fit together.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Dunmore-Kessler is no longer a printing-press company that owns some electronics firms. It is a single communications and information-handling company with one engine. Government programs fund and prove hard technology in telemetry, data links, transmission and terminals. The company then sells disciplined commercial versions to customers who pay for reliability: broadcasters, communications carriers, newspapers and publishers. Printing survives inside that identity only where it becomes electronic (composition, text editing, prepress systems for publishers). The mechanical press lines are run for cash and then separated. The company's center of gravity, including headquarters, talent and capital, sits with the engine rather than with its history. It stops buying growth and grows by turning the same flywheel harder every year, so earnings compound instead of revenue piling up.

**Sentence:** *Reliable communications and information-handling systems for mission-critical customers, built on government-proven technology.*

**Rules it creates:**
- In: broadcast transmission and studio systems, microwave and point-to-point links, data terminals and data communications, satellite and space communications, and electronic composition and editing systems for publishers.
- Out: mechanical presses as a growth bet, hot-metal typesetting, and corrugated-paper machinery.

**Uncomfortable?** Yes. It reclassifies the founding business as a non-core cash source.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. Commercial electronics operating margin is at or above government-contract margin within two years.
2. At least two commercial product lines trace directly to government-funded technology, and each earns more than its company-funded R&D.
3. Publishers buy electronic composition or editing systems from the company. This proves the printing customer base is an asset to the Hedgehog Concept and not just history.

If test 1 fails and printing proves to be the profit engine, loop back to Gate 5 with "electronic printing systems" as the leading candidate.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring** until return on capital from the last five deals is reported and exceeds the cost of capital. This includes attractive bolt-ons.
2. **Stop growth investment in mechanical presses.** Maintain service and parts, harvest cash, and prepare for separation.
3. **Stop hot-metal typesetting** product development and new capacity.
4. **Stop corrugated-paper machinery.** Divest.
5. **Stop rewarding product launches.** Rewrite the promotion criteria around commercial profit and customer adoption.
6. **Stop chasing broad commercial electronics** where large conglomerates win on scale, such as consumer-adjacent or commodity equipment. Compete only where government-proven reliability is the reason to buy.
7. **Stop running five acquired electronics units as separate fiefdoms.** One commercial communications P&L, one government P&L.
8. **Stop reporting consolidated sales growth as the headline.** Report segment return on capital.
