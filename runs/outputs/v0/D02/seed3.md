# Corvane: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Corvane keeps independent restaurants' dish machines running faster than anyone else, one dense metro at a time, and it makes its money on contribution per technician-hour.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** turning demand into affordable placements. 288 of 403 refurbished units (71%) came through Delmar from Harrow's Grill remodels, at $1,140 landed. Harrow's has about 118 of its 610 locations left and plans to finish by the end of its next fiscal year. The other channels cost $2,700–$3,600 a unit and have historically supplied only about 29 units a year.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the supply cliff is real, and it is not on the offsite agenda. Payback on a placement, measured before field labor, moves from about 12 months on a Delmar unit to about 21–27 months on an auction unit and about 46 months on a new machine. Raising the price to $289 does not close that gap. Restoring a 12-month payback on an auction unit would take a price of roughly $400.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop pursuing Larchmont as structured: conveyor units, a 2-hour SLA with service credits, and one customer worth about 25% of revenue.
2. Stop planning a spring third metro while the east territory meets its 4-hour promise only 81% of the time.
3. Stop modeling the business on $1,140 machines and a 63% margin that leaves out field-technician labor.

**Offsite answers:**
- **Third metro:** No. Adopt a written expansion gate instead.
- **Second bay:** Defer it, and tie it to the supply plan.
- **Larchmont:** Decline as structured.
- **$289 for new accounts:** Yes.

**Numbers that would change this answer:**
- Fully loaded technician cost and contribution by zone.
- Delmar's true remaining Harrow's volume, and whether Corvane can buy all of it.
- Non-Delmar supply available at ≤$2,500 landed.
- Churn and redeployment rate, and machine service life.
- Larchmont's real incremental margin, and how much its demand overlaps restaurant service peaks.

Greatness potential: 16/100

*Evidence note: this was an autonomous run. Memory, past runs and web research were unavailable, so the dossier is the only evidence. Every inference is marked [ASSUMPTION].*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is the company an independent chef calls because their neighbor did. In every zone it serves, a broken dish machine is back in service within 4 hours, 98% of the time. Preventive maintenance and sanitizing-temperature records make unannounced health inspections a non-event. It grows by filling in the map, not spreading across it. A new metro opens only when the last one passes a written density test. Its machines come from a sourcing engine it built itself, not from one chain's remodel program. Every returned unit is rebuilt and redeployed, so capital per placement keeps falling. Its unit economics are reported with field labor included, and contribution per technician-hour rises every quarter. It does not depend on Dev closing deals, Teodor running the rota, or Harrow's Grill renovating.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop pursuing Larchmont as structured**, and any SLA tighter than 4 hours that is backed by service credits.
2. **Stop planning a spring third metro.** Expansion happens only through the written gate (see the 90-day plan).
3. **Stop presenting unit economics on $1,140 machines and a 63% margin that excludes field labor.** Board decks show contribution after field labor.
4. **Stop expanding beyond door-type and undercounter machines**: no conveyors, no ice machines, no refrigeration.
5. **Stop selling outside dense zones** in the east territory. New accounts must sit within a set drive time of existing routes [ASSUMPTION: about 20 minutes].
6. **Stop buying capacity ahead of supply.** The second bay and forklift wait for the sourcing plan.
7. **Stop the CEO personally closing most large accounts.** Dev coaches; a seller closes.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- Report the 4-hour response rate weekly, by territory and by zone.
- Never skip a quarterly preventive-maintenance visit.
- Rebuild every returned machine and either redeploy it or log why not.
- Report supply weeks-of-cover (refurb-ready units ÷ weekly placements) every week.
- Write a blameless autopsy for every missed 4-hour call.

**Freedom within the framework:**
- **Boundaries:** independent restaurants, door-type and undercounter equipment, existing zones, a 4-hour promise, a price floor of $289 for new accounts.
- **Inside the boundaries:**
  - Technicians own their routes and customer relationships.
  - Dispatch triages calls freely.
  - Marguerite sets rebuild standards.
  - Sellers price within a set band.

**Bureaucracy check:** at 31 people there's no evident compensating bureaucracy. Keep it that way by fixing seats, not adding rules.
