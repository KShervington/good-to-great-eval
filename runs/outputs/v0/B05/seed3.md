# Varden Communications: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Varden builds the world's best personal wireless devices, and the radio systems and chips inside them, in whichever standard customers and carriers choose, and it measures itself on profit per wireless subscriber served.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** "best devices *in the standards carriers are moving to*." The franchise is analog. Carriers are starting to plan digital networks, and the head of the cellular business has said publicly that tens of millions of analog customers can't be wrong. Close behind is "carriers as willing partners," which the premium distribution program puts at risk.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop running the company to a goal of doubling in size every five years.
  2. Stop adding commitment to the satellite venture before an independent, demand-based review of the launch phase.
  3. Stop the coercive carrier distribution program in its current form.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Wireless is about 60% of sales and the core of the profit engine. It sits on a standard that is about to be replaced, and the leaders of that business are defending the old standard rather than making it obsolete themselves. This is the company's own stated habit of self-renewal turned off at the worst possible moment.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - Segment profitability. If wireless is not the profit engine, the denominator changes.
  - Varden's share in digital handsets and in each competing digital standard.
  - The satellite venture's demand forecasts.
  - Carrier reaction to the distribution program.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Varden is a wireless company, not a conglomerate chasing a size target. It treats each shift in cellular standards as the moment to make its own bestselling products obsolete before anyone else can. It ships the smallest, most reliable phones and the infrastructure behind them in every standard that matters, analog and digital. It treats carriers as partners whose loyalty is earned through product superiority rather than extracted through shelf-space terms. Semiconductors serve that core with radio and embedded chips; they are not a front in a PC-chip war. Capital goes to bets that pass stage gates tied to evidence of demand, so there is no single open-ended commitment the size of a year's earnings. Six Sigma discipline and combative technical debate are pointed at one question, every quarter: *what will make our current best product obsolete, and are we the ones building it?*

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop managing to "double every five years."** Replace it with profit per subscriber and digital share. A size goal turns into an argument for every big bet.
2. **Stop adding commitment to the satellite venture** until an independent review covers demand, handset economics and the subcontract-guarantee exposure. Cap total exposure and seek ways to share or exit the subcontract obligations.
3. **Stop the premium distribution program as designed,** meaning access to the phone in exchange for share quotas and display demands. It uses today's lead to buy ill will that will come due at the next standard shift.
4. **Stop funding analog-only handset roadmaps** beyond the current product cycle. Every new platform needs a digital path.
5. **Stop the head-on PC microprocessor challenge** as a strategic goal. Reposition semiconductor effort toward chips that make Varden's wireless products better.
6. **Stop judging products by internal pride metrics** ("world's smallest") without asking whether they ride the standard carriers are moving toward.
7. **Stop treating emerging-market expansion as growth in itself.** Tie China and other investments to subscriber economics, not to footprint.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Six Sigma quality on every product.
- A standing "obsolete ourselves" review each quarter.
- Stage gates with kill criteria on every bet above a set threshold, for example 10% of annual earnings `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Framework boundaries:**
- Business units are free to engineer and compete internally, including combative debate.
- They are not free to make company-level standard commitments or open-ended capital commitments alone.

**Bureaucracy check:** No surplus bureaucracy is evident. The risk runs the other way. Strong unit autonomy lets one business defend analog without a company-level challenge.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Uncompromising quality (Six Sigma).
2. Renewal: we obsolete our own products.
3. Open, rigorous debate that ends in a decision.
4. Integrity with customers and partners.

**Core purpose:** to let people communicate wherever they are, through technology that is reliable enough to trust.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Every person on Earth can reach anyone, anywhere, on wireless technology Varden designed, in whatever standard comes next.* This follows from the Hedgehog Concept. It is about reach and renewal, not company size.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Six Sigma.
- Standing technology roadmaps with explicit "self-obsolescence" milestones.
- An independent capital-allocation committee with stage gates.
- A carrier advisory council.
- A merit-based succession process that is visible to the organization.

**Verdict: PASS.**
