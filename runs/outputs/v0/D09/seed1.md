## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larder Line is Corbridge's indispensable cold chain. Any grocer, wholesaler or distributor with short-dated meat, dairy or produce, including whatever a markdown app fails to sell by close, calls Larder Line first. That food reaches a matched agency the same day, and the agency confirms it was used. The organization reports how many households got fresh food each week, not just how many pounds it picked up. It runs a deliberately narrow set of routes with trained cold-chain drivers. It says "not us, but here's who" to good ideas outside that lane. Its success no longer depends on one grocery chain or one founder.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Why this denominator:** the route-hour is the scarce resource, because it combines the reefer vans and volunteer time. "Usable" ties the measure to the outcome funders now ask about. "Perishable" ties it to the one thing only Larder Line can do.

**Intersection tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The cold-chain distinction, the anchor donors (Freshway and the wholesaler) and the long-tenured drivers all fit.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: the café, composting, backpacks, bread growth, and the van until the data is in.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. Headline pounds may fall, the board chair's project gets declined, and the founder's "yes" reflex has to change.

**Confirmation tests (it stays a hypothesis until these come in):**
1. A recipient sample shows dairy, protein and produce matched to capacity are the most-used and most-requested categories. Agnes asked for "more dairy, less bread."
2. At least three new perishable donors can be recruited within a year.
3. The county fund and foundations respond to a household-based report.
4. Usable pounds per route-hour can be measured and then raised.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** good food reaches neighbors instead of the landfill.

**Core values:**
1. Same-day, every time.
2. Cold and food-safe, no exceptions.
3. Respect for the agencies and the people they serve. Ask them, and listen.
4. Honest numbers, including the unwelcome ones.
5. Volunteers are partners, not labor.

**BHAG (20 years):** every Corbridge household that relies on emergency food gets fresh protein, dairy or produce each week, moved by Larder Line's cold chain.
