# Larder Line: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Larder Line is Corbridge's same-day cold chain. It moves the perishable food other groups can't carry to the agencies that need it, before it spoils.*

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Freshway's Eastside stores went from **412 to 263 lbs per pickup (−36%)** starting the same spring their markdown app pilot began. Freshway's other 8 stores barely moved (398 → 391, −2%). The app goes chainwide next year, and Freshway is **44% of all pounds**. The board's working explanation is Route 9 construction. If the app is the cause and the other 8 stores follow, about **129,000 lbs a year** disappear. That is roughly Year 0's entire gain (+128,000). `[ASSUMPTION: cause not yet confirmed]`

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** matching supply to agency need and measuring what actually gets eaten. Pounds are counted at pickup. Agencies compost surplus produce and nobody asks how much. The household field on the county application was left blank.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Don't run the Larder Kitchen café inside Larder Line. If Renata builds it, it should be its own entity, with Larder Line as its food supplier.
2. Don't buy the third van ($71,000, about 34% of reserves) this cycle. Fix van #2 for $6,800.
3. Stop the side programs. End the Monday compost route, hand the backpack pilot to the schools or an agency, and pause gleaning.

**Answers to the retreat's three questions:**
- **Third van:** not now. Revisit in Q2 against the explicit triggers in the 90-day plan.
- **Café grant:** not as Larder Line.
- **Newer programs:** none should continue as Larder Line operations. Transition them humanely.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Larder Line is the cold artery of Corbridge's food system. When a grocer, distributor, processor or wholesaler has safe perishable surplus, Larder Line is the obvious call. That includes dairy, meat, prepared food and good produce. It reaches an agency the same day, and the agency is one that asked for that food. Larder Line works alongside markdown apps: it takes what the app can't sell. It reports three things:
- usable pounds delivered,
- the households those pounds reach,
- its own cost to deliver them.

It does not report pounds loaded. It says no to good ideas that don't use the cold chain, including ideas its own board loves. Its drivers, its food-safety discipline and its donor relationships are documented well enough to survive Teddy's retirement and any single donor's change of strategy.

**Denominator:** usable perishable pounds delivered **per route-hour**. Pickup pounds minus agency-reported waste, counting dairy, meat, prepared food and produce the agencies asked for, divided by van-plus-driver hours. `[ASSUMPTION: not currently measured]`

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. It explains why Freshway gives dairy and meat, why agencies want Larder Line's drivers, and why Agnes asks for "more dairy, less bread."
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It excludes the café, the backpacks, the compost route, gleaning, and growth in bread volume.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It says no to the board chair's project and to a funder-friendly story, and it demotes the headline metric.

**Confirmation tests (90 days):**
1. Delivered-and-used data from at least 10 agencies shows that perishable categories have materially lower waste rates than bread and bakery.
2. At least 2 upstream donors (distributors or processors) agree to give cold-chain surplus specifically because Larder Line can carry it cold.
3. Freshway confirms there is still meaningful post-app surplus to rescue on a revised pickup schedule.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop treating the café as Larder Line's next chapter.** Don't submit the city application under Larder Line. If Renata wants to build it, it should apply as its own entity or under a different fiscal sponsor `[ASSUMPTION: city rules allow this]`, with Larder Line as a supplier.
2. **Stop the Monday compost route.** It frees one van-day a week.
3. **Stop running the backpack pilot.** Supply it; don't operate it. This frees two Friday drivers.
4. **Pause gleaning** until agency data shows a produce gap.
5. **Stop buying capacity ahead of supply.** No third van until the Q2 triggers are met.
6. **Stop headlining "pounds rescued at pickup."** Report usable pounds delivered and households. Never leave an outcome field blank again.
7. **Stop delivering what's on the truck.** Deliver what agencies asked for: dairy first, bread only where it's wanted.
8. **Stop saying yes by default.** Every new ask gets the three-question test (Section 6 of the appendix).
9. **Stop explaining declines before diagnosing them.** Any donor down more than 15% for two months gets investigated, not narrated.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** Make sure good food feeds Corbridge's neighbors instead of a landfill.

**Core values:**
1. Same day, every time.
2. Food safety without exception.
3. Agencies decide what they need.
4. Drivers are the organization.
5. Count what reaches people.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** *By Year 15, no safe perishable surplus in Corbridge is wasted for lack of a cold truck. Every surplus pound of dairy, meat and fresh food reaches a household within a day.*
