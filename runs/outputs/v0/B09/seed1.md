# Pacifica Bancorp: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the best and lowest-cost bank in the world for the ordinary households and small businesses of its home state, measured by profit per customer relationship.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** cutting the cost to serve each relationship. The branch network has high fixed costs, the bank lags rivals on ATMs, and lifetime employment makes cost hard to move. Within six years the cheap-deposit subsidy that covered those costs will be gone.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop measuring and rewarding success by asset growth.
2. Stop adding loans to developing countries, and stop booking long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded with short-term deposits.
3. Stop letting lending authority run on decentralized approval without central limits on concentration and country exposure.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The bank's core advantage, capped-rate consumer deposits, is being legislated away over six years. Money-market funds are already draining those deposits. Meanwhile, the asset side has been loaded with fixed-yield mortgages and fast-grown loans to developing countries, farms and construction projects. Funding costs will rise while those asset yields stay fixed and credit risk stays unmeasured.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Loan-loss reserves compared with loss experience, by loan category
- Exposure by country compared with capital
- The mortgage-versus-deposit maturity gap and its sensitivity to rate changes
- Expense ratio compared with peer banks
- Profitability by segment (home-state retail versus international versus other)

If reserves are adequate and the gap is small, the score rises sharply. If not, the first job is repairing the balance sheet, and greatness waits.

**Answer to the board's open question:** The choice between a steady banker and a change agent is a false one. The bank needs a leader who protects the core franchise and changes nearly everything around it: cost, credit discipline, technology and the success metric. That leader must also say no to new businesses. Choosing a change agent hired to *enter new businesses* is the riskiest option available.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Pacifica is smaller in ambition and larger in quality. It is the bank most households and small businesses in its home state call their primary bank. That is the original "little guy" franchise, run with the discipline of a low-cost operator.

It has stopped treating "world's largest" as a goal. It measures itself by profit per customer relationship, with return on assets (ROA) and expense ratio as guardrails. International activity shrinks to what home-state customers actually need, such as trade finance. Credit is decentralized for service but centralized for limits.

It funds its assets with matched maturities, so a rate shock is an inconvenience, not a crisis. It uses automation to lower the cost of each relationship. It keeps its people through attrition and redeployment, not guaranteed seats. It outperforms peers on risk-adjusted returns across a full rate and credit cycle, under more than one CEO.

```
 │       plus prudent, centrally limited lending to known customers   ◄── WEAKEST LINK
 │                          │

```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Turn 5:** Build the baseline for cost per relationship, then close the ATM gap with in-state rivals. Shift routine transactions off branch tellers, and shrink branch costs by attrition, not layoffs.
2. **Turn 2:** Define and track primary relationships. Package deposits, cards and lending so households have reasons to stay that don't depend on the rate cap.

**Doom-loop check:** The bank has been running a different loop:

> grow loans → grow assets → fund cheaply with capped deposits → record earnings → grow faster

That loop is breaking, because the cheap funding is disappearing. Doom-loop signs already visible:
- Big programs to jump-start growth: overseas branches went from fewer than 20 to more than 90, and the venture-capital partnership.
- Reaching for yield in new loan categories.
- A creed of "action" over understanding.

The risk at the transition is a new CEO who fixes the broken loop by launching *new* big programs, such as new businesses or acquisitions. That is the doom loop in its classic form. Gate 8 is `CONDITIONAL`.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop using asset growth as the measure of success,** in reporting, in communication with investors and the press, and in compensation.
2. **Stop new lending to developing countries.** Cap total country exposure as a percentage of capital, and run it down from there.
3. **Stop holding long-term fixed-rate mortgages funded by short-term deposits.** Originate adjustable-rate loans, or sell and hedge the fixed-rate ones.
4. **Stop approving loans without central limits.** Large credits and every concentration in farm, construction and country lending need central approval.
5. **Stop venture-capital direct investments.** Exit the partnership in an orderly way.
6. **Stop expanding the overseas branch network.** Shrink international to what home-state customers need.
7. **Stop adding fixed cost.** Freeze headcount, and reduce through attrition. No new branches without a case built on profit per relationship.
8. **Stop treating "action" as a virtue in credit.** In lending, deciding not to decide is often the right decision.
9. **Stop citing "world's largest" and praise of management as evidence.** These are hubris signals.
