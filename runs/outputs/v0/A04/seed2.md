# Good to Great: Kessling Company

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Kessling will be the best in the world at practical medicines for chronic hormone, metabolic and inflammatory disease, and will run on profit per research dollar.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** safety evidence → trust from regulators and prescribers. Two flagship products have been hit by regulators in five years. The company fought one of those orders in court and lost.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop expanding non-drug businesses. That means no further seed or agricultural acquisitions, and a strategic review of the existing ones.
2. Stop measuring yourself against the largest drug companies on total research spend. Compete on productivity in a narrow field.
3. Stop fighting regulators in court over the science. Answer with better data.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Kessling's next big profit engine, the arthritis anti-inflammatory, belongs to another firm. Its own diabetes franchise is under a safety cloud. The company could become a distributor of someone else's science while it still calls itself research-based.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- 10-year sales, earnings and return on equity compared with peers. These decide whether it is "good" at all.
- Research spend as a share of sales, compared with peers.
- Profit by therapeutic area.
- Share of sales and profit from seeds and agriculture.
- Steroid manufacturing cost compared with competitors. This decides whether "best in world" is real.
- Founding-family ownership stake. This decides whether governance can change.

`Greatness potential: 15/100`

*Why 15:* Very few companies in Collins's research made the good-to-great jump. Kessling has real scientific assets, but several things work against it: a closed family succession pipeline, a scale gap in research, dependence on a licensed product, and an active diversification habit. The plan below gives it a credible path. Following that path requires the board and the family to give up control habits that have lasted 90 years.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Kessling is no longer a smaller copy of the giant research companies. It owns a narrow field: chronic disease rooted in hormones, metabolism and inflammation. That is the territory of its adrenal-hormone first, its low-cost steroid fermentation and its oral diabetes drug, and the arthritis compound fits it too.

Within that field, specialists think of Kessling first for three reasons:
- Its medicines come with the strongest long-term safety evidence in the industry.
- Its process chemistry makes them cheaper to produce.
- Every licensed compound becomes a platform for in-house follow-ons that Kessling owns outright.

It has sold or ring-fenced the businesses outside that field. It allocates research money by profit per research dollar, not by matching rivals' budgets. Its board is independent and chooses CEOs on merit, so the company no longer depends on who marries into the family. The founder's values of practical innovation, care for employees and civic responsibility are kept. His succession model is not.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop non-drug diversification.** Freeze acquisitions and review the seed and agricultural businesses for divestiture.
2. **Stop benchmarking total research spend against the largest drug companies.** Benchmark productivity instead.
3. **Stop litigating regulators over efficacy and safety science.** Respond with independent data.
4. **Stop fixed-dose combination products** unless each component has independent evidence. This is the lesson of the withdrawn antibiotic.
5. **Stop funding research outside the three focus areas** beyond a capped exploratory budget. `[ASSUMPTION: cap around 10–15%, to be set once the portfolio map exists]`
6. **Stop royalty-only licensing.** Every licence must carry follow-on or extension rights, or a parallel in-house programme.
7. **Stop new research investment in antibiotics.** Run the line for cash; do not grow it.
8. **Stop restricting CEO succession to the family.**

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **Scientific integrity.** Data comes before defending a product.
2. **Practical innovation.** Medicine should work better and cost less.
3. **Care for people.** This covers patients, and employees in the founder's tradition.
4. **Responsibility to the home community.**

**Core purpose:** to turn sound science into medicine people can actually live with.

**BHAG (20 years):** own the leading medicine in at least two chronic hormone, metabolic or inflammatory diseases, each discovered in Kessling's own labs.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent board majority, with a CEO succession process open to non-family candidates.
- A capital-allocation rule based on profit per research dollar.
- A therapeutic-area gate on every research programme and licence.
- A board-level safety surveillance committee.
- A licensing rule that requires follow-on rights or a parallel internal programme.
