# Kessling Company: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Kessling will be the best in the world at medicines that act on the body's hormone and inflammation pathways. It will make them at the lowest cost, prove their safety beyond question, and measure itself by profit per research dollar.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** the pipeline of products Kessling owns. Its next big growth product is licensed, and the rights belong to another company. Until Kessling's own labs feed the inflammation and hormone franchise, the flywheel depends on someone else's patent.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop measuring Kessling against the largest drug companies on total research spend and breadth.
2. Stop diversifying into non-drug businesses. Put the seed company under a divestiture review.
3. Stop fighting safety and efficacy rulings in court. Answer with evidence, not lawyers.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the steroid route and the first oral diabetes drug made Kessling's name. That second product is now under a heart-safety cloud. The company already lost a court fight over a product worth about 12% of domestic gross income. The regulator is ruling against Kessling's past, and Kessling's main response so far has been litigation and diversification.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Current profit and return on equity compared with the industry. If these are well below peers, Gate 1 fails, and the work becomes a survival plan rather than a greatness plan.
- Research spending as a share of sales, compared with peers.
- Profit by therapeutic area. This shows whether steroids and hormones really are the profit core.
- Seed and agriculture share of sales and profit.
- Terms of the arthritis licence: term, territory, royalty and follow-on rights.
- The founding family's ownership stake.

Greatness potential: 22/100

*Evidence basis: the dossier only. This session had no memory, past-run search or research tools, so there is no prior baseline and no outside market data. Every inference is marked [ASSUMPTION]. Seven of the eleven gates are CONDITIONAL. That traces to two root problems: **missing current financials** and **insular family governance with an adversarial truth climate**.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kessling stops trying to be a smaller copy of the giant research-based drug companies. It becomes the company that specialists in arthritis, endocrine and inflammatory disease trust first. It uses ninety years of hormone and steroid chemistry, low-cost fermentation manufacture, and the formulation skill the founder started with. It discovers some compounds and licenses others, but only inside that franchise. It treats safety evidence as a competitive weapon: it publishes problems before regulators find them and pulls weak products itself. It wins on profit per research dollar, not on total research dollars. An independent board runs it. The board can pick the best leader whether or not that person is family. The company keeps the founder's care for employees and the home city, but it has sold or spun off businesses that don't serve the franchise.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Turn 3 → 4:** launch the arthritis compound to specialists, backed by the most rigorous post-marketing safety monitoring Kessling has ever run. Make this launch the proof that Kessling's safety evidence can be trusted.
2. **Turn 2:** start an in-house follow-on anti-inflammatory programme now. Renegotiate the licence for follow-on compounds and wider territory. This way the franchise doesn't end when the licence ends.

**Doom-loop check (signs already present):**
- *A big move to jump-start growth:* the seed company was bought with stock six years ago, and the dossier shows no link to the core business.
- *Reacting without understanding:* the company fought the regulator in court instead of producing evidence.
- *Changing direction:* management is openly debating "more research, more licensing, or more diversification?" It has no fixed direction.
- *Concentration bet:* future profits may rest on one licensed product.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop measuring Kessling against the largest drug companies on total research spend and breadth.
2. Stop acquiring non-drug businesses. Put the seed company and any other agricultural units under a divestiture review.
3. Stop going to court over safety and efficacy rulings. Respond with data and accept final outcomes.
4. Stop selling fixed-combination products that lack independent efficacy evidence of their own.
5. Stop starting research programmes outside hormone, inflammation and metabolic chemistry. This includes new antibiotic classes.
6. Stop signing single-product licences that have no follow-on, backup or supply-security terms.
7. Stop drawing top executives and the board majority from family only.
8. Stop promoting the diabetes drug beyond what the current safety evidence supports.

## Appendix: gate workings

**Gate 0, Subject card:**
- *Name and type:* Kessling Company, a business (listed pharmaceutical manufacturer).
- *Steward:* the president and board.
- *Horizon:* decades.
- *Stage:* mature, about 90 years old, under strategic pressure.

**Gate 1, Baseline:**
- *Evidence of good:* about 90 years of survival, 16 years as a listed company, a record of firsts (adrenal hormone, low-cost steroid route, first oral diabetes drug), 6th-largest U.S. antibiotic maker about 16 years ago ($22.6M in antibiotic sales), and enough capacity to win a licence and buy a company.
- *What great would mean for Kessling:*
  - **Superior performance:** profit per research dollar above peers.
  - **Distinctive impact:** specialists would lose their trusted source of hormone and inflammation medicine.
  - **Endurance:** greatness that doesn't depend on one family or one licensed drug.

**Gate 2, Leadership read:**
- *Strengths:* a long tenure provides continuity, and the founder's care ethic is still part of the culture `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- *Gaps:* the dossier doesn't show whether ambition is aimed at the company or at the family's control. Succession has never been tested outside the family. Litigating against the regulator looks like "window" behaviour: blaming outside forces rather than looking in the mirror.
- *One behaviour change:* the president publicly sponsors an independent succession process and an independent safety review.

**Gate 6, Framework:**
- *Framework (fixed):* the franchise boundary, the safety-evidence standard, profit-per-research-dollar hurdles, and licences only with follow-on rights.
- *Freedom inside the framework:* scientists choose targets and methods within the franchise.
- *Bureaucracy check:* any approval layers that exist only because the board lacked independent oversight should go once independent directors are seated `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- *Fanatical consistency:* every product gets a safety dossier stronger than the regulator requires, every time.

**Gate 7, Technology triage:**

**Gate 9, Clock-building mechanisms:**
