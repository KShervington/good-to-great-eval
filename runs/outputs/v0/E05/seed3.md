# Ashby & Crane, Inc.: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
*Ashby & Crane is best in the world at turning an under-performing adult-indulgence brand into its category's #1 or #2 premium brand, through image-led marketing it controls end to end, and at scaling that brand worldwide, measured by profit per global brand.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** **brand reach.** The company's engine is image-led marketing, and its permission to market is shrinking. Broadcast advertising has been banned for seven years, and the federal anti-smoking campaign launched this month points to further limits. Capital allocation is the most urgent *push*, but brand reach is the link most likely to break.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. **Don't buy the soft-drink company.** Treat no as the default unless it clears the three hurdles in the Stop-Doing section. Its #3 "alternative to colas" brand, sold through cola-dominated bottler networks, at roughly 4x the brewer's price, is a core-competence bet. It is not a best-in-world bet.
2. **Stop treating "diversify beyond tobacco" as a goal in itself.** The razors, shaving and gum acquisitions already showed what that produces.
3. **Stop adding beer capacity ahead of demonstrated share and return.** Release capacity in stages, against evidence.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Nearly all of the company's value rests on one cigarette brand. That product was declared the nation's leading public-health enemy this month. Buying a #3 soft drink doesn't fix the problem. Facing it honestly does.

**Missing numbers that could change this answer:** profit by business and by brand; beer return on invested capital; the soft-drink target's financials and deal terms; international margins by market; an estimate of product-liability exposure.

`Greatness potential: 62/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Ashby & Crane is a disciplined **brand-building machine** that owns only businesses where image-led marketing decides who wins, and where it can be #1 or #2. Its flagship becomes the world's best-selling cigarette by winning one international market at a time. Beer earns returns above its cost of capital without chasing Rival A on volume for its own sake. The company refuses acquisitions where distribution, not marketing, decides the outcome. It treats health and liability as facts to be measured and disclosed, not threats to be argued away. It builds reserves and governance on that basis. It also builds a leadership bench so the next 20 years don't depend on two men who have worked together for two decades.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Buying the soft-drink company.** The default answer is no. Reconsider only if all three of these hold:
   - (a) The company can show marketing, not bottler distribution, decides share in lemon-lime, *and* it has a credible path to bottler access that the cola leaders can't block.
   - (b) There is a credible route to #1 or #2 in a category where it can lead, not a permanent #3.
   - (c) The price earns its cost of capital on the target's own numbers, without assumed "marketing magic" synergies.
2. **Diversifying because tobacco is scary.** Fear is not a strategy. Diversify only into categories that pass the Hedgehog test.
3. **Beer capacity built ahead of evidence.** Add capacity in stages, tied to share and return milestones, and don't race Rival A on volume.
4. **Holding sub-scale, non-leading businesses** (razors, shaving, gum, and possibly some packaging lines).
5. **Supporting a long tail of minor cigarette brands** that dilute profit per brand `[ASSUMPTION: brand portfolio not detailed]`.
6. **Treating health and liability as a public-relations problem.** Manage it as a financial and governance fact.
7. **Any marketing that reaches minors or skirts the broadcast ban.** It is wrong, and it speeds up regulation against the core.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** every capital decision is tested against profit per global brand; every business must be #1 or #2 or have a credible path there; the health and liability review reaches the board every quarter.

**Framework and freedom:** brand teams have full creative and market-entry autonomy *inside* the Hedgehog Concept. Capital and acquisitions are centralized.

**Bureaucracy check:** no evidence of excess rules. The risk runs the other way: decisions concentrated in two long-tenured leaders.
