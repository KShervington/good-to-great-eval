# Ashby & Crane, Inc.: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Ashby & Crane builds and grows the world's strongest premium tobacco brands, market by market, and judges every dollar by profit per loyal adult smoker.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from brand-building to new adult adoption. TV and radio advertising is already banned. A national anti-smoking campaign began this month. Limits on public smoking are being pushed. The machine that made the flagship brand depends on visibility, and that visibility is being regulated away, starting in the U.S.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. **Do not buy the soft-drink company.** Two cola giants with bottler networks own that category. Marketing talent does not beat distribution, and the ~$0.5B price is several times what the beer stake cost.
  2. **Stop treating "diversify beyond tobacco" as a goal in itself.** Razors, shaving and gum already show what that produces.
  3. **Stop adding beer capacity ahead of proven returns.** Being #2 behind an entrenched Rival A, bought with heavy advertising and capex, is not yet evidence of greatness.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the core product is under escalating federal health attack, and that attack now includes an active government campaign. Liability is "contained so far," not contained. Every other decision has to be priced against this.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - profit by business and by brand;
  - beer return on capital versus Rival A;
  - the soft-drink target's financials and deal terms;
  - international volume and share;
  - shareholder returns versus the market.

  If beer turns out to earn returns close to cigarettes, it moves inside the Hedgehog. If the target were cheap and highly profitable, the soft-drink "no" would soften to "not at this price." It would not become "yes."

Greatness potential: 60/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Ashby & Crane is not a diversified consumer-products conglomerate. It is the world's best tobacco brand company, with one or two deliberately chosen brand-led adjacencies that pass the same economic test. The flagship brand's identity is guarded like a core asset and carried country by country, which turns international growth into the main engine as U.S. volume comes under pressure. Low-tar products follow the smokers' shift without overclaiming. Beer stays in the portfolio only while it earns its capital. The company deals with the health issue through candor and disciplined risk management, not denial. Succession is built so that the brand machine outlasts the chairman. It refuses the comfortable-sounding escape route of buying its way out of tobacco with businesses where it cannot be best.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) marketing anything heavily advertised; (b) image-led brand building in habit categories; (c) premium cigarette brands; (d) beer | **(c), with (b) as the underlying skill.** It took the flagship from a niche women's filter cigarette to the U.S. #1. It *cannot* be best at soft drinks, where bottler distribution is the moat, or at razors and gum, which have already failed. In beer it is a strong #2, but whether it can be *best* is unproven. |
| **Economic engine** | profit per company; per brand; per thousand cigarettes; per loyal adult smoker; per barrel | **Profit per loyal adult smoker**, operationalized as profit per thousand premium cigarettes, by market `[ASSUMPTION: cigarettes are the dominant profit source; the dossier has no profit-by-business data]` |
| **Deep passion** | the company; its products; marketing craft | Executives are "intensely committed to the company and its products." The strongest evidence of passion is the flagship brand itself, along with a 20+-year leadership partnership built around it. |

**Intersection:** premium, image-led tobacco brands, grown market by market.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The flagship repositioning, the international build and the low-tar launch all fit.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: soft drinks, razors, gum, and capacity-for-share in beer.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It means deepening commitment to a product under public-health attack instead of buying a way out. That discomfort is honest. The strategy only holds if it is paired with the candor and risk disciplines in §5 and §6.

**Confirmation tests (status: working hypothesis):**
1. Profit-by-business data shows cigarettes produce most of the profit at the highest return on capital.
2. Profit per thousand cigarettes is higher than Rival R's and holding or rising.
3. International flagship share rises in each newly entered market within 3–5 years of entry.
4. Beer either reaches a return on capital comparable to cigarettes, which would earn it a place inside the Hedgehog, or it does not, in which case it is run for cash.

## 3. The flywheel

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                              │
 [1] Iconic premium brand identity                     │
     (consistent image + pack, guarded centrally)      │
        │                                              │

 [2] Adult brand loyalty → premium pricing             │

        ▼                                              │
 [3] High PROFIT PER LOYAL ADULT SMOKER  ◄─ denominator│
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 [4] Cash reinvested: permitted brand support          │
     (sponsorship, packaging) + new-market entry       │
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 [5] Country-by-country share gains abroad             │
        │                                              │
        ▼                                              │
 [6] Manufacturing & distribution scale → lower unit   │
     cost, more cash, stronger brand presence ─────────┘
```

**Weakest link: [4]→[1].** Promotion channels keep narrowing. The TV and radio ban is seven years old, sponsorship is the likely next target, and public-smoking limits reduce how often the product is seen in use. If this link fails in a market, the loop runs on loyalty alone and slowly decays.

**Beer as a test loop, not a second flywheel yet:** brand repositioning → share gains → capacity → profit per barrel. It closes only if profit per barrel covers the advertising and capex. That is unknown.

**Current push:**
1. **International.** It grows faster than domestic, it is the hedge against U.S. regulatory pressure, and it is led by the company's most proven executive.
2. **Capital discipline.** Put one hurdle rate and the profit-per-loyal-customer test on every capital decision: beer capacity, the soft drink deal, packaging.

**Doom-loop check: warning present.** The company has a 15+-year pattern of trying to jump-start growth outside tobacco through acquisitions: razors, shaving, gum, beer, and now soft drinks. The stated motive, "diversify beyond tobacco," is a reaction to health pressure. It is not a conclusion about where the company can be best. Beer worked operationally, but its economics are unproven. The soft-drink deal fits the doom-loop pattern exactly: a large acquisition, a new direction, justified by a general skill ("we can market anything").

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Do not buy the soft-drink company.** Not at ~$0.5B, and probably not at all. The category is controlled by two cola giants through bottler networks. A lemon-lime "alternative" is structurally a niche brand. The company's edge is image, and the leaders' edge is distribution.
2. **Retire "diversify beyond tobacco" as a strategic goal.** Replace it with one question for every move: does this pass the Hedgehog test?
3. **Freeze further beer capacity investment** until profit per barrel and return on capital are shown to beat a set hurdle. Do not chase Rival A for share alone.
4. **Divest razors, shaving and gum.**
5. **Stop brand proliferation that dilutes the flagship.** Each new brand or line extension needs a distinct identity and a clear job, such as low-tar for shifting smokers.
6. **Stop any health implication in low-tar marketing that the science doesn't support.** Overclaiming is a direct liability risk.
7. **Stop treating health and regulation as a public-relations contest.** The company should hold no public position on health science that it could not defend in full, under oath, years from now.
8. **Stop third-party packaging growth** that doesn't serve the tobacco engine or meet the hurdle rate.

**Truth climate and charisma check:** "Intense commitment to the products" is both the passion circle and the biggest denial risk. A culture that loves its product will struggle to hear the health facts. A 21-year chairman can suppress bad news without meaning to. The red-flag mechanism is the health and regulatory seat reporting directly to the board, plus a standing agenda item on regulatory and liability exposure. Use blameless autopsies on the razor and gum acquisitions before any new deal.

**Faith statement:** *Adult smokers worldwide will keep choosing a small number of trusted premium brands for decades, and no one builds those brands better than we do. We will prevail only if we face the health facts squarely instead of arguing with them.*

**On "great":** this framework measures commercial and organizational greatness. For this company, the largest brutal fact is also a moral one: the product harms its customers. The plan does not resolve that. It insists on candor, no unsupported health claims, and pricing the risk honestly, because denial is both wrong and the fastest path to decline.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred, to be confirmed by leadership]`:
1. Marketing excellence through a consistent, disciplined brand identity.
2. Adult choice: sell to adults who choose the product, and only to them.
3. Candor about the product and its risks. This is new and non-negotiable.
4. Commitment and ownership: people own outcomes, not tasks.

**Core purpose:** *To make and build the brands adults choose, better than anyone in the world.*

**BHAG (15 years):** *Make the flagship the best-selling cigarette brand in the world and Ashby & Crane the world's leading tobacco company by profit, while staying a company that never has to retract what it says about its products.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- named succession bench for CEO, international and brand heads;
- capital-allocation committee applying the Hedgehog test with a published hurdle rate;
- a flagship brand-stewardship charter so the identity survives any one marketer;
- health and regulatory red-flag reporting to the board;
- annual re-scoring against the scorecard.
