# Good to Great: Ashby & Crane, Inc.

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Ashby & Crane turns one under-valued brand at a time into the defining premium brand of an adult, image-led consumer category, worldwide, and judges itself by profit per premium unit rather than by size.*

**Decision on the live question:** **Do not buy the soft-drink company.** It sits outside the Hedgehog Concept for three reasons:
- The category leaders win through bottler networks that Ashby & Crane cannot match.
- The target brand is already positioned as the alternative to colas, so the company's main skill, repositioning, has nothing left to work on.
- The price is several times what proved the beer thesis.

Put the money and the best people into international cigarettes and a gated beer build-out.

**Weakest flywheel link:** *Brand leadership → profit per premium unit*, outside cigarettes. The beer business has volume, rank and heavy spending, but there is no evidence yet that it earns its capital.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop treating "diversify beyond tobacco" as a reason to buy anything. Start with the soft-drink deal.
2. Stop adding beer capacity ahead of proven profit per barrel.
3. Stop holding razors, gum and (as a growth platform) packaging.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The government has just named the core product the nation's leading public-health enemy and launched its first national campaign against it. Liability is "contained so far," and that is not the same as contained. Endurance depends on how candidly the company handles this.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by business: beer vs. U.S. cigarettes vs. international vs. packaging.
- Shareholder returns vs. the market over 15+ years, which would show whether the company is already great.
- Latest U.S. and world share by company.
- The soft-drink target's financials and deal terms.

Greatness potential: 62/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Ashby & Crane is a focused global house of category-defining adult brands. Cigarettes are its cash engine and international markets are its growth runway. Beer is held to the same return discipline before any further capacity goes in. It no longer buys its way into categories where someone else controls the decisive advantage. It is run by a leadership team that outlasts the current chairman and his long-time partner. It deals with the health evidence and the liability exposure as facts to be managed openly, not as attacks to be denied. That candor is what gives it a chance to stay great through the regulatory decades ahead, not just to have been great.

- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It ties the company more tightly to tobacco at the moment public pressure is peaking, and it rejects the comfortable "diversification" story.

**Confirmation tests (what would prove or kill it):**
1. Profit per premium unit in beer reaches at least the company's cost of capital within three years. If it doesn't, the hedgehog narrows to tobacco plus international.
2. International profit per unit converges toward U.S. levels as brand leadership builds.
3. Premium share holds in the U.S. as advertising channels narrow further.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop pursuing the soft-drink acquisition.** If the board insists on pricing it, adopt a written walk-away rule: proceed only if its projected return beats the same money invested in international cigarettes.
2. **Stop using "diversify beyond tobacco" as an acquisition rationale.** Diversification is a risk position, not a strategy. Any new category must pass the three-circle test on its own merits.
3. **Stop adding beer capacity ahead of demonstrated profit per barrel.** Release capacity in tranches tied to returns.
4. **Stop chasing Rival A on total beer volume.** Compete on premium and innovation (reduced-calorie), not on matching scale.
5. **Stop holding razors, shaving and gum.** Exit them.
6. **Stop treating packaging and specialty paper as a growth bet.** Make it a captive supplier or sell it.
7. **Stop relying on the shrinking paid-media playbook as the only way to build brands.** Advertising restrictions will tighten further `[ASSUMPTION]`. Invest in channels the company controls: packaging, sponsorship, point of sale and trade relationships.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- One sharp identity per brand, held for decades, with no repositioning churn once a brand wins.
- Every capital decision is stated in profit per premium unit.
- Every acquisition proposal must name the brand being repositioned and the advantage the company brings that incumbents lack.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- *Framework:* the hedgehog, the denominator and the stop-doing list.
- *Freedom:* business heads choose creative, markets, sponsorships and product innovation (low-tar, reduced-calorie) inside it.

**Bureaucracy check:** no evidence of compensating rules in the dossier. Watch the post-chairman era for approval layers that substitute for missing trust in a new team.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from behavior, to be validated by leadership]`:
1. Brand integrity: a consistent identity and consistent quality, every time.
2. The courage to be different: reposition when others won't.
3. Long-term ownership over short-term volume.
4. Individual initiative backed by intense commitment.
5. *(Aspirational, needed for endurance)* Facing facts, including uncomfortable facts about our products.

**Core purpose (draft):** *To build brands adults choose with pride, and to earn the right to keep doing so.*

This purpose is `CONDITIONAL`. A purpose beyond profit is hard to state credibly for a product officially named a leading health threat. Whether this purpose holds depends on value #5 being practiced, not just printed.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Own the world's #1 brand in every category we compete in.* This means the flagship becomes the best-selling cigarette brand in the world, and the beer business overtakes Rival A in premium.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A formal CEO succession process with two or more internal candidates and a board timeline.
- A capital-allocation committee that uses profit per premium unit as its standard.
- An independent health-science and liability review that reports to the board every year.
- A brand-stewardship playbook (repositioning → consistency → global rollout) that is written down, so it doesn't live only in two executives.
