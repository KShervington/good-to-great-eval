# Halden Laboratories: Good to Great

## Verdict (one screen)

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halden turns fundamental science into first-in-class medicines for major chronic diseases, backed by evidence that doctors trust completely.* The economic denominator is **profit per breakthrough medicine**, measured over its patent life.

**Weakest flywheel link:** **Rigorous evidence → physician trust.** The new COX-2 painkiller is already being sold to investors as the growth story. The long-term outcome data that would justify broad use does not exist yet. Commercial commitment is running ahead of evidence.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop naming "top-tier growth company" as the company's number-one objective, and stop implying double-digit growth.
2. Stop presenting the COX-2 drug as the growth engine before outcome data exists. Launch it narrowly to the patients for whom its benefit is clearest.
3. Stop counting the PBM as part of the core. Open a formal separation review.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The growth promise does not add up. About **two-thirds of last year's sales growth came from the PBM**: $2.2B of the $3.3B increase. The drug business grew about 8%, and roughly 22% of its sales go generic within two years. Holding 10% pharma growth would need about **$7–8B of new-product sales within three years**. That is nearly the size of the cholesterol and blood-pressure franchises combined. The gap is putting pressure on one unproven drug class.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- COX-2 cardiovascular and long-term safety data.
- PBM profit on its own.
- Pipeline depth beyond COX-2.
- R&D spend and productivity.
- The expected generic-erosion curve.

**Shape of the run:** 6 of 11 gates are CONDITIONAL. That is more than half. Two shared root problems drive them:
- **A growth target has displaced the founding purpose as the organizing idea.**
- **The evidence that would test the biggest bet (COX-2 safety, PBM economics) is missing.**

Greatness potential: 40/100

> *Evidence note:* This run is autonomous and uses the dossier only. Memory search, past-run lookup and web research were not available. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`. The verdicts are disciplined hypotheses, not predictions. Collins's method was retrospective, and some of the companies he called great later declined.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halden is a **discovery company first and a growth stock never**. It accepts a visible two-to-three-year earnings trough as the cliff passes, and it says so publicly. It puts its best scientists on its few largest scientific opportunities. It earns premium prices during patent life because its evidence is the most trusted in the industry: its outcome trials are large, pre-specified, independently monitored and fully published, *including bad news*. It has separated from the pharmacy-benefit business, which is a fine business but not its business. It wins the COX-2 class on proof rather than promotion, even if that means ceding early share to the rival. In ten years its reputation for "medicine for the people" is a working mechanism, not a slogan, and that reputation is what draws the best scientists back to it.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** stating "top-tier growth company" as objective number one, and stop guiding or hinting at double-digit growth through the cliff.
2. **Stop** presenting the COX-2 painkiller as *the* growth story before long-term outcome data exists.
3. **Stop** mass-market promotion of COX-2 to low-GI-risk patients until outcome data supports broad use. Don't fight the rival with promotional spend. Win with evidence.
4. **Stop** counting PBM revenue as evidence of growth. Report pharma and PBM separately in every internal scorecard.
5. **Stop** advancing the second COX-2 compound to late-stage spend before the first compound's long-term safety picture is clear. Two bets on one class double the exposure to class risk.
6. **Stop** treating patent count as a success measure. Count first-in-class approvals and outcome-trial wins instead.
7. **Stop** tying executive pay to EPS growth `[ASSUMPTION that it is tied today]`.
8. **Keep stopping:** a large merger to fill the patent gap.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- Every major medicine gets a large, pre-specified, independently monitored outcome trial.
- All results are published, positive or negative.
- Safety signals reach the board within a fixed, short window.
- The best scientists go to the biggest opportunities.

**Framework and freedom:**
- **The framework (fixed):** the disease-area teams work only on first-in-class or clearly superior medicines for large chronic diseases.
- **Freedom inside it:** each team chooses its targets and methods.
- **Bureaucracy check:** layers of commercial review on scientific decisions are a sign of a wrong-seat problem. Remove them once the safety seat exists.

**Verdict (Gate 6): PASS.** The list is specific and long enough. Adopting it is a 90-day action.
