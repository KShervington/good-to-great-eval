## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halden is a smaller-sounding, more-respected company. It does not promise top-tier growth. It promises that every medicine it brings to market is either first in its class or clearly better than what patients already have, and that the evidence comes before the marketing. Its scientists pick disease areas where fundamental biology gives them an edge, and its disease-area teams turn that edge into medicines doctors trust without being sold to. It runs large outcome trials as a competitive weapon rather than a regulatory chore. It treats a safety signal as the most important news in the building. It accepts lumpy revenue across patent cycles in exchange for a reputation that compounds, so that regulators grant priority review, payers accept premium prices, and the best researchers want to work there. The PBM, if it stays, is run at arm's length. The founding line about medicine being for people, with profits following, is an operating rule and not a slogan.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in world at** | Fundamental research; first-in-class discovery; pharmacy benefit management; global marketing; cardiovascular and metabolic franchises | **Turning fundamental disease biology into first- or best-in-class medicines for major chronic diseases** (it is on track to patent more new compounds than any rival; cholesterol and heart drugs are its two largest categories) | PBM and mail-order pharmacy (a distribution business with no evidence of best-in-world advantage); generics; me-too follow-ons; volume growth for its own sake |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per sales dollar; profit per R&D dollar; profit per molecule; profit per patent-year | **Profit per patent-protected year of a clearly differentiated medicine** | Revenue growth (the PBM inflates it); profit per prescription (rewards volume over differentiation) |
| **Deep passion** | Growth leadership; managed care; science serving patients | **Science that measurably changes patients' lives**, which is the founding philosophy | "Top-tier growth" (a stock-market goal, not something its scientists are passionate about) `[ASSUMPTION]` |

**Denominator:** profit per patent-protected year of a medicine with demonstrated superiority. This builds evidence into the economics. A drug that wins share without proven differentiation earns less patent-life value, and an oversold drug can destroy it.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The cholesterol and heart franchises ($8.9B combined) are the kind of medicine this sentence describes `[ASSUMPTION: that they were first or best in class]`.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: PBM expansion, merger-driven scale and growth targets.
- *Uncomfortable?* Very. It means telling investors growth will dip through the patent cliff.

**Confirmation evidence to seek:**
- Historic returns on first-in-class launches versus follow-on launches.
- Whether outcome-trial data has preceded Halden's biggest commercial successes.
- PBM economic profit versus the pharma business.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** naming top-tier growth as the #1 objective, and stop giving double-digit growth framing to investors.
2. **Stop** expanding the PBM, and freeze new PBM investment until its standalone economic profit is known.
3. **Stop** positioning the COX-2 drug as "the growth story." No promotional claims, target populations or launch scale ahead of outcome evidence.
4. **Stop** funding pipeline compounds that have no stated hypothesis for being first or best in class.
5. **Stop** treating a large merger as a live option to fill the patent gap. This is already the CEO's instinct; make it policy.
6. **Stop** spending on patent-life extensions, reformulations or litigation that add no patient benefit, beyond what protects legitimate rights.
7. **Stop** reporting consolidated revenue growth as the headline metric. Report human-health growth separately from PBM growth.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Patients first; profits follow.
2. Scientific excellence.
3. Evidence before claims.
4. Integrity when the news is bad.

**Core purpose:** to discover medicines that let people live longer and healthier lives.

**BHAG (25-year horizon):** by Year 25, Halden medicines are the proven standard of care, backed by major-outcome evidence, in ten major chronic diseases.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A board drug-safety committee with power to halt promotion.
- A rule that broad promotion follows outcome data.
- Disease-area teams that own both evidence and economics.
- A scientist-leader succession bench.
- Separate human-health reporting.
