# Good to Great: Corvane Systems, Inc. (as of late Year 0)

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Corvane turns semiconductor know-how into compact, reliable digital control and processing systems for machines that cannot fail, and puts capital only where that earns a high return on capital per business unit.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** the step from winning contracts to earning a high return on capital per unit. No unit-level return data exists. Growth is currently paid for with **stock and acquisitions**, not with earned cash.

**Top 3 stop-doing items:**
1. Stop acquiring outside digital, solid-state control systems. No unrelated industries.
2. Stop paying with stock unless the deal raises earnings per diluted share within 12 months.
3. Stop competing head-on in commodity semiconductors and large prime-contractor systems against giants, including Rival L.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Corvane made about five acquisitions in its first year and plans many more. It is doing this without knowing which of its units earn their cost of capital, while one brilliant founder decides where all the money goes. The "good" so far is mostly bought, not proven.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
1. Return on capital by unit.
2. Acquisition prices and how much dilution each caused.
3. Customer concentration.
4. Margins on production work versus study contracts.
5. Who runs each acquired unit.

- **(a)** There is no unit-level economic data.
- **(b)** Strategy and capital are concentrated in one founder whose stated aim is breadth, which runs against the narrowing that greatness requires.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is not a sprawling collection of acquisitions. It is the company critical-systems builders call first when they need digital intelligence that is small, rugged and reliable. It started in defense: navigation, fire direction, satellite data processing. It then carried the same solid-state core into civilian control markets.

Each business unit is run by an accountable general manager. Each unit reports its return on capital monthly. Capital flows only to units and acquisitions that clear a written hurdle. Stock is issued rarely, and only when the deal raises per-share earnings.

The founders' brilliance is turned into a system: written capital-allocation rules, a bench of unit leaders, and a named successor. As a result, the company keeps compounding after they step back.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring outside the Hedgehog Concept.** No unrelated industries, however cheap or exciting.
2. **Stop issuing stock for deals** unless the deal raises earnings per diluted share within 12 months and clears the return hurdle.
3. **Stop bidding against giants** as a prime systems integrator, or in commodity semiconductors. The devices division serves internal systems and specialty niches.
4. **Stop taking study contracts with no path to production** or to the core (some air-defense and ASW studies, possibly antenna design work) `[ASSUMPTION: depends on margin data]`.
5. **Stop open-ended funding of experimental projects.** The learning computer gets a fixed, capped R&D allocation with a yearly kill-or-continue review.
6. **Stop measuring progress by sales, headcount or deal count.** Measure return per unit and earnings per share.
7. **Stop making capital decisions with no written criteria.** Brilliance in one head is not a system.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Monthly unit P&L and return-on-capital report to the founders and the board.
- A written hurdle for every acquisition, plus a blameless post-mortem at 12 months.
- Production delivery on schedule before any new bets.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Freedom:** unit GMs own operations, hiring, engineering and pricing.
- **Centralized:** capital, scope, acquisitions and share issuance.

**Truth climate and charisma check:** a founder who is "widely regarded as exceptionally brilliant" and decides all capital is exactly the profile where bad news gets softened before it reaches him. Three red-flag mechanisms would counter this:
- Unit GMs report return on capital directly to the board.
- Every acquisition gets a 12-month post-mortem with the original projections shown next to actuals.
- An independent director chairs acquisition review.

**Faith statement:** Corvane has founders who already ran exactly this kind of business at scale, a $10M backlog (about 2.2× sales), real production contracts and a public currency. If it compounds only where returns are proven, it will prevail.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Engineering truth over salesmanship.
2. Shareholders' capital is to be respected: every dollar must earn its return.
3. Reliability for missions that cannot fail.
4. Ownership pushed down: GMs own outcomes.

**Core purpose:** to put dependable digital intelligence into the machines the world relies on.

**BHAG (about 20 years):** become the indispensable supplier of solid-state control intelligence for critical systems, defense and civilian, with more than half of profit coming from non-defense customers and top-tier long-run growth in value per share.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A written capital-allocation charter.
- An acquisition committee chaired by an independent director.
- A monthly management information system on unit returns.
- A GM bench with a named chairman successor.
- An annual review of the hedgehog and the stop-doing list.
