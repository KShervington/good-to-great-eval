# Corvane Systems, Inc.: Good to Great run (as of late Year 0)

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Corvane wins where solid-state digital technology is the decisive edge in a niche too small for the giants to defend, and it judges every business, bought or built, by cash returned per dollar of capital.*

`Greatness potential: 20/100`

- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from niche programs to high cash return on capital. Nothing in the dossier shows that any unit earns more than thin government-contract margins. Meanwhile a second loop is already spinning: issue stock, buy a company, report more sales, see a higher stock price. That loop closes on market sentiment, not on cash. It is the doom loop in disguise.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop acquiring outside solid-state digital and control niches. Drop "a wide range of industries."
  2. Stop treating company stock as free currency. Issue it only when the shares are clearly worth less than what you get for them.
  3. Stop counting sales and backlog as success. Report return on capital, unit by unit.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Corvane is about 18 months old. Most of what it is came from five purchases, and one brilliant founder decides where all the capital goes. Its "good" is largely bought, its growth plan is "mainly by buying," and no number in the dossier shows whether the buying creates value.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - return on capital for each acquired unit
  - how much of the $4.5M in sales and the $10M backlog is organic versus acquired
  - acquisition prices and shares issued
  - customer and program concentration
  - the stock's valuation relative to earnings
  - who runs each unit below the founders

If the units earn high returns, Gate 5 firms up. If they earn cost-plus margins, the Hedgehog Concept has no engine and the plan should narrow further.

**Pattern:** 6 of 11 gates are CONDITIONAL, which is more than half. Two shared root problems cause most of them:
- **(a) No operating track record.** There is one year of history, mostly acquired, with no unit-level numbers.
- **(b) Genius concentration.** Strategy, capital and acquisitions all run through one person.

**Evidence basis:** this run used the dossier only. Memory, past-chat search and web research were not available in this session, so no prior baseline exists and the market and competitor facts were not researched. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Corvane is not a conglomerate that owns a bit of everything. It is a decentralized group of small, highly profitable units. Each unit leads a narrow market where solid-state digital electronics gives it a decisive edge:
- rugged field computers
- navigation and control systems
- satellite and communication data processing
- later, industrial and commercial control niches that larger firms ignore

A small headquarters does one thing extremely well. It allocates capital by a single test: cash returned per dollar employed. Sometimes that means building, sometimes buying, sometimes doing nothing. Stock is issued only when it is expensive and bought back when it is cheap. Defense remains a customer but stops being its only lifeline. The system keeps working after the founding chairman steps back, because the capital rules, the unit P&L discipline and the successor bench were built while he was still there.

## 5. Stop-doing list (Gate 6: PASS)

1. **Stop acquiring outside the Hedgehog Concept.** No deals in unrelated industries, however cheap. Retire the phrase "a wide range of industries."
2. **Stop paying with stock unless the shares are clearly overvalued relative to what Corvane receives.** Every stock-funded deal must be judged on per-share value, not total size.
3. **Stop reporting sales and backlog as the headline.** Backlog is not profit. Lead with return on capital and earnings per share.
4. **Stop bidding head-to-head against the giants for prime defense programs.** Corvane should win as the specialist subcontractor or niche supplier.
5. **Stop treating the devices division as a commodity semiconductor maker.** Its job is to give Corvane's own systems a solid-state edge, plus selective niche sales.
6. **Stop self-funding blue-sky research.** That includes the learning computer unless an outside party pays for it and it maps to a niche.
7. **Stop sending every operating decision up to the chairman.** HQ owns capital. Units own operations.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every capital decision gets a written return-per-dollar case and a post-deal check after 12 months.
- Every unit has its own monthly P&L and balance sheet.
- Nothing integrates or closes without the board's capital review.

**Framework boundaries:**
- **Unit general managers have full freedom** over operations, hiring, pricing and bids inside their niche.
- **HQ alone** decides capital, acquisitions, stock issuance and entry into new markets.

**Bureaucracy check:** if HQ starts adding approval layers over unit operations, that is a sign the wrong general managers are in place. Fix the people, not the process.

**Fanatical consistency:** the same return test, applied the same way, to every dollar, every time.
