# Good to Great: Keystone Mortgage Association

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Be the best in the world at pricing mortgage risk, both interest-rate and credit, and moving it to whoever should hold it, earning profit per unit of mortgage risk managed, so more households can afford a home.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** credit-risk pricing. Keystone is fixing its interest-rate problem by issuing mortgage-backed securities (MBS), swapping loans for securities and buying adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs). Each of those moves turns interest-rate risk into credit risk. Keystone has no credit-loss data by loan type, and the audit office already says its foreclosure exposure has probably risen.

**Top three things to stop:**
1. Funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt.
2. Growing guarantee volume before credit risk is priced by loan type.
3. Treating the Treasury-backed funding advantage as if it were profit or skill.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** margins have been negative since Year −3, and nobody, including the dossier, knows Keystone's **net worth at market value**. It could be insolvent on a marked-to-market basis right now.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Net worth at market value. If deeply negative, Gate 1 becomes a FAIL and this turns into a survival plan.
- Portfolio size, yield and cost of funds. These set the time to breakeven.
- Credit losses by loan type. These confirm or kill the "best at credit pricing" circle.
- The current daily loss rate.

Greatness potential: 35/100

*Why 35:* the path to *becoming* great is visible and partly under way. *Staying* great is the hard part. Keystone's edge rests on a government backstop that rewards growth and hides risk, and its overseers already get selective data. Those are decline conditions built into the structure, not mistakes that will fix themselves.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone is no longer a giant savings-and-loan that bets its life on interest rates. It is the country's most trusted machine for **understanding, pricing and distributing mortgage risk**. It:
- buys or swaps loans from thousands of lenders;
- prices each loan for both interest-rate and credit risk, using the largest body of mortgage-performance data anywhere;
- keeps only the risk it can match-fund and capitalize;
- passes the rest to investors through securities it guarantees for a properly priced fee.

Its earnings stay steady whichever way rates move. It reports its market-value net worth and its rate-risk gap every quarter, without being asked. It meets its housing mission through priced, disclosed programs, not hidden subsidies. Its outperformance would hold up even if the Treasury line disappeared. That last test separates a great company from a well-connected one.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** Be the best in the world at pricing mortgage risk and moving it to whoever should hold it, earning profit per unit of mortgage risk managed, so more households can afford a home.

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Largest buyer or holder; cheapest funder; turning mortgages into securities for capital markets; credit pricing from the largest dataset; apartment lending; loan origination | **Turning mortgages into securities and debt for capital markets**, plus **mortgage credit-risk pricing** (potential only, not yet proven) | *Size* is not a skill, and holding loans is what is killing the company. *Cheap funding* comes from the government, not from merit. *Apartment lending* is commercial credit, a different craft. *Origination* belongs to lenders, and the charter points elsewhere. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per $ of portfolio; profit per loan bought; guarantee fee per $; **profit per unit of mortgage risk managed** | **Profit per unit of mortgage risk managed** (rate and credit, risk-weighted, across loans owned and guaranteed) | Profit per $ of portfolio rewards size, which is how the company got into this hole. Profit per loan rewards volume. |
| **Passion** | Housing affordability; capital-markets craft; institutional size | **Closing the affordability gap**, which is the CEO's stated career-long concern. Staff identifying with the housing mission is `[ASSUMPTION]`. | Size and prestige are a passion trap for a government-sponsored company. |

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. Keystone's best moments were supplying liquidity in credit squeezes, which means moving risk capacity to where it was needed. Its losses came from holding rate risk it could not hedge.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: buy-and-hold carry, betting on rate direction, growth for its own sake, and early expansion into apartment lending.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Yes. It means a smaller balance sheet, an end to "largest buyer" as a point of pride, and loss data published in front of a hostile overseer.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. New business earns a positive spread while the rate-risk gap stays inside the board limit.
2. Realized credit losses by loan type stay below guarantee fees.
3. Profit per unit of risk rises even while the share of loans held in portfolio falls.
4. Lenders choose Keystone's swap program over keeping the loans themselves. Rival G is the bellwether.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Precise pricing of rate + credit risk, loan by loan
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │     2. Match-funded debt & guaranteed MBS at near-government cost
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │     3. Cheaper, steadier mortgage money for lenders (incl. in credit squeezes)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │     4. More loan volume & data, incl. moderate-income loans (mission delivered)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │     5. PROFIT PER UNIT OF MORTGAGE RISK  ← denominator
 │            │
 │            ▼
 └──── 6. Retained capital + credibility (rating, investors, regulator, public license)
```

**Current push, the next two turns:**
1. **Link 2, interest-rate risk.** Decide what to do with the remaining fixed-rate book. Match-fund each block with longer or callable debt, or securitize or sell it. Under a board limit, nothing stays funded short by default.
2. **Link 1, credit pricing.** Build loss tables by loan type, covering fixed-rate, ARM, swapped pools and apartment loans. Price guarantee fees from those tables **before** volume grows any further.

**Doom-loop check:**
- **Warning sign: several new directions in about three years.** Keystone has made a $10B sale and launched ARMs, MBS, a swap program and apartment loans. The first four fit the Hedgehog Concept. Apartment loans are the outlier.
- **Fine: the $10B sale.** It was a one-time cleanup, not a program meant to jump-start momentum.
- **Risk: reacting without understanding.** Keystone is shifting risk from rates to credit faster than it is building the means to measure credit risk.
- **Verdict:** the loop closes, but the company is close to a doom loop until link 1 exists.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term debt.
2. Keeping loans by default. If a loan can't be match-funded and capitalized, securitize, swap or sell it.
3. Growing guarantee or MBS volume before credit risk is priced by loan type.
4. Pricing for volume or market share on the strength of Treasury-backed funding. Treating that subsidy as profit.
5. Expanding apartment lending beyond pilot size until a commercial-credit model and loss history exist.
6. Positioning the portfolio on forecasts of where rates are going.
7. Giving auditors and the regulator only the data Keystone chooses.
8. Measuring success by portfolio size or by "largest buyer" status.
9. Delivering mission goals through below-cost pricing buried in the books. Price them, disclose them, and report them on the mission scorecard.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make homeownership reachable by lowering the cost and raising the availability of mortgage money, in good markets and bad.

**Core values:**
1. **Never bet the company on rates.**
2. **Price risk honestly.** Every loan, every fee, every mission program.
3. **Tell overseers and markets the truth first**, including market value.
4. **Widen the door to homeownership**, within what the balance sheet can safely carry.

**BHAG (15–20 years):** make mortgage money as cheap and available in a credit squeeze as in a boom for every qualified household in the country, while keeping enough capital that the Treasury line is never needed.

**Mechanisms that work without the current CEO:**
- a board-set limit on the rate-risk gap;
- an independent chief credit officer;
- guarantee fees set by models, by loan type;
- quarterly public disclosure of market-value net worth;
- full data access for the audit office;
- a capital standard measured at market value and set above any regulatory minimum;
- a successor bench.
