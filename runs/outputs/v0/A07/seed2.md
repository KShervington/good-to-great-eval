# Good to Great: Keystone Mortgage Association

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Keystone becomes the best in the world at measuring, pricing and spreading residential mortgage risk through the capital markets. It earns its return on each unit of risk it chooses to keep, not on each mortgage it buys, so that more families, including those the market overlooks, can afford a home.

**Weakest flywheel link:** *Risk measurement.* The flywheel depends on Keystone knowing the rate and credit risk of what it holds and guarantees better than anyone else does. Today it lacks credit-loss rates by loan type and a market-value net worth figure. Its overseer also says it cannot see the full picture.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop funding long fixed-rate mortgages with shorter debt. Every new fixed-rate purchase must have matched funding or a hedge.
2. Stop measuring success by purchase volume or market share.
3. Stop guaranteeing securities, or expanding apartment lending, before there is a credit-loss price for that risk.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The old business model was an unhedged bet on interest rates, and the bet was lost. Margins have been negative since Year −3, and the company is still not steadily profitable. Its market-value net worth is probably negative `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- **Market-value net worth.** If it is deeply negative, this becomes a survival case and Gate 1 fails.
- **Portfolio size, average yield and cost of funds.** These set the size of the duration gap and how fast the losses run off.
- **Credit-loss rates by loan type.** These decide whether the risk-pricing Hedgehog is real or aspirational.
- **Recent annual net income.**

Greatness potential: 38/100

*Why 38:* The franchise, the funding advantage and the new direction are strong. Keystone could plausibly become great. Staying great is the harder test. Several features push toward leverage and away from candor: the government backstop, the dual mandate to shareholders and the public, and oversight that runs on data the company chooses to share. Those are the "Mighty Fall" conditions. The plan below aims at them directly, but they are structural.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Keystone no longer bets the company on where interest rates go. It runs a matched-funded balance sheet and moves most mortgage risk to investors through guaranteed securities. It keeps only the risk it can price better than anyone else, and it is paid properly for that risk. Its edge lies in two places. The first is cheap, reliable funding from its agency status. The second, which it must build, is the deepest loan-level knowledge of mortgage risk in the country. That knowledge lets it offer lenders the best price and reach lower-income and higher-risk borrowers profitably rather than as a mission tax. It reports its true market-value position monthly, opens its books to overseers, and holds capital for stress conditions rather than for normal ones. If it disappeared, lenders across the country would lose their most dependable source of mortgage money in a credit squeeze. That would leave a hole no thrift or rival agency could easily fill.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Narrowed to |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) Buying and holding mortgages. *Rejected: anyone with cheap money can do it, and holding is exactly what failed.* (b) Lowest-cost mortgage funding. *Real, but granted by the charter, not earned, and politically revocable.* (c) Affordable-housing lending. *A purpose, not a capability.* (d) Measuring, pricing and distributing mortgage risk through capital markets. | **(d)**, built on top of (b). Only Keystone combines a national loan flow, agency funding and a securities guarantee. |
| **Cannot be best at** | Originating loans (barred by charter). Holding unhedged fixed-rate risk. Commercial real estate. Anything outside residential mortgages. | These are ruled out. |
| **Economic engine** | Profit per mortgage bought; profit per dollar of portfolio; fee income per security; **profit per unit of mortgage risk retained** | **Denominator: risk-adjusted profit per unit of mortgage risk (rate plus credit) kept on the books** |
| **Passion** | Market share; a quasi-public institution's prestige; **widening access to homeownership** | **Closing the gap between households that can afford a home and those that cannot.** This matches the CEO's career-long view and the charter's mission. |

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Yes. Keystone was most valuable in credit squeezes, which is when funding access and willingness to take measured risk matter most. It also explains the losses: Keystone took rate risk it never priced.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes. It rules out unhedged holding, volume as a goal, unpriced guarantees and unproven new lines.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means a smaller retained portfolio, slower reported growth in the near term, and risk-based pricing that may draw political fire.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
- Guarantee fees cover realized credit losses by loan tier.
- New-business spread stays positive after hedging costs.
- The duration gap shrinks while purchase volume holds.
- Lower-income loan losses come in within priced ranges.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌──► 1. Low-cost, MATCHED funding
 │       (agency status + longer/callable debt + MBS)
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    2. Best prices to lenders (purchases and swaps)
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    3. More loan volume and loan-level DATA from lenders nationwide
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    4. Superior risk measurement (rate and credit, by loan type)  ◄── WEAKEST LINK
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 │    5. Higher profit per unit of risk retained (DENOMINATOR)
 │       + more creditworthy lower-income borrowers served profitably
 │                    │
 │                    ▼
 └─── 6. Stronger capital, credibility with markets and overseers
```

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Build link 4: market-value reporting, duration-gap measurement and a loan-level credit-loss database.
2. Tighten link 1: match-fund all new fixed-rate purchases, and set a firm policy for the legacy book.

**Doom-loop check:** There is a mild risk. Within three years Keystone sold $10 billion of mortgages and launched ARMs, MBS, a swap program and apartment lending. The first four are coherent turns of one flywheel, a shift from holding risk to managing it. Apartment lending is the outlier: a new line launched before the risk-measurement link exists. Watch for any further "new thing" that jump-starts volume without strengthening link 4.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** funding long fixed-rate mortgages with short-term notes.
2. **Stop** buying fixed-rate mortgages without matched-duration funding or a hedge.
3. **Stop** measuring purchase volume and market share as success. Measure profit per unit of risk.
4. **Stop** guaranteeing securities on loan types with no credit-loss history or price.
5. **Stop** expanding apartment-building lending beyond a capped pilot until credit models are proven.
6. **Stop** letting the company choose what overseers see. Offer full, standardized data access.
7. **Stop** treating the Treasury line as if it were capital. Leverage must be justified by stress tests, not by the backstop.
8. **Stop** keeping mission and profit on separate ledgers. Affordable-housing purchases get risk-priced and tracked inside the same scorecard.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every asset carries a duration and credit stress figure before purchase.
- Market-value net worth is reported monthly.
- Funding is matched to the assets it supports.
- Every guarantee has a price.

**Framework boundaries:** Business units have freedom on product design, lender relationships and volume, within hard limits on the duration gap, credit tier exposure and capital-at-stress.

**Bureaucracy check:** Regulator approvals are external and unavoidable. Inside the company, watch for approval layers that exist because risk managers aren't trusted. Fix those by filling the missing seats, not by adding sign-offs.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** To bring the money of the capital markets to every neighborhood lender, so that more families can own a home.

**Core values:**
1. **Truth about risk.** Book what is real, at market value, and show it.
2. **Widening access** to homeownership.
3. **Discipline over volume.**
4. **Stewardship of public trust.** Keystone's privileges are borrowed, not owned.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** Close the affordability gap: make sound mortgage financing available to every creditworthy household in the country, including those the market now overlooks, without ever needing the Treasury backstop.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Market-value reporting.
- An independent risk function.
- Capital rules based on stress tests.
- Executive pay tied to risk-adjusted return, not earnings growth.
- A standing data protocol for overseers.
- A formal CEO succession plan.
