## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Ten years from now, Annika is the sonographer the regional adult congenital clinic, and possibly the university program, builds its imaging around. She does the Fontan, tetralogy and other complex repaired-heart studies with 3D and TEE support. She wrote the protocols, and she has trained at least two colleagues and a stream of students to do them to her standard. Routine adult volume is a minority of her week, not the bulk of it. Because she built protocols and successors, the clinic would still keep going.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The by-name requests, the protocol, the registry, and her love of mentoring all fit.
- *Rules things out?* Yes. It rules out the travel contract, the lab lead role as written, and routine overtime.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It means turning down $2,850 a week, asking for things she's never asked for, possibly taking a 70-minute commute for unknown pay, and possibly accepting flat income for a while.

**Verdict: `CONDITIONAL`.** The best-at and passion circles are well evidenced. The engine circle is not. Nothing in the dossier shows that congenital expertise pays more per hour than routine scanning [ASSUMPTION: sonographer pay is often graded by role rather than specialty].
- **Condition:** a written offer or pay band, from either employer, for a congenital or advanced-imaging role that matches or beats her current base and doesn't increase her routine scanning hours.
- **If that test fails:** the engine has to come from the no-scan side (teaching and education roles). The honest fallback is that congenital work becomes her passion niche inside a good career, not the engine of a great one. In that case, look again at the lab lead role. It would cut her transducer hours, even though she wouldn't love it.
