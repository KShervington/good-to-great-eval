## 1. The great version in one paragraph

In three to five years, Annika is the adult congenital imaging specialist her region's cardiologists rely on. She holds a seat built around complex studies: 3D, Fontan and other post-surgical anatomy, and TEE support. That seat is either a congenital or complex lead role at her current hospital or a place on the university team. Routine high-volume transthoracic work is no longer most of her day. Her income is at least what she earns now, but it comes from fewer, harder studies, plus a growing teaching line that doesn't load her shoulder. Protocols she wrote and sonographers she trained mean the region's congenital imaging no longer depends on her arm. The test of greatness is narrow and sharp. If she left, the congenital clinic would lose its best imager and its standard of care. And she can do this work into her fifties.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't sign the 13-week high-volume outpatient travel contract.** It maximizes load on the injured shoulder. It puts her three hours from the clinic that knows her. It may endanger benefits (see Brutal fact 3).
2. **Stop volunteering for or defaulting to routine overtime.** Track each overtime hour against the denominator.
3. **Stop scanning through the shoulder without documentation.** Get a formal assessment and an ergonomic review, and ask employee health how occupational strain injuries are handled.
4. **Stop leaving the LTD opt-out as-is.** It saves about $38 a paycheck while exposing the whole household.
5. **Stop answering "probably not" to the lead conversation.** Counter with the seat she actually wants: a complex/congenital lead, not a scheduler.
6. **Stop self-funding specialist development in silence.** Ask the hospital to fund 3D/TEE training. Ask whether the $1,400 registry cost can be reimbursed or recognized.
7. **Stop pursuing all four options in parallel.** Sequence them: price the university first, then negotiate internally, then decide.
8. **Don't apply to the university blind.** Get the pay band and an introduction before investing commute-scale effort. That's 70 minutes each way, roughly 11–12 hours a week.
