# Bellmoor 250: Good to Great run

## Verdict

**The run stopped at Gate 0 (Fit) with a FAIL.** Bellmoor 250 is a ten-day event delivered by a commission whose charter dissolves it about ninety days after the final audit. The framework needs an ongoing endeavor with a horizon of years so effort can compound, and this subject gets one turn of the flywheel. A Hedgehog and flywheel built for it would look rigorous but change none of the decisions that matter in the next twelve months.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** none was produced, deliberately. One built for a dissolving commission would be decoration.
- **Weakest link:** this is a cash timing problem, not a flywheel problem. Calder Mills owes **$160,000 in April**. The town's cash-flow loan covers the tall-ship and fireworks deposits only until May.
- **Top three things to stop:**
  1. Stop treating **180,000 visits** and **25M impressions** as targets. Neither has a stated basis.
  2. Stop deferring the **$96,000 app**. Decide at the next meeting, and the default answer should be no as proposed.
  3. Stop debating **"Bellmoor Days"** as commission business. The chair is right that it belongs to the council.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Calder's champion, Ed Farrow, has retired and no successor has been named. Calder's parent company is consolidating production, and Bellmoor operations are "under review." The outstanding $160k is 37% of all sponsorship and 12% of the total budget.
- **What would change the answer:** a **permanent steward** with a multi-year horizon. That could be the council chartering a successor heritage body, or the library adopting the oral-history archive as a program. If either happens, re-run the framework on that subject.

Greatness potential: 7/100

*Why so low:* "great" in this skill requires **lasting endurance** beyond one event. By charter, the festival cannot endure. It can be excellently delivered and hand off a few durable legacies. That is a worthy outcome, but by the framework's definition it is "very good." The 7 points reflect the chance that the legacy hand-offs seed a permanent steward who later earns greatness.

## The better-suited method: event delivery and legacy hand-off

The commission needs **project-delivery discipline plus a decommissioning plan**, not a greatness plan.

1. **Critical-path schedule** working back from opening day, with go/no-go gates at the March tall-ship deposit and the May loan expiry.
2. **Cash-flow scenario budget** built on three cases: all pledges arrive; Calder pays late; Calder does not pay the $160k.
3. **A pre-agreed cut list.** Decide now, calmly, what goes first if money falls short, so it is not decided in a panic in April.
4. **Grounded targets** built from capacity and comparables, with a stated method.
5. **Legacy hand-off plan.** Every item called "legacy" gets a named permanent owner, a maintenance budget and a transfer date before dissolution.
6. **Post-event evaluation**, written for whoever runs the next civic event: the council, a successor body, or the 300th commission.

The facts below surfaced during the Gate 0 review. They are given as a starting register for that method, not as framework gates.

### Immediate actions (next 90 days)

1. **Find Calder's new decision-maker this month.**
   - Confirm the April installment in writing.
   - Ask whether it can be brought forward before the March deposit.
   - Ask Ed Farrow, warmly, to introduce the successor before he fully steps away.
   - *Owner:* Chair and ED.
2. **Build the three-scenario cash plan and a ranked cut list.** Agree what is cut if the $160k is late or lost, and decide this before the March deposit is paid. Ask the town now whether the cash-flow loan could extend past May. *Owner:* Treasurer.
3. **Re-derive attendance and media targets with a written method.**
   - Base attendance on site capacity, pageant seats, harbour viewing capacity and the regional catchment.
   - Re-check whether ticket and merchandise projections depend on the 180,000 figure.
   - Replace impressions with outcome measures, such as ticketed sell-through and oral-history interviews completed.
   - *Owner:* Program chair and Communications.
4. **Decide the app at the next meeting.** Do not wait until January. The recommended default is **no as proposed**. Revisit only if all three conditions are met:
   - a permanent owner (the library, a school district or a museum) commits to maintaining it after dissolution;
   - the full $96k is funded outside the existing budget;
   - schedule and ticketing move to an off-the-shelf platform.

   If the walking tour is worth keeping, a lower-cost format the library can maintain is worth pricing `[ASSUMPTION]`.
   *Owner:* Steering committee.
5. **Write the legacy hand-off register.** For the archive, the symphony score and rights, the time capsule, and the volunteer and sponsor relationships, record:
   - the permanent owner;
   - the transfer date;
   - any consent needed (volunteers must opt in before their contact details pass to any future "Bellmoor Days" body).

   Offer this register to the council as the raw material for reframe A, *if* the council chooses to create a permanent body.
   *Owner:* Legacy chair and ED.

### Stop-doing (event scope)

- Stop citing 180,000 and 25M as commitments until each has a method behind it.
- Stop deferring the app. Indecision is costing the studio's time and the youth committee's goodwill.
- Stop discussing Bellmoor Days in commission meetings. Refer it to the council with the hand-off register attached.
- Stop calling items "legacy" unless they have a named permanent owner and a maintenance budget.
- Stop committing to new programme elements until the Calder scenario is resolved.
