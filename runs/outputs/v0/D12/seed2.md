# Bellmoor 250: Good to Great review

## Verdict

**The run stops at Gate 0 (Fit): FAIL.** Bellmoor 250 is a ten-day event delivered by a body whose charter dissolves it 90 days after the final audit. Good to Great is built for endeavors that compound over years, and this one is designed not to continue. A Hedgehog Concept or flywheel written for it would be decoration, so this review doesn't produce one. That's a limit of the method, not a criticism of the commission.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** none, because Gate 0 failed. The nearest useful equivalent is a one-line purpose for the festival (below).
- **Weakest link:** there is no flywheel. The weakest link in the plan is the **cash chain**: Calder's $160k (due April) → the bridge loan's cover ending in May → the tall-ship and fireworks deposits.
- **Top three to stop doing:**
  1. Stop considering the **$96k app** as proposed. It is unbudgeted, and no one would own it after dissolution.
  2. Stop steering by the **180,000 visits and 25M impressions** targets until each has a method behind it.
  3. Stop letting **"Bellmoor Days"** onto the commission's agenda. It belongs to the council.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** about 20% of the budget ($270k) rests on **Calder Mills**. $160k of it is unpaid. Its Bellmoor operations are "under review," and the person who secured the pledge has just retired with no successor named.
- **Missing facts that would change the answer:**
  - A council decision to charter a permanent successor body would make a reframed run possible, with the council as steward.
  - The rest change the risk picture, not the Gate 0 result: the terms of Calder's pledge letter, unrestricted cash and contingency on hand, the payment status of the other 10 sponsors' $161k, and venue capacities for each event.

Greatness potential: 4/100

*Why 4:* the skill's definition of great requires **lasting endurance**, and the charter rules that out for the festival itself. The 4 points reflect pieces that will outlast it because they have permanent owners: the oral-history archive hosted by the library, the symphonic work, and the time capsule. Bellmoor 250 can still be an **excellent** festival. It just can't be "great" in Collins's sense.

## Recommended method instead: a pre-mortem and project risk review

A finite event with fixed deadlines needs project discipline: a cash calendar, contingency plans, decision deadlines and a hand-off plan. Three Collins ideas carry over intact: **confront the brutal facts**, **keep a stop-doing list**, and **put your best person on your biggest risk**. The rest of this section applies them in a project frame. It is not a Good to Great output.

### Facts the commission should confront now

| # | Fact | Evidence (from the dossier) | Severity | Suggested owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One sponsor carries ~20% of the budget, and the relationship has lost its champion | Calder $270k of $1,362,500. $160k still due. Ed Farrow retired; no successor named. | **Critical** | Chair and ED personally |
| 2 | Calder's local future is uncertain | Parent company consolidating at its southern plant over 18 months; Bellmoor operations "under review" | **Critical** | Chair and ED |
| 3 | The cash timeline has a one-month buffer at best | Tall-ship final deposit due March; Calder installment due April; bridge loan covers tall-ship and fireworks deposits only until May | **High** | Treasurer |
| 4 | Concentration risk goes beyond Calder | Calder is 63% of sponsorship pledges. The other 10 sponsors total $161k, and their payment status isn't reported `[ASSUMPTION: mostly unpaid]` | High | Sponsorship chair |
| 5 | The attendance target has no basis | 180,000 visits = 18,000 a day, or 5.7× the town's population of 31,400. It came from a workshop. The only comparison is a larger city's unmethoded "over 100,000." | High | Program chair |
| 6 | Earned revenue depends on that unproven attendance | Tickets $121.5k plus merchandise $70k = $191.5k, or 14% of the budget | Medium–high | Treasurer |
| 7 | The impressions target is unanchored | 25M target. The biggest announcement so far reached 1.4M, so the target needs roughly 18 announcements of that size. | Medium | Communications |
| 8 | The app is unbudgeted and would have no owner after dissolution | $96k quote minus $20k offered = $76k gap, about 5.6% of the budget. The commission and all staff end after the audit. | Medium (high if approved) | Steering committee |
| 9 | Volunteers are still short | 410 of 600. Unclear whether the pageant's cast of 300 counts toward this. | Low–medium | Volunteers chair |

**Faith statement:** Bellmoor 250 will succeed if it is sized to its confirmed money and anchored by its strongest real assets. Those are a program already well advanced (symphony movements delivered, tall ship booked), a library-hosted archive that will outlast everyone in the room, and 410 people who have already signed up.

### Actions, in priority order

1. **Resolve Calder now, not in April.** The chair and ED should request a meeting with Calder's site leadership and, if possible, the parent company's community-relations function. Ask for one of three things: written confirmation of the $160k, early payment, or an honest early signal. Check whether the pledge letter is binding `[ASSUMPTION: terms unknown]`.
2. **Build a "Calder-out" budget.** Draft a version of the festival that works without the $160k, and decide in advance what gets cut first. Ask the town now whether the bridge loan's coverage can extend past May.
3. **Build a month-by-month cash calendar to the final audit.** Track commitments against confirmed receipts, not pledges.
4. **Re-base the two unanchored targets.**
   - Attendance: venue capacity × sessions × an expected fill rate, counted separately for each event.
   - Impressions: a tracked figure per announcement.
   - Stress-test the ticket and merchandise projections at 60% of plan `[ASSUMPTION: scenario, not forecast]`.
   - Keep the old numbers as aspirations if the commission likes them, but don't budget against them.
5. **Decide the app in January, using two tests.**
   - *Is it fully funded from outside the existing budget?*
   - *Does a body that outlasts the commission (for example, the library or the town) agree in writing to own, host and maintain it?*
   
   If either answer is no, decline the app as proposed. An augmented-reality app with no maintainer will stop working within a few platform updates, so it won't serve as a legacy. The survey shows intent: 72% of 640 students said they would use it. The commission could meet that interest more cheaply with something the library can maintain, such as QR-linked clips from the oral histories at historic sites `[ASSUMPTION: feasibility and cost unknown]`.
6. **Treat legacy as a hand-off problem.** Finish the oral histories (141 of 240 recorded), since that is the commission's surest lasting contribution. Assign written ownership of the symphonic work's rights and the time capsule's custody to bodies that will outlast the commission.
7. **If the council wants Bellmoor Days, hand it a package, not a promise.** Prepare a hand-off file to deliver at dissolution: staging inventory, sponsor contacts, the volunteer list (with consent to share it), real attendance data and lessons learned. Don't run it or plan it.

### Stop-doing list (project version)

1. Stop deferring the app. Decide in January using the two tests above.
2. Stop budgeting against 180,000 visits and 25M impressions.
3. Stop counting pledges as cash. Track only receipts.
4. Stop adding scope of any kind until Calder is resolved.
5. Stop letting Bellmoor Days take agenda time. Refer it to the council.
6. Stop letting the Hedgehog chapter be the next meeting's homework at the expense of the Calder risk. The chair's instinct toward rigor is right; this is where to point it.

### Who should own what (the one "First Who" lesson that applies)

The commission's most credible person, its chair, should personally own its biggest risk: the Calder relationship. The Calder relationship has no named owner on either side right now. Name one on the commission's side this month, and ask Calder to name one on theirs.
