# Bellmoor 250: Good to Great run

## Verdict

**Result: the run stopped at Gate 0 (Fit). The framework can't make Bellmoor 250 great in Collins's sense, because the subject is designed to end.** The commission dissolves ninety days after its final audit, all four staff contracts end with it, and the festival happens once. Collins's machinery assumes a body that is still around in years 2, 5 and 10. That machinery is the flywheel, the Hedgehog Concept that took years to find, and the test of lasting endurance. Bellmoor 250 can still be **excellent**: delivered well, on budget, with legacy pieces handed to permanent owners. That calls for a different method. It is set out below and starts on the facts the fit check turned up.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** none produced. A one-time event has no repeating engine to find a denominator for.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the loop never closes. There is no second turn, so momentum built in June has nowhere to go once the commission dissolves.
- **Top three things to stop:**
  1. Stop treating 180,000 visits as a target. It has no stated method and equals 5.7 visits per resident.
  2. Stop deferring the $96,000 app. Decide at the next meeting, and default to no as a commission project.
  3. Stop calling anything "legacy" unless a permanent institution has agreed to own it.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** about 63% of pledged sponsorship ($270k of $431k) depends on Calder Mills. Calder's relationship-holder has just retired, its Bellmoor operations are "under review," and **$160k (about 12% of the whole budget) is due in April**. That is after the tall-ship final deposit in March and only a month before the cash-flow loan's cover ends in May.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - *Gate 0 verdict:* only a council decision to create a permanent steward (for example, "Bellmoor Days") would change it, and that would be a different subject.
  - *Delivery picture:* written confirmation of Calder's April installment; how much of the other $161k in pledges has actually been received; whether the town and state funds are in hand; the deposit amounts and their refund terms; venue capacities per session.

`Greatness potential: 5/100`

*Why 5:* the skill defines "great" as including lasting endurance beyond one product or lucky break, and this subject's charter rules that out. The 5 reflects the one route to endurance: the oral-history archive and other legacy assets carried on by permanent stewards such as the library. That would be their greatness, not the commission's.

### Why not run the gates anyway

The chair asked for "the framework, properly," and running it properly means stopping here. Forcing the remaining gates would produce theatre:

- a flywheel that turns once;
- a 10–30-year BHAG for a body with a dissolution date;
- "clock-building" mechanisms with nobody to keep the clock.

The most Collins-like act available is to face the brutal fact that this is the wrong tool for this job. Two of his habits do carry over to a one-time event, because they don't depend on compounding:

- relentless honesty about the facts;
- a disciplined list of things to stop doing.

Both appear below in delivery form.

## What fits instead

Use a **delivery-risk method**:

1. **Pre-mortem.** Assume it's July after the festival and Bellmoor 250 went badly. List why.
2. **Scenario budget.** Model Calder at 100%, 50% and 0%, and attendance at low, mid and high.
3. **Cash-gated critical path.** Map every irreversible commitment against when its funding is confirmed.
4. **Decision log.** Record each open decision with a named owner and a hard date.
5. **Legacy handover plan.** Give each lasting asset a named permanent owner.

Judge success by delivery against a sound plan, not by enduring greatness.

## Next 90 days

These are delivery actions, not flywheel pushes.

1. **Secure Calder before anything irreversible.** Owners: Chair + Sponsorship chair.
   - Ask for a meeting with Calder's senior local leadership and, if possible, the parent company, not just a future community-relations successor.
   - Ask for written confirmation of the April $160k, or for it to be paid early, ahead of the March tall-ship deposit.
   - Find out whether the money comes from the local budget, which is under review, or from the parent.
2. **Build a three-scenario budget and a cash-gated calendar.** Owners: Treasurer + ED.
   - Model Calder at 100%, 50% and 0%.
   - List every deposit with its date, amount and refund terms.
   - Pre-agree a cut order: what scales down first if $160k disappears.
   - Ask the town whether the cash-flow loan can be extended beyond May if needed.
3. **Re-baseline attendance from the bottom up.** Owners: Program chair + ED.
   - Estimate capacity × sessions for each venue and event.
   - Separate unique attendees from visits.
   - Re-derive the ticket and merchandise projections from that estimate.
   - Publish the method, so the number can survive challenge.
4. **Decide the app at the next meeting.** Owner: Steering committee.
   - Lean: **no, as a commission project.**
   - Revisit only if a permanent institution (the library, school district or town) commits in writing to own and maintain it, and outside money fills the $76k gap without touching the core budget.
   - Schedule and ticketing can run on an existing vendor `[ASSUMPTION]`.
   - Tell the youth chair the decision and the reasons directly. The students' enthusiasm is real even if the app isn't the right vehicle; one option is to channel it into the oral-history interviews.
5. **Turn "legacy" into a handover list.** Owners: Legacy chair + ED.
   - For each asset (archive, time capsule, symphony score and performance rights, pageant materials, volunteer list), name a permanent owner and get their written acceptance.
   - Reply to the councillor that "Bellmoor Days" is a council decision. Offer a handover package for the council to use if it chooses.
   - The volunteer list should only be passed on with volunteers' consent.
