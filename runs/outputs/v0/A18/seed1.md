# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
*Duplicon is the best partner in the world for the independent quick-print shop. It supplies the press, the platemaking, the consumables and the service that make short-run printing profitable, and it earns money on profit per active installed press.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** the step from installed press to recurring consumables and service profit. Nobody knows whether Duplicon captures supplies revenue on its own installed base, or whether the quick-print base is still growing. If it doesn't, or the base is shrinking, the flywheel has no engine.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop any plan to become a leader in electronic office systems or plain-paper copying against much larger firms. Rival S's point-of-sale write-off this year shows how that bet ends.
2. Stop investing in new plate-addressing technology. Run addressing for cash and service only.
3. Stop selling duplicators into general offices, where copiers are taking short runs. Focus on print shops.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** both founding franchises are being displaced at once. Plain-paper copiers are replacing duplicating, and computers are replacing plate addressing. Duplicon leads neither replacement technology, and the available evidence cannot show which of its 27 subsidiaries still earn their capital.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by line. These decide whether quick-print or card/embossing is the true hedgehog.
- Quick-print installed-base trend and consumables capture rate. If shops are abandoning offset, the hedgehog fails.
- Credit-card business share and margin.
- How fast addressing is declining. This sets the harvest pace.
- CEO background. A leader set on transformation through big programs is the biggest single risk.

Greatness potential: 15/100

*Why 15:* the disciplined plan gives Duplicon a real chance to become a smaller, solidly profitable niche leader. "Great" means sustained outperformance, though, and the company is starting from a peak six years past, a slowdown dating to the late 1960s, and two core franchises under attack from technologies it doesn't lead. The realistic best case is "very good and durable in a niche." Great is possible but unlikely.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Duplicon is smaller and sharper than today's company. It is the default choice for anyone opening or running a quick-print shop: the press they buy, the platemaking and prepress they rely on, the consumables they reorder every week, and the service network that keeps them running. It makes money on the installed base, not the box: every active press produces steady consumables and service profit, and that profit funds a constant stream of improvements aimed only at the shop's economics. Addressing has been run down deliberately for cash, and lines outside the hedgehog have been sold to owners who can do better with them. Duplicon has also declined, publicly and calmly, to fight Rival X in copiers or the giants in the electronic office. It remains great because its ability to serve shops is built into dealer, training and supplies systems, not into one leader or one product.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The company's clearest recent success is one offset model becoming the standard in quick print.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: copiers, the electronic office, point-of-sale, new addressing technology, engineering graphics, and general-office duplicating.
- *Is it uncomfortable?* Very. It means shrinking a company with 27 subsidiaries and walking away from its founding addressing franchise as a growth business.

**Confirmation evidence (needed within 90 days):**
- Quick-print or offset is the line with the highest return on capital and a stable or growing installed base.
- Duplicon's share of consumables and service on its own presses is high, or can be raised.
- New shop openings are not dropping offset for copiers at the volumes shops actually print `[ASSUMPTION: offset still wins per copy on longer short runs]`.

**Disconfirming evidence:** if card and embossing equipment shows best-in-world share and better economics, it becomes the hedgehog instead, and quick-print gets reassessed. Do not try to build both as growth businesses.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop pursuing leadership in electronic office systems against much larger firms.
2. Stop any move into plain-paper copier manufacturing. If shops want copiers, partner or resell; don't build.
3. Stop new-product investment in plate addressing. Service and harvest only.
4. Stop marketing duplicators to general offices for short-run copying.
5. Stop funding engineering graphics and other subsidiaries outside the hedgehog. Sell them.
6. Stop point-of-sale or retail-systems ideas entirely, given Rival S's lesson.
7. Stop acquisitions until the hedgehog is confirmed.
8. Stop reporting only consolidated results. Every line reports profit and return on capital.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** every capital request names its effect on profit per active press; monthly line-level economics; installed-base and consumables-capture tracking.

**Framework boundaries:** division heads have full freedom inside "makes quick-print shops more profitable." Anything outside that boundary needs a board-level case.

**Bureaucracy check:** a holding structure of 27 subsidiaries probably carries coordination overhead that exists to manage sprawl, not results `[ASSUMPTION]`. Collapse it as lines are sold.
