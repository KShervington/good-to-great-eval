## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Duplicon is smaller, and proud of it. It is the system most of the world's quick-print shops are built on. It supplies the composition equipment that sets the job, the platemaker that turns an original into a plate in minutes, the press that runs it, and the plates, inks, masters and service that keep it running. It earns a steady annuity on every press in the field. It spends its R&D on one question: *how does a shop produce a quality short run faster and cheaper than any alternative, including a copier?* Addressing is run down openly as a cash source that funds this work. Peripheral subsidiaries have been sold. Management measures profit per installed press, not total sales. The company does not chase the electronic-office headline and does not have a write-off like Rival S's. Note that great here means *distinctive and enduring in a narrow world*, not big.

**Challenger Hedgehog Concept (H2):** *Best in the world at embossing identity onto plates and cards, earning profit per card.* This is the common thread between the founding addressing franchise and the post-Depression credit-card business. It would win if line-level data shows the card business growing and profitable, and quick-print supplies margins turn out weak.

**Tests of H1:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. The most popular duplicator made Duplicon a quick-print leader.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: electronic office, copiers, engineering graphics, addressing growth, and possibly cards.
- *Uncomfortable?* Very. It means shrinking a company of about $400M toward its strongest niche.

**Confirmation evidence:**
1. Quick-print equipment plus supplies earns above the cost of capital.
2. Supplies and service make up a large and stable share of that profit.
3. Field data shows offset still beats copying above a definable run length, and that threshold is not collapsing.

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.** The intersection is plausible, but the engine is unverified.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** any heavy investment aimed at leading new electronic office technology against much larger firms.
2. **Stop** competing head-on in plain-paper office copiers.
3. **Stop** growth investment in embossed-plate addressing. Harvest it: service the installed base, price for cash, and plan an orderly decline.
4. **Stop** operating engineering graphics as a core business, and explore a sale.
5. **Stop** holding subsidiaries that lack line-level profit accountability. Each one either fits the Hedgehog Concept or is divested.
6. **Stop** marketing duplicators to general offices as a copying solution. That is the segment copiers are winning.
7. **Stop** measuring success by total sales or size. Measure profit per installed press.
8. **Stop** leaving the card business half-committed. Either make it the Hedgehog Concept (H2) or sell it.
