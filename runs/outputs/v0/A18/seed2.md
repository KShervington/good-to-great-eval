# Duplicon Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Duplicon is the best partner in the world for the small commercial and in-plant printer. It makes money on the supplies and service flowing through each installed press, and it walks away from everything else.*

- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** the claim that profit per installed press (supplies plus service) is large, defensible and not eroding. No one has shown this yet. If generic ink and paper suppliers take the consumables stream, the loop doesn't close.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. No heavy bet to lead "the electronic office" against much larger firms. Rival S's point-of-sale write-off this year shows what that path costs.
  2. No new product line meant to compete head-on with Rival X in plain-paper copying.
  3. No more investment to grow plate-based addressing. Harvest it for cash instead.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the founding franchise (embossed-plate addressing) and much of the duplicating line rest on mechanical, ink-based technology that the office market is already replacing. Duplicon does not lead either replacement technology.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Current profit and return on capital by line and by subsidiary.
  - Duplicon's share of quick-print press placements.
  - Supplies and service revenue per installed press, and its trend.
  - How fast computers are retiring plate addressers in large organizations.
  - Card-embossing volumes and growth, which could promote Candidate B below.

`Greatness potential: 15/100`

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Duplicon is smaller than today's and earns more on each dollar of capital. It has stopped trying to be an office-equipment company. It runs on one idea: keeping the world's quick-print shops and in-plant print rooms profitable. Its presses are the default choice when a shop opens. Its plates, inks, masters and service contracts bring in recurring margin on every installed machine. That margin funds steady, unglamorous improvements to press productivity and prepress (platemaking and simple typesetting for the shop). Addressing is run for cash and wound down deliberately. Engineering graphics and the other peripheral subsidiaries are sold to owners who value them more. The company does not try to beat Rival X at copying. Instead it sits where offset still beats copying, on longer runs, color and paper flexibility, and it gives shop owners the economics to win that business.

**Sentence:** *Duplicon is the best partner in the world for the small commercial and in-plant printer, earning its profit per installed press.*

**Intersection tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The most successful product was a duplicator model that made Duplicon a leading name in quick print.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It excludes the electronic-office leap, copiers, growing addressing, and most of the 27 subsidiaries.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It shrinks the revenue base and turns away from the founding franchise.

**Candidate B (test before rejecting):** *"Best in the world at embossed identity: cards and plates, profit per card produced."* This builds on the founding embossing competence and points it at a market that may be growing `[ASSUMPTION]`. If the card business turns out to be larger and higher-return than duplicating, the Hedgehog Concept should flip to B. Don't run both.

**Confirmation evidence:**
1. Supplies and service gross margin per installed press is at least equal to the margin on the machine sale, and it is stable or rising.
2. Duplicon holds the top share position in new quick-print shop press placements.
3. The quick-print segment earns a return on capital above the company average.

Any one of these failing reopens the gate.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Measure link 3.** Build a line-level P&L that isolates supplies and service per installed press. Until that exists, the flywheel is a hypothesis.
2. **Strengthen link 5.** Find where offset still beats plain-paper copying in shop economics (run length, cost per copy, color, stock) and arm dealers and owners with that case.

**Doom-loop check: warning signs already present.**
- **Sprawl:** 27 subsidiaries across five unrelated lines point to past pursuit of more, not momentum on one loop.
- **Pressure to leap:** "invest heavily to become a leader in new electronic office technology" is the textbook doom-loop move, a big program meant to jump-start momentum the core can no longer supply. Rival S's loss this year is a live example.
- **Missing feedback:** growth has slowed since the late 1960s, but the dossier has no current earnings by line. Reacting without understanding is the risk.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop all plans to lead "the electronic office" through heavy investment or acquisition.
2. Stop developing a Duplicon plain-paper copier to fight Rival X head-on. Where customers need copiers, resell or partner.
3. Stop growth spending on plate-based addressing. Run it for cash and service the installed base.
4. Stop owning engineering-graphics reproduction. Divest it.
5. Stop running 27 subsidiaries as a portfolio. Set a deadline to sell or close every unit that doesn't feed profit per installed press (or profit per card, if B wins).
6. Stop measuring success by total sales. The company's peak of more than $400M is a vanity number. Measure profit per installed press and return on capital.
7. Stop making footprint decisions to protect headquarters-city capacity that the narrower business no longer needs `[ASSUMPTION: the main plant is oversized for the core]`.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

- **Core values `[ASSUMPTION, to be confirmed with long-tenured staff]`:**
  1. Machines that run every day without complaint.
  2. The customer's output is our output.
  3. Earn the repeat order.
  4. Tell the truth about the numbers.
- **Core purpose:** *To make professional-quality printing easy and profitable for anyone who prints for others.*
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** *More of the world's small commercial print shops run on Duplicon than on any other maker, and every one of them makes money doing it.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A quarterly profit-per-installed-press review at board level.
  - A standing stop-doing review in the capital-allocation process: new ventures must show how they raise the denominator.
  - Internal succession for division heads.
  - Installed-base census data owned by the CFO, not by sales.
