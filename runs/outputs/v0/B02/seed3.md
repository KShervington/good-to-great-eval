# Homestead Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Homestead can be the best in the world at standardizing, pricing and guaranteeing American home-mortgage credit. It should earn its return per dollar of credit risk carried on its own capital, so that more families, especially low- and moderate-income ones, own homes at lower cost.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** **trust in the guarantee.** Today that trust rests on the market's assumption of government backing, not on Homestead's own capital. The capital surplus is $0.9B on $887B of assets.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop running the company to a public EPS target, and stop tying executive pay and the company-wide option grant to it.
2. Stop using retained-portfolio growth as the earnings lever.
3. Stop leading investors with "operating" earnings. Lead with GAAP, fair value and risk metrics.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** last year's derivative mark-to-market swing was **$1.77B** (operating $6.39B minus GAAP $4.62B). That is about **twice the entire capital cushion above the legal minimum.** Pay rewards growth that consumes the cushion.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Profit split between the guarantee and portfolio businesses.
- Fair value of derivatives and the amortization policies.
- Credit profile of recent purchases by product, especially goal-qualifying loans.
- Losses under a rate-shock and house-price stress.
- Share-repurchase activity.

If the guarantee business does not earn its cost of capital on its own, this Hedgehog fails. In that case the honest answer may be that Homestead can be large and useful, but not great on its own merits.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is the institution that makes the American conforming mortgage cheap, standard and liquid. It wins because it understands mortgage credit better than anyone, with deep data and pricing tied to real loss behavior. It does not win because it can borrow near Treasury rates. It sizes its retained portfolio to market-making and mission needs, not to earnings targets. It holds capital that would survive a severe rate shock and a housing downturn without leaning on the implied guarantee. It reports GAAP and fair-value results first and treats the regulator's warnings as an early-warning system. It meets its affordable-housing goals with loans it has priced, not just counted. Its earnings grow at roughly the pace of the mortgage market plus share gains from better credit service, which is slower than the 15–21% it has been posting. Its standing would survive a change of CEO, a rate cycle and a political fight over its charter.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop publishing EPS targets and forward EPS guidance.** That includes not replacing the $6.46 goal with a new one. Only 2.4% more growth is needed to hit it, so the real danger is the next target.
2. **Stop tying bonuses, long-term awards and the company-wide option grant to EPS.** Tie them to risk-adjusted profit per dollar guaranteed, capital adequacy and credit performance.
3. **Stop growing the retained portfolio faster than the mortgage market.** Stop using it as an earnings lever. Set a cap tied to market-making and mission needs.
4. **Stop leading with "operating" earnings.** Report GAAP, a fair-value balance sheet, the duration gap and rate-shock sensitivity first, with operating figures as a supplement.
5. **Stop running capital about $0.9B above the legal minimum.** Set and publish an internal, stress-based capital target above the statutory floor.
6. **Stop share repurchases until that capital target is met** `[ASSUMPTION: the falling implied share count suggests repurchases]`.
7. **Stop counting goal-qualifying loans you haven't priced.** Report their credit performance separately.
8. **Stop treating critics and the regulator as opponents to beat.** Treat them as a source of brutal facts.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** to make home ownership more attainable and affordable by bringing stable, low-cost capital to American mortgages.

**Core values:**
1. **The guarantee is sacred.** Never promise what our own capital can't back.
2. **Access with prudence.** Expand ownership with credit we understand and have priced.
3. **Truth first.** We report the hardest number first.
4. **Stewardship over growth.** Size is a result, never a goal.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Every conforming American mortgage can be financed at the lowest sustainable cost through a guarantee the market trusts because of Homestead's own capital and record, not because of an assumed government backstop.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent CRO and a board risk committee with limits that bind management.
- A published, stress-based capital target.
- Duration-gap and rate-shock disclosure every month.
- Pay tied to risk-adjusted profit per dollar guaranteed and to credit performance over several years, with clawbacks.
- A Chief Accounting Officer separated from earnings forecasting.
- Blameless after-action reviews for every limit breach.
- Credit-performance reporting for goal-qualifying loans.
