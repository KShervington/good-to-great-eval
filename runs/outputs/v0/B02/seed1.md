# Good to Great: Homestead Mortgage Association

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Homestead becomes great by being the best in the world at standardizing, pricing and guaranteeing the credit risk of sustainable American home loans. It earns a fair fee for each dollar of credit risk it truly understands, backs that risk with real capital, and stops relying on a leveraged, rate-sensitive, subsidy-funded portfolio to grow.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** turning earnings into **retained capital and credible numbers**. Core capital is 3.2% of assets and only $0.9B above the legal minimum. Assets are about 31.6× capital. A loss of about 0.1% of assets would erase the cushion. Growth is being paid out as EPS instead of being kept as strength.

**Top 3 stop-doing items:**
1. Stop running the company to the public EPS-doubling goal, and stop tying pay to EPS.
2. Stop using the retained portfolio as the growth engine. It went from $415B to $798B in five years, and it earns mainly on the funding subsidy.
3. Stop making "operating earnings" the main story. Lead with GAAP and full fair-value disclosure.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** For about five years, earnings growth (≈17%/yr) has moved in step with portfolio growth (≈18%/yr). The "16 straight years of double-digit growth" are mostly balance-sheet expansion on borrowed government credibility. That expansion sits on almost no capital cushion, with pay tied to the EPS number, under the first formal supervisory action in the company's history.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
1. Amortization and hedge-accounting policies, and whether operating earnings hold up under independent review.
2. Credit profile of recent purchases by product, especially loans bought to meet housing goals.
3. Fair value of net assets and its sensitivity to interest rates.
4. Standalone profitability of the guarantee business after a full capital charge.

Greatness potential: 15/100

> Why so low: the plan is right but expensive. It means slower EPS, smaller portfolio growth and higher capital, and it cuts against the pay system, the public promise and the politics. The structure itself (private shareholders, an implied public guarantee, mandated housing goals) pulls against "lasting endurance." The accounting unknowns could also turn out to be worse than "unknown."

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is the most trusted credit guarantor of American home loans. Its underwriting standards define what a sound mortgage is, and it prices credit risk accurately enough that private investors will buy slices of it. It holds real capital, well above the legal floor, so its implied government backing stays a backstop and never becomes the business model. Its retained portfolio is a modest, liquidity-supporting tool, not the profit engine. It reports GAAP numbers that need no "operating" asterisk. It meets its affordable-housing mission with loans families can actually keep, and it measures that by how those loans perform, not by dollar volume committed. If Congress removed its special privileges tomorrow, it would still be the best guarantor in the market. That is the test of greatness it doesn't pass today.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the EPS-doubling goal** as a public promise and as a management target.
2. **Stop tying executive pay to EPS.**
3. **Stop growing the retained portfolio.** Hold it flat now, then shrink it toward a liquidity-support role.
4. **Stop leading with "operating earnings."** GAAP comes first, operating figures sit in a reconciliation table, and the definitions stay fixed.
5. **Stop running capital near the legal minimum.** Adopt a voluntary floor well above it.
6. **Stop tolerating large duration gaps.** Set a hard board limit (illustratively ±6 months `[ASSUMPTION]`) and disclose it monthly.
7. **Stop meeting housing goals with loans or securities the company wouldn't buy on credit merit alone.** Report goal loans' performance separately.
8. **Stop treating regulators and Treasury critics as opponents to outmaneuver.** Resolve the supervisory action, and invite scrutiny of capital and accounting.
9. **Stop saying "credit risk is minimal" and "well hedged"** without loan-level and fair-value disclosure behind those claims.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** Make sustainable home ownership possible for American families, especially those the market underserves.

**Core values:**
1. **Sound loans only.** A mortgage that hurts the family is not a mission accomplishment.
2. **Numbers that need no asterisk.**
3. **Capital before growth.**
4. **Earn the charter.** Behave as if the privileges could be withdrawn tomorrow.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Make the American mortgage guarantee so well-priced and so well-capitalized that private capital stands in front of taxpayers on every loan, and no taxpayer ever pays for a Homestead loss.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A board-set voluntary capital floor.
- A hard duration-gap limit with monthly public disclosure.
- An independent CRO and CAO.
- A scorecard-based pay plan.
- Loan-level performance disclosure by vintage and product.
- Regular credit-risk transfer to private investors, so the market tests Homestead's pricing.
