## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Kestrel is the steel company that earns more per ton than anyone in its products, through every cycle. It is not the biggest. It runs small, rural, scrap-based mills that feed its own joist plants and sell the surplus to outside customers. A tiny headquarters sets a few hard standards and leaves plant managers free inside them. Workers earn far more than industry pay because they produce far more, and nobody is laid off; in bad years everyone, starting at the top, takes the cut. It grows one mill at a time and opens the next only when the last one meets standard. It declines businesses, products and political shortcuts that don't lower cost per ton. Equipment suppliers sell competitors the same furnaces and casters. What competitors cannot buy is the culture that runs them, and that culture is the moat.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop all conglomerate behavior.** No acquisitions outside joists and simple steel, and never pay with stock for a business that doesn't lower cost per ton.
2. **Stop opening mills ahead of proof.** No new mill is announced until the most recent one meets its cost-per-ton and uptime standard.
3. **Stop treating trade protection as strategy.** Kestrel should not join industry lobbying for quotas. A low-cost producer that asks for shelter undercuts its own Hedgehog, and protection mostly props up higher-cost rivals. *(This is a recommendation with a real tradeoff: imports are underpricing Kestrel today. Fight dumping through existing legal channels if needed, but don't build the plan on protection.)*
4. **Stop extending the product range beyond what scrap-based mills make cheaply.** That means no flat-rolled, specialty or high-grade products with current technology. Downstream products qualify only if they consume Kestrel steel and compete on cost.
5. **Stop growing headquarters.** No corporate staff departments, and no new management layers beyond the current four.
6. **Stop urban and union-dependent site selection.** Keep plants small and rural.
7. **Stop counting tons that break safety procedure.** Remove output credit for production made by bypassing safety rules.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **No privileged class.** Same hard hats, small offices, few layers.
2. **Pay for performance.** People share in what they produce.
3. **Share the pain.** No layoffs. In downturns, pay falls first and furthest at the top.
4. **Frugality.** Every dollar is judged by what it does to cost per ton.
5. **Everyone goes home safe.** This value is newly made explicit after last year's fire, and it overrides output.

**Core purpose:** *To prove that a fair, flat company of American workers can make steel better and cheaper than anyone, and to share what it earns with the people who make it.*

**BHAG (20–25 years):** *Out-earn the integrated giants on return on capital through every cycle, and become one of the largest steelmakers in the country on the minimill model.*
