# Ironbridge Steel Corporation: Good to Great

## Verdict (one screen)

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Ironbridge can be the best U.S. supplier of high-quality flat-rolled steel to manufacturers within freight reach of its Lake Michigan and tidewater plants. The measure is cash profit per ton of installed capacity.

**Weakest flywheel link:** *Concentrated capital → lowest cost per ton.* Nothing in the evidence shows that Ironbridge can beat the landed import price, even at its newest plant. Its wages are contracted to rise at least 3% a year while industry shipments may fall about 25%.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop protecting profit with price increases.
2. Stop putting capital into plants and products outside the Hedgehog: the older plants, commodity bars and shipbuilding.
3. Stop treating import protection as the strategy.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Last year's record profits came from a price jump after controls ended and from customers stockpiling. They did not come from any cost advantage, and that tide is already going out.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Cost per ton by plant, compared with landed imports and with minimills. If the Lake Michigan plant can't get below the landed import price, this Hedgehog fails and the honest plan becomes managed contraction.
- Pension, retiree and plant-closure obligations, which decide whether shrinking to the good plants is affordable.
- Return on capital over three or more years.
- Labor cost per ton and current headcount.

Greatness potential: 10/100

**Plain warning:** 6 of 11 gates came out CONDITIONAL. There are two root causes:
- **(a)** Ironbridge has no evidence of where, or whether, it is cost-competitive.
- **(b)** Its leadership culture is insular and status-oriented, and it protects profit through price rather than cost.

Fix those two things and most of the conditions clear.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ironbridge is a smaller company with a better business. It concentrates its capital, engineering talent and management attention on two plants, Lake Michigan and the mid-Atlantic tidewater works. There it makes auto-, appliance- and construction-grade flat-rolled steel at a delivered cost that imports landing on the coasts and minimills limited to bars cannot match. It earns more cash per ton of installed capacity than any U.S. integrated producer, through the whole cycle and not just at price peaks.

It does this by doing the following:
- It shrinks or exits older capacity and commodity products in an orderly way.
- It ties both executive and hourly pay to productivity instead of the calendar.
- It publishes its cost position against foreign mills and minimills every quarter, so bad news cannot hide.

Its leaders are judged by the plants and not by the headquarters tower.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Rejected and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at (narrow world)** | Size and integration; structural shapes; commodity bars; tin plate; shipbuilding; flat-rolled sheet near Midwest and East customers | **High-quality flat-rolled steel delivered to manufacturers within freight reach of Lake Michigan and tidewater** | *Size* is not a skill. *Bars* are already being lost to minimills on cost. *Shapes* are a long-product heritage line that is exposed to minimills moving up `[ASSUMPTION]`. *Shipbuilding* is a wartime peak, not a current edge. *Lowest global commodity cost* belongs to the rebuilt foreign mills. |
| **Economic engine: denominator** | Profit per ton shipped; profit per employee-hour; **cash profit per ton of installed capacity** | **Cash profit per ton of installed capacity** | Profit per ton *shipped* rewards running any tonnage at a margin and hides the cost of old, under-used plants. With mills at 78–80% utilization, the right denominator punishes idle, obsolete capacity and rewards full use of the best plants. Profit per employee-hour is tracked as a supporting measure. |
| **Deep passion** | Making steel that holds up bridges, buildings and vehicles; the works towns; heritage size; executive status | **Making the steel American manufacturers build with, at a quality that bears weight** `[ASSUMPTION: workforce and engineering pride. The dossier has no direct evidence on passion]` | Status and size: the headquarters tower and the pay history suggest some passion is aimed at prestige, which cannot be the circle. |

**The Hedgehog sentence:** *Ironbridge will be the best U.S. supplier of high-quality flat-rolled steel to manufacturers within freight reach of its Lake Michigan and tidewater plants, and will earn the most cash per ton of installed capacity in the industry.*

**Tests:**
- **Does it explain past wins?** Partly. The $500M Lake Michigan expansion "to win Midwest sales" is exactly this logic. The postwar peak came from scale in a protected market, which no longer exists.
- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: bars, most shapes, shipbuilding, the older plants and self-sufficiency in raw materials as a goal.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Very. It implies shrinking the original works and the western Pennsylvania and upstate New York plants, which takes in the home town.

**Confirmation tests:** this is a hypothesis, not a finding.
1. The cost per ton at Lake Michigan and at tidewater is at or below the landed import price in each plant's market. If it isn't, the "best at" circle fails.
2. Auto and appliance customers rate Ironbridge sheet quality at least equal to imports. This needs a customer survey and defect data.
3. Closure and pension costs for non-core plants can be financed without starving the core plants.
4. Minimills remain unable to make flat-rolled steel. Watch every quarter: the word "yet" in the dossier is the threat.

**Current push (the next one or two turns):** turn 1 → 2.
- Get the truth about cost.
- Freeze capital spending outside the Hedgehog.
- Start the productivity-pay conversation with the union at Lake Michigan.

Nothing downstream works until cost per ton is known and falling.

**Doom-loop check: several signs are present.**
- Four presidents in 15 years, which suggests new directions under each regime `[ASSUMPTION]`.

The loop closes on paper. What it lacks is a single consistent push.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop protecting profit with price increases.** Cost reduction becomes the profit lever.
2. **Stop capital spending on the original works and the western Pennsylvania and upstate New York plants** beyond legally required environmental and safety work. The freeze lasts until the cost study rules on each plant.
3. **Stop competing in commodity bars** against lower-cost scrap-based minimills.
4. **Stop treating import protection as the strategy.** Trade enforcement may be a backstop, but it is not a plan, and the voluntary restraints are already six years old.
5. **Stop investing in shipbuilding** and begin an exit review.
6. **Stop monument and status spending:** no new headquarters projects, perks or office symbolism. Executive bonuses not tied to cash profit per ton of capacity end.
7. **Stop owning raw-material assets for self-sufficiency's sake.**
8. **Stop rotating presidents.** Set one leader, one Hedgehog and a horizon of five years or more.
9. **Stop framing the choice as modernize *or* protect *or* cut.** The answer is to modernize narrowly (the two core plants), cut broadly (everything else), and hold protection as a backstop at most.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

- **Core values:**
  1. Steel that bears weight: quality without exception.
  2. Pay earned through productivity, from the shop floor to the chairman.
  3. Face the numbers first.
  4. Fair dealing with customers, workers and works towns, including when shrinking.
- **Core purpose:** to make the steel that American manufacturers and builders trust to carry the load.
- **BHAG (20 years):** *No manufacturer within freight reach of an Ironbridge plant buys imported sheet, because Ironbridge's is cheaper delivered and better.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - Executive and hourly pay tied to the denominator.
  - The quarterly cost league table.
  - A capital rule: no spending outside the Hedgehog without a board exception, recorded in the minutes.
  - Formal succession, with each president serving five years or more and a named successor two years out.
  - Outside board seats.
