# Halvard Strings: Good to Great run

## Verdict (one screen)

**Halvard is already great by the skill's tests.** Fourteen years of growth, 24–29% operating margins, about 34% ROIC, and 31% of professional orchestra string players. The work now is staying great. The biggest threat is on this session's agenda.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** *Halvard makes the strings that professional violin-family players choose in blind tests, winds them on its own machines, and sells them only through the specialist shops that serve those players, at a premium it never discounts.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the specialist-shop channel. North American independent shops fell about 3% last year, and Halvard does not control this link. The second weak point is cello and bass winding capacity, where lead times slipped to five weeks.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Do not launch Halvard Six as proposed (mass-retail guitar strings at 31% gross margin).
  2. Decline the private-equity student brand at 1.1× revenue.
  3. Do not ask the Listening Room to judge guitar strings. A panel of violin-family players cannot answer for guitarists. That answers the CEO's "who answers it" question: Halvard has no one who can.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Halvard Six largely re-enters a business Halvard deliberately left fourteen years ago, and that exit is what created today's company. Halvard Six would also take winding capacity (a second shift on two machines) from cello and bass sets that just slipped to five-week lead times.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Gross profit per winding-machine hour, core versus guitar.
  - Halvard Six's projected *operating* margin, net of chain placement fees.
  - Whether the chain's terms respect minimum advertised price.
  - Revenue concentration across the 1,900 shops.
  - The size of the professional and advanced classical-string market outside North America and Europe.

Greatness potential: 72/100

- **Weakest link: step 5, the shop channel.** Consolidation runs at about −3% a year in North America, and Halvard does not own this step. A mass-retail guitar presence [ASSUMPTION] would tell specialist shops that Halvard is becoming a big-box brand.
- **Second risk: step 7, capacity.** Five-week cello and bass lead times break step 4.
- **Current push (next one or two turns):**
  1. Protect cello and bass capacity and lead times, especially through the summer roof replacement.
  2. Build an explicit shop-health program so step 5 keeps up as the channel consolidates.
- **Doom-loop check:** The flywheel's history is clean: fourteen years of one direction. **The warning sign is this session.** A big new program (Halvard Six) and an acquisition (the student brand) are on the same agenda as answers to a 2% core market. That pattern, jump-starting momentum with large moves, is the doom loop's signature.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Do not launch Halvard Six as proposed.** It is mass-retail guitar strings at a projected 31% gross margin, against 61% in the core.
   - $19M at 31% is about $5.9M of gross profit. The core's $46.8M at 61% is about $28.5M.
   - Added to the Year −1 base, blended gross margin falls to about 52%, before chain fees, four sales staff and the second shift.
2. **Decline the private-equity student brand.** It is the segment Halvard exited. The 1.1× revenue price [ASSUMPTION] signals thin margins, and a seller consolidating out of student brands knows something.
3. **Do not ask the Listening Room to test guitar prototypes,** and do not reconstitute the panel to approve something outside the violin family.
4. **No big-box, end-cap or general-marketplace channels** for any Halvard-branded product. This keeps the existing policy, now written down.
5. **No winding capacity for non-core lines** while cello or bass lead times exceed two weeks.
6. **No promotions or discounting below MAP to hit growth targets.**
7. **Stop making revenue growth % the headline board metric.** Replace it with the scorecard in section 8.

*If the board cannot let go of guitar:* the only disciplined version is a separately governed test with all of the following:
- capped capital;
- specialist guitar shops only, with no chain end-caps;
- an independent panel of professional guitarists with veto power;
- a gross-profit-per-winding-hour bar equal to the core's;
- a pre-agreed kill date.

The recommendation remains no.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:**
  1. The player decides, blind.
  2. Never betray the shop's trust; the price is the price.
  3. Own the craft: we wind our own.
  4. Credit goes to the players and the team.
- **Core purpose:** to give string players the sound they hear in their heads.
- **BHAG (20-year):** *Halvard on at least one string of a majority of professional violin-family players in the world's orchestras, across three continents.*
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - A written Listening Room charter covering scope, veto and rotation, owned by a named successor to the CEO.
  - The MAP and channel policy, board-approved.
  - A capital-allocation rule: keep about nine months of costs in cash and return the excess to owners rather than buy growth.
  - Winding knowledge transfer through paired apprenticeships.
  - The metal hedging policy.
  - A designated CEO successor with a dated transition.
