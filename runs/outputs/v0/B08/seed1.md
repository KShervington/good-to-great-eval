# Meridian Radio Corporation: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Meridian builds the most dependable, easiest-to-service home-entertainment sets in America and sells them through the best-run dealer network, earning more profit per dealer than anyone. It uses whatever technology delivers that dependability at the lowest cost.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** Turning reliability into cost. Meridian's quality comes from hand-wiring plus inspection, with **1 in 6 production workers working as inspectors**. Its cost rises with every set built. Doubling color volume on that method doubles the cost problem.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop making "No. 1 in color, like we did in black-and-white" the goal, and don't borrow to buy that crown.
2. Stop defining quality as hand-wiring. Define it as field reliability and serviceability, and let engineering pick the method.
3. Stop open-ended pay-TV spending. Set a kill date tied to the regulators' decision and a licensing exit.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Meridian's quality is tied to a production method, not built into its designs. Meanwhile, leadership publicly dismisses both forces that make that method fatal:
- integrated circuits ("not ready on any significant scale")
- low-cost Japanese imports ("cheap products, no threat")

The imports are entering at the low end, which is where entrants usually start.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Cost per chassis and first-year failure rate for hand-wired vs. printed-circuit vs. solid-state sets. If hand-wiring is far more reliable at a small cost gap, Gate 5 changes.
- Import unit volumes and price trend.
- Color market share and Rival R's color capacity.
- Full-year margins, debt and capital spending for Years −5 to 0.
- Profit per dealer.

Greatness potential: 18/100

*Why 18:* The franchise is real: No. 1 in black-and-white, best-in-industry dealer and inventory control, and a 100× stock record. But the plan asks leadership that was formed in the founder's era to do three hard things at the top of a boom:
- give up the identity it is proudest of
- give up the crown it most wants
- take seriously the competitors it currently dismisses

Most companies in that position don't.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Meridian is not the company with the most color sets or the most hand-soldered joints. It is the company whose sets fail least, are fixed fastest and make its dealers the most money. "Quality" has moved from a method (hand-wired, inspected) to an outcome that engineers design into solid-state, modular chassis. Those chassis cost about what the imports cost to build and are still the ones repairmen tell families to buy. It adds color capacity only as fast as dealers can sell through at full margin, with no debt raised for prestige. Leadership studies imports and integrated circuits monthly instead of dismissing them. A named, prepared successor exists, so the company's next thirty years don't depend on the founder's playbook. Pay TV became a licensing royalty or a closed chapter, not a decade-long drain.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Mostly yes. Meridian passed Rival R in black-and-white through brand, serviceability and dealer pull, not technology leadership.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: hand-wiring as identity, the color crown, pay TV as an operating business.
- *Uncomfortable?* Very.

**Confirmation tests:**
1. Field failure data: hand-wired vs. printed-circuit sets at equal design maturity.
2. Dealer economics: do Meridian dealers earn more per sale or per square foot than Rival R dealers?
3. Buyer research: do families buy because "hand-crafted" or because "it won't break, and if it does, it's easy to fix"?

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop pursuing No. 1 in color as a goal.** Add capacity only against measured dealer sell-through at full margin.
2. **Stop borrowing for prestige capacity.** The company had no debt in Year −6. Any debt needs a demand-cycle case: first-purchase wave vs. replacement.
3. **Stop equating quality with hand-wiring.** Freeze hand-wired chassis for new models once a solid-state equivalent meets the reliability spec.
4. **Stop scaling inspection headcount with volume.** Quality that has to be inspected in costs the same on every unit.
5. **Stop open-ended pay-TV investment.** Cap spending, set a kill date tied to the regulators' decision, and pursue licensing.
6. **Stop public pronouncements dismissing ICs or imports.** Replace them with published internal analysis.
7. **Stop defaulting to the founder's playbook.** Decide from data and the Hedgehog Concept.
