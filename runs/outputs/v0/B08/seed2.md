# Good to Great: Meridian Radio Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
*Meridian builds the home entertainment sets America's dealers and repairmen trust most, using whatever technology makes them most dependable, and measures itself by profit per dealer.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** engineering reinvestment in new technology. Quality is tied to a method (hand-wiring, vacuum tubes) and not to a measured result (field dependability). If solid-state sets from rivals become as reliable and cheaper, the repairman's reason to recommend Meridian disappears.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop treating hand-wiring as the definition of quality. Make it earn its place on measured cost and failure data.
2. Stop setting strategy by the goal of beating Rival R to No. 1 in color unit share.
3. Stop open-ended pay-TV spending. Cap it and tie it to a dated regulatory decision.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Leadership publicly dismisses the two forces most likely to reshape the industry: low-cost Japanese imports ("no threat") and integrated circuits ("not ready"). Meanwhile the brand is tied to a production method those forces make more expensive every year.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Hand-wired vs. printed-circuit cost and field-failure data
- Import volumes, price points and growth rate
- Meridian's color share and profit per color set
- Full-year financials, debt and capital plans for Years −5 to 0
- Cumulative pay-TV spending

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Meridian is the brand that American dealers stake their reputation on and repairmen recommend without hesitation. It earns that trust because its sets fail least and are fastest to fix. It does **not** earn it because they are hand-wired. It adopts printed circuits, transistors and integrated circuits as soon as its own data shows they make sets more dependable, and it designs them to be modular and serviceable. That keeps the repairman as an ally even as repairs become rarer. It treats Japanese imports as a cost and design benchmark to study every quarter, not as junk to sneer at. It wins color through dependability in the premium segment, not a unit-share race with Rival R. It funds growth mostly from earnings. It keeps the inventory discipline that is already best in the industry. Its leadership can run without any one person, and its BHAG trajectory is measured in field-failure rates and dealer profit, not headlines.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** marketing "hand-wired" as the meaning of quality. Market *measured dependability* instead.
2. **Stop** defining success as overtaking Rival R in color unit share.
3. **Stop** open-ended pay-TV funding. Set a spending cap, a decision date tied to the regulatory ruling, and a licensing or spin-off option.
4. **Stop** public leadership statements that dismiss imports or ICs. Statements like these commit the organization to denial.
5. **Stop** adding color capacity, especially with debt, based on boom-quarter momentum. Every expansion needs a downturn-and-import scenario case.
6. **Stop** relying on end-of-line inspection as the main way to get quality. Build it into the design and process.
7. **Stop** chasing small, low-price segments where imports compete on cost alone. `[ASSUMPTION]`: there is no import price data, so this needs validating.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every product decision is judged by field-failure rate and profit per dealer.
- Inventory and dealer-stock control stays at today's industry-leading standard.
- Competing imports are torn down and costed every quarter.
- Growth is funded mainly from earnings.

**Framework and freedom:** Engineers and plant managers may choose *any* construction method or technology, provided it meets dependability, serviceability and cost targets. The targets are fixed; the method is free.

**Bureaucracy check:** The inspection-heavy workforce may be compensating for process design, not for the wrong people. Treat it as a process problem to solve, not a staffing rule to defend.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values (unchanging):**
1. Dependability above all.
2. Stand behind every set; dealers and repairmen are partners.
3. Claim only what we can measure.
4. Pioneer convenience for the home.
5. Financial prudence: grow from earnings.

**Core purpose:** to bring dependable entertainment and information into American homes.

**BHAG (20–25 years):** For a generation, be the home-entertainment brand American families trust most, with the industry's lowest field-failure rate in every category we enter.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A succession plan reviewed by the board every year.
- A technology council with board access.
- A field-reliability data system built with repairmen.
- Stage-gated capital approval.
- A dealer advisory council.
- A quarterly import teardown review.
