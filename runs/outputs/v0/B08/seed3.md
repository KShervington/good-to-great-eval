# Good to Great: Meridian Radio Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Meridian builds the most dependable, easiest-to-service television in American homes, and sells it through the dealers and repairmen who trust it most.*
**Denominator:** profit per dealer `[ASSUMPTION]`. It is not yet measured.

**Weakest flywheel link:** how reliability gets made. Today it comes from hand-wiring and heavy inspection (one production worker in six is an inspector). That method is labor-heavy and tube-era, and it cannot carry printed circuits, transistors or integrated circuits. If reliability can't be re-engineered into solid-state, modular designs, the loop breaks.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop treating *hand-wiring* as the brand. The brand is *dependability and serviceability*, and hand-wiring is only today's way of delivering it.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The quality promise is tied to a production method the industry's cost and technology curve is leaving behind. Leadership has publicly dismissed both the technology that replaces it (integrated circuits) and the competitors who will exploit the gap (low-cost imports).

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Meridian is the household name for *the set that doesn't let you down*, whatever the screen technology. It no longer advertises hand-craftsmanship. It engineers reliability *into* the design, using solid-state, modular, plug-in chassis that a repairman can diagnose and fix in minutes. That keeps the repairman and dealer network, its most distinctive asset, loyal and profitable. It competes in color as the premium-reliability choice, not as a volume rival to Rival R. It adds capacity in steps matched to orders, not to boom-time ambition. It watches low-cost imports with a red-flag system, not contempt. Its leadership has moved from the founder's lieutenants to engineers and operators chosen for the next era. Success is measured by profit per dealer, first-year failure rate and the cost gap per set, not by unit share.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop marketing "hand-crafted" as the core promise.** Market dependability and serviceability, which survive a change of production method.
2. **Stop defending hand-wiring by default.** Keep it only on models where a cost and reliability study shows it wins.
3. **Stop pursuing "No. 1 in color" by volume.** Rival R pioneered color and leads it. Compete as the premium-reliability choice.
4. **Stop sizing capacity, or borrowing, for peak boom demand.** Add color tube and set capacity in phases triggered by orders and dealer sell-through.
5. **Stop open-ended pay-TV spending.** Cap the budget and tie the next dollar to the regulators' decision. If approved, license or partner. Do not build an operating business outside the Hedgehog Concept.
6. **Stop dismissing imports in public and in private.** Replace opinion with teardown data and a red-flag threshold.
7. **Stop inspecting quality in as the main quality system.** Shift spending to design and supplier reliability.
8. **Stop letting the president's public position on integrated circuits stand as company policy.** Reopen it as a question with evidence, not a settled answer.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every model meets a published field-failure and service-time standard before it ships. This keeps the founder's "until it's ready" spirit, measured instead of felt.
- Dealer inventory discipline is never relaxed to hit a quarter.
- Every capacity decision includes a downturn scenario.

**Framework boundaries (freedom within a framework):**
- *Fixed:* the reliability standard, the dealer and inventory rules, and the capital-staging rule.
- *Free:* engineering teams choose the method (hand-wired, printed or solid-state) that meets the standard at the lowest cost.

**Bureaucracy check:** the one-in-six inspector ratio is partly a rule that compensates for a production design, not for people. Fix the design and the inspection layer can shrink humanely, through redeployment rather than cuts.

**Fanatical consistency:** every set is easy for a repairman to fix, every time.
