# Tallypaw: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept:** *Tallypaw is the independent vet clinic's own inventory and ordering brain. It is built by people who have run clinics, it sits on the clinic's side of the counter, and it earns its money from clinics that stay for years.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** acquiring new clinics. That link is rented: 38% of ARR comes through VetSource, and most of the rest depends on Maya's demo calendar.

**Top three things to stop:**
1. Stop the dental build. Park the LOI and bring the two engineers back to inventory.
2. Stop pursuing corporate chains. Both pilots failed, and chains are not the customer.
3. Stop having Maya run most demos. Her time belongs on VetSource and on building a sales motion that others can repeat.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** VetSource sells the very module that is Tallypaw's moat, and it accounts for 38% of ARR (about $910K). It has announced its own native inventory product, its contract renews in five months, and it has not replied to two emails. The board recorded all of this under "housekeeping."

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**

- The reseller terms: exclusivity, non-solicit and transition clauses.
- S&M spend, new logos and CAC broken down by channel.

- How many independent clinics are reachable beyond VetSource's region.

Greatness potential: 28/100

*Evidence note: this run had no access to memory, past runs or web research. That means no earlier scorecard as a baseline, and no checks on competitors, market size or vet-industry consolidation. The dossier is the only evidence. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Tallypaw is the inventory and ordering system that independent vet clinics trust because it works for *them*, not for a distributor. It is distributor-neutral. It shows a clinic what it should stock, what is expiring and where to buy it, and it proves the savings in dollars. It acquires clinics through practice managers recommending it to other practice managers and through a sales team anyone can run, not through a partner who has become a competitor or through the CEO's personal demos. It keeps clinics for years, and gross profit per clinic-year rises every year. It never builds dental software and never chases chains, because its people know exactly what they are best at. If it disappeared, independent clinics would be left with only distributor-owned tools.

**Tests:**

- **Does it rule things out?** Yes: dental, chains, and reminders as a growth product.
- **Is it uncomfortable?** Very. It kills the CEO's idea and the Head of Sales's plan, and it means competing openly with a partner that drives 38% of ARR.

**Status:** working hypothesis. It is confirmed if:
- (a) reminder-only clinics that are *upsold* to inventory see their churn fall. That would rule out the possibility that healthier clinics simply self-select into inventory, which the CTO's correlation cannot exclude.
- (b) Direct-channel CAC payback comes in under about 18 months. `[ASSUMPTION threshold]`
- (c) Win rate holds once VetSource's native tool ships.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ① Clinic-built inventory & ordering engine
        ↓
 ② Clinic saves money and time (fewer stockouts, less expired stock)   ← not yet measured
        ↓
 ③ Clinic stays and expands → ↑ gross profit per clinic-year
        ↓
 ④ Practice managers recommend Tallypaw to practice managers
        ↓
 ⑤ New independent clinics acquired DIRECTLY at falling CAC            ← WEAKEST LINK
        ↓
 ⑥ More ordering data → sharper reorder suggestions and benchmarks
        ↓
     back to ①  (gross profit also funds ①)
```

**The loop closes.** Link ⑤ is weak because 38% of the business arrives through VetSource and most of the rest through Maya personally. Link ② is unproven because there is no "dollars saved per clinic" number, which is the proof that would power ④ and ⑤.

**Current push, for the next one or two turns:**
1. **Upsell inventory to the ~90 reminder-only clinics** (29% of 310). This raises gross profit per clinic-year and should cut churn. It is the cheapest turn available.
2. **Own the relationship with every clinic Tallypaw serves**, within the terms of the reseller agreement. Pair this with a distributor-neutral ordering integration, so that VetSource's native tool becomes a choice the clinic makes rather than a cliff for Tallypaw.

**Doom-loop check:** signs are present.
- Two jump-start bets were proposed in two board meetings: chains, then dental.
- The board asked for a shorter roadmap and the answer was deferred.
- There is a reactive risk: answering VetSource's "intelligence" announcement with a hype feature race.

None of these has yet become a pattern of repeated pivots. They need to be stopped now.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the dental build.** No engineers, no pilot. Revisit only if independent-vet headroom has been proven exhausted, with numbers.
2. **Stop corporate-chain pursuit.** No new pilots, no enterprise features such as multi-site consolidation. Qualification rule: independent, 1–4 vets.
3. **Stop Maya running most demos.** Turn her pitch into a script and train others to run it.
4. **Stop treating reminders as a growth product.** Put the module in maintenance mode, renegotiate with the SMS vendor or add a second one, pass through costs where possible, and shift to email where clinics accept it.
5. **Stop waiting on VetSource email replies.** Escalate to an executive-to-executive meeting.
6. **Stop adding roadmap items.** Cap the roadmap at three themes per quarter, all inside the Hedgehog Concept. Deliver the list the board asked for *before* any new initiative.
7. **Stop filing partner concentration under "housekeeping."** It becomes a standing top-of-agenda item.
8. **Don't sign a bigger or longer lease in March** before the VetSource outcome is known. Choose the shortest sensible term.
9. **Don't answer VetSource's "intelligence" announcement with a hype feature race.** Answer it with measured clinic savings and with neutrality across distributors.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core purpose:** help independent vet clinics thrive and stay independent.

**Core values:**
1. **We were one of them.** Clinic reality comes before software convenience.
2. **The clinic's side of the counter.** No hidden distributor interests, ever.
3. **Own the mistake first.** Blameless post-mortems, with leaders going first.
4. **Named humans, not tickets.** Support that clinics can thank by name.

**BHAG (15-year horizon):** *Most independent vet clinics in every market we enter run their pharmacy and supply room on Tallypaw, and independents outlast the chains partly because of it.*

**Clock-building mechanisms** (so this works without Maya):
- A written, trainable sales playbook run by practice-manager sellers.
- A partner-concentration rule: no single channel provides more than 25% of new ARR. `[ASSUMPTION threshold]`
- A Hedgehog test, done in writing, for every roadmap item.
- An "ARR at risk" first agenda item at every board meeting.
- A call to every churned clinic, logged by cause.
