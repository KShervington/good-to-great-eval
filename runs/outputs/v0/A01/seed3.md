# Good to Great: Halvorsen-Pike Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> *Halvorsen-Pike will be the best in the world at turning fiber know-how into everyday branded paper products that people trust. Its engine is profit per consumer brand, and it will sell its commodity mills to pay for the fight.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** product innovation in absorbent materials. Leakage complaints about the diaper and the thin expertise in synthetic nonwovens mean better advertising would only push products that are losing.

**Top three stop-doings:**
1. Stop funding commodity mills (coated papers, newsprint, the Canadian joint venture, pulp) and put them up for sale.
2. Stop spreading capital across all businesses by tradition.
3. Stop the air-fleet charter idea and any other "make the cost center a profit center" side ventures.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The company is fighting the world's best consumer marketer with half its capital and none of its conviction. A half-funded consumer business against Rival P loses. It has to choose.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Segment profit and return on capital. If consumer earns *below* the mills on capital employed, the Hedgehog Concept fails.
- Brand-level share trends in tissue, feminine care and diapers.
- Category growth rates.
- Realizable sale value of the mills and timberland versus their modernization capex.
- The CEO's realistic working horizon.

Greatness potential: 40/100

*Why 40:* The ingredients are real. It invented and still leads a category, its consumer markets are growing, and it has a CEO with strong Level 5 signals. Against that, very few companies make a sustained good-to-great jump. The plan also requires a culturally explosive divestiture, a head-on fight with Rival P while two of three newer consumer products are losing, a capability gap in nonwovens, and a CEO with a poor prognosis.

> **Run note:** Autonomous mode. No memory, past chats or web research were available, so the dossier is the only evidence. No prior run exists to baseline against. Every inference is marked `[ASSUMPTION]`. All of this is a set of disciplined hypotheses, not a guarantee. Collins's method looks backward, and some of the companies he called great later declined.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halvorsen-Pike is a consumer paper-products company, no longer a mill company that happens to own brands. It has sold its commodity mills and, after a value test, its timberlands. It puts that capital into a narrow set of household brands built on its century of fiber knowledge: facial tissue, toilet tissue and towels, feminine care and diapers. It wins on product superiority first and advertising second. It runs every decision through one denominator, profit per consumer brand. Its leadership bench is a blend of the best mill operators (now running converting plants) and seasoned consumer marketers. It outlasts its current CEO. Its distinctive impact is that it is the credible fiber-based alternative to Rival P in personal care. If it vanished, retailers and consumers would lose the one scaled competitor with deep fiber science.

**Confirmation tests (what would prove or kill it within about 12 months):**
- Segment ROC shows consumer earns above cost of capital and above the mills. *Kills it if false.*
- The diaper reaches leakage parity or better with Rival P in blind consumer testing.
- Facial-tissue share holds or grows while advertising rises.
- Mill and timberland sale proceeds at least match the hold-and-modernize value `[ASSUMPTION: likely, given the reinvestment burden]`.

**Current push:**
1. Fix the diaper (component 2) before spending more on advertising.
2. Free capital (one-time fuel) through a single, clearly announced divestiture decision.

**Doom-loop check:**
- *Risk present:* the mill sale is a big move. It must be **one** coherent decision explained by the Hedgehog Concept, not the first of a run of restructurings.
- *Risk present:* the temptation to "out-advertise" Rival P before the product is better. That is reaction without understanding.
- *Risk present:* a new CEO plus a health crisis can bring several changes of direction in quick succession. Keep one story and repeat it.
- *Not yet present:* no acquisitions are proposed to jump-start consumer momentum. Keep it that way, except a targeted nonwovens capability buy (see Gate 7).

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop** modernizing coated-paper and newsprint mills. Put them up for sale.
2. **Stop** allocating capital across all businesses by tradition. Every dollar must pass the profit-per-consumer-brand test.
3. **Stop** the air-fleet charter and maintenance venture. It is attractive, unrelated and distracting.
4. **Stop** operating pulp mills and sawmills as businesses. Buy pulp on the market `[ASSUMPTION: supply is available]`.
5. **Stop** treating timberland as identity. Value it as an asset and sell unless holding clearly wins.
6. **Stop** advertising the diaper harder until it wins blind leakage tests.
7. **Stop** debating whether to compete with Rival P. The debate itself is the failure mode. Decide once.
8. **Phase 2 review:** specialty, envelope and cigarette papers. Keep only if segment data show above-cost-of-capital returns. Otherwise, sell.

- Monthly blind product-test results go straight to the CEO.
- Segment ROC is published internally every quarter.
- A blameless post-mortem is held on the diaper launch.

**Faith statement:** *We invented the category the world names after our product, and we know fiber better than any marketer, so once we stop feeding the mills we can win.*

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Product superiority before promotion.
2. Face the facts, especially our own.
3. Craftsmanship with fiber.
4. Responsibility to the people and towns that built us, honored humanely through every change.

**Core purpose:** to make everyday life a little cleaner, softer and more comfortable for families.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** become the leading consumer paper-products company in the world, and beat Rival P in most of the categories where we compete head-on.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Capital allocation by profit per consumer brand, enforced by a written rule rather than the CEO's judgment.
- A standing blind-test gate before any advertising increase.
- A named successor and a leadership bench that blends operators and marketers.
- Quarterly segment ROC review at board level.

### Gate detail: light gates

**Gate 2, Level 5 leadership.** The CEO shows humility: he is shy, plainly dressed and uninterested in publicity. He also shows a "mirror" habit of blunt self-criticism and holds very high standards. His willingness to work through treatment suggests fierce resolve, though that resolve is untested on a strategic bet. *Gap:* he came up through law and finance rather than the mills, which may weaken his authority with operators, and the company may not survive his absence. *Behavior change:* within 90 days, publicly name a successor path and build a bench so the strategy doesn't depend on his health.
