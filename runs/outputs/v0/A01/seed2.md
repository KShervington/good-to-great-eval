# Good to Great: Halvorsen-Pike Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halvorsen-Pike will be the best in the world at branded, paper-based personal-care products, and will judge itself by profit per consumer brand.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** product innovation in absorbents. The company has no synthetic nonwoven capability, the diaper leaks, and tampons are taking share from its pad. Brand and distribution cannot make up for a product that is behind.

**Top three stop-doing items**
1. Stop funding modernization of the printing-paper and newsprint mills. Sell those mills, including the namesake mill.
2. Stop spreading capital evenly across every business by tradition. Fund only work that is inside the Hedgehog Concept.
3. Stop the air-fleet charter and maintenance profit-center idea, and shrink the fleet.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** the company is funding two capital-hungry fights at once and losing both. The commodity mills take most of the capital and lag the market. Rival P is beating both of the newer consumer products. Without a choice between them, the default outcome is losing on both.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer**
- **Segment return on capital across a full cycle.** If the mills earn above their cost of capital through the cycle, the case for selling them weakens.
- **Diaper and feminine-care share trends**, to see whether the slide is still recoverable.
- **Growth rates by category.**
- **CEO prognosis and a successor.** This is a governance fact, not a number, but it matters as much as the others.

**Gate tally:** 7 PASS, 4 CONDITIONAL, 0 FAIL. Most CONDITIONALs trace back to two root problems:
- **No segment economics.** Nobody can see where value is made.
- **A senior bench and identity bred in the mills.** The people and the self-image fit the business being left, not the one being entered.

Greatness potential: 40/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halvorsen-Pike is a focused consumer-products company that grew from its paper roots. It owns the leading brand in each personal-care paper category it chooses to compete in, starting with facial tissue, the category it invented. It funds that position with proceeds from selling its commodity mills. It wins on product, combining its deep cellulose-fiber know-how with new nonwoven skills to make tissue, pads and diapers that perform better. It then pushes those products through heavy brand advertising and distribution. It does not try to beat Rival S on mill cost or out-spend Rival P everywhere. It picks the categories it can lead and fights without letup in those. Its leaders come from consumer businesses as well as manufacturing. Its performance outlasts the current CEO, because the strategy is encoded in how capital is allocated, how leaders are chosen and what gets measured, not in one person's judgment.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Best in the world at branded, paper-based personal-care products, measured by profit per consumer brand.*

The pad brand was the long-time leader. (d) **Too narrow** to sustain the company. **Choose (c).** It *cannot* be best at mass-scale commodity paper, and it cannot yet be best in diapers. |

| **Passion** | Mill craft and heritage; "paper that touches people's lives"; competing head-on against the best. | This is the weakest circle. **Intersection tests**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. The company's most durable asset is a consumer category it invented and still leads. Its 20 years of commodity and specialty-paper acquisitions coincide with lagging the market.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: printing papers, newsprint, specialty, envelope and cigarette papers, the aircraft business, and probably most timberland.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means selling the namesake mill and fighting Rival P directly.

**Confirmation tests (within about 24 months)**
- Consumer-segment return on capital beats the mills' through-cycle return.
- Facial-tissue share holds or grows.
- A diaper relaunch stops the share decline.
- No more than one or two key consumer hires leave.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Concentrate capital on chosen consumer categories
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   2. Fiber + nonwoven innovation → products that perform better
   │          │                                  ◄── WEAKEST LINK
   │          ▼
   │   3. Heavy brand advertising + distribution
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   4. Share leadership in each category (#1 or a close #2)
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   5. Scale → lower unit cost + retailer leverage
   │          │
   │          ▼
   │   6. Higher PROFIT PER CONSUMER BRAND (denominator)
   │          │
   └──────────┘  reinvested in R&D and advertising
```

Mill and timber proceeds give the wheel a one-time first push. They are not part of the loop. The loop has to run on profit per consumer brand.

**Current push (next one to two turns)**
1. Fix the absorbent product. Run a root-cause program on diaper leakage, and build or acquire capability in nonwoven materials.
2. Defend the leading position in facial tissue with more advertising and product upgrades. This brand is the proof that the flywheel works.

**Doom-loop check**

The current state shows clear doom-loop signs:
- Capital is spread by tradition rather than understanding.
- Twenty years of acquisitions in specialty paper never changed returns.
- The air-fleet profit-center proposal is reaching for "more" outside the core.
- There is no stated strategy.

In the great version, the risk is grasping for salvation: buying a large consumer company to skip the slow work, or spreading the mill-sale proceeds across too many categories. **Verdict: the loop closes. PASS.**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. Stop funding modernization of printing-paper and newsprint mills. Sell them, including the namesake mill. Prefer buyers who will keep them running.
2. Exit the jointly owned Canadian newsprint complex.
3. Sell the specialty, envelope and cigarette paper businesses. They were acquired, they are not personal care, and they sit outside the Hedgehog Concept.
4. Stop the air-fleet charter and maintenance idea. Shrink the fleet to what the business actually needs `[ASSUMPTION]`, or sell it.
5. Stop allocating capital by tradition or seniority. Every dollar must serve profit per consumer brand.
6. Stop acquisitions made for size or diversification.
7. Stop holding timberland as an identity asset. Monetize land that is not needed for fiber supply. `[ASSUMPTION]`: keep only the pulp and fiber supply that gives consumer products a real cost or quality edge, and buy the rest on the market.
8. **On probation, not yet stopped: diapers.** The category is inside the Hedgehog Concept. If the relaunch has not halted the share loss within about 24 months, exit it rather than keep bleeding capital against Rival P.

**Truth climate:** The CEO's blunt self-criticism is an advantage, and nothing suggests a charisma problem; he is the opposite of a showman. Two risks remain:
- **Mill-bred executives may filter consumer bad news.** Add red-flag mechanisms: monthly share data by brand, reviewed openly, and blameless post-mortems on the diaper.
- **Directors' doubts could push the CEO into caution.** He should lead with questions and keep debate open.

**Faith statement:** *A company that invented a consumer category and still leads it a generation later can win with focus, if it stops feeding the businesses that cannot.*
