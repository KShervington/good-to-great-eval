## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
*Brennard will be best in the world at making the reliable, standardized products that hospitals and clinicians use to diagnose, feed and treat patients at lower total cost of care, and it will judge itself by profit per hospital customer.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** **hospital trust.** The best Hedgehog candidate depends on the hospital channel, and that is the same channel where the company's contaminated IV bottles were linked to 434 infections and 49 deaths. The company switched seal material but never regained its IV lead.

**Top 3 stop-doing items:**
1. Stop building consumer brands (golf balls, household gloves, non-dairy creamer, tabletop sweetener). Stop funding them and prepare them for sale.
2. Stop competing with the big drug companies for blockbuster discoveries. Brennard has no breakthrough in years, none in sight, and a research-led rival.
3. Stop spreading capital across five divisions without division-level return data.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The company is still trying to grow by diversifying, the same reflex that followed the sweetener ban. Meanwhile it has never publicly fixed the cause of the worst failure in its history, which happened in the channel it most needs. Leadership has been "excused." The organization has not yet faced the facts.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- **Profit and return on capital by division.** If hospital products or nutritionals earn poor returns, the Hedgehog Concept moves.
- **Infant-formula market share and margin.** This decides whether nutrition is the anchor or a passenger.
- **Diagnostics economics.** This decides whether it is a growth engine or a distraction.
- **Research spending as a share of sales,** plus how much of that research could be redirected.
- **Recent company return on equity and earnings trend.** This confirms that "good" is real (Gate 1).

Greatness potential: 35/100

*Why 35:* The assets for a coherent Hedgehog exist: a hospital sales channel, IV and nutrition lines, a young diagnostics unit, the founding "standardized products for doctors" DNA, and a hospital-products head who already sees cost pressure coming. Against that, few companies ever make this transition. The steward personally championed the diversification this plan reverses. The trust deficit is self-inflicted and severe. No financials confirm the engine.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard is the company a hospital turns to when it needs to lower the cost of diagnosing and treating patients without lowering quality. It sells a tight, connected line to the same customers: IV solutions and hospital supplies, clinical and infant nutrition, diagnostics, and a few hospital-use drugs such as its IV anesthetic. Each product is engineered for reliability and backed by evidence that it saves the hospital money or time. It no longer sells golf balls, creamer or gloves, and it no longer bets the company on finding the next blockbuster drug. Quality has independent authority to stop any shipment, and the recall is treated as a founding lesson, not a buried embarrassment. Capital goes only where profit per hospital customer rises. The company outperforms because every product makes the next sale to the same hospital easier, not because of one lucky molecule.

**Sentence:** *Reliable, standardized products that help hospitals and clinicians diagnose, feed and treat patients at lower total cost, measured as profit per hospital customer.*

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Partly. It covers the founding model, the IV leadership before the recall, and the formula business sold through medical channels. It does not explain the antibiotic, which was a discovery win. That win came from the strategy the company can no longer execute.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: all consumer products, the race for blockbuster drugs, and acquisitions outside the hospital or clinical channel.
- **Uncomfortable?** Very. It reverses the CEO's diversification strategy and drops the research-company identity that the previous CEO invested in.

**Confirmation tests (working hypothesis):**
1. Division data shows hospital products and nutritionals earn above-company returns on capital, or would without recall costs.
2. Hospital buyers confirm in structured interviews that cost of care is becoming a purchasing criterion. This would validate the hospital head's thesis.
3. Hospitals that buy two or more Brennard lines are more profitable per account than single-line customers.

If tests 1 and 3 fail, move to **profit per employee** and a narrower "nutrition-led" concept.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Golf balls.** No link to health care or hospitals. Sell.
2. **Household rubber gloves.** A consumer item with consumer marketing costs. Sell, unless an industrial or clinical version fits the hospital line `[ASSUMPTION]`.
3. **Non-dairy creamer.** A food consumer brand. Sell.
4. **Tabletop sweetener.** It sits in the category where the company already lost a third of consumer revenue to a ban, and it needs consumer marketing. Sell.
5. **The race for blockbuster new drugs.** Keep hospital-use drugs and harvest the off-patent antibiotic for cash. Stop funding discovery aimed at competing with the largest drug companies.
6. **Spreading capital evenly or by advocacy.** No capital goes anywhere without division-level return data and a Hedgehog test.
7. **Acquisitions to restart momentum.** Freeze them until the Hedgehog is confirmed.
8. **Fighting regulators instead of designing for compliance.** The sweetener protest failed. Spend that energy on quality systems.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Patient safety comes before shipment, schedule or sales target.
2. Standardization and reliability. Every unit is the same, every time.
3. Evidence over advocacy, in medicine and in capital decisions.
4. Candor. Bad news travels up fast and without blame.

**Core purpose:** *To make good medical care more reliable and more affordable for the clinicians who deliver it.*

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *Within 20 years, be the first supplier any hospital in the world calls when it wants to lower the cost of care without lowering its quality.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Independent quality function with stop-ship authority and board reporting.
- Capital allocation rule: every investment is scored on its effect on profit per hospital customer.
- An annual brutal-facts review held before the budget.
- A formal CEO and division-head succession plan, renewed every year.
- Cost-of-care evidence required before any new product reaches the sales force.
