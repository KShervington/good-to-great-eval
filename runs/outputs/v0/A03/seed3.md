# Good to Great: Brennard Laboratories

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Brennard becomes the hospital's most trusted maker of everyday clinical essentials (fluids, clinical nutrition and diagnostic tests) that lower the total cost of care.*

**Denominator:** profit per hospital account `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Weakest flywheel link:** trust and reliability. The flywheel starts with "hospitals can count on Brennard products." The IV recall (434 infections, 49 deaths) broke that link, and the company has not regained its IV lead. No growth plan works until quality is visibly fixed.

**Top three stop-doing items:**

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard is the company a hospital administrator calls first when trying to deliver the same care for less money. It makes the unglamorous, high-volume products every ward uses daily: IV solutions, clinical and infant nutrition, and increasingly the diagnostic tests and instruments that tell doctors what is wrong faster and more cheaply. It is known for reliability strict enough to stop a production line on its own suspicion. It earns more profit from each hospital relationship every year, because each new product line rides the same channel and the same trust. It has stopped chasing blockbuster drugs and stopped buying consumer brands. Its sales force, manufacturing and research all point at one question: what does this hospital spend too much on, and can we make that cheaper and safer? It no longer depends on any single product, such as a sweetener or an antibiotic, that a regulator or a patent expiry can take away overnight.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Repair link 1.** Set up an independent quality function with stop-ship authority, complete a sterile-line audit, and tell hospitals openly what changed. Nothing downstream turns until hospitals believe this.
2. **Prove link 3–4 with one bundle.** Choose a small set of hospitals and show that combining Brennard fluids, nutrition and diagnostics cuts their cost per patient-day `[ASSUMPTION: feasibility untested]`.

**Doom-loop check:** Signs of the doom loop are present.
- New direction after new direction: pharma, then consumer, now hospital, with no stated strategy.
- Acquisitions used to create momentum: consumer brands bolted together over several years.
- Reaction without understanding: the company fought the sweetener ban instead of redesigning its portfolio risk.
- Advocates pulling in three directions.

The flywheel above closes logically. The danger is treating it as the fourth new direction. It must be adopted as the *only* direction, with years of consistent pushes.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Sell or exit golf balls, household rubber gloves, non-dairy creamer and the tabletop sweetener.** Medical exam gloves stay open only if they pass the hospital-account economics test.
2. **Stop the blockbuster drug-discovery race.** Keep only targeted pharma that serves hospitals (anesthesia, anti-infectives) and manage the off-patent antibiotic for cash.
3. **Stop allocating capital across five divisions without a common test.** All capital requests are scored on profit per hospital account.
4. **Stop acquiring outside the hospital channel,** however cheap or attractive the target.
5. **Stop launching products that depend on mass consumer marketing.**
6. **Stop treating regulators as adversaries to protest.** Design the portfolio so no single regulatory decision can remove a third of a division.
7. **Stop accepting any quality deviation for the sake of schedule or cost.** One recall erases years of flywheel turns.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- A quality sign-off on every sterile lot.
- A quarterly profit-per-account review by division.
- Every new product must answer: *does it lower a hospital's cost of care?*

**Framework boundaries:** Division heads have full autonomy over *how* they serve hospitals, within the hospital channel, the quality veto and the capital test.

**Bureaucracy check:** Expect post-recall caution to have spawned approval layers. Once the independent quality function exists, cut the layers that exist only because quality ownership was unclear `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: draft for leadership to test]`:
1. Patient safety comes before schedule, cost or sales.
2. Tell regulators, customers and ourselves the truth early.
3. Lower the cost of care, including our own costs.
4. Steady, disciplined craftsmanship over flashy bets.

**Core purpose:** Help doctors and hospitals deliver better care for less.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** Every hospital in every market Brennard serves runs on at least one Brennard system (fluids, nutrition or diagnostics) because it is the safest and lowest-cost choice.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- An independent quality function with stop-ship authority that any CEO cannot override quietly.
- A profit-per-account capital-allocation rule.
- A named succession bench reviewed annually by the board.
- A blameless-autopsy standard for every quality event.
