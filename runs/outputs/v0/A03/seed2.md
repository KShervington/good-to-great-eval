# Brennard Laboratories: Good to Great

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** **trust in reliability.** The recall of contaminated intravenous solution was linked to 49 deaths. A cost-effectiveness promise is worthless to a hospital if the product can harm patients. Until quality is beyond question, the flywheel cannot turn.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Brennard is narrow on purpose. It does not race the big research houses for blockbuster drugs, and it does not sell golf balls. It is the supplier hospitals trust to make care cheaper and safer. That means three things:

- **Intravenous solutions and supplies** that never fail.
- **Clinical nutrition**, starting with infant formula, that hospitals and pediatricians rely on.
- **Diagnostics** that bring the precision of the electronics industry to the lab, making each test faster and cheaper.

Every product must answer one question: does it lower the total cost of caring for a patient? Every capital decision is judged on profit per employee. The physician founder's original idea, standardized and reliable medicine, becomes the whole company's identity again. The difference is that today's buyer is the cost-conscious hospital, not the individual doctor.

**Tests of the candidate:**

- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It means walking away from the chief executive's own consumer strategy and from the previous chief executive's research build-up.

**Evidence that would confirm it (working hypothesis):**
1. Divisional numbers show that hospital products, nutritionals and the hospital-side drugs earn above-company-average profit per employee.
2. Hospital purchasing managers rank total cost of care as a top-three buying criterion, as the new hospital head expects.
3. Diagnostics can show a measurable cost-per-test advantage within two to three years.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. **Component 1:** an unarguable quality restoration in intravenous solutions, with an independent quality function and published results.
2. **Funding the push:** exit the non-health consumer goods, and move the freed capital and talent into diagnostics and the hospital line.

**Doom-loop check: many warning signs are already present.**
- *Reaction without understanding:* the sweetener ban was followed by more consumer diversification, not by a hard look at why the company depended on a single product.
- *New direction after new direction:* the company has tried research expansion, then consumer brands, then electronics-style diagnostics. Three camps still argue for three different strategies.
- *Acquisitions to restart momentum:* the consumer group was assembled quickly, and it includes golf balls.
- *Inconsistency:* capital is spread across five divisions with no single test for funding.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Sell or wind down golf balls, household rubber gloves, non-dairy creamer and tabletop sweetener.** None of them lowers the cost of health care.
2. **Stop funding the race for blockbuster new drugs.** Keep enough research capability to support the antibiotic and the hospital drugs. Don't try to beat Rival U at discovery.
3. **Stop allocating capital by division politics.** Every request must show its hedgehog fit and its effect on profit per employee.
4. **Stop diversifying by acquisition to replace lost profit streams.** The consumer group shows where that leads.
5. **Stop treating quality as a cost center.** Sterility and reliability are the product.
6. **Stop fighting regulators in public as a first response.** Engage early and anticipate. Don't build a business that depends on regulatory goodwill holding.
7. **Stop chasing attractive adjacencies outside medicine**, such as seeds, food ingredients or household goods, however profitable they look.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Zero tolerance for sterility or quality deviations. Any employee can stop a line.
- Every product has a written total-cost-of-care case for the hospital.
- Capital goes only where profit per employee can rise.

**Framework boundaries:** division heads have full autonomy over how they compete, inside three walls: hospital and clinical customers only, a cost-of-care case for every product, and quality standards set by an independent function.

**Bureaucracy check:** the post-recall caution is likely to breed approval layers `[ASSUMPTION]`. Don't fix trust with paperwork. Fix it with the right quality leader and clear stop-the-line authority.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. Quality you can stake a patient's life on.
2. Honesty about results, especially bad ones.
3. Thrift: lower cost for the customer starts with lower waste at home.
4. Practical science in service of care.

**Core purpose:** to help people get well by making good medical care more dependable and more affordable.

**BHAG (25-year horizon):** *Become the company hospitals worldwide trust first to lower the cost of diagnosing, treating and feeding patients, with a Brennard product touching the care of most hospitalized patients in the markets it serves.*

**Mechanisms that work without the current steward:**
- Denominator-based capital allocation.
- An independent quality function that reports to the board.
- A formal succession plan with internal candidates.
- An annual hedgehog review of every product line.
