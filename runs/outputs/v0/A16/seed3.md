# Good to Great: Carrow Tobacco Company (as of late January, Year 0)

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Carrow wins by being the first to take the format smokers are moving toward to national scale, with a few giant brands made at the lowest unit cost.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** **Honest research → next format.** Carrow's own research department called tobacco an important cause of lung cancer about ten years ago. The company did not act on it, and the industry still disputes it in public. The step where Carrow learns what smokers will move toward is the step it has kept switched off.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop publicly disputing that smoking causes disease. The Surgeon General's report and Carrow's own memo both contradict that position.
2. Stop buying businesses Carrow cannot be best in the world at, starting with any acquisition after the fruit punch maker.
3. Stop copying the large rival's many-brands approach. Carrow's engine is a few very large brands, and its one failed launch cost about $30M.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The U.S. government now says Carrow's product causes lung cancer. Carrow's own files said so about a decade earlier. Every public denial from here on adds to legal, regulatory and trust liability.

**Missing numbers that could change the answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by brand and by business, including the fruit punch business.
- Current share by brand compared with Rival H's cowboy-imagery filter brand.
- Any early consumer data on how the report is changing smoking.

If profit per brand is already falling, or the report causes a steep volume drop, the Hedgehog Concept shrinks. The honest conclusion would then be a well-run "good" company returning cash to shareholders, not a "great" one.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Carrow tells the truth about its product first. It accepts the Surgeon General's finding, cooperates on warning labels, and puts its own research department, including the decade-old memo, under board-level scrutiny instead of in a drawer. On that footing it does again what made it big three times before. It reads where smokers are moving, which is now driven by health fears, and it is first to bring that format to national scale. It does this through one or two very large brands made in its single low-cost plant. It does not dissipate tobacco cash into food and beverage businesses where it has no edge. Cash it cannot reinvest at hedgehog returns goes back to shareholders, who can diversify for themselves. It goes overseas only where it can build a few big brands and apply the same disclosure standards it applies at home.

**Uncomfortable caveat.** Collins's "distinctive impact" test asks whether the subject would leave a hole that could not easily be filled. For a company whose product causes disease, the honest answer is no, and the hole would not be a loss to customers. The framework can make Carrow a disciplined, durable business. It cannot make the product good for the people who use it. This run defines "great" mainly as sustained market outperformance (the business column of the vocabulary map) and treats candour about harm as a precondition, not an extra.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop disputing in public** that smoking causes disease.
2. **Stop new non-tobacco acquisitions.** No second fruit punch.
3. **Stop treating diversification as a hedge.** Buying businesses you can't be best at doesn't reduce risk. It dilutes returns, and shareholders can diversify more cheaply themselves.
4. **Stop launching brands to match the large rival's pace.** Launch only when a format shift is real and Carrow can be first to scale.
5. **Stop funding brands that will never be major franchises.** Prune toward two or three giants.
6. **Stop relying on advertising volume as the main launch lever.** Plan for advertising limits now.
7. **Stop making any health-implying product claim** (filters, "milder", lower delivery) that independent measurement does not support. Unsupported claims of that kind are the next liability after denial.

**Truth climate:** It is failing. A known internal finding was ignored for a decade, and leadership's public line answers the question before asking it. **Red-flag mechanism needed:** the research department reports to the board directly, and its findings cannot be edited by marketing or the executive office.

**Charisma check:** There is no data on individual leaders. The risk is structural: an all-insider top team plus an industry-wide public position suppresses bad news without any single dominant personality.

**Faith statement:** Carrow has three times seen where smokers were going and got there first at scale. If it faces the health facts instead of fighting them, it can do so again on honest terms.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values `[ASSUMPTION: inferred, not stated by Carrow]`:**
- Tell the truth about the product.
- Few brands, done hugely well.
- Lowest-cost craftsmanship.
- Loyalty to the home town.

**Core purpose:** To make the tobacco products adults choose, as honestly and as well as they can be made.

This purpose is deliberately modest. A loftier one would not survive the brutal facts.

**BHAG (25-year horizon):** *Most of Carrow's volume is in formats that independent science rates as substantially less harmful than today's cigarette, and Carrow got there first.*

This BHAG grows out of the Hedgehog Concept: be first to the format smokers move toward. It is consistent with the economic engine only if such formats can carry big-brand profits, and that is unproven `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Mechanisms (clock building):**
- An independent science function reporting to the board.
- Brand profit and return on capital reviewed every quarter.
- Hedgehog review required before any acquisition.
- Fixed criteria that a launch must meet.
- Outside directors.
