## 1. The great version in one paragraph

This section was not produced, because the run stopped at Gate 1. What can be said is what **"good"** has to look like before a greatness run makes sense. Halstead would be:

- solvent, with funded runway through the compact launch and at least 12 months past it;
- at or above break-even at its current share;
- holding dealer inventory at industry-normal levels;
- shipping a new compact family whose quality meets or beats the rival benchmark;
- holding its market share steady for two to three years instead of losing it.

Once it reaches that point, the draft definition of great below becomes the target.

**Draft definition of great** (the three tests from *Social Sectors*, set now so the bar is explicit):

1. **Superior performance:** profit and returns above Rival G and Rival F, sustained for 10 or more years.
2. **Distinctive impact:** Halstead owns at least one segment narrowly enough that buyers and dealers would feel a hole if it disappeared.
3. **Lasting endurance:** results survive the current CEO and outlast a single platform launch.

## 3. The flywheel

**Not run.** The current doom loop is shown below so it can be broken:

```
Product mix out of step with demand
        ↓
Unsold inventory (80,000 units / ~95 days)
        ↓
Discounting + losses ($205M Y−1; $207M in Q2 Y0 alone)
        ↓
Capital markets closed → dependence on aid
        ↓
New products arrive late (compacts: fall Year 1)
        ↓
(back to) Product mix out of step with demand
```

**Current push (survival):** cut the inventory link and the cash link at the same time. The compact launch is the only thing that can break the "product late" link, so its funding has to be protected.

**Doom-loop signs present:** repeated mistiming of demand swings over six years; a merger raised and abandoned; the strategic path still undecided; reliance on a single large rescue (federal aid) and a single large program (the new compacts).

**Truth-climate and charisma check:** a celebrated, publicity-focused leader taking over in a crisis is exactly when bad news gets softened. Red-flag mechanisms are needed now, such as a weekly cash and inventory report that goes to the board unedited.

**Faith statement:** Halstead still has scale ($13.6B in sales), a national dealer network, and a front-wheel-drive car aimed at the demand shift happening now. If it confronts its numbers before it sells its story, it can earn the right to try for great.

**Collins diagnostic:** in *How the Mighty Fall* terms, Halstead shows **Stage 4 (grasping for salvation)** signs: a rescue request, merger hopes, a celebrated new leader, and one big bet. Collins's reading is that recovery from Stage 4 is possible, but only by returning to disciplined fundamentals, not by finding a bigger savior.
