# Good to Great: Brennard Steel Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Brennard makes steel at the lowest cost per ton in its home market by putting small, bonus-paid, lightly managed teams into new mills built around the newest workable steelmaking technology, and it judges every decision by profit per ton.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** "Lowest cost per ton." Rivals are copying the model with about 10 new minimills, which would add roughly 40% to industry capacity. Scrap inflation hits every minimill equally. And nobody can show Brennard's per-ton cost lead with numbers.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. No second iron-making plant, and no expansion of the iron-carbide process, until the existing plant meets pre-set cost and uptime tests. Shut it if it fails them.
2. Stop treating the historic growth rate as a goal. Approve capacity on projected profit per ton at *trough* prices, not on tons added.
3. Stop putting new capital into fasteners and other fabrication lines unless they can show a per-ton cost edge. Foreign suppliers dominate fasteners.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Brennard plans to roughly double capital spending (from $263M to more than $500M) in a year when sheet prices are falling. Rivals are adding about 40% capacity over three years, and the cost lead that would justify the spending has not been measured against them.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Cost per ton and profit per ton, by mill, compared with rival minimills.
- The iron-carbide plant's actual output and cost per ton, compared with scrap at about $155 per gross ton.
- Profitability of the fabrication businesses.
- Market share by product.

If the per-ton cost lead is small or shrinking, the Hedgehog Concept fails. Brennard would then have to find a narrower "best at," such as particular products or regions.

Greatness potential: 70/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard is the steelmaker that stays the low-cost producer after everyone has copied its model, because the advantage was never the minimill. The advantage is the people system that runs the minimill: weekly output bonuses, four layers of management, a headquarters of fewer than 25 people, and no layoffs. Add the habit of being first to put workable new technology into a purpose-built plant. It earns the highest profit per ton in its home market through the coming capacity glut. It funds each new mill from its own cash and keeps debt near zero. It treats iron-making as a measured experiment, not a rescue plan. It comes out of its first CEO change with the culture written into mechanisms and an independent board, not resting on one man's presence. It grows when profit per ton says so and holds back when it doesn't.

**Tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. Each step change (the first mill, thin-slab sheet, the second sheet mill) was a new mill plus new technology plus the bonus system.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes. It excludes iron-making expansion until it is proven, growth for its own sake, fabrication lines without a cost edge, and buying old mills to chase tons.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Yes. It means possibly building less while rivals build more, and possibly shutting the high-profile iron venture.

**What would confirm it within 12 months:**
1. Per-ton cost by mill sits in the lowest quartile against rival minimills.
2. Profit per ton holds within about 25% of last year's level through the sheet-price decline.
3. The third sheet mill reaches its design cost per ton on the schedule set before startup.

**Doom-loop check:** PASS, with three watch signals.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop expanding iron-making.** Build no second plant and commit no new capital to it until the existing plant shows, over two quarters, a cost per ton below scrap and uptime of about 85% or better `[ASSUMPTION: threshold to be set by management]`.
2. **Stop accepting "start-up problems are to be expected"** without dated milestones. Every new process gets kill criteria before it starts up.
3. **Stop using the historic growth rate as a target.** Capacity is approved only if its projected profit per ton at trough prices beats the company average.
4. **Stop adding sheet capacity into regional markets** where announced rival mills will compete for the same customers and the same scrap. Locate new mills by scrap access and gaps in competition.
5. **Stop putting new capital into fasteners, bearings and other fabrication lines** that can't show a per-ton or per-unit cost edge. Joists and deck stay: they are the original captive customer for the mills.
6. **Stop running with an insider-only board.**
7. **Stop counting tons as the headline success measure,** internally and to investors. Lead with profit per ton.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Weekly team output bonus.
- No layoffs; shortened hours in downturns.
- Four management layers at most.
- HQ under 25 people.
- Greenfield, newest workable technology.
- Steel and steel products only, home country only. Revisit this only through the profit-per-ton test.
- **New:** a safety standard that applies before any bonus is paid.
- **New:** monthly profit per ton reviewed for every mill.

**Framework vs. freedom:** HQ sets capital allocation, the per-ton targets, safety and the core policies. Plant GMs own everything else: hiring, crewing, process, customer mix.

**Bureaucracy check:** the dossier shows almost none, which is a strength. Don't let the benchmarking seat or new board members grow into an HQ staff function.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values (do not change):**
1. Pay for output: people share directly in what their team produces.
2. Egalitarianism: few layers, no executive privilege, the same rules for everyone.
3. Shared pain instead of layoffs.
4. Frugality: every dollar of overhead is a cost per ton.
5. **Every worker goes home safe.** This value is added, and it must not be traded for the bonus.

**Core purpose `[ASSUMPTION]`:** to show that steel can be made at home, profitably and through every cycle, by people who are treated as owners of their output.

**BHAG (about 20 years):** be the most profitable steelmaker per ton in the home market every year of a full cycle, and on that base become its largest steel producer by tonnage.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- The weekly bonus formula.
- Plant-GM autonomy, with plant GMs serving as the CEO bench.
- A per-ton scorecard reviewed by an independent board.
- Kill criteria for new technology before it starts up.
- A written capital-approval test.
- A defined, time-limited non-executive role for the chairman.
