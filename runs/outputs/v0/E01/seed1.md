# Good to Great: Brennard Steel Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Brennard makes commodity steel from scrap at the lowest cost per ton in its home market. It does this by combining a lean, pay-for-output, no-layoff culture with being first to run proven new steelmaking technology in mills it builds from scratch.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** being first with new technology. Rivals are copying the thin-slab sheet model with about 10 new minimills (+~40% industry capacity). As they do, the cost lead has to come more from culture and less from technology. Sheet, the segment where the technology lead mattered most, is already softening.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop expanding iron-making past the current plant until it proves a delivered cost per ton below scrap, against a hard deadline.
2. Stop treating the historic growth rate as a target. Build a mill only if it would be lowest-cost in its product at trough prices in a market with 40% more capacity.
3. Stop running a board made almost entirely of insiders through the first CEO change in 30 years.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Brennard is about to add a third sheet mill and spend more than $500M this year (about 1.8× last year's earnings). It will do this into a segment where orders fell and prices were cut last year, just before ~40% more industry capacity arrives. Much of that new capacity copies Brennard's own model.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Cost and profit per ton by mill, compared with the best rival minimills. If Brennard is not in the lowest quartile, the Hedgehog Concept fails.
- Actual cost per ton and output of the iron-carbide plant.
- Profitability of fabrication businesses, especially fasteners and bearings.
- Market share by product.

Greatness potential: 70/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Brennard comes through the coming capacity glut more profitable per ton than the new minimills that copied it. It stays profitable at trough prices and lays no one off. Its advantage is no longer "we had thin-slab first." It is that no rival can match its output per worker, its overhead of fewer than 25 people at headquarters, or its weekly team bonus system. It keeps adopting proven-elsewhere technology first, but only inside steelmaking and only where it lowers cost per ton. It has stopped treating raw-materials chemistry and growth for its own sake as strategy. Leadership passes cleanly from a 30-year founder-era chairman to an insider CEO and then to a bench of plant general managers. A board with real independent voices tests every new mill against one question: will it be the lowest-cost producer of its product at the bottom of the cycle?

**Confirmation evidence (what would turn this hypothesis into fact):**
1. Every mill sits in the lowest-cost quartile of its product category.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop expanding iron-making.** No further capital until the existing plant has run 4 consecutive quarters `[ASSUMPTION on window]` at a delivered cost per ton below scrap. If it misses, cap it or exit.
2. **Stop using "problems are to be expected"** as the public and internal account of iron carbide. Replace it with numbers against plan.
3. **Stop treating the historic growth rate as a goal.** Growth is an output of profit per ton, not a target.
4. **Stop matching rivals' capacity announcements.** No fourth sheet mill decision this year. Re-test this year's $500M+ plan project by project against trough prices with 40% more industry capacity.
5. **Stop pioneering unproven processes outside steelmaking** at commercial scale. Keep "first to apply proven-elsewhere equipment" (the thin-slab model). Drop "first to prove a new chemistry" (the iron-carbide model).
6. **Stop new capital in fasteners and bearings** until unit profitability and cost position against foreign suppliers are known.
7. **Stop paying output bonus on shifts with a serious safety breach** `[ASSUMPTION: current bonus design has no safety gate]`.
8. **Stop board composition by insiders alone.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** HQ under 25 people; four layers; weekly team output bonus; shortened hours, never layoffs, in downturns; greenfield mills with the newest proven technology; steel and steel products only; home country only.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- *Framework (central):* capital allocation, the hedgehog test for new mills, safety standards, the per-ton scorecard.
- *Freedom (plants):* everything else, including hiring, operations, the local bonus mechanics, and suppliers.

**Bureaucracy check:** current bureaucracy is minimal, which is a strength. The risk is a new CEO adding HQ staff or approval layers to replace the chairman's informal authority. Watch HQ headcount as a leading indicator.

**Fanatical consistency:** the weekly bonus, paid on time, every week. A per-ton cost league table every month. No layoffs, ever.
