# Good to Great: Northfield-Brandt Corporation

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Northfield-Brandt invents and leads branded, everyday absorbent personal-care categories, and measures itself by profit per consumer brand.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** *Reinvestment.* The consumer brands compete for capital with an airline and a set of commodity mills. They also face Rival P, which has larger research and advertising budgets. Capital that goes to non-core businesses does not fund the brands.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop the $120M airline expansion and separate aviation (airline and services) from the company.
2. Stop reinvesting in the paper class (newsprint, printing, tobacco and technical papers). Set it up to be sold or spun off.
3. Stop launching premium-priced niche products that don't win everyday use. The anti-viral tissue is the lesson here.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The company is fighting the world's strongest packaged-goods marketer with a smaller budget while still funding three unrelated businesses. It is also making those capital decisions without the segment profit data this review needs. A 64-year-old chief executive of 20 years has no settled successor.

- Current shares in facial tissue and diapers

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Northfield-Brandt is a focused consumer personal-care company. It has nothing else to manage and does not need anything else. It earns its returns by inventing a better everyday absorbent product (a facial tissue, a waistband, a training pant, an incontinence brief), taking the category lead, and building that lead into a brand that earns more per brand than anyone else's. The airline, the aviation services unit and the paper mills have become independent companies with their own owners, and the cash and management attention they used now go into product development, brand building and disciplined European expansion. It does not try to beat Rival P everywhere. It beats Rival P in the categories it chose, it says no to everything else, and it keeps doing this after the current chief executive leaves.

**Weakest link:** step 5. Today, reinvestment money leaks to the airline's $120M expansion (about 28% of last year's net income) and to commodity mills that need regular reinvestment. Rival P can outspend the company on research and advertising. The company needs every available dollar in this loop.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
1. Release capital by separating aviation and the paper class, then put that money into the product and brand-building steps for diapers, training pants and adult incontinence.
2. Defend the training-pants advantage before Rival P copies it. That means a next-generation product pipeline and patent protection.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the airline expansion.** Freeze any uncommitted part of the $120M. Separate the airline and aviation services through a spin-off or sale.
2. **Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.** Sell or spin them off.
3. **Stop treating tobacco and technical papers as core.** Assess their margins, then separate them, probably together with the rest of the paper class. Tobacco-industry ties also carry reputational risk for a consumer health brand. `[ASSUMPTION]`
4. **Stop launching premium niche products with limited everyday use.** Require evidence of everyday use and acceptable pricing before scaling, so the anti-viral tissue mistake isn't repeated.
5. **Stop trying to match Rival P's advertising dollar for dollar across all categories.** Concentrate spending where the company can be #1 or #2.
6. **Stop approving capital without segment and brand-level returns.** This applies especially to the next European tranche.
7. **Stop turning internal support functions into profit centers.**
8. **Keep captive pulp only if it's cheaper.** Make or buy pulp on cost and security of supply, not on habit. `[ASSUMPTION]`

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every capital request is judged on profit per consumer brand.
- Category entry requires a credible path to #1 or #2 within five years. `[ASSUMPTION on horizon]`
- Product superiority comes before advertising spend.
- Segment and brand return-on-capital data is reviewed quarterly by the board.

**Framework boundaries:** Brand teams have full freedom on product, pricing and marketing within absorbent personal care. Leaving that category requires the board.

**Bureaucracy check:** The airline's history shows a framework loose enough to let in any business that could make a profit. Close that gap with the Hedgehog Concept, not with more approval layers.
