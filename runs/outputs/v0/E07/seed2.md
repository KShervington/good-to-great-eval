# Good to Great: Calloway Reading Partners

## Verdict (one screen)

**Calloway is great at small scale, and it has started to fall.** Its mature schools still deliver what two randomized trials found. Its 31 newer schools have declined for two straight years, and the board packet asks the board to speed up the conditions that produced that decline. **Do not adopt One Million Readers or accept the $40M grant as written.** Restore the model first, then grow at the pace the model can bear.

- **Hedgehog Concept:** *Calloway is the best in the world at turning struggling six-year-olds into readers through daily tutoring by fully trained, closely coached tutors, inside districts that keep renewing because the results are real.*
- **Denominator:** cost per *additional* child reading at spring benchmark. Not students reached, and not cost per student.
- **Weakest flywheel link:** tutor preparation and coaching.
  - Training was cut from 30 to 18 hours.
  - Coach load rose from 12 to 19 tutors per coach.
  - Tutor retention fell from 81% to 68%.
  - The tutor exit surveys name these same two problems.
- **Top 3 stop-doing:**
  1. Stop growth whose pace is set by a calendar or a funder.
  2. Stop the 18-hour training and the drift in coach load.
  3. Stop the math pilot and ReadAlong.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** first- and second-year schools fell from **69% to 63% to 58%** at benchmark while mature schools held at 74%. The gap has tripled, from 5 points to 16. "Ramp-up" cannot explain a gap that is getting wider. Five new states would put every new school into the cohort that is currently failing.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - (a) New-school results split by tutor training hours and coach ratio. If the gap comes from harder schools rather than a thinner model, the diagnosis changes.
  - (b) The cost of restoring 30-hour training and a coach load of 15 or fewer.
  - (c) The revenue mix between district contracts and philanthropy, and why cost per student × students ($18.0M) is higher than the $14.6M budget.
  - (d) Whether the philanthropy would accept quality-gated terms.
  - (e) How One Million Readers counts a child as "reached."

Greatness potential: 55/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Five to ten years out, every Calloway school reaches mature-school results (74% or more at benchmark) by its second year. That holds for new and old schools, in the home districts and in any new state. An independent evaluation of the model *as it is actually delivered today* repeats the 0.27–0.31 SD finding every three to four years. Calloway grows every year, but a new district or state opens only after the previous cohort clears the quality bar. Each launch is staffed with fully trained tutors and coaches who have manageable loads. Districts still renew every year. The waitlist becomes a way to choose partners, not pressure to take everyone. Cost per additional reader falls because results rise, not because training gets cut. The tutor-to-teacher pipeline is a second dividend that makes districts depend on Calloway. The model is written down and owned by a program leader who has authority over launches, so it would survive the departure of any one person, including the CEO.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

> **Calloway is the best in the world at turning struggling six-year-olds into readers through daily tutoring by fully trained, closely coached tutors, inside districts that keep renewing because the results are real.**

*Status: working hypothesis. It is strong on past evidence and unconfirmed for new sites and new states.*

| Circle | Inside the hedgehog | Outside it (cannot be best at) |
|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | Early-reading intervention for struggling six-year-olds: a daily 30-minute dose from a trained, coached tutor inside district schools. Two RCTs (0.31 and 0.27 SD). Cost about 30% below the sector median ($1,830 vs ~$2,600). | Middle-school math. Parent apps. National storytelling as an end in itself. Rapid launches across many states: there is no evidence Calloway can do this, and its own newest schools suggest it cannot yet. |
| **Resource engine** (money, time, brand) | **Money:** district contracts renewed for 12 straight years, plus philanthropy that funds proven results. **Time:** tutors and coaches, which are the real constraint. **Brand:** two RCTs, a 100% renewal record, 22% of tutors becoming partner-district teachers, and two superintendents citing Calloway. | A brand built on CEO visibility and counts of children reached. |
| **Passion** | The founding "stubborn idea" that 30 minutes a day teaches a six-year-old to read. 84% staff engagement against a 71% sector benchmark. Tutors who stay in education. | Becoming a national player for its own sake. [ASSUMPTION: some leadership energy has moved here, based on 6 → 23 conferences.] |

**Choosing the denominator**

| Candidate | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per student ($1,830) | Reject | It falls when the model is thinned. It fell 4% while the cost per additional reader rose 8%. |
| Students reached (1M) | Reject | It counts a child who did not learn to read the same as one who did. |
| **Cost per additional child at spring benchmark** | **Choose** | It rises when quality slips and falls only when results or real efficiency improve. |

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 | Year 0, mature schools | Year 0, 1st–2nd yr schools |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark lift vs. similar non-served students | 35 pts | 33 pts | 31 pts | 35 pts | 19 pts |
| **Cost per additional benchmark reader** | **$5,457** | **$5,667** | **$5,903** | ~$5,229 | **~$9,632** [ASSUMPTION: same cost per student] |

*Caveat:* the comparison group is district data, not a randomized control, so these figures show direction only. The RCTs remain the causal evidence. Even so, the direction is clear: a reader produced in a new school costs about 1.8× as much as one produced in a mature school.

**Does the concept pass the three tests?**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes: 12 years of renewals, two RCTs, and a steady 74% in mature schools.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: math, ReadAlong, calendar-driven expansion into new states, and training cuts.
- **Uncomfortable to commit to?** Very. It means renaming the plan and renegotiating or turning down $40M.

**Evidence that would confirm it:**
1. New schools close most of the gap (to 70% or more) once training and coaching are restored.
2. An independent evaluation of the *current* model finds an effect of 0.25 SD or more.
3. One new district outside the current three reaches 70% or more by its second year before any new state launches.

## 3. The flywheel

```
   ┌─► 1. Fully trained tutors + close coaching        ◄ WEAKEST LINK (broken)
   │      (30 hrs pre-service · ≤12–15 tutors per coach)
   │                      │
   │                      ▼
   │   2. Daily 30-minute sessions, delivered with fidelity
   │                      │
   │                      ▼
   │   3. Children reach benchmark in EVERY cohort
   │      → cost per additional reader falls            ◄ denominator
   │                      │
   │                      ▼
   │   4. Proof: independent evaluation + 100% renewals ◄ second weak link
   │      + tutors-turned-teachers as district champions   (evaluation seat empty)
   │                      │
   │                      ▼
   │   5. District demand (waitlist) + results-based funding
   │                      │
   │                      ▼
   └── 6. Gated growth: open only what can be staffed at full
          strength; reinvest in tutors and coaches
```

The loop closes. Results earn renewals and funding, and that money flows back into tutor and coach capacity. Right now, though, the flywheel is being pushed from step 6 (growth) while step 1 is being cut to pay for it. That is the turning direction of a doom loop.

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. **Restore step 1 in the 31 newer schools this school year.** Make the 12 cut training hours a required in-service, and bring coach load to 15 or fewer by January.
2. **Rebuild step 4.** Hire the research director now, report results split by school cohort every term, and commission an independent evaluation of the current model.

**Doom-loop check**

| Doom-loop sign | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Big program to jump-start momentum | **Yes** | $40M, five new states in 30 months, a one-million-reader goal |
| New direction after new direction | **Emerging** | Math pilot, ReadAlong, One Million Readers, all in one year |
| Reaction without understanding | **Yes** | Training cut "to accelerate deployment." The new-school decline is explained as ramp-up without any diagnosis. |
| Inconsistency | **Yes** | The model's ingredients (training and coaching) changed while its name and claims stayed the same |

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop accepting growth whose pace is set by a calendar or a funder.** New sites open only when the previous cohort reaches 70% or more at benchmark [ASSUMPTION: threshold] and its coaches are already hired.
2. **Stop the 18-hour training.** Go back to 30 required hours before a tutor sees a child. Online modules can be added on top, never used as a substitute.
3. **Stop letting coach load drift.** Cap it at 15 and aim for 12.
4. **Stop the middle-school math pilot** at the end of this school year.
5. **Stop ReadAlong** before the beta launches.
6. **Stop measuring success by reach.** Retire "One Million Readers" as written and re-base the plan on additional readers at benchmark.
7. **Stop explaining away new-school results as ramp-up** until a diagnosis supports that explanation.
8. **Stop using the dashboard in place of evaluation.** The dashboard tells you *what* happened. An evaluation tells you *whether the model works*.
9. **Stop housing training and coaching under Growth.**
10. **Stop the conference circuit above about 8 a year** for the CEO.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values**
1. Every child can learn to read, and it is our job to prove it one child at a time.
2. The dose is sacred: 30 minutes, every day, from a trained and coached tutor.
3. Evidence over story.
4. Tutors are grown, not just deployed.
5. We are partners, not vendors, and we earn every renewal.

**Core purpose:** No child's life should be shaped by failing to learn to read.

**BHAG (about 25 years):** End early reading failure in every district Calloway serves, so that every struggling six-year-old reads at benchmark within two years, independently verified. Serve enough districts that the national numbers move.
