# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great run

## Verdict (one screen)

**Hedgehog Concept:** *We turn struggling six-year-olds into readers through thirty minutes a day with a well-prepared, closely coached tutor, producing more readers per dollar than anyone, and we grow only as fast as new sites can match our best.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** tutor preparation and coaching. Training is down 40% (30 to 18 hours). Coach load is up 58% (12 to 19 tutors per coach). Departing tutors cite exactly those two things. The evidence link is also cut: there has been no outcome study and no research director for nine months.

**Top three stop-doings:**
1. Stop the five-states-in-30-months launch in its current form.
2. Stop cutting training and coaching to lower cost per student.
3. Stop the math pilot and ReadAlong, both off-mission and both started because districts asked.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** new schools are getting worse every year. First- and second-year schools reached benchmark at 69%, then 63%, then 58%, while mature schools held at 74%. Ramp-up explains why new schools trail mature ones. It does not explain why each new cohort does worse than the last. The $40M grant would put more sites than ever on the version of the model that is performing worst.

**On tonight's three decisions:**
1. Don't adopt One Million Readers as written.
2. Don't accept the grant as offered. Counter with a fidelity-gated version, and be willing to walk away.
3. Approve an amended budget that restores training and coaching, funds the research director, and drops the launch team and ReadAlong.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Board size: do three funder seats amount to control?
- Whether the funder will accept phasing.
- New-school results broken out by coach ratio and training cohort, to test whether the cuts caused the decline.
- Completion rates for the online modules.
- What "cost per student" includes: $1,830 × 9,840 = $18.0M, against a $14.6M budget.
- Funding mix between district contracts and philanthropy.

Greatness potential: 55/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway is the original stubborn idea, defended. Every child it serves gets thirty minutes a day with a tutor who had full pre-service training and a coach with time for them. Every new school reaches mature-school results within two years, and the organization opens new schools only when the last cohort has proven that. A standing evaluation function re-proves the effect on the model *as it is currently run*, not on a version from seven years ago. Growth comes first from the 41-school waitlist in districts where Calloway already has coaching infrastructure. It enters new states one at a time, through proof sites seeded with experienced coaches. Funders and districts pay for readers, not for reach. Calloway says no to math, apps and adjacent asks so it can stay best-in-world at one thing. Its cost per student stays well below the sector median even after the coaching corps is restored. The CEO's visibility serves the model rather than outrunning it, and the training and coaching system lives in a seat and a set of mechanisms, not in one person.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence (working hypothesis):** *We turn struggling six-year-olds into readers through thirty minutes a day with a well-prepared, closely coached tutor, producing more readers per dollar than anyone, and we grow only as fast as new sites can match our best.*

This is not a new idea. It is the founding idea with the discipline put back in. The drift of the last two years has been away from it.

**Why the denominator matters.** Proxy calculation: (benchmark rate minus comparison rate) × students ÷ total cost.

| | Year −2 | Year −1 | Year 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Additional readers at benchmark | ≈2,560 | ≈2,845 | ≈3,050 |
| Total cost (students × cost per student) | $13.96M | $16.12M | $18.01M |
| **Additional readers per $1M** | **≈183** | **≈177** | **≈169** |

Cost per student fell 4%, but impact per dollar fell about 7.5%. The efficiency metric the organization celebrates is hiding a decline in the thing it exists to produce. *Caveat: the comparison group comes from district data and is not a causal counterfactual. Only an RCT gives a true effect, which is why the evaluation seat matters.*

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes: the RCT, the replication, renewals, the waitlist, the cost advantage.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: math, ReadAlong, the five-states-in-30-months pace, reach-based targets, the conference circuit.
- *Uncomfortable?* Very. It means renegotiating or declining $40M and admitting a decline the CEO's letter leaves out.

**Confirmation evidence (12–24 months):**
- New-school benchmark reverses upward: at least 63% next spring, within 5 points of mature schools by Year 2.
- A current-model effect estimate of at least 0.25 SD.
- Readers per $1M back at 183 or above by Year 3.
- Renewals continue.

## 3. The flywheel

```
 ┌─► 1. Well-prepared tutors
 │       (30 h pre-service, mandatory)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    2. Close coaching (≤14 tutors/coach)
 │       ◄── WEAKEST LINK (now 19:1, 18 h)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    3. Daily 30 min, delivered with fidelity
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    4. More readers at benchmark per $
 │       (the denominator)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    5. Fresh, independent proof
 │       ◄── BROKEN (no study, no research director)
 │            │
 │            ▼
 │    6. Renewals, waitlist, funders,
 │       tutor recruits, tutors-to-teachers
 │            │
 │            ▼
 └─── 7. Reinvest in prep + coaching FIRST,
         then add sites the next cohort can bear
```

**Current push (the next two turns):**
1. Restore links 1–2 to the Year −2 design. By the organization's own numbers, that design cost only about $80 more per student ($1,910 against $1,830), roughly $0.8M a year at today's scale. Even fully restored, cost per student stays about 25% below the sector median.
2. Reconnect link 5. Hire the research director now and run an outcome study on the *current* model this spring.

**The doom loop it is currently in:** a scale target leads to cutting training and coaching "to accelerate deployment." New-site quality falls, and the drop gets explained as ramp-up. The response is a bigger growth program ($40M, five states) to show momentum, plus new products (math, ReadAlong) launched because partners asked. That produces more new sites on a thinner model.

All four doom-loop signs are present:
- Reacting without understanding: a 4% stipend raise in response to exit surveys that blame preparation and coaching, not pay.
- New direction after new direction: math, the app, five states.
- A big program to jump-start growth: the grant.
- Inconsistency: core mechanisms cut while the brand is promoted.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the five-state, 30-month launch** in its current form. Replace it with gated entry, one proof state at a time.
2. **Stop opening schools faster than the last cohort can match mature schools.** Gate rule: no net new schools in a year when first- and second-year schools are more than 5 points below mature ones.
3. **Stop cutting training and coaching to lower cost per student.** Treat cost per student as a constraint to respect, never as the goal.
4. **Stop the middle-school math pilot** at the end of this school year.
5. **Stop ReadAlong** before the January beta.
6. **Stop counting "reach."** Count children reading at benchmark.
7. **Stop deferring the research director** "until after the expansion launches."
8. **Stop using the real-time dashboard in place of outcome evaluation.**
9. **Stop letting partner requests set the product strategy.**
10. **Stop the conference circuit.** Six a year at most.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (the fanatical consistency):**
- 30 minutes a day, every day, for every child.
- At least 30 hours of pre-service training, all mandatory. Online modules can add to it but never replace it.
- No more than 14 tutors per coach, with a target of 12.
- An annual internal outcome study, plus an independent evaluation every 3–4 years.
- The new-site parity gate on growth.

**Freedom within the framework:** sites and coaches decide scheduling, coaching technique, how to fit into the school, and local tutor recruiting. They don't decide dosage, training hours, coach ratio or measurement.

**Bureaucracy check:** the merged "Talent and Growth" structure and the 4% stipend raise both compensate for a missing program-quality seat rather than fixing it. Pay was not the reason tutors gave for leaving.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** every child who struggles to read at six learns to read.

**Core values:**
1. Every child can learn to read.
2. Evidence over story: we re-prove the model as it is actually run.
3. Tutors are educators we prepare and coach, not volunteers we deploy.
4. Frugal everywhere except the mechanism.
5. We partner with districts. We don't try to be everything for them.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *One Million Readers, re-dated and redefined: one million children reading at spring benchmark through Calloway-quality tutoring by Year 20.*
- The math: about 1.35M student-years at mature-school quality, which means compounding about 18% a year for twenty years. That is close to the 14–18% growth of the last two years, sustained for two decades *without losing quality*.
- The audacity is in the endurance, not the sprint. The brand name survives, and the definition becomes honest.

**Clock-building mechanisms.** When the founding COO left, the quality system eroded within about a year, which shows the system lived in a person.
- A new-site parity gate written into board policy.
- A board Program Quality and Evaluation committee.
- An evaluation cadence written into the budget: an annual internal study plus an independent study every 3–4 years.
- A training and coaching standard owned by a seat, with mandatory, tracked completion.
- CEO evaluation based on readers per dollar and new-site parity.
- A tutor-to-coach career ladder.
- Leading-indicator alarms on the dashboard: coach ratio, training completion, dosage and tutor retention by site.
