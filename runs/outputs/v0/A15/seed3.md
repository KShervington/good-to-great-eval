# Good to Great: Halberd Tobacco Company

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
*Halberd will be the best in the world at turning a very few cigarette brands, Ridgeway first, into the strongest and most consistent brand identities in the industry. It will win adult smokers who are already switching brands, carry those brands across borders, and earn more profit per thousand cigarettes than any rival.*

**Weakest link in the flywheel:** the step from *brand identity* to *smokers switching to Ridgeway*. That step currently runs on broadcast image advertising (the cowboy on TV), and limits on cigarette advertising are now being debated in Congress. If that channel closes, the flywheel loses its main push.

**Top 3 stop-doing:**
1. Stop buying small unrelated companies (razors, shaving cream, gum). Freeze all acquisitions now.
2. Stop spreading advertising money across the long tail of brands. Fund Ridgeway plus one or two premium filters.
3. Stop publicly disputing the health science on positions the company has not examined itself.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** two weeks ago the government's own expert committee concluded that the product causes lung cancer. The company is answering with public denial instead of an internal reckoning. Nobody inside Halberd knows how sales or regulation will move.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Ridgeway's current U.S. share and trend.
- Profit per thousand cigarettes, by brand.
- P&L of the non-tobacco businesses.
- Post-report smoking behaviour, split filter vs. non-filter.
- International share of profit.

If Ridgeway has stalled, the Hedgehog Concept is wrong. If the diversified units are highly profitable, the stop-doing list changes.

**Overall:** viable path to great, built on thin evidence. 7 of 11 gates are CONDITIONAL. There are two root causes: **no current financial numbers**, and **an unresolved health question with a weak truth climate**.

Greatness potential: 30/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halberd is a narrow company that wins by focus, not size. It funds a handful of brands, led by Ridgeway. It never changes Ridgeway's identity: one image, one distinctive box, one consistent blend. Ridgeway takes share from much larger, more diffuse rivals at every moment when smokers reconsider their brand. The filter shift is the current example, and regulation-driven change will be the next. The company exports and licenses those same brands into every market that will take them, so each new country compounds the same identity rather than adding complexity. It does not pretend that the health findings don't exist. It builds an internal truth function that tells leadership what the science and the sales data actually say, and it designs its brands to survive warning labels and advertising limits. It doesn't scatter capital into razors and gum as a hedge. Under the Collins test, "great" means sustained outperformance of the market over 15+ years, achieved without depending on one CEO's personal taste.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop acquiring small unrelated companies.** Freeze now. Any future non-tobacco move must be one deliberate, coherent bet that passes the Hedgehog test, not a collection of small hedges.
2. **Stop funding the long tail of brands.** Harvest the older non-filter brands, possibly including the namesake brand `[ASSUMPTION: it is non-filter and declining]`.
3. **Stop matching rivals' new-brand launches.** Halberd can't out-launch a bigger company.
4. **Stop changing Ridgeway's identity.** No new figures, no repositioning, no box redesign.
5. **Stop CEO-level public claims that the science is wrong.** Make no scientific statements the company hasn't itself verified. Route evidence questions through the new evidence function.
6. **Stop any marketing whose reach is mainly people who don't already smoke, especially the young.** It is ethically indefensible given the report, and it is the fastest route to severe regulation. The flywheel should run on brand switching among adult smokers.
7. **Stop treating packaging and paper as a growth platform** unless it serves Halberd's own packs.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every marketing dollar goes to a focus brand.
- Ridgeway's identity is consistent in every market.
- Monthly brand-share and profit-per-thousand review.
- Bad news reaches the board unfiltered.

**Framework boundaries:**
- *Fixed:* brand managers and country leads can't change the focus-brand list or the core identity.
- *Free:* they choose media, retail tactics and local execution within those limits.

**Bureaucracy check:** none is visible in the dossier. Watch whether approval layers grow around the CEO's personal brand control.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values (proposed, to test with leadership):**
1. **Craft:** blend and pack quality that smokers can feel.
2. **Brand integrity:** one identity, kept consistently.
3. **Underdog nerve:** winning by focus, not size.
4. **Tell ourselves the truth:** this one is new, and it conflicts with the current public stance. Adopting it is the test.
5. **Adult choice:** sell to adults who have already chosen to smoke, and only to them.

**Core purpose (candidate):** to make the cigarette brands that adult smokers are proudest to carry.

Honest note: a purpose that goes beyond money is thin for a product now officially linked to cancer. This is why Gate 9 is CONDITIONAL.

**BHAG (25 years):** make Ridgeway the best-selling cigarette brand in the world. This follows directly from the Hedgehog Concept: one identity, carried across borders.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A written Ridgeway brand bible owned by a successor team, not the CEO.
- A board-level evidence report every quarter.
- A capital-allocation rule: no spending outside the focus brands without board review against the Hedgehog Concept.
- Country leads who replicate the identity rather than reinvent it.
