# Halberd Tobacco Company: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halberd wins by putting nearly all its money behind a very few image-led filter brands, Ridgeway first, and carrying one unchanging image into every market it can reach. It judges every decision by profit per brand.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** image → reach. Ridgeway's growth depends on mass advertising, and it only started on television last year. Advertising limits are exactly what Congress and the FTC are now debating. The flywheel has to be able to turn on the pack, print and outdoor alone.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop spreading advertising money across a long tail of brands.
2. Stop buying small, unrelated businesses (razors, shaving cream, gum) as a "hedge."
3. Stop staking the company's credibility on publicly disputing the Surgeon General's report.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Two weeks ago the federal government's expert committee concluded that the product causes lung cancer, and the FTC has proposed pack warnings. Nobody knows how big the volume hit will be. The plan must work in a world where that finding stands and regulation tightens. It cannot rely on the finding being overturned.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- Profit and return on capital by brand. This decides how hard to concentrate.
- Ridgeway's current share.
- Post-report consumer behavior, filter versus non-filter.
- Profitability of the non-tobacco units.
- Advertising spend per share point compared with rivals.

Greatness potential: 55/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halberd is no longer a six-way also-ran with a drawer full of brands and side businesses. It is a focused brand-builder.
- **Ridgeway** is the flagship. It has one image (the cowboy), one box (the crush-proof flip-top) and one blend, held unchanged year after year and taken market by market across the world through exports and licensing.
- **The premium filter brand** from the luxury house gets real backing as a second brand. Everything else is harvested.
- **Capital** follows profit per brand, not habit.
- **Smallness becomes an advantage.** Halberd cannot outspend the giants overall, but it can outspend them on the one brand that matters.
- **Health and regulation** are faced in the open. Halberd complies early, funds research it will actually listen to, and builds its brand so that it survives the loss of television.

**The three open decisions, answered by the Hedgehog Concept:**
- **Concentrate or spread?** Concentrate hard. Fund Ridgeway first, the premium filter brand second, and harvest the rest.
- **How far overseas?** As far as the Ridgeway image travels intact. International is the flywheel's second engine, not a side bet.
- **How much in non-tobacco?** None of the small unrelated kind. If the board insists on a hedge, make one deliberate, meaningful move into a consumer category where image-led brand-building and distribution genuinely transfer, and only after the core is funded. `[ASSUMPTION: a hedge is a board-level requirement]`

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Spreading advertising across many brands.** Fund Ridgeway first and the premium filter brand second, then give the rest minimal support and harvest them.
2. **Acquiring small, unrelated businesses.** Freeze new purchases now and review the razor, shaving-cream and gum units for divestment.
3. **Leading with public denial of the Surgeon General's findings.** It ties the company's credibility to a position that further evidence could keep eroding. Shift to "we take the report seriously, will comply, and are funding research."
4. **Changing the Ridgeway image.** No new figures, no campaign-by-campaign slogans, no repositioning.
5. **Matching the rival's stream of new brand launches.**
6. **Licensing abroad without image and quality controls.**
7. **Waiting for certainty before acting.** This was the 1950s filter mistake. Decide on current evidence and adjust.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- One image everywhere.
- Every capital request scored on profit per brand.
- Brand-level numbers reviewed monthly by the CEO.
- Blend and box consistency.
- Full, early compliance with whatever labeling the law requires.

**Framework boundaries (freedom within a framework):**
- **Fixed:** the image, the brand count and the denominator.
- **Free:**
  - Country managers set distribution, pricing and channels.
  - The agency chooses executions within the image.

**Bureaucracy check:** Any committees or overhead that exist only to manage tail brands or unrelated subsidiaries go away with them.
