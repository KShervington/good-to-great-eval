# Good to Great: Halberd Tobacco Company

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Halberd wins by concentrating everything behind a few consistently built brands, Ridgeway first, and earning more profit per brand-loyal adult smoker than anyone else, at home and worldwide.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** image advertising turning into adult switching. The cowboy campaign and the new TV push are Ridgeway's engine, and ad limits are now being debated in Congress and at the FTC.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Stop spreading advertising money across the whole portfolio.
  2. Stop buying unrelated businesses (razors, shaving cream, gum) as a health hedge.
  3. Stop publicly disputing the Surgeon General's finding.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** the government's expert committee has concluded that the product causes lung cancer. Warnings are proposed and regulation is coming. Halberd's CEO is publicly denying the company's central fact, which is the opposite of the Stockdale Paradox.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Ridgeway's current share and growth rate.
  - Profit and return on capital by brand.
  - Profitability of the non-tobacco units.
  - Early consumer data on quit and switch rates after the report.

  If Ridgeway's growth is flattening, or the premium brands carry the profits, the Hedgehog Concept shifts.

`Greatness potential: 58/100`

*Evidence basis:* this ran in autonomous mode. No memory, past chats or web research were available, so every finding rests on the dossier alone. Inferences are marked `[ASSUMPTION]`.

*Note on the subject:* this plan applies Collins's business definition of great, which means sustained outperformance of the market. That definition says nothing about whether customers end up better off. Here the government's experts have just concluded that the product causes cancer. The plan therefore rules out two things by design: any reliance on denying the evidence, and any marketing aimed at minors. Readers should weigh the strategy with that limit in view.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Halberd is no longer the sixth-place company that tries to match larger rivals brand for brand. It is the most focused company in the industry. One flagship, Ridgeway, has a single unchanging image and a single unchanging product: the same blend, the same flip-top box and the same cowboy. One or two premium companions sit beside it. All of it is carried into every market the company can reach through exports and tightly controlled licenses. Its profit per loyal smoker is the highest in the industry because it spends behind few brands and keeps them consistent for decades. It treats the health evidence as a fact to plan around rather than a claim to fight in public. It is building product science, regulatory capability and pack-based brand identity before advertising limits arrive, so that the momentum it has already earned survives them.

- **Does it rule things out?** Yes. It excludes razors, gum, brand sprawl, and new-brand races.
- **Is it uncomfortable to commit to?** Yes. It concentrates the company on cigarettes in the very month the health case became official.

**Confirmation evidence needed:**
1. Ridgeway's share is still rising after the report.
2. Ridgeway's profit per unit is at or above the portfolio average.
3. Licensed markets are growing without diluting the brand.

**Status:** working hypothesis.

**Current push:**
- **(a)** Shift the ad budget decisively into Ridgeway now, while filter switching is still under way and before advertising limits land.
- **(b)** Make the pack itself carry the brand. The flip-top box, the colors and the cowboy identity should work without TV, so that link 1→3 survives restrictions.

**Doom-loop check:** there are early signs.
- Four unrelated acquisitions in four years.
- A temptation to answer a rival's "stream of new brands" with brands of Halberd's own.
- Public denial of the science, which is reaction without understanding.

None is fatal yet. Each is listed on the stop-doing list.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop funding the long tail of brands.** The namesake brand and the other legacy brands get maintenance spending only.
2. **Stop unrelated acquisitions.** No more razors, gum, shaving products or "hedge" businesses. Freeze deal-making and review the existing units for sale.
3. **Stop publicly disputing the Surgeon General's conclusion.** It damages credibility with regulators and blinds the company internally.
4. **Stop answering rivals' new-brand launches with new brands.** Breadth is the larger rivals' game.
5. **Stop changing Ridgeway's image.** No new figures, no repositioning. The cowboy is the brand.
6. **Stop licensing on loose terms.** Each foreign licensee must meet one blend, pack and image standard, or there is no license.
7. **Stop any marketing that reaches minors.** This holds as an ethical line and as a regulatory inevitability [ASSUMPTION about regulatory direction].

**Truth climate:** this is weak. When the leader's public position is that the central fact is unproven, people below him have little room to bring bad news. A red-flag mechanism is needed: an internal science and consumer-research review that reports to the board regardless of what the company says in public.

**Charisma check:** the risk is real. A hands-on, product-obsessed CEO who personally designed the flagship is exactly the kind of leader whose certainty can suppress dissent.

**Faith statement:** *Halberd will prevail because it has the industry's most coherent brand and the discipline to concentrate on it, as long as it faces the health facts instead of fighting them.*

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **Craft:** the product is made the same way every time.
2. **Consistency:** the brand image does not change once it works.
3. **The underdog's drive** to win against bigger rivals.
4. **Candor about evidence, internally and with regulators.** This is a recommended value, not a current one [ASSUMPTION].

**Core purpose:** to make the cigarette adult smokers choose and stay loyal to, with full honesty about the risks. That candor is what makes this purpose defensible after the report.

**BHAG (20–25 years):** *Make Ridgeway the best-selling cigarette in the world.* It follows directly from the Hedgehog Concept and from the exporter advantage. The moral weight of this goal is noted in Fact 7.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- Brand standards that are written down and enforced across all licensees.
- An internal science function that reports to the board.
- A CEO successor bench.
- A permanent long-term agency partnership.
- A capital-allocation rule: no spending outside the Hedgehog Concept without board approval.

## 9. Next 90 days

1. **Get the facts.** Produce brand-level profit and ROC, unit-level economics for the non-tobacco businesses, and a rapid consumer survey on how smokers are responding to the report. *Owner: CFO and head of market research.*
2. **Reallocate the ad budget** to Ridgeway plus one premium companion, using the fact base from action 1. *Owner: CEO and Chicago agency.*
3. **Freeze non-tobacco acquisitions** and begin a sale review of the razor, shaving-cream and gum units. *Owner: CEO and board.*
4. **Change the health posture.** End public denial, hire a scientific and research director and a regulatory lead, and build scenarios for warnings and ad limits. These should include pack-based brand identity and filter R&D. *Owner: CEO; general counsel.*
5. **Standardize global Ridgeway.** Write a single license standard covering blend, pack and image, and rank priority export markets. *Owner: international head.*
