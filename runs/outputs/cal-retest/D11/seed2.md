# Heart Failure Transitions Program (HFTP), St. Aldric Regional Medical Center: Good to Great

## One-screen verdict

**HFTP is a well-run team, but it is not yet proven to work.** Its headline result holds at 30 days and nearly disappears by 90. Before it takes on anything new, it should prove or repair its effect beyond day 30. Do not take on Hospital at Home now.

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *We find and fix the medication and home failures that bring St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients back, and we stay until they stop coming back.*
- **Weakest flywheel link:** the step from "fix the failures" to "patients stay home through day 90." The Year 0 gap at 90 days is 1.2 points.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Don't lead the Hospital at Home launch now.
  2. Stop presenting the enrolled vs. not-enrolled 30-day rate as the program's effect.
  3. Stop graduating every patient at day 30 by the calendar.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** 90-day readmission was **32.7% for enrolled patients vs. 33.9% for non-enrolled**. The 5.9-point gap at 30 days shrinks to 1.2. Between days 31 and 90, enrolled patients were readmitted *more* often (16.8 vs. 12.1 points). The program may be delaying readmissions, not preventing them. This figure appears only in a pharmacist's accreditation checklist, not in the memo.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - A risk-adjusted 90-day comparison, with nursing-facility, declined and unreachable patients separated out.
  - 90-day mortality and days alive out of hospital.
  - Finance's cost and margin per heart-failure readmission, plus bed occupancy.
  - Heart failure's share of the federal readmissions penalty in dollars.
  - A log of the medication problems the pharmacists find.

Greatness potential: 25/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great HFTP does not enroll everyone in the same six touches and say goodbye at day 30. At discharge it identifies the heart-failure patients most likely to come back. That includes the ones the current phone-based process loses: decliners, the unreachable, and the medically complex. It puts its scarcest skills on those patients:

- pharmacists who untangle regimens like Mr. D.'s double diuretic;
- community health workers who fix what is wrong in the home;
- a licensed social worker for the hardest social situations.

It stays with each patient until clinical criteria say they are stable, not until a penalty window closes. It measures itself on days alive and out of hospital through day 90, risk-adjusted. It can show Finance the net value it returns per program dollar. It becomes indispensable to the hospital because its result is real and durable, not because it absorbed new service lines.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *We find and fix the medication and home failures that bring St. Aldric's highest-risk heart-failure patients back, and we stay until they stop coming back.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Ruled out, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** (narrow world: heart-failure patients in St. Aldric's region) | 30-day readmission reduction; home-based acute care; medication reconciliation and home-failure repair; reaching the hard-to-reach | **Finding and fixing medication and home failures for the highest-risk patients** | 30-day reduction may be a timing effect. Acute home care (Hospital at Home) needs physician oversight, 24/7 escalation and acute monitoring. HFTP has done none of these. |
| **Resource engine** (budget and headcount leverage) | Penalty avoided; enrollment volume; 30-day readmissions avoided; hospital days avoided | **Hospital days avoided through day 90, per program dollar** | Enrollment volume measures activity, not value. 30-day avoidance rewards deferral. |
| **Passion** | Home; patient stories; expansion | **Keeping people at home** ("make it home and stay home"; Mr. D.; 214 scales) | "Leading a bigger department" is a career goal, not the team's passion. |

**Denominator:** *hospital days avoided through day 90, per program dollar.* Translating this into dollars depends on numbers Finance has not yet produced: cost per readmission and whether beds are capacity-constrained. Under fee-for-service, an avoided readmission can also mean lost revenue `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Tests:**
- **Explains past wins?** Partly. Mr. D. (a medication fix) and the scales (a home fix) fit. The program-level result is unproven.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: Hospital at Home, enrollment as a headline target, and uniform intensity.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It turns down a promotion-shaped opportunity, and it may mean lighter touch for low-risk patients.

**Confirmation tests (12 months):**
1. A risk-adjusted 90-day effect exists for the highest-risk group.
2. Pharmacist logs show medication problems are common and get resolved.
3. Patients who received a home or medication fix show more days at home than matched patients who did not.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Don't lead the Hospital at Home launch now.** Recommend the CMO appoint a separate leader. Offer HFTP as the transitions partner. Revisit in 12 months if HFTP's own effect is proven.
2. **Stop reporting enrolled vs. not-enrolled 30-day readmission as the program's effect.** The groups are not comparable: nursing-facility, declined and unreachable patients fill the comparison arm.
3. **Stop graduating every patient at day 30.** Graduate on clinical and social criteria.
4. **Stop treating 75% enrollment as the headline goal.** Hold the weekend shift until the 90-day analysis is done, and use that capacity for Friday pharmacist coverage first.
5. **Stop giving every patient the same six touches.** Tier intensity by risk.
6. **Stop relying on the phone as the only route into the program.** Unreachable by phone currently means excluded.
7. **Stop citing "94% would recommend" as evidence of value.** Only 391 of 1,146 patients (34%) responded, and satisfaction is not an outcome.
8. **Stop letting the penalty window set the protocol.** The program's clock should follow the patient's risk, not the federal readmissions program's measurement window.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every enrolled patient gets pharmacist reconciliation, and the problems found are logged.
- Every patient is tracked to day 90.
- The 90-day dashboard is reviewed monthly with the VP.

**Framework and freedom:**
- *Framework:* risk tier assignment, a pharmacist touch, 90-day tracking and graduation criteria.
- *Freedom:* within a tier, navigators and CHWs choose the type, timing and frequency of touches.
- *Bureaucracy check:* the fixed six-touch calendar is a rule standing in for judgment. With an experienced, low-turnover team, the team's judgment can replace it.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** keep people living with heart failure alive and at home.

**Core values:**
1. Days at home are the measure.
2. Tell the truth about outcomes, including the ones that don't flatter us.
3. Fix root causes, not symptoms.
4. The hardest-to-reach patient comes first.

**BHAG (10-year, one line):** By Year 10, St. Aldric's heart-failure patients spend more days alive at home in the 90 days after discharge than those of any hospital in the state, and no readmission is caused by an unreconciled medication.

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A monthly 90-day dashboard.
- The red-flag reporting rule.
- A written risk-tier protocol and graduation criteria.
- Ana developed as deputy.
- Pharmacist cross-coverage.
- A named Finance counterpart.
