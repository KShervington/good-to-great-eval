## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Northfield-Brandt is a focused personal-care and tissue company. It does one thing better than anyone, Rival P included: it invents absorbent and tissue product categories and then owns them. Facial tissue, premium diapers, training pants and adult incontinence are the existing proof. There is no airline, no aircraft hangar and no newsprint mill. The capital from those businesses has gone into material science, converting capacity and brand support for category leaders. Europe is entered one category at a time, and only where leadership is plausible. Every brand has its own P&L, and capital follows profit per category-leading brand. The chief executive who led the first transformation has handed over to a successor who runs the same discipline without needing him. Returns stay ahead of the market and of Rival P through a CEO change, a commodity-cycle downturn and the next diaper-technology war.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop the airline expansion** beyond firm commitments, and run a process to separate aviation (airline and maintenance) through a sale or spin-off.
2. **Stop reinvesting in newsprint and printing-paper mills.** Exit them through the cycle.
3. **Stop owning tobacco and technical papers.** Separate them into a focused owner.
4. **Stop allocating capital without returns by segment and brand.** Retire total-sales growth as a goal.
5. **Stop expanding pulp capacity** beyond internal consumer needs.
6. **Stop launching premium novelties with no category-defining use.** Every launch must answer: can this become #1 or #2 in a category?
7. **Stop matching Rival P everywhere.** Fight in categories and countries where leadership is winnable. In Europe, stop committing new plants until existing ones show a path to leadership.
