## 1. The great version in one paragraph

A great Larder Line is narrower and more consequential than it is today. It is the organization Corbridge relies on for **cold, perishable, high-value food**: dairy, meat and other protein. That food spoils in hours and no one else in the city can move it safely. It treats Freshway's markdown app as an ally rather than a threat. Shoppers buy what they will, and Larder Line reliably takes what's left, with pickup timing written into an agreement. It delivers **by demand, not by supply**: every week agencies tell it what they moved, what they threw out and what they need. It reports households reached and food actually used, not just pounds loaded. Its volunteer drivers stay for years because the work clearly matters. It survives Teddy, because the routines, the data and a successor are already in place. It says no to good ideas, including the café, so that it can be the best at one thing.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop pursuing the Larder Kitchen as a Larder Line program.** Don't apply for the $150,000 grant as lead. That is about 24% of the current budget, for a business whose comparable took three years to break even. Offer a letter of support and a food-supply agreement instead.
2. **Stop the Monday composting van route.** The scarcest asset, a reefer van, is hauling compost.
3. **Sunset the backpack pilot.** Hand it off within the school term.
4. **Pause gleaning.** Agencies already compost surplus produce.
5. **Stop using total pounds at pickup as the headline.** Keep tracking it, but lead with households and used pounds.
6. **Stop leaving the households field blank.** "Industry standard" is not an answer to a funder's question.
7. **Stop delivering by supply.** No more pushing extra bread or produce on agencies that can't move it.
8. **Don't buy the third van this year.** Fix van #2 first. Revisit once the Freshway outlook is known.
9. **Stop saying yes by default.** Every new ask must answer one question: does it make the cold chain better? If not, who else can do it?
