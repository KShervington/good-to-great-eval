# Good to Great: Stanwick Mailing Systems

## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Stanwick runs the business mail room: the meters, mailing machines, service and financing that get a company's mail out the door. It grows by earning more profit per business customer, not by entering markets where others lead.*

- **Weakest flywheel link:** turning the field-service relationship into more mail-room products and higher **profit per customer**. Nobody has measured it. The diversification so far went into copiers, retail tags and point-of-sale (POS) instead of deeper into the mail room.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. No more ventures in markets led by bigger, better-funded firms. The $42M POS write-off was the lesson.
  2. No new growth capital for copiers until they prove a return above cost of capital.
  3. Stop treating total revenue and "office equipment company" as the goals.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** Stanwick is choosing its growth direction during a CEO handover with **no profit or return-on-capital figures by business**. Its one measured bet outside the mail room cost about 11% of a year's revenue.
- **Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
  - Return on capital by line. If copiers earn well above cost of capital on the same field force, the Hedgehog could widen to "the business back office."
  - Profit per customer.
  - Revenue per meter customer.
  - The new CEO's track record.
  - How the new Postal Service plans to price metered mail.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Stanwick has stopped being a mid-sized office-equipment conglomerate. It is the indispensable operator of America's business mail rooms. Each leased meter is the anchor of a relationship. A field technician visits regularly, the customer trusts Stanwick with prepaid postage, and Stanwick's credit arm finances every machine that touches outgoing mail: scales, folders, inserters, addressers and, later, electronic meters. Stanwick knows postal rules and rates better than its customers do, so it saves them money every time it visits. It measures itself by profit per business customer. It says no to copier price wars and retail electronics. It wins even though its patents are free to competitors, because its moat is the installed base and the service network, not intellectual property. It outlasts its 54-year chairman because its capital rules and metrics carry on without him.

**Denominator:** profit per business mailing customer.

**Tests:**
- *Explains past wins?* Yes. Meters, leasing and the credit subsidiary all deepen the same relationship.
- *Rules things out?* Yes: copiers, POS and probably price-marking.
- *Uncomfortable?* Yes. It puts a #1-ranked business (price-marking) and a six-year-old division (copiers) outside the core.

**Confirmation evidence (status: working hypothesis):**
1. Customers with several mail-room products earn materially more profit per customer than meter-only customers.
2. Field-service cost per customer falls as products per customer rise.
3. Copiers earn below cost of capital. If they earn well above it on shared field infrastructure, revisit the Hedgehog and consider widening it to "the business back office."

The cure is consistency on this loop for years, not another bold move.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop entering markets led by bigger, better-funded firms.** No more retail electronics or POS.
2. **Stop funding copier growth.** No price war with the copier leader. Freeze capital pending a ROC review.
3. **Stop measuring success by total revenue** and by the share of sales that isn't meters.
4. **Stop buying growth.** No acquisitions outside the business mail stream.
5. **Stop relying on patents as the moat.** The decree makes them free. Invest in service, the installed base and postal know-how instead.
6. **Stop extending price-marking into adjacent retail systems**, which is the same path that led to POS.
7. **Stop the credit arm financing non-core or third-party equipment.**

**Non-negotiable disciplines:**
- Every new product must sell to existing mailing customers and be serviceable by the existing field force.
- Capital goes by profit per customer and return on capital, never by revenue growth.
- Every service visit leaves the customer's mailing cost lower or their mail room simpler.

**Framework boundaries:** Inside the mail room, regions and teams have autonomy on how to serve and sell. Outside it, the answer is no unless the board changes the Hedgehog.

**Bureaucracy check:** no data `[ASSUMPTION]`. Look for approval layers that grew up to supervise the diversified units. Those layers should shrink as the units do.
