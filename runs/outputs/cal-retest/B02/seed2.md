# Homestead Mortgage Association: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Homestead can be the best in the world at underwriting, standardizing and guaranteeing American mortgage credit, and it should earn its living from the fee on each dollar of credit risk it can carry on its own capital.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** investor trust. Investors accept Homestead's guarantee and debt mainly because they assume Washington stands behind them. Thin capital and auditable numbers are not what earns that trust today. If the assumed backing weakens, the loop stalls.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop using the retained portfolio as the earnings growth engine.
2. Stop paying executives and staff on a management-defined "operating" EPS number, and retire the public EPS targets and the 16-year streak.
3. Stop running interest-rate mismatches large enough to draw supervisory action. The −14-month duration gap was one.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Homestead is levered about 31:1 and holds only **$0.9B of capital above the legal minimum against $887B of assets**, about 0.1% of slack. The people steering it are paid on an earnings figure they define themselves. On the GAAP basis, which they do not define, earnings fell 22%.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Hedge positions and derivative values.
- Accounting policies for amortizing premiums, discounts and hedges.
- Credit profile of recent purchases by product.
- Earnings split between the portfolio and guarantee businesses.

A clean independent accounting review plus strong standalone guarantee economics would raise the score materially. Signs of earnings management would lower it sharply, and Gates 2 and 3 would likely turn to FAIL.

Greatness potential: 15/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Homestead is a **credit utility with a mission, not a leveraged spread fund with a charter**:
- It earns most of its profit from guarantee fees priced to the credit risk it takes, and holds capital that a hard, published stress test shows can absorb a severe housing downturn without taxpayer help.
- Its retained portfolio is small, hedged tightly and kept for liquidity and mission purposes, not for growing EPS.
- It reports one set of numbers (GAAP), explains the volatility plainly, and pays its people on multi-year, risk-adjusted results.
- It meets affordable-housing goals by guaranteeing loans it has priced and underwritten well, so the families it serves keep their homes.
- When the next downturn comes, it is the institution that keeps the conforming market liquid. Critics who now attack its structure end up relying on it.

Under this plan, growth will be slower and the earnings streak will end. That cost is real, and it is the cost of becoming great.

1. When the guarantee business is broken out and fully capitalized, it earns returns above its cost of capital.

3. Credit performance of affordable-goal loans holds up when they are priced for risk.

The dossier has the data for none of these tests. **Verdict: CONDITIONAL.**

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop growing the retained portfolio for earnings.** Freeze net growth now, then shrink it toward a liquidity and mission size.
2. **Stop public EPS targets and streak language.** No more "doubling" and no more "16 straight years."
3. **Stop paying on operating EPS.** Pay instead on multi-year GAAP results, capital strength, risk limits and credit performance.
4. **Stop presenting "operating" earnings as the main measure.** Lead with GAAP, explain the volatility, and show any non-GAAP figure as a reconciliation with an independent sign-off.
5. **Stop running interest-rate gaps beyond a tight, board-set band.** This includes gaps tolerated "temporarily" during refinancing waves.
6. **Stop meeting affordable-housing goals with credit that is not priced for its risk.** Goals met with loans that later default work against the mission.
7. **Stop treating the implied government backing as a competitive advantage.** Plan as if it could be withdrawn.
8. **Stop holding capital near the legal minimum.** Treat the legal minimum as a floor that should never come into view.
9. **Stop fighting critics' capital and portfolio proposals by reflex.** Adopt the reasonable parts first, on your own terms.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core values:**
1. **Numbers you can trust.** One set of books, explained plainly.
2. **Stewardship of public trust.** Act as if the backing could disappear tomorrow.
3. **Credit discipline.** Every loan we touch should be one we would hold.
4. **Homeownership that lasts.** Success is families who stay in their homes, not loans originated.

**Core purpose:** to keep American mortgage credit liquid, fairly priced and stable through every cycle, so that families, especially those with modest incomes, can own homes they keep.

**BHAG (20 years):** through the next housing downturn and beyond, keep the conforming mortgage market open and liquid **without ever needing a dollar of taxpayer support.**

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A capital policy set by the board well above the statutory minimum, with a published annual severe-stress test.
- An independent CRO and Chief Accounting Officer with direct board lines and power to halt activity.
- Pay tied to multi-year GAAP results, risk limits and loan-level credit performance, with clawbacks.
- A board-set duration-gap band, enforced by automatic escalation.
- Loan-level disclosure of credit data to investors and the regulator.
