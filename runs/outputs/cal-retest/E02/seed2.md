# Ravenhill Drug Company: Good to Great

## Verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Ravenhill wins by being the most convenient pharmacy-anchored drugstore in every market it chooses to saturate: the best corner, the fastest prescription, the easiest refill. It measures everything by profit per customer visit.*

**Weakest flywheel link:** visits → profit. Prescriptions are the fastest-growing part of sales and the part payers are squeezing. More visits only build greatness if each visit earns more, through front-end attach, photo, and payer terms with a floor.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Buying chains to match a rival's store count.
2. Accepting third-party prescription contracts below a per-visit profit floor.
3. Opening any store that isn't the best corner in a market Ravenhill can saturate, including entering three new metros at once.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** prescriptions rose from 38.2% to 43.4% of sales in two years, while payers push prescription margins down and gross margin has started to slip (28.4% → 28.0%). The engine's growth is coming from its most-pressured part. Meanwhile, a merger is forming a rival with roughly twice the store count, built partly to gain leverage with payers.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- **Profit per customer visit versus profit per store.** If per-store economics explain past wins better than per-visit, the denominator changes.
- **Same-store sales.** If all growth comes from new stores, the flywheel isn't really turning.
- **Freestanding versus strip-center returns.**
- **Profitability of mail service and the managed-care subsidiary.**
- **Shareholder returns versus the market.**

Greatness potential: 70/100

**Overall:** good, with a credible path to great. The gate log shows 8 PASS, 3 CONDITIONAL and 0 FAIL. All three CONDITIONALs share one root problem: Ravenhill doesn't yet measure the unit economics its Hedgehog depends on.

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Ravenhill is the drugstore people reach without thinking about it: on the corner they already drive past, with a drive-through window and a prescription ready at any store in the network. It doesn't try to be the biggest chain by store count. It tries to earn the most per customer visit and to be dense enough in each chosen market that it is the default pharmacy there. It grows by picking and owning its own corners, not by buying other chains' leftover locations. It deals with payers from strength, because it offers convenient access to their members, and it walks away from contracts that don't pay. Its technology makes every visit faster and every pharmacist more productive. It still carries no debt and still exits anything that doesn't fit, on a deadline, as it has done for two decades.

- Ravenhill: $10.4B ÷ 2,085 stores ≈ **$5.0M sales per store**.

**Confirmation tests:**

- **Rules things out?** Yes. It rules out scale acquisitions, standalone PBM growth, mail-order as a growth bet, price wars, and mall or strip sites.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It means letting a rival become larger by store count without responding in kind, and walking away from some payer volume.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop considering scale acquisitions** made to keep pace with consolidating rivals. Exception: tuck-ins that are best-corner sites in markets where Ravenhill already has density.
2. **Stop accepting payer contracts** whose per-visit economics fall below a published floor, including the front-end attach those visits bring.
3. **Stop opening stores on second-best sites** (strip centers, malls, or any corner that isn't the best in its trade area).
4. **Stop entering new metros faster than density can follow.** Enter at most one of the three planned new metros this year unless each has a credible path to market leadership `[ASSUMPTION: density threshold to be defined]`.
5. **Stop treating "3,000 stores" as the goal.** The goal is profit per visit and market density. Store count follows from those.
6. **Stop open-ended format tests.** Rule on the mini-store within 12 months.
7. **Stop building the PBM as a business in its own right**, especially if its incentives (steering members to mail order, cutting pharmacy reimbursement) work against the stores.
8. **Stop adding general-merchandise categories** that don't sell on a quick convenience trip `[ASSUMPTION: some such categories exist]`.

## 7. Core ideology and BHAG

**Core values** `[ASSUMPTION: inferred from history, confirm with leadership]`:
1. The pharmacist's trust comes first.
2. Convenience is respect for the customer's time.
3. Financial conservatism: grow from earnings, not debt.
4. Exit what doesn't fit, on a deadline, without nostalgia.

**Core purpose:** to make it easy for people to stay healthy in their own neighborhood.

**BHAG (~20 years):** *Put a Ravenhill pharmacy on a convenient corner within easy reach of most Americans, and earn the industry's highest profit per customer visit the whole way there.*

**Clock-building mechanisms:**
- A codified site-selection model that applies the corner and density tests.
- A published payer profitability floor.
- A per-visit scorecard used in every store review.
- An exit-deadline rule for any business that falls outside the Hedgehog.
- A succession bench reviewed by the board every year.
