# Pacifica Bancorp: Good to Great verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** Pacifica can be the best bank in the world at serving the households, farms and small businesses of its home state at the lowest cost to serve, measured by **risk-adjusted profit per employee**.

**Weakest flywheel link:** *low-cost operation.* The branch network carries high fixed costs, the bank is behind in-state rivals on ATMs, and lifetime employment keeps the cost base rigid. Capped deposit rates used to hide all of this. Deregulation removes that cover over six years.

**Top three stop-doing items:**
1. Stop measuring and rewarding success by asset growth.
2. Stop growing developing-country and other cross-border lending under decentralized approval authority.
3. Stop holding new long-term fixed-rate mortgages on a balance sheet funded by short-term deposits.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** Record profits rest on a regulatory subsidy, cheap capped deposits, that has now been legislated away. Those deposits fund two things. One is a fixed-rate mortgage book that loses value as funding costs rise. The other is a fast-grown book of developing-country, farm and construction loans approved under decentralized authority. Nothing in the evidence shows anyone can size the losses in either.

**Missing numbers that would change the answer:**
- loan-loss reserves against actual loss experience, by category
- developing-country exposure as a multiple of capital
- the mortgage/deposit maturity gap
- expense ratio against peers
- headcount
- capital ratios

If reserves are thin and country exposure is a large multiple of capital, this stops being a greatness problem and becomes a survival problem. Gates 1 and 5 would then flip to FAIL.

**Successor call:** The board's choice between a "steady banker" and a "change agent" is a false one. Pacifica needs someone who will change the cost base and the credit culture, and who will narrow the business instead of entering new ones.

Greatness potential: 25/100

*The franchise is exceptional. But the bank already shows signs of decline stages 1–2, its losses are probably already embedded in the loan book, and the plan asks a large, lifetime-employment, growth-acclaimed institution to shrink its ambitions during a hard rate cycle. Even if it is executed well, enduring greatness is the less likely outcome.*

*Evidence basis: memory, past-run lookup and web research were unavailable in this run. Everything below rests on the dossier alone, and every inference is marked [ASSUMPTION].*

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

The great Pacifica is the bank of the ordinary people and small enterprises of its home state. That is the mission it was founded on, and it is the only market where it holds an advantage nobody can copy. It wins after deregulation by having the lowest cost to serve a customer in the state: it was early to records computerization, so it should be first to automate transactions. It lends mainly to customers it knows, under a centralized credit discipline that treats "no" as a respectable decision. It matches the maturities of its assets and liabilities so that rate swings are survivable rather than fatal. It reports one number above asset size: risk-adjusted profit per employee. It is smaller in ambition than today, possibly smaller in assets, and much harder to kill.

## 2. The Hedgehog Concept

**Sentence:** *Be the lowest-cost, most trusted bank for the households, farms and small businesses of our home state, and judge ourselves by risk-adjusted profit per employee.*

| Circle | Candidates considered | Kept | Explicitly *not* best-in-world at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best in the world at** | (a) World's largest bank; (b) international entrepreneurial lending; (c) home-state consumer and small-business banking; (d) consumer banking innovation | (c), with (d) as its accelerator | Sovereign and developing-country lending; venture capital; wholesale money-center banking; running unhedged interest-rate bets |
| **Economic engine** | profit per $ of assets; profit per branch; profit per customer relationship; **risk-adjusted profit per employee** | Risk-adjusted profit per employee | Asset growth, which in this business is the denominator of risk rather than of profit |
| **Deep passion** | Being the biggest; "action"; serving "the little guy" | Serving the little guy (founding purpose) and consumer innovation | Size for its own sake |

**Why this denominator:** Once deposit rates float freely, ordinary banking becomes a commodity, and the winner is the low-cost operator. Per-employee profit attacks the brutal fact directly, which is fixed costs plus lifetime employment. "Risk-adjusted" is there so the number can't be gamed by reaching for yield. The runner-up, profit per customer relationship, fits the founding purpose better but does nothing about cost. [ASSUMPTION]: this choice should be revisited once the expense-ratio data exists.

**Intersection tests:**
- *Does it explain past wins?* Yes. The bank was built "loan by loan and branch by branch" in its home state, and its consumer-product innovations spread across the industry.
- *Does it rule things out?* Yes: the venture-capital fund, new overseas branches, the sovereign-loan growth and portfolio fixed-rate mortgages.
- *Is it uncomfortable to commit to?* Very. It gives up the "world's largest" identity and puts pressure on lifetime employment.

**Confirmation tests (12–24 months):**
1. Home-state core deposits hold share as ceilings phase out.
2. Cost per transaction falls as ATM coverage closes the gap.
3. Risk-adjusted profit per employee rises while assets are flat.
4. Credit losses in home-state lending run below those in the growth categories.

**Current push (next one or two turns):**
- **Turn 1: Get the facts and stop the bleeding in [4].** Centralize credit authority, freeze growth in cross-border lending, and stop adding fixed-rate mortgages to the portfolio.
- **Turn 2: Push [1].** Close the ATM gap, and start reducing the cost base through attrition rather than layoffs.

**Today's real flywheel is a different loop, and it is breaking:**

> cheap capped deposits → lend into new categories and countries → assets grow → "world's largest" acclaim → more growth

Deregulation removes the first link.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop using asset growth as the measure of success** in reporting, in executive pay and in how the bank talks about itself.
2. **Stop growing loans to developing countries.** Make no new country commitments until the exposure-to-capital audit is done. Run off the existing book with discipline.
3. **Stop decentralized approval of large or concentrated credits.** Decentralization stays for small home-state lending.
4. **Stop originating long-term fixed-rate mortgages for the portfolio.** Offer adjustable rates, or originate fixed-rate loans for sale. [ASSUMPTION: a secondary market is available.]
5. **Stop the venture-capital direct-investment program.**
6. **Stop opening overseas branches.** Keep only those that serve home-state customers' trade.
7. **Stop growth in construction and agricultural lending beyond centrally set concentration limits.** Home-state farmers stay customers; concentration does not grow.
8. **Stop treating "action" as the overriding virtue in credit decisions.** In lending, a mistake of decision can cost far more than a mistake of indecision.
9. **Stop net headcount growth outside customer-facing and credit roles.**

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core purpose:** Make sound banking available to the ordinary people and small enterprises of our home state.
- **Core values:**
  1. Serve the little guy as well as the big one.
  2. Prudence before growth.
  3. Candor about risk; bad news travels fastest.
  4. Thrift: every dollar of cost is the customer's dollar.
  5. Innovate for the customer, not for the press.
- **BHAG (20-year horizon):** Become the most profitable large U.S. bank per employee, and the primary bank of most households and small businesses in our home state.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:**
  - an independent chief credit officer and risk committee
  - board-set limits on the maturity gap
  - executive pay tied to risk-adjusted profit per employee
  - a standing CEO succession pipeline, so the next transition is not improvised
  - mandatory post-mortems on credit losses
  - annual stop-doing reviews
