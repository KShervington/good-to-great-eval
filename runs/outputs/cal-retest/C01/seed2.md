## One-screen verdict

**Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):**
> Wexley turns clinicians' everyday frustrations into powered instruments and patient-handling equipment that make caregivers faster or care cheaper, in niches where it can be #1, and sells them through specialists who work alongside those clinicians.

**Weakest flywheel link:** *Specialist selling.* The company has virtually no effective sales force. Products can't reach surgeons and nurses, and frustrations can't flow back in to become the next product.

**Top 3 stop-doing items:**
1. Stop running the company without a budget or goals, with every decision made at the top.
2. Stop paying salespeople a flat salary unconnected to results.
3. Stop treating an IPO or an implant play as the next big move before the core engine is measured and running.

**Most urgent brutal fact:** The sales engine is gone, and nobody can say whether the company is profitable today. The only earnings figure in the dossier is a record from 22 years ago.

**Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
- Recent net income and margins **by product line**. These decide whether instruments or beds anchor the Hedgehog Concept.
- Sales history for the last 5–10 years, to show whether Wexley is growing, flat or declining.
- Market share in beds, stretchers and powered instruments.
- The hospital capital-spending and reimbursement outlook.

Greatness potential: 35/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Wexley is a disciplined, decentralized medical-device company. It no longer depends on one inventor or one family's judgment. Engineers and specialist salespeople spend their days with orthopedic surgeons, OR staff and nurses, and they turn what those clinicians struggle with into products that pass the founder's test: more efficient caregivers or lower treatment cost. Each product family is run as a business with its own budget, earnings goal and specialist sales team, and is expected to be #1 or #2 in its niche. Powered orthopedic instruments grow alongside the rise in joint-replacement surgery, without Wexley betting the company on implants against a pharma-backed rival. Earnings grow every year against a public internal target. The founder's name on the turning frame becomes a reputation the whole organization earns again with each new product.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop making every decision at the top.** Product lines get budgets and owners.
2. **Stop paying salespeople flat salary with no link to results.** Move to specialist reps on incentive-heavy pay, piloted first `[ASSUMPTION]` that incentives fit this market.
3. **Stop operating without a formal budget or managerial goals.**
4. **Stop developing any product that fails the founder's test** of a more efficient caregiver or a lower treatment cost. The rule exists; it needs enforcing.
5. **Stop treating implants as a near-term destination.** Don't go head-to-head with a pharma-backed rival's approved cemented hip. Revisit only if clinician pull and flywheel momentum make it a natural next niche.
6. **Stop treating the IPO as the next move.** No offering until there are 2–3 years of budgeted, audited earnings growth.
7. **Stop depending on one inventor.** Build a clinician-driven idea pipeline.
8. **Stop carrying bed and stretcher variants where Wexley isn't #1 or #2 and earns below average** `[ASSUMPTION]`; confirm with line P&L before acting.

**Non-negotiable disciplines:** an annual budget and earnings goal per unit; a monthly review of sales and earnings against budget; every product reviewed against the founder's test; every engineer in a hospital or OR on a regular cadence.

**Freedom within a framework:** unit heads run their business, people and pricing. In return they hit budget and the earnings goal and stay inside the Hedgehog Concept.

**Bureaucracy check:** the company's problem today is too little system, not too much. Add measurement, not layers. Don't add approval steps to make up for managers who should be off the bus.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

- **Core values:**
  1. Solve real clinical problems.
  2. Every product earns its place by saving caregiver time or treatment cost.
  3. Own the result.
  4. Integrity with the clinicians who trust us.
- **Core purpose:** Help caregivers deliver better care with less effort and at lower cost.
- **BHAG (20-year):** In every specialty Wexley enters, become the instrument brand surgeons reach for first, as the founder's frame once was.
- **Clock-building mechanisms:** the budget and goal process; decentralized P&L units; the clinical advisory network and idea log; a product review gate using the founder's test; a succession bench for every unit head; and the CEO's own successor named within 5 years.
