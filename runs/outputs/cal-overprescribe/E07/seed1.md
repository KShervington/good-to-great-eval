# Calloway Reading Partners: Good to Great

**Verdict:** This subject is only good, and its current model has run its course. Exit the core business within 18 months, pivot to a new market, replace the leadership team and adopt a new Hedgehog Concept built around the new market.

**Top stop-doing item:** stop operating the core business as it is run today.


## One-screen verdict

**Calloway is good, and in its mature schools it is close to great. The board is being asked tonight to scale the version of the model that is getting worse.**

- **Hedgehog Concept (working hypothesis):** *Calloway turns ordinary adults into deeply trained, closely coached daily tutors who get struggling six-year-olds reading, and it grows only as fast as it can keep that true.*
- **Weakest link in the flywheel:** deep training and close coaching, which produce prepared tutors who stay. Pre-service training was cut 40% (30 to 18 hours). Coach load rose 58% (12 to 19 tutors per coach). Tutor retention fell 13 points, and new-school benchmark rates fell 11 points.
- **Most urgent brutal fact:** schools in their first or second year fell from 69% to 63% to 58% at benchmark across three cohorts. "Ramp-up" does not explain this, because new schools reached 69% two years ago under the same ramp. A five-state launch would make almost every site a new school.
- **Top three stop-doing items:**
  1. Don't accept the $40M grant on its current terms (five states in 30 months, plus three nominated board seats). Counter-propose fidelity-gated expansion instead.
  2. Stop deploying tutors with 18 hours of training and 19:1 coaching.
  3. Stop the math pilot (exit at year end) and shelve ReadAlong.
- **Tonight's three decisions:**
  1. Do not adopt One Million Readers as written. Redefine it as readers *at benchmark*, on a 20-year horizon.
  2. Do not accept the grant as offered. Authorize a counter-proposal.
  3. Approve a revised Year 1 budget that funds fidelity restoration, the research director, and growth from the waitlist, instead of the launch team and ReadAlong.
- **Missing numbers that would change this answer:**
  - New-school results adjusted for student baseline. If the new schools serve much harder populations, part of the decline is composition, not fidelity.
  - Completion rates for the optional online modules.
  - Benchmark results split by tutor training cohort (18 hours vs. 30 hours) and by coach ratio.
  - The philanthropy's real flexibility on its terms, and who funds the new states after Year 5.
  - Board size.
  - Why cost per student × students ($18.0M) is higher than the $14.6M budget.

Greatness potential: 58/100

## 1. The great version in one paragraph

Great Calloway is the organization a district superintendent calls when she wants every struggling first-grader reading, because nobody else turns ordinary adults into effective reading tutors as reliably. Its new schools perform like its mature schools within two years, because every tutor gets the full training and a coach with time for them. It knows its effect is real because it pays for independent evaluation on a fixed cadence and puts its weakest number at the top of every board letter. It grows by density first: its 41-school waitlist, its existing districts, and the tutors who become district teachers. It enters new states only when the last cohort of new schools proves that the model survived the trip. It measures success in readers made per dollar, not children reached. It would keep working the same way if the CEO left tomorrow.

**Confirmation tests (status: working hypothesis):**
- **Explains past wins?** Yes. Both trials and the period of strong new-school results ran under 30-hour training, and `[ASSUMPTION]` coach ratios near 12:1.
- **Rules things out?** Yes: math, ReadAlong, pace set by a funder, and "reach" as the goal.
- **Uncomfortable?** Yes. It means renegotiating a $40M offer and telling a charismatic CEO that the code is not yet cracked for new sites.
- **Evidence that would confirm it:** the Year 1 new-school cohort, with training and coaching restored, recovers to at least 65% at benchmark. Split results show that tutors with 30 hours of training and coach ratios at or below 14:1 outperform tutors with 18 hours and 19:1.
- **Evidence that would disconfirm it:** new-school results stay flat after fidelity is restored. That would point to site selection or population, not training and coaching.

## 5. Stop-doing list

1. **Stop:** accepting the $40M grant on its current terms. Counter-propose: phase 1 is fidelity restoration plus waitlist growth. Phase 2 is one new state. States 3 through 5 are released when new-school benchmark thresholds are met. One nominated seat or an observer role, not three.
2. **Stop:** deploying tutors with 18 hours of pre-service training and optional modules. Restore 30 hours, or make the modules required with verified completion before a tutor works with children.
3. **Stop:** opening schools where the coach ratio would exceed 14:1, moving toward 12:1 in schools in their first or second year.
4. **Stop:** the middle-school math pilot (exit at year end) and the ReadAlong beta (shelve now).
5. **Stop:** measuring success by students reached or cost per student. "One Million Readers" as written counts reach. It also invites counting app users as "readers" `[ASSUMPTION about how reach would be counted]`.
6. **Stop:** delaying the research director hire until after expansion.
7. **Stop:** explaining new-school results as "ramp-up" without an analysis behind the claim.
8. **Stop:** growing the CEO's external speaking calendar. Cap it at roughly 8 events a year `[ASSUMPTION: threshold]`.
9. **Stop:** using stipend increases as the retention lever. Tutors cited preparation and coach time, not pay.

**Non-negotiable disciplines (fanatical consistency):**
- Every tutor works 30 minutes a day with each child, has full pre-service training, and has a coach with time for them. No exceptions for speed.
- Every board dashboard splits results by school cohort. The weakest number leads the CEO's letter.
- An independent outcome evaluation runs at least every 5 years, and the internal outcome study is reinstated annually.
- No new region opens until the last new-school cohort reaches the readiness threshold.

**Freedom within a framework:**
- *Framework (fixed):* dosage, training hours, coach ratio cap, assessment protocol, evaluation cadence.
- *Freedom (local):* coaching style, how sites schedule, how sites build relationships with schools, recruitment channels, how district partnerships are shaped.

**Bureaucracy check:** no rules yet exist only to compensate for unprepared people. They will, though. Under-trained tutors typically produce tighter scripts and compliance checks. Fix preparation instead of adding control.

## 7. Core ideology and the BHAG

**Core purpose:** every child who struggles to read at six learns to read.

**Core values:**
1. **Fidelity before reach.** We would rather teach fewer children to read than reach more children and teach fewer of them.
2. **Evidence over story.** We believe our results only when someone independent has checked them.
3. **Tutors are educators in the making.** We invest in them as people, not as deployment capacity.
4. **Partners, not vendors.** We succeed when the district succeeds.

**BHAG (20-year horizon):** *One Million Readers, counted as children who reach benchmark, not children reached.* Keep the brand and change both the definition and the clock.

Derived arithmetic: compounding 16–17% a year from today's 9,840 students, at about 72% benchmark, gives roughly 0.8 to 1.1 million children at benchmark over 20 years. That rate is just above Calloway's recent 14–18% growth. It is audacious because it requires two decades of sustained fidelity, eventually including new states. It is grounded because the model has already shown this growth rate.

**Clock-building mechanisms (so the system works without the current CEO):**
- A written readiness gate for expansion, owned by program quality and research, not by Growth.
- The training and coaching system codified as a curriculum document that doesn't depend on any one person.
- A coach ratio cap written into the budget rules.
- An independent evaluation every 5 years and an annual internal outcome study, both protected in the budget.
- A cohort-split dashboard as the first page of every board packet.
- A CEO succession plan, plus a funder relationship map that isn't centered on the CEO.
